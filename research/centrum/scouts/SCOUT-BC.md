## Shortlist

### Sweep 4: hidden history

#### Burgwallen-Nieuwe Zijde

**BC-01. The two houses the Victoria Hotel could not buy.** Pin 52.377144, 4.898038; Damrak 1
corner Prins Hendrikkade (the two small dark gables built into the hotel front, old nos. 46-47).
Hook: in 1889 the hotel company bought and demolished the whole corner except two early
17th-century houses whose owners, tailor Pieter August Carstens and spirits dealer Johannes
Frederik Verburgt, asked too much; the hotel was built around them. No. 46 was a knife shop from
1602 to 1742. The story inspired Thomas Rozenboom's novel "Publieke Werken" (Libris Prize 2000,
filmed 2015). Local voice: Amsterdamsche Courant, 10 June 1889: "De perceelen van den kleermaker
Karsten en den slijter Verburgt werden wegens te hooge eischen niet gekocht en zullen
vermoedelijk worden ingebouwd." Lead: https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/de-huisjes-van-het-victoria-hotel-amsterdam ;
second https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Hotel . Status: standing, a souvenir shop inside
(ONH, undated, checked 2026-11-24 from text only). Caveats: monument status of the two houses not
checked. Rank: **Amazing**. New place.

**BC-02. Nieuwezijds Kolk: the castle under the street and the corn measurers' house.**
Pin 52.376110, 4.895000; Nieuwezijds Kolk (Korenmetershuisje). Hook: in 1994 digging for new
building at the Nieuwezijds Kolk found foundations of a brick castle (built c. 1280-1290,
probably by Floris V, gone by 1304), counted as the oldest structure of Amsterdam; possible
castle remains also lie under Dirk van Hasseltssteeg 2-6. Beside it stands the Korenmetershuisje
(1620), the guild house of the corn measurers with measure, barrel and strickle carved over the
door, one of the few guild houses left and one of the few free-standing buildings in the old
centre. Local voice: "Kasteel aan de Amstel". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasteel_van_Amsterdam ;
second https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korenmetershuisje_(Amsterdam) . Status: guild house
standing (rijksmonument); the castle is underground, no marker found in the sources read.
Caveats: check on site for any paving marker. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-03. Torensluis: the footprint of the Jan Roodenpoort tower.** Pin 52.374440, 4.889720;
Singel at the Torensteeg. Hook: the widest bridge of its day was built beside the Jan
Roodenpoortstoren, a medieval wall tower given a 55 m spire in 1616-17 (probably Hendrick de
Keyser) that served as public clock tower and prison; the provost lived below. The tower went in
1829 because the poor city could not maintain it, as did the Haringpakkerstoren at the Singel's
IJ end. The vaulted cellars in the bridgehead survive; Stadsherstel shows a photo of the tower's
footprint on the Torensluis. Both towers were measured before demolition so they could be
rebuilt; Stadsherstel offered to rebuild the Haringpakkerstoren for its 50th birthday and backed
off on 12 April 2010 for fear of the UNESCO nomination. Local voice: neighbours on the Singel
lived "bij de Toren" (Stadsherstel, from the archives). Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Roodenpoortstoren ; second
https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/singel-157/ . Status: cellars said to be open to the public
after restoration (nl.wiki), current use not verified 2026-11-24. Caveats: Multatuli statue sits
on the same bridge (not this theme). Rank: Nice. Group with: Haringpakkerstoren (gone, corner
Singel/Prins Hendrikkade, pin 52.379170, 4.895280 NZ).

**BC-04. The Heilige Stede site: the miracle chapel's replacement.** Pin 52.370220, 4.891870;
Rokin between Wijde and Enge Kapelsteeg. Hook: the chapel built in 1346-47 where the Miracle of
Amsterdam (1345) happened became a Protestant chapel, then a horse stable after 1578; Catholics
revived the procession as the silent Stille Omgang in 1881 from a 1651 route document, and the
Reformed church demolished the chapel in 1908 despite protests. The 1912 replacement (church plus
shops) saw Princess Beatrix confirmed in 1956, was a Turkish mosque 1972-1980 and a conference
centre after. Local voice: "Nieuwezijds Kapel"; the procession "Stille Omgang". Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwezijds_Kapel_(1912) ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilige_Stede . Status: building standing (rijksmonument since
2001), a tourist attraction inside since 2005. Caveats: DC dropped the Mirakelkolom as thin;
this is the building, not the column. Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-05. Hotel Polen fire, 1977.** Pin 52.372197, 4.892578; Papenbroekssteeg through Rokin
Plaza, Rokin/Kalverstraat. Hook: on 9 May 1977 Hotel Polen burned down: 33 dead, the deadliest
fire in the Netherlands since the Second World War; the wooden-framed building had no smoke
detectors and had failed a fire inspection. Rokin Plaza (1981-83) replaced it, with the
Papenbroekssteeg running through. Part of the hotel's De Distel tile tableau survives in the
Keramisch Museum Goedewagen. Local voice: the name came from the "Poolsche Koffiehuis" in the
Kalverstraat. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_in_Hotel_Polen . Status: gone, exact
spot known. Caveats: no on-site memorial found in sources read; single source. Rank: Okay.
New place.

**BC-06. De Papegaai, the Kalverstraat hidden church.** Pin 52.371236, 4.891831; Kalverstraat 58.
Hook: a Catholic station church from 1672 in a rented house on the shopping street; the house
had a compass on its gable (owner Willem Compas), yet the church was nicknamed the Parrot. The
present church (1848-49) was the first neo-Gothic church in Amsterdam. Local voice: "De
Papegaai". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Papegaai_(Amsterdam) . Status: open church.
Caveats: single source; check opening hours. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-07. Kromelleboogsteeg and the Roode Leeuw stones.** Pin 52.372504, 4.892140; Kalverstraat 1
(behind Dam 20). Hook: Kalverstraat 1-5 run back to the closed Kromelleboogsteeg, the alley once
called "Roode Leeuwsteeg" after the inn De Roode Leeuw (from 1454) on its corner with the Dam.
Gable stones on the Peek & Cloppenburg building still show a lion with "Roode Leeuw". Kalverstraat
1 is an early Wijdeveld design (1915-17, jeweller Citroen). Local voice: "Rooleeuwsteegje". Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Roode_Leeuw_(Amsterdam) ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalverstraat_1 . Status: alley closed; stones visible. Rank: Okay.
New place.

**BC-08. The Singel doelen and the 1606 armoury.** Pin 52.367981, 4.889342; Singel 421-425.
Hook: Singel 421 is the Handboogdoelen (c. 1509), the archers' guild house, nicknamed
"Garnalendoelen"; next to it the Militiegebouw (1606), the city armoury with tall doors for the
cannon, later a silk-winding workhouse for girls (1682), the French conscription hall, the city
council chamber in 1926-28, and the room where Max Euwe played part of his 1935 world title
match against Alekhine. The crossbowmen's Voetboogdoelen beside it became the West India Company
headquarters in 1674; its site and that of the Sint-Catharinakerk (demolished 1939) became the
University Library. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militiegebouw ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handboogdoelen_(Amsterdam) . Status: standing; the University
Library left the complex in autumn 2025 (nl.wiki), new use unknown. Caveats: check access.
Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-09. Oude Lutherse Kerk: Lutherans in a warehouse.** Pin 52.368330, 4.889148; Singel 411
corner Spui. Hook: from 1600 Lutherans worshipped in a warehouse called De Vergulden Pot on this
spot; the church followed in 1632-33. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oude_Lutherse_Kerk .
Status: standing, used by the University of Amsterdam for ceremonies (to verify). Rank: Okay.
New place.

**BC-10. Die Port van Cleve: where Heineken began.** Pin 52.374125, 4.890771; Nieuwezijds
Voorburgwal 176-180. Hook: brewery De Hooiberg stood here from 1592 (the widow Weyntgen Elberts);
Gerard Adriaan Heineken bought it in 1863 and moved the brewing to the Stadhouderskade once the
Nieuwezijds Achterburgwal was filled in; the builders converting it to a beer house found the
gable stone "Die Port van Cleve" that gave the name (1870). Spuistraat 129-131 (gable cartouche
1662) belonged to the complex. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Die_Port_van_Cleve ;
second https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spuistraat_129-131_(Amsterdam) . Status: hotel. Caveats:
commercial hotel; the hook is the brewery site. Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-11. Olieslagerssteeg: leper house, oil pressers and Maison de Bonneterie.** Pin
52.368060, 4.891940; Olieslagerssteeg, Kalverstraat/Rokin. Hook: Amsterdam's first leper house,
the Sint Jorishof, stood on the Kalverstraat here; its chapel became the carpenters' guild house
(the "Kistenmakerspand"); oil pressers worked in the alley and sold Haarlemmerolie as a cure-all;
in 1901 Maison de Bonneterie replaced the chapel (rijksmonument since 1974; ONH describes its
1909 interior). Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olieslagerssteeg_(Amsterdam) ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint_Jorishof_(Amsterdam) and
https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/modepaleizen-in-amsterdam . Status: steeg open; store status not
verified 2026-11-24. Rank: Okay. New place.

#### Burgwallen-Oude Zijde

**BC-12. Sint Olofspoort: houses on the gate and the chapel wall.** Pin 52.376284, 4.899940;
Sint Olofspoort 1, Nieuwebrugsteeg 13, Sint Olofssteeg 8. Hook: the Sint Olofspoort, a city
gate beside the 15th-century Sint-Olofskapel, was demolished in 1618 and houses were built on
its site. Sint Olofspoort 1 (c. 1605) uses the chapel's west wall as its back wall; nos. 9-15
were sold in 1618 on condition of not using the old gate wall. Nieuwebrugsteeg 13 was the very
first house bought by Vereniging Hendrick de Keyser (1918), with a gable stone of a hat maker's
workshop ("hoede kramer"). Round the corner Het Jopenvat (1659) fills a triangular plot between
two alleys under a beer-barrel stone. Local voice: the gate name survives as the street name
"Sint Olofspoort". Lead: https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/de-huizen/nieuwebrugsteeg-13 ; second
https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/de-huizen/sint-olofspoort-1 and
https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/de-huizen/het-jopenvat , https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint-Olofskapel .
Status: standing, private (HdK rents them). Rank: **Amazing**. New place.

**BC-13. Het Aepgen, the wooden house on the Zeedijk.** Pin 52.376486, 4.899364; Zeedijk 1.
Hook: c. 1550, one of only two wooden house fronts left in the city; the legend says sailors
paid their lodging with monkeys. Local voice: "in de aap gelogeerd" (to be had, from the monkey
lodging story). Lead: https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/de-huizen/het-aepgen ; second
https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/nieuws/huis-uitgelicht-het-aepgen . Status: standing, café
In 't Aepjen downstairs. Caveats: the monkey payment is legend; well known locally. Rank: Nice.
New place.

**BC-14. Spooksteeg: the ghost of the Zeedijk.** Pin 52.375144, 4.900899; Spooksteeg, between
Zeedijk and Oudezijds Achterburgwal. Hook: in July 1753 and again in 1853 wailing was heard
here that searches could not trace; the 1853 scare drew crowds every night and the alley was
renamed from Waterpoortsteeg to Spooksteeg in 1855. The legend blames Helena, who killed her
sister Dina over a sailor. Local voice: Algemeen Handelsblad 1853: "sedert een tiental dagen,
duizenden elke avond naar de Zeedijk, waar, zo heet het, een spook door akelige geluiden de rust
van eenige huizen stoort." Lead: https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/het-spook-van-de-zeedijk . Status:
alley open. Caveats: DC dropped this for blogs only; ONH now gives the 1853 newspaper quote; the
sisters' tale is legend. Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-15. Mata Hari and the Determeyer skybridge.** Pin 52.374666, 4.899855; Korte Niezel,
Oudezijds Voorburgwal 27-33. Hook: uniform maker Determeyer bought one house after another and
in 1929 built a covered bridge over the Korte Niezel to join the upper floors; it still carries
the departed firm's name. From the early 1970s the complex was a notorious illegal gambling
palace, "Mata Hari"; the city had housing corporation Ymere buy it in 2007 to stop it coming
back. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mata_Hari_(rijksmonument) . Status: standing
(rijksmonument); bridge visible from the alley. Caveats: single source. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-16. Oudezijds Voorburgwal 61: four hundred years without piles.** Pin 52.374131, 4.899070.
Hook: a late-16th-century merchant's house (roof timbers dated 1592-1596) with an osendrop on
both sides; Stadsherstel's foundation survey found no piles at all: the walls stand straight on
the ground ("op staal"). Later a doll factory "Hollandia" (1908) and a bicycle store in the
cellar. Local voice: Stadsherstel, "Vierhonderd jaar zonder palen maar met bijzonder marmer".
Lead: https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/oudezijds-voorburgwal-61/ . Status: standing, homes,
restored 2018. Caveats: DC dropped this as single source; Stadsherstel is the owner and did the
survey, which makes it the primary source. Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-17. Ship Chandlers Warehouse: all its 17th-century glass.** Pin 52.376176, 4.901939;
Geldersekade 8. Hook: built 1623 as a tobacco shop, then a ship's chandler (Jan Vinke, 1804) whose
grandson founded the shipbrokers Vinke & Co. here in 1860; it is said to be the only house in
Amsterdam with original 17th-century leaded windows on every floor, and claims to be the city's
oldest shop. Rococo tobacco ornament over the door (1775). Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Chandlers_Warehouse . Status: standing. Caveats: "oldest
shop" is a claim; ONH gives the pharmacy (BC-18) the same title. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-18. Apotheek Van der Meulen, a pharmacy since 1696.** Pin 52.374337, 4.900623 (approx);
corner Geldersekade/Stormsteeg. Hook: apothecaries have worked on this corner without a break
since 1696; the interior of about 1880 is painted in its original Berlin blue, Bentheim grey and
ochre. Lead: https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/de-geldersekade-en-de-oudste-apotheek-van-amsterdam .
Status: not verified 2026-11-24 (is it still a pharmacy?). Caveats: exact door not pinned. Rank:
Nice. New place.

**BC-19. Geldersekade 78: the swindler Barmat.** Pin 52.374536, 4.901017. Hook: in the 1920s
the "master swindler" Barmat ran a business here; his scandal became, against his will, a
centrepiece of Hitler's antisemitic propaganda. Earlier a perfume factory and a tobacco trade.
Local voice: Stadsherstel, "Hitler gebruikte deze meesteroplichter als schrikbeeld in zijn
propaganda". Lead: https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/geldersekade-78/ . Status: standing, homes.
Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-20. The Bethaniën quarter: convent crypt, cows and Krishna.** Pin 52.372222, 4.898333;
Barndesteeg/Bethaniënstraat. Hook: the north wing of the 15th-century Bethaniënklooster survives
in the Barndesteeg with a vaulted crypt from about 1450; the nuns fattened cows for the civic
guards' dinners, hence the Koestraat. In 1970 the first Hare Krishna temple in the Netherlands
opened at Bethaniënstraat 37-39 ("500 years after the convent there were monks here again").
Local voice: Stadsherstel, "500 Jaar na het Bethaniënklooster waren hier weer monniken". Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethani%C3%ABnklooster ; second
https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/bethanienstraat-37-39/ . Status: standing (rijksmonument
1970), student rooms in the roof. Caveats: group with the existing draft "Barndesteeg"
(places/oudezijde-1.yaml) and DC-18 (Koestraat). Rank: Nice. Group with: draft Barndesteeg.

**BC-21. De Star: the wall of a vanished church, and the Spinhuis gate.** Pin 52.370670,
4.896360; Spinhuissteeg. Hook: the Augustinians' hidden church De Star (in a warehouse from 1698,
rebuilt as a big hall church 1848-49) closed in 1929 as the centre emptied and was almost
entirely demolished in 1955; only a long high wall with Ionic pilasters still stands in the
Spinhuissteeg. Opposite, the Spinhuis gate recalls the women's house of correction of 1597 where
inmates spun and sewed. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Star_(Amsterdam) ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinhuis_(Amsterdam) . Status: wall standing. Rank: Okay.
New place.

**BC-22. Pijlsteeg: an alley through a hotel.** Pin 52.372320, 4.895300; Dam to Oudezijds
Voorburgwal. Hook: once the Apostolensteeg, renamed after mayor Dirk Pijl (late 14th century);
a keurboek of 1413, one of the first Amsterdam documents on prostitution, names it one of the
two streets where it was allowed. Now Hotel Krasnapolsky has built over and around it, but it
stays public; three gable stones from demolished houses sit in the wall at the Wintertuin
entrance, one a wooden chair "Anno 1728" from a chair maker's house. Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pijlsteeg_(Amsterdam) . Status: open. Caveats: Wynand Fockink is
listed under Food and drink. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-23. The murder of Magere Josje, 1957.** Pin 52.373690, 4.898530; Oudezijds Voorburgwal 83.
Hook: sex worker Johanna Scheide-Oudes, "Magere Josje", was found dead in her room on
11 August 1957; Appie Baantjer, then in the murder squad, led the case and never solved it.
Local voice: "Magere Josje". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moord_op_Magere_Josje . Status:
house standing; nothing marks it. Caveats: single source; handle with care. Rank: Okay.
New place.

#### Nieuwmarkt/Lastage

**BC-24. The Nieuwmarkt scaffold: Sjako and the only guillotine.** Pin 52.372573, 4.900503;
Nieuwmarkt square. Hook: the master thief Sjako was broken on the wheel and beheaded here after
his capture in 1717; on 15 June 1812 the French guillotine was used in Amsterdam for the first
and only time, here, on poisoner Hester Rebekka Nepping, her lover and her maid. Local voice:
Sjako, "de Robin Hood van Amsterdam". Lead: https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/de-guillotine-in-amsterdam ;
second https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/sjako-robin-hood-amsterdam . Status: nothing marks it.
Caveats: the Waag is A-list; this is the square's story, not the building. Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-25. Zuiderkerk: mortuary, oldest bell, the architect's grave.** Pin 52.370000, 4.899722.
Hook: the first church built for Protestant worship in Amsterdam (1603-11, Hendrick de Keyser,
the tower's niches left empty); in the Hunger Winter of 1944-45 it was a mortuary where the dead
waited for burial. The tower still rings the "Salvator" (1511), the oldest bell in the city,
taken from the Oude Kerk. De Keyser's gravestone can be seen through a glass plate in the floor.
Tower visits with a guide in summer. Local voice: the bell's motto "Salvator vocor". Lead:
https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/de-zuiderkerk-kerk-mortuarium-en-museum ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuiderkerkstoren_(Amsterdam) . Status: open (exhibition space).
Caveats: DC parked two Zuiderkerk lines for "a Zuiderkerk place": fold them in. Rank: Nice.
New place.

**BC-26. Mozes en Aäronkerk: the Moroccans' church asylum, 1975.** Pin 52.368279, 4.902911;
Waterlooplein 205. Hook: from 7 to 15 November 1975 about a hundred undocumented Moroccan workers
went on hunger strike in the church; on 17 October 1978 these "kerkmarokkanen" got residence
permits. Local voice: "kerkmarokkanen". Lead:
https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/wereldplek-in-amsterdam-mozes-en-aaronkerk-verleende-kerkasiel .
Status: church standing. Caveats: single source. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-27. Cold War shelters under the metro.** Pin 52.366958, 4.903261; Waterlooplein metro
station (Nieuwmarkt station 52.371944, 4.901111). Hook: in the 1960s the city folded atom
shelters into the new east line: Nieuwmarkt station was built to hold 3,000 people behind steel
doors, and a bunker for the mayor and council was added at Waterlooplein with a link to the city
hall. Waterlooplein station also has a permanent display of finds from its digging. Lead:
https://www.onh.nl/verhalen/bijna-vergeten-veertig-verborgen-bunkers-in-amsterdam ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterlooplein_(metrostation) . Status: stations open; shelters
opened only on Open Monumentendag. Caveats: DC-30 is the Nieuwmarkt metro art; group or keep
apart. Rank: Okay. Group with: DC-30.

**BC-28. Goslerhuisje, last house of the Joden Houttuinen.** Pin 52.369856, 4.901685;
Jodenbreestraat 1. Hook: a low, slightly skewed lock-keeper's house, the only building left of
the Joden Houttuinen, cleared in the late 1960s for the Maupoleum (itself demolished in the
1990s); named after A. Gosler and Sons, iron dealers and demolition contractors. A few doors on,
the Jewish cinema Tip Top (Jodenbreestraat 25-27) was demolished in 1953; the theatre school on
the site named its small hall Tip Top. Local voice: "Maupoleum", called one of the ugliest
buildings in the city. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goslerhuisje ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tip_Top_Theater . Status: standing (rijksmonument 1973). Rank: Nice.
New place.

**BC-29. Schottenburch, the oldest warehouse.** Pin 52.371960, 4.902290; Kromboomssloot 18-20.
Hook: a double warehouse with a 1636 stone, already on Pieter Bast's map of 1597; its plot runs
askew to its neighbours, possibly from before the Lastage came inside the walls (c. 1585). From
1979 to 1988 an artists' initiative held shows and performances here. Local voice: "oudste nog
bestaande pakhuis van Amsterdam". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schottenburch . Status:
standing. Caveats: the "oldest" title is contested (nl.wiki says "one of the oldest"). Rank:
Nice. New place.

**BC-30. Saaihal, the Staalmeesters' hall.** Pin 52.368345, 4.897091; Staalstraat 7. Hook: built
1641 by Pieter de Keyser as part of the Staalhof, the cloth-control centre known from Rembrandt's
"De Staalmeesters". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saaihal_(Amsterdam) . Status: standing.
Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-31. Christ Church on Hendrick de Keyser's workshop.** Pin 52.368582, 4.897734; Groenburgwal
42. Hook: the English church stands on the site of Hendrick de Keyser's stonemason's yard.
Lead: https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/groenburgwal-42-christchurch/ . Status: standing.
Caveats: check what Stadsherstel shows of the yard. Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-32. Diamantslijperij Boas.** Pin 52.369587, 4.904066; Nieuwe Uilenburgerstraat 173-175.
Hook: diamond-cutting factory of 1879 with a free-standing boiler house, a survivor of Uilenburg's
Jewish diamond industry. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamantslijperij_Boas . Status:
standing. Rank: Okay. New place.

### Sweep 5: behind the gate

#### Burgwallen-Nieuwe Zijde

**BC-33. Rasphuispoort and the swimming pool behind it.** Pin 52.367729, 4.891063; Heiligeweg,
opposite the Voetboogstraat. Hook: Hendrick de Keyser's gate (c. 1603) to the Rasphuis, where
young men rasped Brazil wood into dye powder; the relief shows a cart of logs drawn by lions,
bears, boars and tigers, under a woman with a whip labelled "Castigatio". Behind it, invisible
from the street, the Heiligewegbad (1896-1987), at its opening the biggest indoor pool in Europe
and subject of one of the first Dutch films (1896); its steam bath was a discreet meeting place
for gay men in the 1940s and 1950s. Now a side entrance of the Kalvertoren mall. Local voice:
"Heiligebad". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasphuispoortje ; second
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligewegbad . Status: gate standing. Rank: **Amazing**.
New place.

**BC-34. Makelaers Comptoir.** Pin 52.375346, 4.892667; Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal 75. Hook: the
brokers' guild house of 1633 on a triangular plot, with the best-preserved 17th-century front
hall in the city; Vereniging Hendrick de Keyser lets it for small events, the only way in.
Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makelaers_Comptoir ; second: the Hendrick de Keyser house
page (read, URL not kept). Status: standing. Rank: Okay. New place.

**BC-35. Oude Braak and Nieuwezijds Armsteeg: the dike-arm lanes.** Pin 52.376800, 4.895400;
Oude Braak. Hook: the Oude Braak, once "De Break" after a dike breach, was a web of alleys
between Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal and Nieuwendijk; only part of it is public now, the rest is for
residents, with old warehouses. The curving Nieuwezijds Armsteeg next to it began as a dike arm;
digging in 1984 found the 2 m clay wall of 1342 with its palisade. Local voice: part was called
"Kromme Elleboogsteeg" until the council dropped the name. Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oude_Braak ; second https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwezijds_Armsteeg .
Status: partly closed. Rank: Okay. New place.

#### Burgwallen-Oude Zijde

**BC-36. Het Veilinghuisje: the house everyone thought was demolished.** Pin 52.375410,
4.899230; Oudezijds Voorburgwal 28 (on the inner court). Hook: a small auction house of a known
bookselling family (Bom, from 1882) on an inner court boxed in by Warmoesstraat, Oudezijds
Armsteeg, Oudezijds Voorburgwal and Heintje Hoekssteeg, invisible from the street. It was due to
be demolished in 1989 and everyone thought it had been, so the city left it off its monument
survey; Stadsherstel found it standing, had it listed and restored it. The court also held one of
the first sugar bakeries (dug up), and next door at no. 30 the Anthony Theater's floor
collapsed in 1991. Local voice: Stadsherstel, "werd in 1989 gesloopt. Althans dat was de bedoeling
en iedereen dacht ook dat dat gebeurd was." Lead: https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/oudezijds-voorburgwal-28/ ;
second https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/oudezijds-voorburgwal-28-brouwerij-de-prael/ . Status:
standing; glimpses from the De Prael brewery premises (brewery status not verified 2026-11-24).
Rank: **Amazing**. New place.

**BC-37. Gebed zonder end.** Pin 52.369761, 4.893771;
alley from the Kuiperssteeg to the Grimburgwal, entered under a gate. Hook: the name, "Prayer
without end", probably recalls the convents of the "Stille Zijde"; the alley runs over the
grounds of the Sint Claraconvent (c. 1397). The name is painted on the wall because the sign
kept being stolen. Local voice: "Gebed zonder end". Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebed_zonder_end . Status: open, pedestrian. Rank: Nice. New place.

#### Nieuwmarkt/Lastage

**BC-38. Uilenburger Synagoge, reached only by alleys.** Pin 52.370212, 4.905264; Nieuwe
Uilenburgerstraat 91. Hook: an Ashkenazi synagogue of 1766 built behind the street houses,
closer to the water than the road, for centuries reachable only through passages; it survived
the slum clearances and was looted in the war. The screened yard was later the city's
monument yard, which left a bridge stone, a city arms tableau from the Oude Haarlemmersluis (set
1957) and a stone for the restoration workshop (closed 1974). Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uilenburger_Synagoge . Status: used by Beit Ha'Chidush and for
concerts. Caveats: check public access. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-39. Oudezijds Huiszittenhuis: the poor-relief courtyard.** Pin 52.368849, 4.901637;
Waterlooplein 69 (gate on the Nieuwe Amstelstraat). Hook: built 1655 (Willem de Keyser) to hand
out bread and peat to the poor; behind it a courtyard with arcaded galleries, and a regents' room
with a painted ceiling and a Van der Helst; the Academie van Bouwkunst has used it since 1946.
Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudezijds_Huiszittenhuis . Status: school; courtyard access
not verified. Rank: Nice. New place.

**BC-40. A.S. Onderwijzerhof, a hofje from the 1980s.** Pin 52.367095, 4.903389. Hook: a small
square-cum-hofje made during the 1980s renewal of this part of the Jewish quarter. Lead:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.S._Onderwijzerhof . Status: open. Rank: Okay. New place.

  monumental brown café". Renamed from "Zum Pilsner Club" at the start of the war.

  not checked.
