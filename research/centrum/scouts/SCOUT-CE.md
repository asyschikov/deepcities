## Shortlist

### Sweep 6: modern and contested

#### De Weteringschans

**CE-01. Weteringschans 81-89: where the Grote Wetering squat fell, with Sinterklaas at the barricade (gone).** Pin 52.361200, 4.887130 (the 1983 building on the site, Weteringschans 81-89, on the corner of the Spiegelgracht line). A dairy cooperative's eclectic block of 1892-93 (Joseph Cuypers, son of the Rijksmuseum architect) was bought in the 1970s by the property dealer Gerard Bakker, who set out to demolish it; alderman Jan Schaefer stood for renovation, the empty part was squatted, Bakker started knocking down without a permit and was ordered by the court to restore it, and a request to list the building was refused. On 2 December 1980 the riot police cleared it in one of the hard evictions of that year; the city even sent Sinterklaas along, which backfired. The squatters were driven in a city bus to the Bijlmer prison and released at Amstel station. The new block of shops, offices and homes stood by 1983. Local voice: Ons Amsterdam "Hier gebeurde het: Weteringschans 2 december 1980" (to read in the browser). Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grote_Wetering_(Amsterdam) ; second: Ons Amsterdam article (linked from the wiki), Delpher papers of 3 Dec 1980. Status: the 1983 building stands; nothing marks the eviction. Caveats: gone thing with a strong story and an exact spot; the Peper en Zout offices (wetering-3) are the other half of the same Bakker fight: could be a paragraph there instead. **Okay**; new place or merge into "Peper en Zout, the hated offices that became monuments".

**CE-02. The robot car park under the Vijzelgracht and its poem cabins.** Pin 52.361500, 4.891700 (the glass lift cabins on the Vijzelgracht, over the metro station; check: Location of the cabins). Over the Noord/Zuidlijn station Vijzelgracht a 120 m concrete box holds 270 cars parked by a fully automatic system: the driver leaves the car in a glass cabin on the street and a machine takes it down. The cabins, designed with the neighbourhood by Angie Abbink and Raymond Verkade, echo the weavers' houses nearby and the steel bridges, and the street paving runs on into them; city poet Marjolijn van Heemstra's lines are set in the glass (lettering by Roosje Klap) and light up when sun or headlights hit them. Opened 29 August 2023; the city took parking places off the street in return. The Vijzelgracht itself was filled in 1933, so the name is a fib. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkeergarage_Vijzelgracht ; second needed (gemeente project page or Benthem Crouwel). Status: open (2023). **Okay**; new place.

**CE-25. Melkweg, the milk factory that became a hippie "cosmic centre".** Pin 52.364837, 4.881273 (Lijnbaansgracht 234A). A sugar refinery from 1854 (successor of the 18th-century "De Granaatappel", Spakler & Tetterode) became a dairy in 1920 and closed in 1969; in summer 1970 a youth project moved into the empty factory with a tea house, a restaurant and one hall for music, film and theatre, repeated in 1971 and 1972, and since 1973 it has been open for good: a non-profit foundation that grew from a hippie hangout into the city's pop stage (U2's first gig outside Ireland, Nirvana, Prince), with a cinema and an exhibition space. Since 2009 the Rabozaal hangs between the Melkweg and the Stadsschouwburg. Together with the drafted Paradiso, the other half of the 1970s free-space pair round the Leidseplein; Paradiso is pitched on its church past, this one on the occupation of an empty factory. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkweg_(Amsterdam) ; second: https://www.melkweg.nl/en/about-us/history/ (read 24 Nov 2026). Status: open. Caveats: a commercial-scale venue many visitors know; not on A-LIST.md (judge against it); the building's monument status to check. **Nice**; new place.

#### Weesperbuurt/Plantage

**CE-03. Weesperstaete, "De Doodskist".** Pin 52.361600, 4.907300 (Weesperplein, corner Sarphatistraat; Weesperplein 8 to confirm). Alexander Bodon's nine-storey office of 1969, called "the coffin" by Amsterdammers, was a dark glass box that Ids Haagsma called a copy of Mies van der Rohe's Seagram Building; Bodon wanted two more floors and a crown but the aesthetics committee refused. It stands back from the Sarphatistraat because that street, like the Weesperstraat, was to become a wide traffic artery in the 1960s "cityvorming", and a metro entrance is built into its base. Let to the university (psychology) in 1971, then a polytechnic; stripped and given opening windows in a renovation after 2003; now the city's development company. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weesperstaete ; second: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/weesperstaete/ (Arcam: "Doodskist in het centrum", the dark glass replaced by clear glass in ZZDP's 2005 renovation, done in consultation with Bodon's son, so the coffin is a little lighter now). Status: in use. Caveats: the nickname is in both sources. **Okay**; new place, or a Look for in the drafted Weesperflat / Diamantbeurs places.

**CE-26. Professor Tulpplein: a professor who never was, and the houses on a sinking bastion.** Pin 52.360050, 4.906132 (Professor Tulpstraat 10, the even side; the square in front of the Amstel Hotel). The council named the square on 29 September 1875 after Nicolaes Tulp, Rembrandt's anatomy lecturer, who was called "professor" but never held a chair: he taught the surgeons' guild. The houses of the Professor Tulpstraat were built in 1875 on top of the demolished bastion Oosterblokhuis and in the 20th century began to subside; plans to pull them down met fierce resistance from the neighbourhood and Stadsherstel saved most of them. At no. 4 on the square the Amstel Hotel's livery stable grew into a diamond-trading building that drew diamond dealers to the street (municipal monument 2002, also as Jewish heritage). Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Tulpplein ; second needed (Stadsherstel project page or the monument register for Prof. Tulpplein 4). Status: in place. Caveats: the Amstel Hotel (plantage-2) holds the square's no. 1; rename/myth item, light on things to see beyond the street. **Okay**; new place.

**CE-11. Weesperstraat, the Jewish quarter's high street turned city motorway.** Pin 52.363900, 4.906000 (Weesperstraat at the Nieuwe Prinsengracht, by Uytenhaak's block; check: Location). The narrow "Wazepergas" of the Jewish quarter (Vroom & Dreesmann opened their first shop at no. 70 in 1887) was emptied by the deportations and stripped for firewood in the hunger winter; the 1953 reconstruction plan made it a four-lane road from the IJ tunnel to the Wibautstraat, the remaining houses were pulled down in the late 1950s and early 1960s and the street was shut to all traffic 1961-65. The east side got the big 1960s blocks (Hertzberger's student flat, Arthur Staal's Metropool, now the Zoku hotel); the west side stayed bare while the first metro line was sunk in concrete caissons built on the surface (1970-77, with protests in 1975), and was rebuilt only in the 1980s and 1990s, most visibly by Rudy Uytenhaak (1992), whose concrete screens carry the rhythm of canal houses up to the traffic. Plans to narrow the road stalled for years; by 2022 part of it was cut back to two lanes. Look for the 1990s "miniature town" fronts, the canal bridges named after Jewish Amsterdammers, the Namenmonument on the green strip. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weesperstraat_(Amsterdam) ; second: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/woningen-weesperstraat/ (Arcam guide). Status: in use; narrowing ongoing. Caveats: neighbours DE-28 (embroidered wall), DE-23, AE-33 and the drafted Weesperflat; this item is pitched on the cut itself. **Nice**; new place.

**CE-12. Steven Holl's sponge on the Sarphatistraat.** Pin 52.362450, 4.916700 (the pavilion on the Singelgracht side of Sarphatistraat 410; check: Location). In 2000 the American architect Steven Holl turned the 19th-century state medicine store (Rijksmagazijn van Geneesmiddelen) into the head office of housing association Het Oosten (now Stadgenoot), keeping its columns, timber trusses and cast-iron stairs, and added a pavilion on the water wrapped in perforated copper plate over a second perforated skin, with bright colour fields that flicker through the holes ("Patterns in a chromatic field"); at night it glows. One of the few buildings in Amsterdam by a foreign star architect of that generation, and best seen from across the Singelgracht. Lead: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/campus-stadgenoot/ ; second: https://www.archdaily.com/201033/flashback-sarphatistraat-offices-steven-holl-architects . Status: in use (Stadgenoot campus, extended 2012 with "The Bell", Sarphatistraat 370). Caveats: offices, outside only. **Nice**; new place.

**CE-13. Roeterseiland: the bridge building with its legs cut off.** Pin 52.363300, 4.911100 (gebouw B/C over the Nieuwe Achtergracht, from the Theodore Limpergbrug; check: Location). The university's 1960s science buildings on the old tram depot were remade from 2012 for the social sciences, economics and law: AHMM removed the four lowest floors of the building that bridged the Nieuwe Achtergracht, so that the canal became the open spine of the campus instead of a culvert, with the restaurant hanging just above the water; on its roof, the highest of the complex at 46 m, the UvA planned a silent vertical wind turbine (2021). CREA, the students' cultural centre, kept the front of the 1845 diamond factory with a new inner wall against the music. Neighbours won the courtyard "Petit Versailles" from a planned teaching block (DE-31). Lead: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/uva-roeterseiland/ ; second: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roeterseiland and https://www.buurtorganisatie1018.nl/nieuws-uit-1018/2021/11/2/windturbine-op-dak-uva . Status: in use; wind turbine not confirmed as built (check). Caveats: all buildings have letters A to M, but there is no F (Wikipedia, a nice line). **Okay**; new place, or merge DE-31 into it.

#### Grachtengordel-Zuid

**CE-21. Thorbecke, who turned his back on his square and nearly lost it to the fair.** Pin 52.365058, 4.895853 (the statue at the head of the Thorbeckeplein, OSM). Unveiled on 18 May 1876, the day after the old Reguliersplein was renamed for the statesman, the statue by Ferdinand Leenhoff looks not over "his" square but down the Reguliersgracht. Amsterdammers suspected the city of planting Rembrandt and Thorbecke on the old fairground squares on purpose to block the September kermis, which was banned that very year; in the Kermisoproer of 11 September 1876 some 300 rioters went for the mayor's windows, and their song demanded "Thorbecke van het plein". The square, a strip of nightclubs with racy shows until the 1970s, was redone in 2010-11 (statue away nine months, lent for 14 weeks to the art centre Stroom in The Hague), its bandstand went to Artis and its Sunday Modern Art Market of 25 years moved to the Rembrandtplein. Thorbecke is said to have disliked Amsterdam. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorbeckeplein ; second: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermisoproer . Status: in place; listed (rijksmonument, with its railing). Caveats: next to the A-list Rembrandtplein but a separate square; statue is theme A ground too; "disliked Amsterdam" is trivia, tell it as hearsay. **Amazing** (a start-here pick: a statue you can stand at, a riot, a renaming and a stubborn gaze); new place.

#### Oostelijke Eilanden/Kadijken

**CE-14. The marechaussee's black box with 1,750 windows.** Pin 52.373600, 4.917000 (north end of the Marineterrein by the Kattenburgerstraat; check: Location). Wansleben Architekten's office for the Royal Military Police (2008) copies the exact footprint and the fortress-like courtyard of the Admiralty's sea store of 1656 (now the Scheepvaartmuseum) at the other end of the same terrain, but clad in black stone with 1,750 identical sunken windows that hide the floors and keep the staff unseen from outside; a corridor between façade and offices guarantees anonymity, while the inner court is bright with glass and coloured wooden shutters. Train passengers and cyclists off the Jan Schaeferbrug see it first. Lead: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/marinekazerne-amsterdam/ ; second needed (Wansleben site). Same terrain, by the water opposite NEMO: two navy schools of 1962 raised on pilotis "by Le Corbusier's five points"; one of them (Gebouw 027E, SLA 2016) got wooden slats in the pattern of European flags over its big south windows for the Dutch EU presidency of 2016 (Arcam, https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/gebouw-027e-marineterrein/). Status: in use (military, closed). **Okay**; new place, or a Look for in "Marineterrein, the navy yard behind the wall" (with 027E as a second Look for).

**CE-04. Het Funen: sixteen blocks, sixteen architects, and a park that isn't one.** Pin 52.369400, 4.931000 (the central footpath of the Funenpark; check: Location). On the old Van Gend & Loos goods yard, empty and run down from the late 1970s, Frits van Dongen laid out 551 homes in sixteen free-standing blocks, each by a different office (NL Architects, Claus en Kaan, Dick van Gameren and others), in a car-free green where all paving is five-sided tiles of many sizes and greys. It was named in 2003 after the Danish island; despite the name it is a street, not a park. The archaeologists found the walls of bastion Zeeburg, now shown in Gabriel Lester's "Tussentijd" (AE-30). Amsterdammers voted the last block, "Verdana", the city's best new building of 2011, against the experts, and the scheme won the national Gouden Piramide the same year. Local voice: the fight next door: in March 2020 developer VORM wrapped the trees on the corner of the Panamalaan and Cruquiuskade in nets so birds could not nest and delay its "Fibonacci" tower, which the Funen residents opposed (1018, 25 March 2020; objections to the zoning plan ran to Jan 2019). Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funenpark ; second: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/het-funen/ (Arcam: bastion Zeeburg, the last of the 26, sold for demolition in 1877; the L-shaped block with glass scales is the noise screen for the rest). Status: built 2000s, in use. **Nice**; new place (AE-30 as its Look for line).

**CE-05. Mediamatic Biotoop, the art lab on the old city yard.** Pin 52.376617, 4.913154 (Dijksgracht 6). Mediamatic, a media-art foundation since 1983 (Mediamatic Magazine, the 1993 Doors of Perception conference, the "El HEMA" Arabic Hema of 2007), moved here in 2014 after Bugaboo bought the Van Gendthallen from under it, and turned the former municipal works yard into a "biotope": exhibition space in the Sluisdeurenloods, a bio lab, an aquaponics farm, allotments, a little harbour and a plant-based restaurant. The founders left in 2024 after forty years; a young team now runs it. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediamatic ; second: https://www.mediamatic.net/en/contact (open Mon to Fri 12:00 to 17:30, Sat 14:00 to 17:30; restaurant TestTafel Wed to Sat evenings, read 24 Nov 2026). Status: open (Nov 2026). Caveats: the Sluisdeurenloods is drafted in eilanden-1 ("Sluisdeurenloods, the wooden shed that moved for the IJ tunnel"): merge there or keep both and cross-refer; the 2020 "Serres Séparées" (diners in small greenhouses on the quay during the pandemic) is the hook the world knows: a second source is needed before using it. **Nice**; new place or merge into the Sluisdeurenloods.

**CE-17. The navy's firing range, now a roof garden facing NEMO.** Pin 52.374000, 4.914450 (the former Schietbaan at the north-west tip of the Marineterrein; check: Location of the stair). A long, windowless concrete block where the navy did its shooting practice, for years left to graffiti writers, was reopened on 12 October 2023 with a new steel frame and a wide stair that is also a grandstand, leading to a roof garden with a view over the Oosterdok, NEMO and the sunset; a bread hatch of the Noord bakery Kometenbrood sits below. Everything is reused, because everything on the terrain is temporary: the steel came from an old cow shed in Brabant, the timber stood fifteen years in a Frisian sheet-pile wall. It also housed the city's first "Buitenbroedplaats" for young artists (May 2021). Local voice: the 1018 paper calls it a special place for the view and the sunset. Lead: https://www.buurtorganisatie1018.nl/nieuws-uit-1018/2023/8/28/de-schietbaan-op-het-marineterrein-is-omgevormd-tot-een-nieuwe-plek ; second: https://marineterrein.nl/experimenten/transformatie-schietbaan/ (read 24 Nov 2026). Status: open (Marineterrein site 2026). Caveats: temporary use, can change; pin approximate. **Nice**; new place.

**CE-18. Wilhelmina Blombergplein: named for a woman the city thought was dead.** Pin 52.365287, 4.923156 (Wilhelmina Blombergplein 3, behind the Oranje-Nassau Kazerne). A square made in 1990 when the barracks site was turned into homes, named that year after Wilhelmina Blomberg (1908-1997), who fought for better housing in the Jordaan. The city does not name streets after living people; during the naming it simply did not know she was still alive, so she lived seven more years with a square of her own. Look for the "Stenen tapijt" (stone carpet) laid by Agnes van Genderen across the whole square, and two little elephants at the foot of the Oliphantsbrug over the Singelgracht (maker unknown). Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmina_Blombergplein ; second needed (the city's street-name register, straatnamen.amsterdam.nl, for the 1990 decision). Status: in place (Wikipedia photos). Caveats: the barracks are drafted ("Oranje-Nassau Kazerne, Napoleon's barracks where the Artis lions slept"): could be a Look for there. **Okay**; new place or Look for.

**CE-19. Nieuwe-Hoofdhof, a hofje of the 1970s with an Aldo van Eyck sandbox.** Pin 52.370737, 4.917181 (Nieuwe-Hoofdhof 45; the sandbox in the inner square). Amsterdam has hofjes from the 14th century on; this one was made in the clearance of Kattenburg in the 1970s, when the old street plan was partly wiped out, and named only on 21 April 1993, after the bastion Nieuwe Hooft. A footpath runs through a gate on the Kattenburgerplein to the Kattenburgerkade. A relic of the first renewal years survives in it: a sandbox by Aldo van Eyck, who designed some 700 playgrounds for the city after the war, and a 1970 sculpture, the twin sister of Theo Niermeijer's "Het westen ontvangt en ontmoet 't oosten". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe-Hoofdhof (sandbox photographed 2022) ; second needed (a Van Eyck playground inventory). Status: sandbox there in 2022. Caveats: the Kattenburg renewal and fake fronts are BE-23 and BE-24: group with them if preferred. **Okay**; new place or group with BE-23.

### Sweep 7: curiosities, green corners and odd museums

#### De Weteringschans

**CE-06. Pipe Museum, the world's smokes in a 1670 canal house.** Pin 52.364126, 4.885348 (Prinsengracht 488). A private collection begun in 1969, shown on the Frederiksplein (1975-82), in Leiden (1982-95) and here since 1995; renamed from "Pijpenkabinet" in 2013. Over 30,000 objects in store and about 2,000 on show: pre-Columbian pipes, clay pipes dug up in Dutch soil, opium pipes, meerschaum carvings and the pipe makers' moulds, in period rooms; every visitor gets a personal welcome and tour. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Pipe_Museum ; second: https://pipemuseum.nl/en/ (Mon to Sat 12:00 to 18:00, 15 euro, read 24 Nov 2026). Status: open (Nov 2026). Caveats: the old domain amsterdampipemuseum.nl no longer resolves (do not use); paid entry. **Nice**; new place.

**CE-07. Max Euwe Centrum, chess in the old remand prison.** Pin 52.362795, 4.883176 (Max Euweplein 26, since the move of 31 Dec 2023). A small museum of the only Dutch world chess champion (1935) and a library of about 11,000 chess books (a Ruy López of 1584 among them), set up after Euwe's death with mayor Ed van Thijn's help and since 13 December 1991 in the former Huis van Bewaring; about twenty volunteers; the city stopped its subsidy in 2016. Each summer, around 1 July, a chess festival takes over the square, where a giant board is set in the paving (check). Local voice: after 32 years the owner wanted the space back; the centre resisted, lost, and moved some 50 m to no. 26 in the same building, paid for by a donors' campaign (maxeuwe.nl, Dec 2023). Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Euwe_Centrum ; second: https://maxeuwe.nl/en/max-euwe-centre-mec-open-again/ (reopened Feb 2024; free entry, Tue to Sat 12:00 to 16:00, read 24 Nov 2026). Status: open (Nov 2026). Caveats: BE-08 tells the prison and Anne Frank; this item is pitched on the chess; giant board to confirm on site. **Nice**; new place, or group with BE-08.

**CE-08. De Nieuwe Schatkamer: the gold vault you can walk into.** Pin 52.359364, 4.900123 (Frederiksplein 61). After its rebuild De Nederlandsche Bank opened its basement, where the country's gold and banknotes were once stored, to anyone from March 2025: an exhibition on money and gold in the old vault ("kluis experience"), a lab for testing forged and damaged notes, the bank's money and art collections, debates; the garden and quay are open too. Lead: https://www.dnb.nl/de-nieuwe-schatkamer/ ; second: https://www.denieuweschatkamer.nl/ (current exhibition on the digital euro, June to Dec 2026). Status: open (Nov 2026). Caveats: opening hours for individual visitors to confirm. **Nice**; group with DE-44 (DNB garden) as one place: "De Nederlandsche Bank: garden, quay and gold vault open to all".

#### Weesperbuurt/Plantage

**CE-09. Sarphatistraat 66, the doll's house that stares back.** Pin 52.361241, 4.909193 (Sarphatistraat 66). A richly stuccoed neoclassical house of 1873-78 by J.H. Schmitz, built for diamond dealers who never lived in it, home to diamond workshops until the 1970s and a small hotel until about 2022; after a renovation it was for sale in 2024 for almost seven million euro, and behind the windows of the bel-étage dolls stand looking out at the street, which earned it the nicknames "poppenhuis" and "spookhuis". Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarphatistraat_66 (photos June 2024) ; second needed. Status: dolls seen June 2024; no later evidence (check: Still there?). Caveats: private house, municipal monument; one source. **Okay**; new place.

**CE-15. Artisplein: Artis for free, from a chair by the pond.** Pin 52.366469, 4.913261 (the "Watertafel" fountain on the Artisplein, corner Plantage Kerklaan / Plantage Middenlaan). In 2014 Artis pulled down its Geological Museum to open a public square (design Michael van Gessel) between the restored members' rooms (Ledenlokalen) and the Groote Museum: anyone may pull up a chair by the pond and watch the flamingos, and the long fountain at the park side runs along the "Hollandse Polder", an aviary that shows the meadow landscape before the city came, with what Arcam calls the largest collection of spoonbills in the world. A zoo that turned one corner into a city park without a ticket. Lead: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/artisplein/ ; second: OSM (Watertafel, Hollandse Polder) and Artis's own site (to check opening hours of the square). Status: open (Arcam, page current 2026). Caveats: Artis is A-list; this is pitched on the free square beside it; the spoonbill claim is Arcam's alone. **Nice**; new place.

**CE-22. Luther Museum in De Wittenberg, the Lutherans' old people's home.** Pin 52.365524, 4.908850 (Nieuwe Keizersgracht 570). A museum that opened on 22 June 2019 in the building the Evangelical Lutheran diaconate put up in 1772 as its home for old men and women and named after the town where Luther posted his theses; the regents' rooms, the corridor and the church hall are the main exhibits, with paintings (Breenbergh, Ruisdael), silver and furniture from the diaconate's collection. It tells a story most Amsterdammers never heard: in the 17th century about a fifth of the city was Lutheran, many of them German and Scandinavian immigrants. It runs concerts in the church hall and current exhibitions ("Vrij Denken", Luther and Erasmus, from 28 Oct 2026), and made a podcast on the churches and slavery (2021). Lead: https://luthermuseum.nl/en (read 24 Nov 2026) ; second: Buurtorganisatie 1018 news, "Luther Museum in De Wittenberg gaat op 22 juni open" (May 2019) and later concert notices (2019-2021). Status: open (Nov 2026; hours on the site, ticketed). Caveats: the 1772 building is a listed monument, check the drafts for a monument record of De Wittenberg; the Van Brants Rus hofje draft (plantage-1) already mentions Wittenberg's nurses. **Nice**; new place.

#### Oostelijke Eilanden/Kadijken

**CE-20. The floating ball in the Oostenburgerpark, and the llamas.** Pin 52.368969, 4.925823 (the Kogelbron, OSM). After the 1980s clearance of Oostenburg the left-over open space became a park (named 18 October 1989), and the city ordered a "Kugelbrunnen" from the German artist Christian Tobin (then Christian Mayer): a red granite ball lying in a stone cup, lifted by a thin film of water so that it floats and a child can spin it by hand; placed about 1987, older sister of the one at the RAI. Since 2021 the park is also a stop on the islands' mosaic route (five mosaic sculptures, started by two residents); here it is a pair of llamas. From the park you look at the INIT building and the back of the VOC's East India warehouse. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kogelbron_(Oostenburgerpark) ; second: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oostenburgerpark and OSM. Status: llamas photographed May 2022; is the ball still floating? (check). **Okay**; new place.

**CE-23. Plukbos, the food forest behind the Wittenburg blocks.** Pin 52.372253, 4.921629 (the garden on the inner terrain between Jacob Burggraafstraat, Grote Wittenburgerstraat, Tweede Wittenburgerdwarsstraat and the Kattenburgervaart; check: entrance). About 1,000 m2 of inner courtyard on Wittenburg turned by neighbours into a small urban food forest with fruit trees, herbs, a greenhouse and vegetable beds, part of the "green carpet" of Centrum; it is open day and night "for quiet neighbours and passers-by" and you may pick a fruit or cut your own tea as you walk. A tuinclub for children after school, a garden day with lunch every first Sunday, compost for the street, and each winter a "Kerst-BnB", an asylum where potted Christmas trees spend the year. It came out of the Groenplan Oostelijke Eilanden (2018-19). Lead: https://plukbos.nl/ (agenda for Nov 2026) ; second: https://vanamsterdamsebodem.nl/stadshelden/plukbos/ and https://www.buurtorganisatie1018.nl/groen-oostelijke-eilanden . Status: active (agenda Nov 2026). Caveats: gardening times are fixed, but access is said to be always open (check on site). **Nice**; new place.

**CE-24. Dijksgracht-Oost, the railway bank where a polecat stopped the bike path.** Pin 52.375700, 4.918000 (the overgrown bank between the Dijksgracht-Oost and the railway, between the Kattenburgerstraat and the Mariniersbrug; approximate). A strip of scrub along the tracks east of the station that residents have defended for a decade: in 2015 a petition to save the "groene oase" of the Dijksgracht, in 2022 a court case over the foraging ground of common pipistrelle bats (lost), after which ecologists still halted the clearing because birds were nesting; and in February 2026 polecat droppings turned up in one of the marten boxes, so the city had to stop building its cycle path until September: the polecat is on the red list with extra protection in Noord-Holland. The city now promises brush piles and shrubs along the whole path. A curious place to look for city wildlife at dusk, between NEMO, the trains and the new towers. Lead: https://www.buurtorganisatie1018.nl/nieuws-uit-1018/2026/3/31/fietspad-dijksgracht-oost-bunzing-vertraagt-aanleg-en-omwonenden-verzetten-zich-tegen-het-nieuwe-kruispunt ; second: https://www.buurtorganisatie1018.nl/nieuws-uit-1018/2022/5/11/dijksgracht-oost-broedende-vogels-en-foerageeergebied-van-de-dwergvleermuis-voorlopig-veiliggesteld (both 1018: a second, independent source still needed, e.g. the city's project page or the Omgevingsdienst exemption). Status: works paused until Sept 2026, path not before early 2027. Caveats: a construction zone; nothing to "see" by day but the bank; pin approximate; one source family. **Okay**; new place.

#### Grachtengordel-Zuid

**CE-16. Hans Brinker Budget Hotel, proudly the worst.** Pin 52.363679, 4.888055 (Kerkstraat 136). A hostel since 1970 in a former Catholic convent (the Sint-Piusgesticht; Cuypers's chapel of 1868 is gone), with 507 bunk beds, famous far beyond Amsterdam for its anti-advertising by Erik Kessels of KesselsKramer: before-and-after photos of guests leaving in worse shape than they came, and boasts of a view of a brick wall. Arcam lists it in its architecture guide with the hotel's own claim that guests come for the atmosphere, not the architecture. Lead: https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/hans-brinker-budget-hotel/ ; second needed (KesselsKramer or an advertising history source). Status: open (Arcam 2026). Caveats: a commercial hostel; the story is the advertising. **Okay**; new place.

**CE-10. The krullen of the canal belt: Amsterdam's open-air urinals.** Pin 52.364351, 4.889736 (the krul at Keizersgracht / Nieuwe Spiegelgracht). The curled iron urinal was the city works department's answer of about 1870 (an inventor had proposed hollow advertising columns); the first double krul went up in 1877 at the Paleis voor Volksvlijt on the Frederiksplein. The see-through top let policemen check that only one man was inside, because the krullen were also cruising spots (some 40 still used that way in 1980). In the 1960s the city wanted them gone; they stayed partly to keep drunk men from falling into the canals. In 2017 35 were left; the register lists 39 in 2026, eight of them in this zone (Amstelveld, Utrechtsestraat, Keizersgracht/Nieuwe Spiegelgracht, Lijnbaansgracht 282, Nieuwe Keizersgracht/Weesperstraat, Nieuwe Achtergracht/Roetersstraat, Plantage Kerklaan by Artis, Kadijksplein). Women still have nothing comparable, which became the "plasrecht" fight after a woman was fined in 2015. Their modern cousins are nearby: eight urilifts that rise out of the pavement in the evening and sink back in the morning, and two planted "Green Pee" urinals by the Rembrandtplein. Lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaskrul ; second: https://maps.amsterdam.nl/openbare_toiletten/ (register, 24 Nov 2026). Status: in use. Caveats: the plasrecht case needs its own source before use; a sensitive history told plainly. **Amazing** (start here: the most Amsterdam piece of street furniture, with a hidden history); new place (one pin for all; the other krullen as Look for lines).
