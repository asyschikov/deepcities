## Shortlist

### Water and infrastructure

**G-07. The hole in the quay: a 600-year-old sluice under the Nieuwmarkt.** Pin 52.373100,
4.900450; head of the Geldersekade at the Nieuwmarkt (Burgwallen-Oude Zijde, on the border with
Nieuwmarkt/Lastage). In the quay wall at the very end of the Geldersekade is a half-round opening
under sandstone arches, 1.75 m wide, closed by a floating boom. It is the mouth of a 170 m culvert
that runs under the Nieuwmarkt and under the Waag itself to the Kloveniersburgwal, and halfway
along it sits a sluice with heavy wooden double doors that has worked for some six centuries (it is
officially bridge 495 and sluice 202). It kept the salt IJ water of the Geldersekade apart from the
fresh Amstel water of the Kloveniersburgwal. The odd part: it was paid for by a polder board some
40 km away, the Grootwaterschap Bijleveld between Harmelen and Montfoort, which won the right in
1413 to drain its water through Amsterdam; the sluice was rebuilt here in 1454 and the city gate
that became the Waag was built right over it in the 1480s. Bijleveld handed it to the city in 1492.
When the Nieuwmarkt was laid over the canals in 1614 the culvert was simply lengthened. A side
branch drained the Huidenvettersloot, an open sewer with rats "the size of a beaver" until 1899.
Local voice: Bijleveldsche sluis. Lead: Ons Amsterdam, "De middeleeuwse sluis onder de Waag",
https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/de-middeleeuwse-sluis-onder-de-waag ; second source:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijleveldschesluis (brug 495; culvert renewed 1988-1990 when old
remains were found). Status: in use (Ons Amsterdam, undated web article; wiki current). Caveats:
the Waag is A-list, but the culvert and its quay opening are not; pin estimated at the head of the
Geldersekade, walk-check the opening (wiki puts the structure at 52.372619, 4.900436, under the
square). Rank **Amazing**. New place.

### Monuments and moved fragments

**G-01. Leprozenpoort: the lepers' gate that walked down the street.** Pin 52.369926, 4.901111;
Sint Antoniesluis 21-24 (Nieuwmarkt/Lastage). The 17th-century entrance gate of the Leprozenhuis,
the lepers' house that stood where the Mr. Visserplein traffic square is now. It came from the
workshop of Hendrick de Keyser (possibly cut by his English brother-in-law Nicholas Stone). In the
tall fronton, under the city arms with the imperial crown, two niches hold a leper man and a leper
woman with their clappers (the wooden rattles lepers had to sound). The gate was taken down for the
IJ-tunnel approach, stored, and rebuilt by Stichting De Pinto in the 1970s/80s along the Sint
Antoniesluis, a few hundred metres from its first spot, during the restoration of Huis De Pinto.
Hans 't Mannetje restored the gate; Henri van Haaren recut the badly damaged reliefs, so what you
see are copies. Local voice: also called the Lazaruspoort (the Leprozenhuis was the Lazarushuis,
the lane beside it the Lazarussteeg). Lead: VVAB, De Lamp van Diogenes 104/105 (Sept 1987), via
https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://amsterdamsebinnenstad.nl/ ; second source:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leprozenhuis_(Amsterdam) (section Leprozenpoort, citing Minne
Dijkstra, "Poortjes van Amsterdam 1571-1782", 2008/2009). Status: standing (Dijkstra 2009; the
drafts only mention the Leprozenhuis as a charity landowner). Caveats: not found in the RCE register
under its own name; check whether it is listed with Sint Antoniesluis. Pin is from the PDOK address
centroid of no. 22; walk-check which side the gate faces. Rank **Nice**. New place (could group
with AC-14 Grenspaal, a few steps away and also by Hans 't Mannetje).

**G-02. The Heerenlogement gate on the Keizersgracht.** Pin 52.368591, 4.884916; Keizersgracht
365 (Grachtengordel-West). The little entrance gate of the Oudezijds Heerenlogement, the city's
grandest inn (an English merchant called it "the noblest taverne in the world" in 1653), which stood
on the Grimburgwal from 1647 to 1874 and ended as the city's best-known auction house. When it was
pulled down for the Binnengasthuis, the last auctioneer, J.P.A. Kilsdonk, had the gate carried to
his new auction rooms here, so the name Heeren-Logement moved with it. City arms in the tympanum,
the imperial crown on top, flanked by two lying lions (the lions are renewals: the originals were
wrecked in a rough 1975 rebuild). The rest of the building scattered: festoons in the Rijksmuseum's
fragment building, the fronton lions via the Stedelijk and the Rijkspostspaarbank to the ING
office on the Haarlemmerweg, the city arms in a rock garden and then a restoration workshop, the
crown still missing. Lead: Binnenstad 278 (2016), Gert Eijkelboom and Gerrit Vermeer, "De
omzwerving van de leeuwen van de Grimburgwal",
https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://amsterdamsebinnenstad.nl/binnenstad/278/heerenlogement.php ;
second source: RCE monument 2328, Keizersgracht 363-367 ("met hierheen overgeplaatst poortje van
het Oudezijds Herenlogement (1647)"), https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/2328 .
Status: in place (RCE register; article 2016). Caveats: rijksmonument; check whether the VVAB
plan to reunite the fragments at the Grimburgwal ever happened. Rank **Nice**. New place.

**G-03. Orphan gable tops: the gables that played musical chairs.** Pin 52.364023, 4.886495;
Prinsengracht 755 (Grachtengordel-Zuid), with the others as Look for. About 135 canal houses wear
a gable crown taken from a demolished house elsewhere: the city stored the carved tops in its
monument yard and sold them for re-use, and for more than half nobody wrote down where they came
from (Theo Rouwhorst, "Bewaard voor Amsterdam. Historische geveltoppen herplaatst 1945-2015",
2016). The best chain: the neck gable of 1701 at Prinsengracht 755 carries little sea gods blowing
shell horns that came from Amstelstraat 49, taken off when that house was simplified in 1938 and
stored at Frankendael; Amstelstraat 49 in turn got a top in 2003 taken from Haarlemmer Houttuinen
193. VVAB called it a stoelendans (musical chairs). Look for: Bloemgracht 19 (52.374871, 4.882246),
the rococo bell gable placed in 1980 as top no. 1 of the monuments office, traced to "De drie
geblomde cassaes", Kalverstraat 222, demolished for Vroom & Dreesmann; Bloemgracht 73 (52.374424,
4.880249), not an 18th-century house at all but a 1976 rebuild of a house demolished in 1966,
crowned with a different top bought from the city yard in Duivendrecht on 16 November 1972;
Amstelstraat 49 (52.366281, 4.899977). The jordaan-5 draft already has Rozenstraat 35-43, a row of
1934 with five tops from elsewhere: merge this item with it or link the two. Leads: Binnenstad 310 (David Mulder, Prinsengracht 755),
Binnenstad 306 (Bloemgracht 19), Binnenstad 308 (Bloemgracht 73), all via
https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://amsterdamsebinnenstad.nl/ ; second source: RCE monument
4398 ("een van elders overgebrachte hals met schelpblazende zeegodjes aan de vleugelstukken"),
https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/4398 . Status: in place (RCE; articles
2023). The tritons are comic putti with scaly fish tails, from the workshop of Anthonie Turck
(c. 1720), and the find was pure chance: a photo of Amstelstraat 49 from 1926 turned up while the
writer looked for a picture of the Blauwbrug. The row of five, built in 1701 as speculation, is named
after the continents: Europa (747), Asia (749), Afrika (753), Amerika (755), with 751 nameless but
dated 1701; most of it is a 1959-60 rebuild. Caveats: all rijksmonumenten. Rank **Nice**. New
place (one pin, the rest as Look for).

**G-04. Singel 390: the globe that was melted down and came back.** Pin 52.369374, 4.887183;
Singel 390 (Grachtengordel-West). Apollo and Minerva sit on the raised cornice of this early
18th-century house, and between them sat a copper celestial globe, 1.20 m across. In 1914 it was
taken down and melted for its copper. Hotel Ambassade, which owns the house, had a new globe made
and hoisted it on 11 December 2002. Lead: Binnenstad 197/198 (March 2003); second source: RCE
monument 5381 (cornice with Apollo and Minerva), https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/5381 .
Status: globe back since 2002 (latest evidence 2003); check it is still up. Caveats: rijksmonument;
hotel. Rank **Okay**. Group with G-03 (moved and replaced gable crowns), or new place.

**G-05. Kalverstraat 10: the booksellers' palace above the shops.** Pin 52.372291, 4.891931;
Kalverstraat 10 (Burgwallen-Nieuwe Zijde). Look up above the shop window: a raised cornice crowned
by busts of Minerva and Mercury. On this plot Clement de Jonghe, Rembrandt's print publisher, kept
his shop "De gekroonde konstkaert" from 1662; then came a coffee house and art shop, then Gerard
Valk, the globe maker. Rudolf Wetstein, printer of Bibles in Malay and of the VOC's ship psalm book
(12,700 still in stock when he died), rebuilt it in 1727 as a 35 m deep "art and book palace"; the
map maker Isaac Tirion and sea-chart sellers followed, and Frederik Muller's auction house and
bookshop until 1883. Wetstein's chronogram stone (capital letters adding up to 1728) went to the
KOG in 1883. Lead: Binnenstad 266 (2014), Pieter Vlaardingerbroek, "Kalverstraat 10: boeken, kunst
en koffie", https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://amsterdamsebinnenstad.nl/binnenstad/266/kalverstraat-10.php ;
second source: RCE monument 2138 (gable with busts, XVIIIb). Status: facade in place (RCE).
Caveats: rijksmonument; nothing of the book trade left inside. Rank **Okay**. New place.

**G-06. Koningsplein 10: the 3-metre chocolate shop that rose from the ashes.** Pin 52.367118,
4.888815; Koningsplein 10 (Grachtengordel-Zuid). Between the big stores of the Koningsplein, a
house front less than three metres wide with a bay window and a little balcony in carved Art
Nouveau woodwork. It was built in 1901 (architect P. van der Vliet) as a chocolaterie for J. Bruin,
later held a jeweller, and burned out in 1936 (a gas heater, the papers said, after a false burglar
alarm had woken the whole street months earlier). A plain modern front replaced it, which wore down
to a blank white wall with a cash machine. In the 2020s architect Kees Doornenbal rebuilt the 1901
front from drawings and photos, milling ornaments and frames out of single wooden boards. Today it
is only the entrance and stairwell for 22 flats above the neighbouring shops. Local voice: VVAB
called it "snoepje en sieraad voor de stad". Look for: Kalverstraat 114, where Doornenbal had a
Spanish sculptor recut the 17th-century neck-gable top taken off in 1937, and next door at 116-118
the copper front of 1931 by Gerard Loogman, found intact behind a 1980s glass curtain wall
(Binnenstad 301/302). Lead: Binnenstad 308 (2022), "Reconstructie Koningsplein 10",
https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://amsterdamsebinnenstad.nl/binnenstad/308/reconstructie-koningsplein-10.php ;
second source needed (Cuypersbulletin, or the Stadsarchief beeldbank for the 1901 front). Status:
rebuilt (2022). Caveats: not listed (orde 3). Rank **Okay**. New place.

### Houses with a story (Hendrick de Keyser houses)

**G-08. De Vier Heemskinderen: four knights on one horse at the Leidsegracht corner.** Pin
52.367617, 4.886278; Herengracht 394, north corner of the Leidsegracht (Grachtengordel-West). The
baker Jan Specht bought the corner plot where the 1614 and 1663 city extensions meet and in 1671
built a tall neck gable "daar de vier Heemskinderen in de gevel staan": the stone shows the four
sons of Aymon from the medieval romance, Adelaart, Ritsaart, Writsaart and Reinout, all riding
their magic horse Beyaert. There was a bakery here almost without a break. The stone was put on a
postage stamp in 1975. Look for: the 17th-century windows in the top are unchanged; the shop front
is late 19th century. Lead: Vereniging Hendrick de Keyser, https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/de-huizen/de-vier-heemskinderen ;
second source: RCE monument 1832 (Herengracht 394). Status: standing, let as homes by the Vereniging
(page read 2026-11-24). Caveats: rijksmonument, private; a different Vier Heemskinderen stone of
1899 sits in the nieuwezijde-1 draft (city register 210015), do not mix them up. Rank **Okay**.
New place.

**G-09. Gables that swim: Abraham Zeeman's sea fish and the dolphins of De Hond.** Pin 52.379327,
4.892490; Brouwersgracht 50 (Haarlemmerbuurt). Around 1715 Abraham Zeeman (the name means
"seaman") had the claw pieces beside his neck gable carved as sea fish, a pun on his own name. It
was a real tenement: a cellar flat with a little shop, a lower house and two room flats above.
Hendrick de Keyser bought it worn out in 1918, one of its first houses. Look for: Herengracht 59
"De Hond" (52.377938, 4.891257), a classicist front of 1659 for the Mennonite merchant Isacq van
Halmael, whose small claw pieces are dolphins. Lead: Vereniging Hendrick de Keyser,
https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/de-huizen/brouwersgracht-50 and .../de-huizen/de-hond ; second
source: RCE monuments 760 (Brouwersgracht 50) and 1529 (Herengracht 59). Status: standing, let as
homes (pages read 2026-11-24). Caveats: rijksmonumenten, private. Rank **Okay**. New place.

**G-10. Huis Serne: a century of theatre costumes, and a ceiling that ended a painter.** Pin
52.367932, 4.897140; Groenburgwal 56 (Nieuwmarkt/Lastage). A tall neck-gabled merchant's house of
about 1720 near the Binnen-Amstel. The banker Willem Borski, whose fortune his widow later put into
founding De Nederlandsche Bank, was born here in 1765. From 1883 it belonged to the Serne family:
Arend Serne and Helena van Hoekelen, keen amateur actors, hired out stage costumes, and for more than
a hundred years and four generations "A. Serne en Zoon" filled the rooms, fitting booths and attics
with costumes and props for opera, operetta, student masquerades and costume balls; the front was
restored to its 18th-century look for the firm's centenary in 1966. Inside hangs a clumsily painted
ceiling piece: contemporaries said a "mislukt platfond" the young Hermanus Noordkerk painted for his
father around 1720 made him give up painting and study law (he became a known jurist and a friend of
Betje Wolff). Lead: Vereniging Hendrick de Keyser (bought 2019),
https://www.hendrickdekeyser.nl/de-huizen/huis-serne ; second source: RCE monument 1273 (Groenburgwal
56). Status: owned by the Vereniging since 2019 (page read 2026-11-24). Caveats: rijksmonument; the
ceiling is inside, so only the facade is public unless an open day is found; that the Groenburgwal
ceiling is that very "failed" one is the Vereniging's attribution. Rank **Okay**. New place.

## Second sources for other scouts

- **BC-14 Spooksteeg:** Ons Amsterdam, "Het spook van de Zeedijk" (Peter-Paul de Baar, 10 July
  2003), https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/het-spook-van-de-zeedijk . The 1853 brochure is in the
  city archive; the haunted house is Zeedijk 42 (in 1853 the shop of J.H. Schlätter), on the corner
  of the alley then called Waterpoortsteeg and called Spooksteeg by 1855. On 24 July 1953 hundreds
  waited at cafe De Waag (Tante Cor and Ome Bertus) after Het Parool announced the ghost; Panorama
  had staged its ghost photo on 10 June 1953. The tanner Gerhards of the legend cannot be traced.
- **BE-18 Nieuwe Kerkstraat:** Ons Amsterdam, "Lief en leed in de Jodenkerkstraat",
  https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/lief-en-leed-in-de-jodenkerkstraat . Nicknames: Jodenkerkstraat
  and Christenkerkstraat (the Magere Brug the border), Portugese Kerkstraat for the east part,
  Sjoelstraat for its three sjoels. No. 26 first sjoel 1841 (facade largely intact); nos. 10-14
  Harry Elte's sjoel of 1913; no. 149 the Russensjoel of 1889, founded by Russian Jews fleeing the
  pogroms, restored and back in use since 1987; no. 147 the Matrozensjoel, 1896; no. 127 the
  Metaarhuis ("Aan de Keizersgracht erin en aan de Kerkstraat eruit"); no. 16 the Fernandes
  Nuneshuis, stones defaced in 1943 and restored in the mid-1990s.
- **DC dropped "Warmoesstraat 90, oldest house":** Binnenstad 253 (Aug/Sep 2012): dendrochronology
  dates the house to about 1485, older than the Houten Huys; found by bouwinspecteur Wytse Teerling
  and confirmed by Bureau Monumenten en Archeologie; fire in 2010. Still nothing visible from the
  street, so the drop stands unless a plaque exists.
- **BC-04 Heilige Stede:** the gable stone was unveiled on 11 March 2001 by Hans 't Mannetje
  (Binnenstad 187).
- **BC-25 Hendrick de Keyser's grave:** restored and unveiled on 29 January 2010 by the Comite
  Eerherstel (Binnenstad 238).
- **AC-02 photo studios:** Binnenstad 254, "De oudste fotoateliers".
- **DE-06 Koning William yard, Hoogte Kadijk 145B:** amsterdamsloopt.nl lists the yard (built 1947
  and 1950) as demolished in 2025, https://amsterdamsloopt.nl/wordt-gesloopt-2019-3/ . The item
  becomes a gone place: drop it or give it attributes.status Gone.
- **DE-33 Hildo Krop's studio, Plantage Muidergracht 105:** amsterdamsloopt.nl lists "Atelier
  Hildo Krop" (18th century, 1879 and 1921) as demolished in 2015,
  https://amsterdamsloopt.nl/gesloopt-2015-2/ . This clashes with the 2016 fight DE cites; check
  on the spot what stands.
- **Spuistraat drafts (nieuwezijde-3: Keuken van 1870, the Tabakspanden; the Van Arkel bakery at
  the Raamstraat corner):** Ons Amsterdam, "Stinksloot wordt Spuistraat",
  https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/stinksloot-wordt-spuistraat . The filled-in Nieuwezijds
  Achterburgwal was renamed Spuistraat in November 1867 and got a double row of chestnuts; the
  Volksgaarkeuken opened at no. 4 three years later (De Keuken van 1870 from 1970, closed 2012);
  Aletta Jacobs held her consultations for the poor next door from 1882. The Blauwe Tram ran from
  the Spui to Zandvoort, 1914 to 1957. The 1974 Historische Gids called the Van Arkel bakery "one
  of the ugly and showy houses the architect saddled our city with". Hendrik Tabak, a greengrocer's
  son, bought up much of the street from 1930 and let it rot (hence Tabakspanden); during the war
  the NSB Kringhuis was at no. 231 while Marten Toonder's Hulpstudio at no. 28 printed resistance
  papers. Squats: Vrankrijk (216-218) from 1982, then no. 199, nicknamed Slangenpand for its
  paintwork.
- **Any Zeedijk item (local voice):** Ons Amsterdam, "Het herstel van de Zeedijk begon bij de
  bewoners" (Willem Oosterbeek), https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/het-herstel-van-de-zeedijk-begon-bij-de-bewoners .
  On 18 May 1983 some 150 residents of the Crisiscomite Zeedijk-Nieuwmarkt occupied the city hall
  with icing sugar as a stand-in for heroin; in 1984 the city and banks set up the NV Economisch
  Herstel Zeedijk (3.4 million guilders) to buy up boarded houses; tante Trui, one of the
  residents who never left, drove the first pile of the Barbizon Palace in 1986; the Kletskop
  (Zeedijk 10, 1988) was the first "normal" cafe at the head of the dike, kept alive by hotel
  staff and the Warmoesstraat police; bakker Lobs at the Stormsteeg corner was another pioneer,
  with shop girls from Volendam. About 60 houses up to the Stormsteeg were bought and let by 1990.
- **Artis (any Plantage item on the Groote Museum):** the fixed interior of the Oostzaal or
  Tijgerzaal was stripped in 2020 (https://amsterdamsloopt.nl/gesloopt-2020/).
- **Haarlemmerbuurt drafts, Brouwersgracht 157-159:** demolished in 2016 with only the front of
  157 kept (https://amsterdamsloopt.nl/gesloopt-2016-2/).

## Look for lines

- **Centraal Station lions (for any CS / Oosterdok item):** of the 22 sandstone lions that stood on
  A.L. van Gendt's railway viaducts (from 1875), none is left at the station. All 16 big lions
  have been traced (four in the Beatrixpark, one in Schellingwoude, others as far as Vlissingen and
  Velsen-Noord); the six smaller Korte Prinsengracht lions are lost. The only surviving pedestal sits
  hidden behind concrete on the south-west corner of the Oostertoegang. Sources: Binnenstad 250

## Leads for other scouts

- **Heijmeijer van Heemstede foundation** (Binnenstad 292, 303): quietly restores the burial
  gates of protestant churches and lost saints' statues. A theme A sweep could list which gates and
  statues it redid.
- **Theo Rouwhorst, "Bewaard voor Amsterdam. Historische geveltoppen herplaatst 1945-2015" (2016):**
  lists 135 houses with a gable crown from elsewhere; worth a library read for more G-03 links.
- **Gaper of drogist Het Heertje, Herenstraat 7** (Binnenstad 278): one of the last gapers in the
  centre, stolen; check whether a new one hangs.
- **Grimburgwal lamp posts and balusters** (Binnenstad 299): unique ones saved after the quay
  collapsed on 1 Sept 2020; for the oudezijde drafts.
- **Maarten Hell, "Condooms in de Kalverstraat"** (2021): local voice for any Kalverstraat item
  from David Kouwenaar's "Tussen Dam en Muntplein" (1949): a drover milking she-asses on a doorstep,
  newspaper renters, the Huizen fishmonger with his pale-blue barrow.
- **Foeliedwarsstraat 44-50** (Binnenstad 290): a castle-like water-gate gable stone with a date
  stone of 1660, and a 1910 first stone laid by Abram Allegro, aged four and a half; theme A.
- **Oostertoegang lion pedestal:** only surviving one, behind concrete on the south-west corner;
  the Genootschap Leeuwen van het Centraal Station wants the sandstone wing building uncovered.
