## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Herengracht 20 (1700)**: amsterdam-monumentenstad.nl notes that the national register's description (five storeys, ox-head stones, stoop posts) actually belongs to Nieuwe Herengracht 20. The real Herengracht 20 is an 18th-century house raised around 1920 with no stoop. Worth reporting to the RCE; filed as no story.
- **Herengracht 362–364 (1818) and 366/368**: the national register says "in 1622 gebouwd"; the slice dates (1660–1662), 370's own register text and all other sources give 1660–1662. Typo in the register; the draft uses 1660–1662.
- **Herengracht 319**: AOK says the Van Wees tasting room opened in 1974, the business itself says 1973. Used 1973. The business is now called Proeflokaal A. van Wees (formerly De Admiraal).
- **Herengracht 255**: architect initials F.W.M. (AOK) vs W.J.M. (city text, Wikipedia list); style called North French Gothic (city) vs 16th-century French Renaissance (AOK), so the text says "late Gothic and early Renaissance of northern France". The sculpture by Lambertus Zijl rests on "the literature" cited by the city text; written as "is said to follow designs by". Hotel name not established (the Wikipedia list says "former Rembrandt Hotel"); text only says it has been a hotel since 1975.
- **Herengracht 141**: the city text treats the Exchange/Westerkerk stone as recalling the original function; AOK says the stone is by Rien Hack (20th-century sculptor) and the bronze Mercury is a later addition. The text gives the signature and calls the Mercury added, without dating either.
- **Huis Nienhuys**: the en.wikipedia claim that Nienhuys left Sumatra after being indicted over the death of seven workers rests on one weak citation (article tagged "more citations needed"); left out. The labour remark ("contract labourers recruited in China and Java") is from nl.wikipedia Deli Maatschappij; the quoted "barbaric conditions" passage there is tagged "bron?" and was not used. A reviewer may want a stronger source (e.g. on the coolie ordinance) before going live.
- **Herengracht 168**: current use not established (the theatre museum left in 2009; the Grachtenboek mentions a doorway through to 170). It is therefore in the Bartolotti group, with no claim about access to 168 itself. Pavonia and the enslaved labour come from en.wikipedia (Michiel Reyniersz Pauw; Pavonia, New Netherland, citing Hodges 1999).
- **Cromhout houses**: Stadsherstel's timeline gives the current use as business space, although its text says public access was a condition of the 2020 sale. No visiting information claimed; www.cromhouthuis.nl returned an error.
- **Grachtenmuseum**: grachtenmuseum.nl did not respond to curl; current operation rests on nl.wikipedia (exhibition in 2026). No opening hours given.
- **Herengracht 395**: AOK says "Let op de kat!" (mind the cat) but no source says what or where the cat is; left out. Could be checked on site.
- **De Drie Traancoopers**: no source explains the name; the text only glosses the word traan (train oil).
- **Van Brienen**: Arnout Jan (Grachtenboek, 182) and Arnold Johan (Wikipedia, 284) are treated as one man, as both sources link the two houses to him. Number 182's current use not checked beyond the 1935 office conversion.
- Wikipedia's search API rate-limited this session (raw pages still worked); Bing/Brave/DDG searches returned almost nothing useful.

## Leads

- **Herengracht 238**: Isac Jacovici, watch wholesaler born in Romania in 1874, lived and worked here from 1919; shot in Kamp Amersfoort in 1943 (amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Herengracht/238). Check for a Stolperstein or a joodsmonument.nl entry.
- **Herengracht 302**: Joseph Loopuit, ANDB and SDAP leader, lived here around 1900–1903 (AOK).
- **Herengracht 259**: J.P. Nord Thomson, pudding-powder maker and consul-general of Persia, named the building Noord-Holland (and a Keizersgracht building Zuid-Holland); earlier the Reflex camera company had its works here, 1895–1898 (AOK). Check whether a name is on the front.
- **Herengracht 278**: Casparus Commelin, city historian and printer, lived in the earlier house 'het Salpetervat' with the city printing office on the first floor (city text). Possible line in a route text.
- **Herengracht 323**: AOK mentions a depot of the Amsterdamsche Rijtuig-Maatschappij there around 1900.
- **Oude telefooncentrale / Gemeentetelefoon (1955)** next to Herengracht 303: its construction in 1919 caused the demolition of the house at 303; the 1955 building is mentioned in the city texts of 289 and 303.
- **Hendrick de Keyser podwalk** "Hoor Hendrick Amsterdam" includes De Drie Traancoopers.
- **Combiticket Huis Bartolotti and Museum Het Schip** (hendrickdekeyser.nl): link between Centrum and West.
