# jordaan-3: notes

73 records. 18 places: 12 with a story of their own and 6 groups, holding 28 records in all.
No A-list records. 5 records belong with places in other slices: 4 with Eelke van Houten's
second-hand gables (jordaan-1) and 1 with a Willemsstraat group still to come (jordaan-6).
40 records: no story found.

How the research went: I read the full city and national register texts for every record and the
raw nl.wikipedia wikitext for all 18 records with an article, plus the street and topic articles
(Lindengracht, Palmgracht, Noordermarkt, Europarking, Plaquette Februaristaking, Monument
Jordaanoproer, Theo Bosch, Raampoort). I also used hofjesinamsterdam.nl for the six hofjes, and
fetched noorderkerk.nl and timeandstyle.com for current use. **The shared WebSearch budget ran out
after three searches.** So the planned one-search-per-record check on the "no story found" files
was not done. Those calls rest on the full register text and Wikipedia where there is one. Several
current-use claims carry `check: Facts` for the same reason (see Conflicts). No Commons API calls
were made: photos are only the slice's `wikidata.images`.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Merges into jordaan-1 (needs a decision).** 3633, 3634, 214043 and 214036 are Van Houten houses
  and fit "Eelke van Houten's second-hand gables" in jordaan-1.yaml. I did not edit that file.
  Together with the ten numbers jordaan-2 proposed, the monument_number field may pass 200
  characters; check before adding. 214044 and 213015 are listed in the city register with "E. van
  Houten" as client (his archive), but no reused top is recorded, so I filed them under no story.
- **Willemsstraat (204046)** belongs with the philanthropic Willemsstraat block in jordaan-6
  (records 200403, 204056 and others there). Left for that slice.
- **Search budget.** WebSearch was exhausted after three queries (shared limit). Current use is
  therefore unconfirmed for: Lindenhofje (children's hospice, sources 2004 and 2018), Hamer- en
  Bouwershofje (Okura staff housing, hofjesinamsterdam 2018 only), Turnzaal (use after De Krakeling
  left in 2020), Tichelkerk (church use confirmed only in the 2005 register text; an Omroep Brabant
  piece of December 2024 hints at an Amsterdam Capuchin church with an uncertain future, but does
  not name it), Tooneelschool (Het Witte Huis, 2023). Each has `check` set. Confirmed by fetch:
  Noorderkerk (services listed for 22 November 2026), Raampoort (Time & Style store page).
- **Toneelschool dates.** The slice gives `listed: 2001`, nl.wikipedia says it was entered in the
  register on 22 April 2022; `listed` left out. Wikipedia also gives two moves (to the Westeinde
  and in 1976 to De Nieuwe Liefde); the text says only "in the 1970s". "Nearly a century" in the
  summary rests on 1878 to the 1970s.
- **Turnzaal listing year.** Slice says 2001, wiki says 22 April 2002; left out. Wiki says one club
  was Jewish and lost almost all its members in the war: single source, left out.
- **Bossche Hofje date.** National register 1645, hofjesinamsterdam "about 1648" and same-day land
  purchase with the Raepenhofje; the place uses 1648.
- **Lindenhofje founding.** City register quotes D'Ailly for 1614, hofjesinamsterdam and
  nl.wikipedia give 1616; text uses 1616.
- **De Weichsel.** The city register for 206051 calls the vanished foundry a bell foundry, the one
  for 204063 the city foundry ('t Giethuis); text says city foundry. The Korthals Altes link is
  hedged in the register ("may be seen as"); the text keeps that hedge.
- **Leidsegracht 108.** The 1663–64 date and Stalpaert link are Wikipedia only; `check: Facts`.
  Stalpaert himself is not named in the text.
- **Europarking.** The Tabak quote is the headline of a 1972 newspaper article (cited in the
  wiki); text paraphrases it. "Huisjesmelker" (slumlord) from wiki is softened to "practices as a
  landlord". Skaters (wiki, no source) and the night shelter under the garage are left out.
- **Leidsekade 68–69 (208013)** has polychrome sculpture the register calls "negerkoppen" (Black
  heads) beside painted lions. Could be a "Look up" place with careful framing; I left it out as
  decoration without a story.
- **Concordia (214014).** The register only says it was "possibly" built for Concordia at the same
  time; it is in the group as part of the Lijnbaansstraat front, not named in the text.

   Lindenhofje → Suyckerhoff-hofje → Raepenhofje and Bossche Hofje → Palmgracht colonial advert →
   Palmdwarsstraat.
2. **The Singelgracht strip, from ramparts to car park**: Marnixstraat workers' homes → De Weichsel
   (short detour) → Hamer- en Bouwershofje → Bureau Raampoort → Tooneelschool → fruit market blocks →
   Europarking → ATVA → Turnzaal.
3. **Housing reformers**: Concordia's hofje → (jordaan-1) Hof van Parijs and Bloemstraat →
   Marnixstraat workers' homes → ATVA (Van der Pek and Louise Went) → Palmdwarsstraat (Theo Bosch).
