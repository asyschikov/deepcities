## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Hotel American extension (518484)**: the 1927-1928 wing on the Leidsekade has no story apart from the hotel; the main hotel (3403) is in slice wetering-2. Suggest adding 518484 to that place's monument numbers.
- **Grill's Hofje date and founders**: the national register gives 1721; the TU Delft study "Urban Oases" gives 1727 and says it was built by a Swedish silversmith and his German wife (Anthonie Grill). A Haarlem hofjes paper names Anthony Grill as founder in 1721; a biographical dictionary says only "Grill". Text says "the 1720s" and gives both dates. I.H. van Eeghen, "Het Grill's hofje", Jaarboek Amstelodamum 62 (1970) would settle it; not online.
- **Hodshon-Dedelhofje**: only the register text (1842) is available. Urban Oases lists a "Hodshon-Dedelhof 1" (1730, also called Blokshofje) and "Hodshon-Dedelhof 2" (1842); the relationship is not explained, so it is left out.
- **Het witte huisje**: register says built 1875-1899, moved in 1913 from the Hemonylaan (from Ons Amsterdam, Sept 1994) and that two such posts were left (1995); M for Amsterdam says 1897, decommissioned 1924, three left (adds Buiksloterweg 9B of 1915) and that it now houses goldsmith Mélanie Pigeaud. The 1897 date, 1924 and the current use rest on one blog; left out. Text says "late 19th century" and names the other two posts.
- **Amstelsluizen operation**: register (2001) describes capstans with windlass and wooden bars; M for Amsterdam says they were manual "until a few years ago" and are now hydraulic. Text only says the capstans stand by the gates, not that they are used.
- **Dikker & Thijs move**: the register says the oyster salon came in 1921; nl.wikipedia says Leidsestraat 96 in 1921 and no. 82 a year later. Text says "the early 1920s". The bird on the roof: register says "phoenix?"; text says "perhaps a phoenix".
- **Bakery Leidsestraat 81**: still a bakery in 2006 per the register; the current bakery (Kwakman) is only in directory listings. `check: Still there?` set.
- **Jacob Olie**: nl.wikipedia says he photographed from his home at Huidekoperstraat 19; PhotoLexicon only says he lived there from 1878 and had two studios in the garden. Left out the photographing-from-the-window claim. Death date differs (23 April per nl.wikipedia, 25 April per PhotoLexicon); text gives only the year. Current use of the building (empty upper floors in 2005) not found.
- **Leidsekade 79 / Mata Hari**: the nl.wikipedia Leidsekade article says Greetje Zelle (Mata Hari) once lived at no. 79; no other source found. Not used. Mata Hari is used only in the Hirsch place (sat for Merkelbach, per nl.wikipedia Jacob Merkelbach).
- **Leidsekade 98-99 / Leidseplein 35**: the city register (2009) values the 1934 shopfront of Flowershop Ivy by Zanstra, Giesen & Sijmons (Groep 32) as a rare surviving functionalist shop. I could not find what the shop is now or whether the interior survives. A good Okay place if someone checks on site.
- **Derde Weteringdwarsstraat 15**: the register says the 1888 rebuild was for the church board of the Evangelische Gemeente. Religiana says the Weteringkerk of the Vrije Evangelische Gemeente was a theatre of about 1800 on this street turned into a church around 1870. Possibly the church's front building; not confirmed, so filed as no story found.
- **The Bulldog**: the opening date (1 April 1985) and current use come from The Bulldog's own site. The founder's arrests, mentioned in some sources, are left out.
- **City Theater**: nl.wikipedia says it closed in 2007 for restoration and reopened 2 Dec 2010 as Pathé City; 2026 listings show it running.
- **Hirschgebouw**: nl.wikipedia also mentions a 2022 hostage-taking in the Apple Store; left out.
- **De Balie**: nl.wikipedia (Kleine-Gartmanplantsoen 10) mentions plans of 1965 for Bouwes and Sheraton hotels and a ten-year fight; the De Balie article dates the threat to the late 1970s. Text says "late 1970s".
