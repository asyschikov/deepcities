## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Henri Polaklaan 14**: register says built 1876, nl.wikipedia (Amsterdamsche Schaakhuis)
  says 1878. `built` is "1876–1878"; the text says "the 1870s".
- **Palacheschool**: the register names the rabbi "Juda Palache (1858–1927)"; nl.wikipedia and
  joodsamsterdam give Isaac Juda Palache, died 1926. Used 1926. Wikipedia says none of the
  286 pupils of 1941 was left by 1943; the register says most were deported and murdered.
  The text says both, without the numbers.
- **Van Brants Rus Hofje**: the national register says "Daniel Marot jr.", nl.wikipedia lists
  it among works attributed to Daniël Marot (1660/61–1752). Written as "attributed to Daniël
  Marot", without saying which.
- **Amstelrank (Nieuwe Herengracht 20)**: the national register dates it 1689; the poem over
  the door and hofjesinamsterdam.nl say the first stone was laid on 9 December 1789. Used 1789.
  Its current user is given as Het Wereldhuis (nl.wikipedia) and Huis van de Tijd
  (hofjesinamsterdam.nl, 2020); the text only says the buildings are offices.
- **Corvershof**: the founders' story (ages at death, punning arms) rests on
  hofjesinamsterdam.nl, with nl.wikipedia (Joan Corver) confirming the founders. Worth a check
  of the arms on the front.
- **Amstel 135-141**: the register says three old houses, nl.wikipedia says four. Used three
  (register, which quotes Van Walraven's letter).
- **Hidden synagogue, Nieuwe Keizersgracht 33**: left out joodsamsterdam's single-source
  anecdote that the sexton's wife sent an SS raid on Rosh Hashana 1944 to the Keizersgracht.
  The sexton is not named (private person); the painter Anton Witsel is.
- **Washing house**: "the tile panel with the name Rosenthal-May Zusterhuis" is in the city
  register's present tense; I have not seen a recent photo of it. If it is gone, drop that
  Look-for line.
- **Diamantbewerkersbrug**: dismantled for renovation around 2020, return expected 2024;
  Commons has a photo dated 7 October 2026 showing it in the Nieuwe Achtergracht, so it is back in place. It is a Look-for line only.
- **Hogesluis**: the borough check puts the register point in Centrum; the bridge spans the
  Centrum/Zuid line in the Amstel.
- **Amstel 53**: filed as no story found because there is nothing to see that tells the story
  (a plain front of 1881). If a Jewish care route is made, it could join the Portuguese Jewish
  hospital as a one-line mention.
- **Geology institute**: the register does not say what Krop's scenes show beyond
  "palaeontology and geography"; the text does not describe individual panels. Someone on
  site could add two or three concrete Look-for lines.

## Leads

- **De Joodse Invalide**, Weesperplein / Nieuwe Achtergracht 100 corner (rijksmonument, not in
  this slice): Elte 1915 and J.F. Staal 1935–1937, the Jewish home for invalids; nl.wikipedia
  "De Joodse Invalide".
- **Schaduwkade**, Nieuwe Keizersgracht between Amstel and Weesperstraat: name plaques on the
  quay edge for some two hundred murdered residents (2013); nl.wikipedia "Nieuwe Keizersgracht
  (Amsterdam)".
- **Joodsche Raad office**, Nieuwe Keizersgracht 58 (wartime seat until September 1943);
  same article.
- **Fernandes Nuneshuis**, Nieuwe Kerkstraat 16: hofje for poor Portuguese Jewish women with a
  Hebrew and Dutch gable stone, residents deported 11 September 1942;
  https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/nieuwe-kerkstraat/ and https://hofjesinamsterdam.nl/fernandes-nuneshuis/.
- **Former fire station "Willem"**, Nieuwe Achtergracht 26-34 (1909); nl.wikipedia "Nieuwe
  Achtergracht".
- **Diamantbeurs**, Weesperplein / Nieuwe Achtergracht (G. van Arkel, 1910–1911).
- **Krekelhuis**, former mortuary (1895) in the Mien Ruys garden behind H'ART; nl.wikipedia
  "Amstelhof (gebouw)".
- **Barbara Broekman, "Recht & Onrecht"**, 30 m artwork in the atrium of UvA building A,
  Roetersstraat; nl.wikipedia "Roeterseiland".
- **Dr. Meijer de Hondbrug** (brug 257, Weesperstraat over the Nieuwe Achtergracht) and
  **Rita Vuykbrug**, more named bridges near the wartime-names group.

## Route ideas

1. **The Jewish Weesperbuurt beyond the museums** (about 2 km): Harry Elte's first synagogue,
   Hidden synagogue of the Nieuwe Keizersgracht, Washing house and nurses' home of the Jewish
   hospital, Palacheschool, Diamond factories of the Nieuwe Achtergracht, Bridges with wartime
   names, Portuguese Jewish hospital (Henri Polaklaan), De Burcht.
2. **Charity on the eastern canals** (about 1.5 km): Corvershof and the couples' homes behind
   H'ART, Van Brants Rus Hofje, Occo Hofje, Wittenberg (Luther Museum), The city's
   teacher-training school, Theo Thijssen's school on the paupers' graveyard.
3. **Amstel east bank** (short): Hogesluis, Gateway to the city carpentry yards, Postwar flats
   on the Amstel, then up the Nieuwe Prinsengracht past Bombach's wall on the old cattle market
   to Hildo Krop's carvings on the old geology institute.
4. **Henri Polaklaan in ten minutes**: De Burcht, Portuguese Jewish hospital, Jac. Baars's own
   house, Amsterdam chess house, The sculptor's house, Apostolic church in the street wall,
   then the Wertheim fountain and the Johan van Hulstbrug.
