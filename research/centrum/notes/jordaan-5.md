# jordaan-5: notes

75 records (Rozengracht, Rozenstraat, Tuinstraat, the Tweede ...dwarsstraten). 16 places: 7 with a
story of their own and 9 groups, holding 44 records in all. No A-list records. 1 record belongs with
a place in another slice (Sint Andrieshofje, jordaan-1). 30 records: no story found.

How the research went: I read the full city and national register texts for every record and the
raw nl.wikipedia wikitext for all 44 records that have an article, plus the topic articles (Lucas
Bols, Fatih-moskee, Rozentheater, Ritsaert ten Cate, Boom Chicago, Club Mazzo, Vereniging Ons Huis,
Rozenstraat 12, Helena Mercier, Johanna ter Meulen, Theo Ruiter, Rozengracht 133/114/238, the
Tuinstraat, Rozenstraat and Tweede Laurierdwarsstraat street articles). hofjesinamsterdam.nl for
the four hofjes. Current use checked by curl on the places' own sites: boomchicago.nl (Rozengracht
117), cocamsterdam.nl (COC Amsterdam, Hotel Mercier, Rozenstraat 12), fatihcamii.nl (Rozengracht
150-152), all fetched 24 November 2026. **No WebSearch was available**, so the "no story found"
calls rest on the full register text plus the Wikipedia article (all 30 have one or a long city
text); amsterdamsegevelstenen.nl is JavaScript-rendered and could not be read with curl, so the
gable-stone facts come via the Wikipedia articles that cite it. No Commons API calls: photos are
only the slice's `wikidata.images`.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Van Houten**: this slice has its own Van Houten cluster (10 records) and the coordinator already
  expects jordaan-1's "Eelke van Houten's second-hand gables" to overflow 200 characters. I drafted
  "Rozenstraat's 1934 row under borrowed gable tops" as the Rozenstraat split. Merge or keep as a
  sister place. Rozengracht 183-193 (5071-5073) went to the Rozenhofje instead, because the hofje
  commissioned them. The origins of the individual tops rest on Amsterdam Monumentenstad as quoted
  by nl.wikipedia; the body says so.
- **De Zaaier site**: nl.wikipedia (Fatih-moskee) says the church stands on the site of Constantia,
  the 1890 hall of the free socialists where Domela Nieuwenhuis spoke, bought by the parish in 1899.
  The city register says it was built on the site of the Sint Jozefkapel. Both may be true (the hall
  may have become the chapel), but no source says so: left out.
- **Roothaanhuis talent contest** (Johnny Jordaan and Tante Leen): only nl.wikipedia, which cites an
  Ons Amsterdam piece (2005) that I could not fetch (onsamsterdam.nl refuses automated readers). Kept
  in the De Zaaier text; cut it if a second source is required.
- **Rijpenhofje founding year**: 1736 (hofjesinamsterdam.nl, death of the founder), 1737 (city
  register of Rozengracht 114), 1747 over the gate (handover to Lam en Toren). Text follows the hofje
  site.
- **Zeven Keurvorsten**: the 15th-century panel sold to the Rijksmuseum in 1940 rests on
  hofjesinamsterdam.nl only; I left out the painter's name it gives. The altar panel that doubled as
  a hatch into a resident's box bed (an escape route for a priest) is also only there: left out.
  Address: register 199-225, hofje site 197-233; used the register.
- **Fire station date**: register 1892 (design), Wikipedia building from October 1893; used
  1893-1894.
- **Rozentheater listing year**: slice 2001, Wikipedia 22 April 2002; used 2001.
- **Theo Ruiter**: the number of people he got released is disputed on Wikipedia (88 vs 15); the text
  says it is uncertain. Whether a café still runs at Rozengracht 160 is unknown: `check: Still
  there?`.
- **Ons Huis** as "the first community house on the European mainland" (Wikipedia, unsourced): left
  out.
- **Rozengracht 226**: Amsterdam Monumentenstad (via Wikipedia) says it later became an animal clinic
  for pets of the poor and that Simon Carmiggelt wrote about it in *De Jordaan* (1965). One source:
  left out; worth a check.
- **Rozengracht 66** (5086): Monumentenstad says the silversmiths Helweg lived and worked here 1847-1965
  and that Jozef Israels and Ferdinand Hart Nibbrig worked in the back house (Wikipedia adds Pieter
  Lastman, which looks wrong for the period). One source, so filed under no story found; the HH
  fanlight is a nice detail if a second source turns up.
- **Bols**: the company's wartime purchases of looted Jewish property (Wikipedia) concern a warehouse
  on the De Ruijterkade, not this site, so not in the text.
- Current use not re-checked this round (no search): Rozengracht 95-105 shops and offices (register
  2005), fire station a house (register 2007), Roothaanhuis restaurant and flats (Wikipedia 2026),
  Rozengracht 106 horeca (Wikipedia 2026), hofje occupancy rules (hofjesinamsterdam.nl, 2018).

## Route ideas

- **Rozengracht end to end** (about 1.2 km): Ons Huis (Rozenstraat 12) and the volkskoffiehuis,
  the three gable stones (48, 80, 81), the Astoria tiles (58), Bols (95-105, 106, 110), Club Mazzo
  (114), Rijpenhofje, Rozentheater, Roothaanhuis and De Zaaier, Café Theo Ruiter (160), Rozenhofje
  and its 1935 trio, then the timber clinic, fire station and the Rozengracht bridges (jordaan-1).
  Back along the Rozenstraat: 250-254, 222, the Mercier stone at 215-225, the Tweede
  Laurierdwarsstraat, and the 1934 row at 35-43.
- **Housing reformers of the Jordaan**: Ons Huis, Rozenstraat 211-225, Ter Meulen's Tuinstraat blocks,
  on to the Willemsstraat (jordaan-6) and the Bloemstraat buildings for the poorest (jordaan-1).
- **Tuinstraat hofjes**: Regenboogs Liefdehofje, Zeven Keurvorsten, Claes Claesz. Hofje (jordaan-1).
