## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Kleine Komedie date**: national register says 1788, nl.wikipedia 1786; text says "the 1780s".
- **Amsterdamsche Bank**: slice says 1929-1932, register text 1926-1932 for the new build; used 1926–1932. Berlage's share is unclear in the register; text says so. The paternosters are mentioned as built; whether they still run was not checked.
- **Wertheim & Gompertsz and Hamburger's lead works**: current use only known from the 2004 listing text; texts say "when the city listed it in 2004".
- **Huis met de Bloedvlekken**: that the signs were written in blood is legend (Atlas Obscura snippet: "rumoured"); Atlas Obscura refused fetching, only the search snippet was read. Whether reddish marks are visible today: `check: Facts`. Builder Gijsbert Dommer rests on the slice's client field plus a Lonely Planet snippet.
- **Six Collection**: visiting terms from CODART (2012); `check: Facts` on the current arrangements.
- **Swigtershofje**: only the national register describes it; no source on who runs it now or whether the chapel survives; `check: Facts`. A Commons photo description (Rudolphous, WLM 2010) says it was founded in 1744 from the legacy of the Catholic book and map seller Isaac Swigters, for elderly needy Catholic women, with an inscription from Psalm 41. Single user-written source, left out of the text; worth confirming (e.g. Stadsarchief) and adding.
- **Tante Saarbrug (brug 31)**: city register gives 1725, the quay stone says Anno 1734. Built field "1725–1908" covers the group.
- **Blauwbrug**: nl.wikipedia says the design followed the Pont Alexandre III; that bridge is later (1896–1900), so the claim was left out.
- **Johanna Borskibrug**: register 1922, nl.wikipedia 1923 (year stones Anno 1923); text follows the stones.
- **Herengracht 458**: current use not found; text says only that it is private. The 2006 restitution is not in the sources read, so the text only says the heirs negotiated for years.
- **Gouden Bocht group**: photo RM1651 Herengracht 475.jpg is tagged as monument 1650, so not used. "Finest house on the bend" for 475 is reputation only (nl.wikipedia Gouden Bocht).
- **Herengracht 579**: the only suitable Commons photo is categorised as Herengracht 581 (the other half of the front); not used.
- **KattenKabinet**: the entrance photo on Commons is categorised Herengracht 395; not used.
- **De Kroon**: one listing says it was named after Wilhelmina's coronation of 1898; single weak source, left out.
- No record in this slice belongs to an A-list sight. De Kroon stands on the Rembrandtplein (A-list as a square) but is pitched on its own painted details.
