## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **West-Indisch Pakhuis**: nl.wikipedia West-Indisch Pakhuis gives headquarters 1647–1674; the Oudeschans article says 1646. Used 1647. RCE describes a pediment between the two tops; the WIC arms in it are only named in an RCE photo caption, so left out.
- **Scheepvaarthuis**: RCE gives 1911–1916, Wikipedia building 1913–1916; used 1913–1916 for the build (design began 1911). The seahorses, Bogtman glass and slate cladding are from nl.wikipedia, not the register.
- **Brug 283**: the seahorse lanterns are "probably" by Kramer in both the register and Wikipedia; written as uncertain.
- **Bantammerbrug**: Van der Mey design "probably" 1914, built 1921: written as probable.
- **Staalmeestersbrug**: the city register date does not match the rebuilds (1928, almost entirely renewed 1964), so `built` is left out. The 1987 Amsterdamned film location and the "artist labelled the tower Westerkerk" anecdote on Brug 226 were left out (single source, not visible).
- **Christ Church**: J.J. Janssen as designer of the front is "possibly" in the source: written as "probably also the designer". Sunday service time differs between listings, so no time given.
- **Van 't Hoff lab**: UvA says appointed 1877 and plaque on 17 November 2021; the Groenburgwal article says 1878–1891 and gives a different day. Used UvA. The café/exhibition use was current in 2021; not confirmed for 2026, so written in the past tense ("At the time").
- **Goslerhuisje**: 1695 (joodsamsterdam, BAG) against "18th century" (RCE): both given. The owner and his family are private people and are not named; only the firm name, which is the building's name.
- **Huis Wilhelmina**: the city register links 1898 to Wilhelmina's wedding; she married in 1901. 1898 was her coming of age and inauguration (6 September 1898). Used the inauguration and said nobody knows who chose the name.
- **Binnen Bantammerstraat**: "about a thousand" forced repatriations is IISG's estimate, written as "believed". Kong Hing (1928), called the first Chinese restaurant for Dutch customers, is placed at no. 11 only by a Commons user comment: left out. The 2006-era note that Binnenkant 39 housed a Chinese youth association was unverified: left out.
- **De Ruijterkade**: KWIM founding given as 1882 in some places and first sailing 1884 in Wikipedia; only the 1884 sailing is implied. No. 140's architect is given as both J.A. Berg and Nieuwenhuys: left out. No. 139's 1921 wing (attributed to Jan Kuyt with "probably") dropped. Current use of 125 not confirmed beyond "office".
- **Blauw Jan**: the dwarf is named in Wikipedia (a historical person exhibited, 18th century); not named in the text, out of caution.
- **Poppenhuis**: the 1903 school at Groenburgwal 30 belonged to the same Vincentius society; a physical link between the two plots was not found, so the group rests only on the society and the year.
- **Engelbewaarder**: the Sunday sessions run "except in summer" per one guide; iamsterdam lists 2026 dates. The Roth photograph and the Postbar interior come from nl.wikipedia only.
- **Kikkerbilssluis**: name origin explicitly unproven in the source; written that way.

## Route ideas

- **Ships, spices and stokers** (Waalseiland, Oudeschans, harbour front, about 2 km): Scheepvaarthuis, Brug 283, Binnen Bantammerstraat, Bantammerbrug, Huis Wilhelmina, West-Indisch Pakhuis, Kikkerbilssluis, along the Oudeschans to the Scharrebiersluis, back across to De Ruijterkade.
- **Kloveniersburgwal and Groenburgwal** (about 1.5 km): Trippenhuis, De Engelbewaarder, workers' hall (Blauw Jan), Poppenhuis and De Kloof, round to Groenburgwal 30, Van 't Hoff lab, Christ Church, Staalmeestersbrug (Monet view), then via the Zwanenburgwal to the Goslerhuisje.
