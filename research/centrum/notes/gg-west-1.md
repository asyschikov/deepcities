## Conflicts and uncertainties

- Blauwburgwal bombing: the city register text (203005) gives "12 mei" and 46 dead; NOS, nl.wikipedia, Amsterdam op de kaart and Wynia's Week all give 11 May 1940 and 44 dead. Used 11 May and 44.
- Blauwburgwal: the unexploded fourth bomb still in the Herengracht is in Amsterdam op de kaart, Wynia's Week (from Geukes Foppen's book) and nl.wikipedia (NRC 7 Dec 2019); kept. The second explanation (aiming at the post office behind the palace) cut for length only.
- Blauwburgwal 22: Hendrick de Keyser gives 1977 as year of purchase, nl.wikipedia 1976; neither year is in the text. Whether the cellar is still a bakery is unclear, so the text does not say so.
- Matagalpa: nl.wikipedia notes the founder died in Bergen-Belsen in 1945 (a footnote based on FamilySearch); left out as a single weak source.
- Torensluis: nl.wikipedia says the restored cells are used for exhibitions and events; no current confirmation found (web search budget ran out), so `check: Facts`.
- Keizersgracht 316: the national register says three houses were demolished, nl.wikipedia two; the text says "the existing houses on the corner".
- Twentsche Bank: Amsterdam op de kaart dates it 1941, the register 1939 (design) with a 1940 stone; "built 1939–1941" covers both. The current mix (homes and offices) is from BAG.
- Raadhuisstraat bridges: nl.wikipedia mentions police action on the bridge during the 1966 royal wedding riots and three-rail tram track until 1957; left out (single source each).
- Brug 25: the horse and cart that fell into the canal before the 1910 rebuild rests on nl.wikipedia alone; not used.
- Transvaalsche Boer: the client was a widow and the shopkeeper her son; private people, not named.
- Joes Kloppenburg is named because the bridge carries his name officially and his case is public; the attacker is not named.
- The web search budget ran out early in the slice; status checks for current use were done through BAG (api.data.amsterdam.nl) instead.
