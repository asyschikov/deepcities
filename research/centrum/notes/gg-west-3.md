## Conflicts and uncertainties

- Leliegracht 22 (Astoria group): the city register says Van Houten's chocolate mill of 1815 stood here; izi.travel puts it in a coach house on the Prinsengracht. Kept the register version, attributed to the 1815 mill only.
- Moeder Godskerk: the register calls it the first neo-Gothic church in the Netherlands; nl.wikipedia says first outside Brabant and Limburg. Left the "first" claim out.
- De Zaaier (Keizersgracht 22): the NH Nieuws article gives no house number; matched through Van Straaten, 1837 and the Commons category. Strong but indirect.
- Groenlandse Pakhuizen: the whale-oil pits and the Bührmann 1922 and 1970s flats details rest on the archived BMA page plus the register.
- Multatuli Museum: opening days differ between Time Out, OSM and older pages; the text says "a few days a week, check before you go".
- Keizersgracht 318: Commons shows the front in blue sheeting in April 2026; whether the restoration has finished is unknown.
- Molenpad 10-16: register text (2007) says still a garage with homes above; current use not confirmed, so `check: Still there?` is set.
- Huis Marseille: hours (daily 10–18, Thursday to 21) from the museum's FAQ via search, November 2026.
- Felix Meritis: the society's admission rules for women and Jews are from nl.wikipedia only; kept as the article is well sourced. Removed a Look-for line about a name inscription that no source confirmed.
- Removed as unsourced during writing: Rode Hoed and the Synod of Dort, the Rode Hoed name coming from a hat workshop, "the old name stuck" for De Zaaier, a stylistic judgement on the Coymanshuis, the 1958 colour origin of the Roode Huys name, a deep-garden claim at the Huis met de Hoofden.
- Langestraat 3 is not in this slice (the row starts at 5 in the register); the place still tells the 3-19 story as the register does.
- Commons and Wikipedia rate-limited (429) several times; photo checks were partly done in a slow background retry.
