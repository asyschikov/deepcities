## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Paradiso and the A-list.** Not in `A-LIST.md`, so drafted, pitched on the Vrije Gemeente ("never a church") rather than the venue. It is close to A-list for many visitors; decide whether to park it. Outlook set (renovation and extension, plans January 2026).
- **No Amazing.** Candidates if one is wanted: Noordse Bos (the 1670 weavers' scheme, though its best surviving houses, Vijzelgracht 4–8 and 20–26, are not in this slice and appear only as Look-for), or Villa Heineken.
- **Burgerweeshuis vs Walenweeshuis.** The city register texts of 201041 and 201060 call the Burgerweeshuis "het voormalige Walenweeshuis". That is wrong: the Walenweeshuis stayed with the Walloon church until 1967. The Burgerweeshuis (city orphanage) was one of the three charities of the 1670 scheme and owned the plots (Theo Bakker, AODK). Text follows the latter.
- **Weavers' houses beside the Walenweeshuis.** Theo Bakker says the row of three beside the Walenweeshuis subsided badly during the metro works; nl.wikipedia lists Vijzelgracht 4–8 and 20–26 among the listed weavers' houses. I wrote "the three weavers' houses beside the Walenweeshuis" without numbers.
- **Spiegelgracht 27.** nl.wikipedia says "around 1760, designed by Philips Vingboons" (impossible, Vingboons died 1678). The national register says Noortse Bos type, about 1670. Used the register.
- **Nooteboome Uytkijk dates.** The register says "founded 1778"; Amstelodamum 1949 gives the joint will of 1774 and the conversion only after Jan van Mekeren's death in 1791. Text uses 1774 and 1791; `built` is "1724; almshouse from about 1791". The 1835 house rules (cats, oliebollen) are from the regulations, not the founders' will, and are presented that way. Left out: Van Mekeren directed a third of the capital into English or colonial plantation loans (Amstelodamum); true and in the source, cut for length, could be added.
- **Commiezenhuisje date.** AODK and nl.wikipedia Weteringpoort give 1842; nl.wikipedia Weteringschans says 1848. Used 1842.
- **Havana.** Register: built 1875, closed 1914. AODK: stable converted 1873, taken over and closed 1913. Used AODK (it cites newspapers).
- **Utrechtsestraat 132.** Register dates the front 1904, AODK 1901. Used 1901. The register also doubts that "G. Boon" designed the glass.
- **Utrechtsestraat 129 and 104.** The gilded-pig lettering is described in the 2004 register text; the tobacconist on 104 is attested by AODK in 2018. Both flagged `check: Still there?`.
- **Vijzelgracht 1.** Stadsherstel says it bought the corner in 1967, nl.wikipedia says 1968; used 1967. Wikipedia's "Stadsherstel's 1000th building" rests on that one source and is left out. Café Myrabelle's site is live (checked 24 Nov 2026).
- **Walenweeshuis.** Wing dates differ (register 1683 and 1726; nl.wikipedia 1679, 1683, 1728); no wing dates in the text, register dates in `built`. Status after March 2025 unknown without a search: `check: Facts` (has restoration started?) and `outlook: Major change ahead`.
- **Villa Heineken.** Slice says listed 2001, nl.wikipedia says October 2005; used the slice value. Still the head office per AODK (2017); not re-checked.
- **Holtkamp.** "Basic design said to be by Piet Kramer" is AODK's hedge and is kept as "is said". Opening hours from the shop's own site (24 Nov 2026).
- **Weteringschans 96.** Architects E. van der Eyk and J.A. de Waal come from AODK only (register gives none). The first matron (a baroness) is not named.
- **Weteringschans 231–251.** Van der Pek's absence 1898–1902 is rendered "a break around 1900". 241 (estate agent Gerke, who may have designed it himself) and 253 left out of the group as they don't carry the "builders moved in" story.
- **Peper en Zout.** Hartog van Banda was a perkenier (nutmeg planter) on Banda Neira; the source says nothing about the labour on his plantations, so nothing is added.
- **Paradiso capacity, Fabchannel, vinyl club** etc. left out as venue trivia.

## Leads

- Weteringschans 88: Adolf Friedemann, author of the first Theodor Herzl biography (1914), lived here 1928–1932 (https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Weteringschans/88-90). Possible plaque or Jewish-history line.
- Tweede Weteringplantsoen: Amsterdam's first public playground (1880, funded by Nicolaas Tetterode), U.J. Klaren memorial stone (1956) in the playground mural, Van Randwijk memorial wall, Zeelandbank (1953 flood memorial bench by Nico Onkenhout, 1957), replica of Hildo Krop's Moeder Aarde, Amsterdam School switch box by P.L. Marnette in front of 1–17; park being redesigned 2025–2026, Van Randwijk monument to be moved (https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweede_Weteringplantsoen).
- Weteringschans 22: villa lived in by Mathilde Willink and Frank Govers (https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weteringschans).
- Weteringschans 81–89: site of the squat De Grote Wetering, cleared 1980/81, now offices (same page).
- Weteringschans 193: music shop Charles, open since March 1954 (AODK); current status unchecked.
- Spiegelgracht 38: tile tableau of a red cat on the side of the pothuis (https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiegelgracht_38).
- Weteringstraat 19 (corner 1e Weteringdwarsstraat): a bakery without a break from 1671 to 1958, Bakker Claassen (Theo Bakker PDF, http://www.theobakker.net/pdf/noordsebos.pdf).
- Westeinde: Zadkine's La demeure humaine (1968) at De Nederlandsche Bank (https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westeinde_(Amsterdam)).

## Route ideas

- **Weteringbuurt and the Schans, about 2.5 km:** Paradiso, Industrieschool, Commiezenhuisje and the lock, Peper en Zout, Nooteboome Uytkijk, Spiegelgracht 27, Walenweeshuis, Vijzelgracht 1, Holtkamp (coffee stop), Noordse Bos blocks on the Vijzelgracht, wetering-1's almshouses on the Eerste Weteringdwarsstraat, Villa Heineken, Weteringschans 96, the builders' row, the Lutheran orphanage, then the Utrechtsestraat shop fronts, Havana, the central kitchen and De Posthoorn.
- **Orphans of Amsterdam (theme):** Walenweeshuis, the Lutheran orphanage here and its predecessor on the Lauriergracht (jordaan-2), the Burgerweeshuis's rental blocks (Noordse Bos), the Paleis van Justitie paupers' orphanage (wetering-2), Amsterdam Museum as the Burgerweeshuis (A-list, landmark only).
- **Care and charity on the Weteringschans:** children's clinic (96), Lutheran orphanage (261), Industrieschool (31), central kitchen (Utrechtsedwarsstraat 28-30).
