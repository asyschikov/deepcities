## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Swarte house address**: the 2008 to 2010 texts (WJGR, Binnenstad) put it on the plot of no. 194/196; Ymere's own notices (2010, 2012) and Wikipedia give Willemsstraat 204 (204–210). No. 194 no longer exists in the address register, so the plot was renumbered; the draft uses 204 and the PDOK point.
- **Hermanusje van Alles**: Wikipedia says puns since 1980, changed monthly; Zeven 2025 says fifty years of puns (the book title is "50 jaar etalage"), changed every two months. The draft says "some fifty years" and "every two months or so". Owner and daughter are not named.
- **Westerstraat 202 stone**: WJGR (September 2013) says it was placed on 3 August 2013 and calls the building the Aloysiusschool with the address Westerstraat 297 (the Theo Thijssen school's address); Wikipedia says 2014 and Westerstraat 202. The draft follows the contemporaneous WJGR date and the Wikipedia address (photo of the stone at 202, November 2020).
- **Blaeu**: Wikipedia says the workshop started in 1635, Stadsherstel "around 1637": both given. The Stadsherstel walk calls Joan Willem's brother; he was his son (Wikipedia), so that was not used. Bloemgracht 76 is not known to be listed; no heritage fields.
- **Bloemgracht 82**: 4en5mei records a stone unveiled in 1945 with "Dick Wolters" (a nickname) and "Ko Stevesen" (misspelt, wrong birth date); WJGR reports a new stone unveiled on 4 May 2014, and its 2023 text quotes the correct names. Whether both stones hang there is the check: Facts. The WJGR 2023 piece names the downstairs neighbour as suspected betrayer and Maarten Kuiper among the killers; 4en5mei says the circumstances are unclear, so the draft says it was never settled and leaves Kuiper out. The resident who held the 2013 and 2014 open houses is not named.
- **De Baadster**: the Parool ran a piece on 24 July 2026 ("Hoe het beeld de baadster onverwacht alsnog weer opdook", paywalled, cited on nl.wikipedia); not read. The draft rests on the Krop museum newsletter and Wikipedia.
- **Electric Ladyland**: the latest dated report is WJGR 2016 (and Vice 2011); the website (copyright 1999–2004) still links to an appointment system. Hence check: Still there?.
- **Phone box**: the 2020 article says the box was coming "binnenkort"; the 2021 column shows it in use. Pin on Boomstraat 67 from iamsterdam's listing (not used as a source); check: Still there? covers the spot as well.
- **Palmgracht 68**: the VVAG page gives no year for the Raksasa stone (the scout said 2009); no year in the draft. The owner (VVAG chairman) is not named.

## Route ideas

- **Bloemgracht printers and resisters**: Bloemgracht 76 (Blaeu) and 82 (the resistance attic) face each other across the Tweede Leliedwarsstraat; walk up to Electric Ladyland and the Blijvend Applaus houses at the Egelantiersgracht corner, then east to r2-jordaan-1's Steeman, Landman and Seeman.
- **Jordaan statues that moved**: Nieuwe Naatje (Rokin, Bullebaksluis, Marnixplantsoen), De Baadster (lost 2004 to 2019) and the Volksvrouw on the Marnixplein, with r2-jordaan-1's Bevrijdingslinde and Vaste klant nearby.
- **Westerstraat oddities**: Hermanusje van Alles (138), the soap boiler's stone (202), Perlee (119, r2-jordaan-1), then north via the Boomstraat phone box to the Palmgracht stones and the Swarte house in the Willemsstraat.
