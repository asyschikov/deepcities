## Conflicts and uncertainties

- Planciusblok: length 200 m (nl.wikipedia, VAK article) vs 120 m (aodk); no length given in the text. Completion 1856 (RCE, VAK article, aodk) vs "first block ready in 1858" (nl.wikipedia Planciusstraat); used 1856. Rank Amazing is my call (first large block of the first Dutch housing association, built as a public model); downgrade to Nice if the map wants Amazing only for things that look spectacular.
- Planciusschool: aodk says the railway company HIJSM paid for the new school; the 1914 newspaper (Nieuws van den Dag 7-8-1914, Delpher) and nl.wikipedia only say the old school's site went to the State for the railway viaduct doubling and the city bought the Teertuinen plots. Payer left out. The building-history name sequence (Bickersschool, then Planciusschool) follows both sources; the exact closing year (c. 1978 per Wikipedia infobox) is not stated.
- Komter block: the V&D fire is 4 January 1940 in nl.wikipedia (Volkskrant 5-1-1940) and 10 February 1940 in De Telegraaf of 13 August 1953. Text says only "1940". Number of homes: 32 (aodk, register 12 + 20) vs 30 planned (Telegraaf 1953); used 32. Current shop: Ekoplaza Foodmarqt per aodk (updated February 2026); text says "an organic supermarket".
- Femina Muller: the city register calls it "the very first Dutch kinderbewaarplaats"; that is contradicted by the 1864 Coronel report on existing nurseries and by the fact that the Vereeniging already existed. Left out. The Canon Sociaal Werk PDFs (Sozio 2010, Parool 2022) could not be fetched with curl (404 page); facts from them rest on search snippets and are all repeated by aodk.
- Korte Marnixstraat: the city register says no. 7 had a "smelterij" on the ground floor; aodk (population register, address books) says a farrier's workshop (hoefsmid), and nl.wikipedia describes horseshoe reliefs; used the farrier. The register spells the builder "Appen" for no. 2 and "Appel" elsewhere; used Appel. No. 1's designer: register "unknown", aodk Appel. The current use of the corner café (Haarlemmerplein 31) is not confirmed (register 2005: still a café); the text only says it became a café in 1929.
- Prinseneiland 89: the city register's history is muddled (church from 1876 per Kruizinga; warehouse of 1891 by A.L. van Gendt per MIP; church 1891–1928 per galeries.nl). aodk, with the 1892 tender and consecration notices from Delpher, gives the 1892 rebuilding by F.J. Krop for F.W. Schwartz and use to c. 1926, then the 1928 car store; I followed aodk. The register's claim that painter Jozef Verheijen rented the attic during the war rests on one gallery site; left out.
- Roogans: register says tar-boiling works (teerkokerij), aodk says a warehouse for the tar trade; the text says "a building of the tar trade" and attributes "tar-boiling works" to the register.
- 't Seepaert: register dates it 1943 (design 1942); the slice says 1935–1940. aodk says "probably a pharmaceutical factory"; left out. Squatting in 1980 rests on aodk alone.
- Rederij Goedkoop: the 2017 city register mentions a restaurant on the ground floor; a current letting brochure (funda in business) only shows offices, and no restaurant could be confirmed, so the text says offices. The Gerrit van 't Net mosaics in the entrance hall are inside; not mentioned in Look for.
- Singel 2 (5249): WebSearch hit the session budget and nl.wikipedia started rate-limiting (429) before a dedicated search; only the RCE one-liner and the Singel article were read. Worth one more search in a later pass (gable stone "De Kruiwagen", 1606–1611).

## Leads

- Haarlemmerplein 29: the 1901 corner shop opposite Haarlemmerplein 31, built as its pair to frame the entrance to the Korte Marnixstraat (city register 207005).
- GEB electricity box of the 1920s–1930s in front of Korte Marnixkade 9 (city register 212016); could join a switch-box theme with the Buiten Vissersstraat one (haarlemmer-1 leads).
- De Roo Vos, Nieuwe Teertuinen 24 (rijksmonument, 1765): tar store, soap works, printing works and sail-maker; Het Parool was printed here illegally during the war (nl.wikipedia Nieuwe Teertuinen). Strong hidden-history lead.
- Nieuwe Teertuinen 11A–14A: four Gulden Veem warehouses Essen, Worms, Liefde and Hoop, used for tobacco into the 1950s (nl.wikipedia Nieuwe Teertuinen).
- Komter & Mastenbroek's studio on the Prinseneiland in the former studio of Breitner (nl.wikipedia Auke Komter, gallery); Breitner worked on the Prinseneiland until 1914.
- Open Atelierdagen on the Western Islands around Whitsun (nl.wikipedia Westelijke Eilanden).
- The 2025–2026 clean-up of the Western Islands canals, 38,000 m³ of polluted silt removed, kingfishers back (Parool 12-5-2026, via nl.wikipedia); Westerdok clean-up planned from December 2026.
- Tram 3 loop at the Korte Zoutkeetsgracht and the narrowed tram passage under the Planciusstraat railway viaduct (nl.wikipedia Planciusstraat).

## Route ideas

- Western Islands and Planciusbuurt loop (about 2 km), linking with haarlemmer-1's Bickerseiland places: Haarlemmerplein and the Korte Marnixstraat, Korte Marnixkade 4, under the railway to the Planciusblok and the Planciusschool, over the Sloterdijkerbrug to De Roogans and the Apostolic church, Liefde en Eendracht and across to De Atlas on the Vierwindenstraat, back over the Drieharingenbrug to 't Seepaert, then the Bickerseiland workshops. Westerdoksdijk 40 is a 10-minute detour along the Westerdok.
- Haarlemmerbuurt social history: Femina Muller's crèche (Vinkenstraat), Komter's flats (Korte Prinsengracht), the Posthoorn schools, and the Planciusblok.
