## Conflicts and uncertainties

- Peek & Cloppenburg leaving the Dam (end of February 2027) rests on FashionUnited citing Het Parool, 2 Nov 2026; set as outlook, recheck in spring 2027.
- NACO-huisje: Stadsherstel says the tenants mean to use it as a boarding point; actual use not confirmed, so the text says "mean to".
- Steiger 10 office (518410): not confirmed standing after the works behind the station; set `check: Still there?`.
- Nieuwezijds Kapel: the exact spot of the 16th-century Heilige Stede gate in the Kapelsteeg is unconfirmed; set `check: Location`. A 2007 sale claim on Wikipedia is unsourced and left out.
- De Papegaai: Sunday hours differ between the parish's Dutch page (12.00 to 14.30) and its English page (10.30 to 14.00); used the Dutch one. Where exactly the parrot mosaic and the "kwartier voor God" sign hang, and whether the roof turret is visible, should be checked on site; the Look for list stays vague.
- Korenmetershuisje: the Watergeuzen 1577 story is attributed to Geert Mak in the text. The national register gives 1630, Wikipedia and Stadsherstel 1620; used 1620.
- Victoria Hotel: the celebrity guest list on nl.wikipedia is tagged "Bron?" and left out.
- Damrak: Roode Leeuw "15th century" claim is promotional only and left out.
- Kalverstraat 1: current tenant unknown, so the text does not name one.
- Molsteeg 4: the register notes an intact shop interior; current shop not confirmed, so the interior is not described. The child's name is on the stone, not in the text (private person).
- Munttoren guardhouse: the urinal and its gay cruising history is from nl.wikipedia (citing Versteegen); told in the past tense.
- Commons API returned 429 throughout this run, so no file pages could be opened. Every photo used is the item's own Wikidata image from the slice (P18 of that monument); none was eyeballed.
