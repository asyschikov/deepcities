## Conflicts and uncertainties

- Photos: the Commons API returned 429 throughout, and the coordinator then stopped Commons calls. Every photo used is the record's own Wikidata image from the slice (P18); none was opened or eyeballed. Athenaeum and the tile sermon (municipal monuments) have no photo.
- Maagdenhuis: national register gives 1738-1787, Wikipedia 1783-1787; used 1783-1787. Left out any claim that the 1969 occupation caused the 1970 university reform law (Wikipedia is ambiguous).
- Rasphuis: Wikipedia articles disagree on when it closed (1815 vs redundant in 1892); text only says it was demolished in 1892. Gate attribution to De Keyser has a question mark in the register: written as "attributed".
- NZV 282: Tingel Tangel start date differs (Stadsherstel 1957, nl.wikipedia 1963): no year given. Current name Theater De Richel confirmed by I amsterdam listings for 2026.
- Politieposthuisje: register says listed 2001, Wikipedia 22 April 2002; used 2001. Café Het Stadspaleis per Wikipedia (2026) and Wanderlog.
- Die Port van Cleve: Heineken bought De Hooiberg in 1863 (nl and en wiki) or 1864 (one search result); used 1863. "First electrically lit building" (en wiki, 1879) left out as weakly sourced. Where the numbered steaks are served now differs between sources (hotel restaurant vs Bouillon d'Amsterdam); text only says the restaurant began numbering them in 1874.
- Kasteel van Aemstel: register says the gable tiles show scenes from Gijsbrecht's life; Wikipedia says the panel shows the name guarded by the river gods of Amstel and IJ (after a dropped Chronos design). Used Wikipedia; worth checking on site.
- De Standaard moved in 1917 (Wikipedia) or 1918 (register); used 1917. Handelsblad building's current use not found, so not stated.
- Leesmuseum: current use unclear (Fashion for Good 2018-2024, now possibly retail); not stated. Whether Mak van Waay auctioned looted Jewish property in the war is disputed in the sources found (one DBNL text says not, but notes German advertising); left out entirely.
- Passage Central: Albert Heijn on the ground floor since 2009 per Wikipedia; not rechecked for 2026.
- Hajenius: the register's "Obernkirchener zandsteen" kept as Obernkirchen sandstone.
- Athenaeum: interior architects named Wiek Röling and Patrice Girod (The Low Countries) vs "L.C. Roling en J.P. Girod" (city register); used the former. Co-founder Johan Polak only appears in a search summary, so only Rob van Gennep is named.
- Arti et Amicitiae: exhibition halls Tue-Sun 12-17 from arti.nl (Nov 2026).
- Six lanterns: register says placed by order and at the cost of Willem I; Wikipedia says he died in 1843 before they were placed (May 1844, under Willem II) and its reference note says the register mentions municipal funding. Who paid is left out.
- Raadhuisstraat 11 / NZV 226 (207033): the register's "supermarket and KPN offices" is outdated; the building is the W Hotel. Not drafted.
- Candida group mixes national (518442) and municipal (207037, 201018) records: heritage set to "National + municipal monument" as in nieuwezijde-1.
