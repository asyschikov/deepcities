## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Greiner front (S-078)**: PhotoLexicon describes the putti frieze with Daguerre and Niépce in a passage that also mentions his second studio at Damrak 24, so it is ambiguous which building it means. RCE 3766 confirms the frieze "with scenes about photography" is on Nieuwendijk 89; the scout cites Parool (20 May 2023) for the medallions there. Written as Nieuwendijk 89. The frieze makers "Van den Bossche en Crevels" are from PhotoLexicon only.
- **Greiner court supplier**: a search snippet said "Hofleverancier 1879"; not read in a source, left out.
- **Last elm plant year**: Bomenstichting walk says 1889; LRMB says 1900–1910 / "around 1900"; city list 1900. Written as "in the years after the station was built (1889)".
- **Lex Horn walls**: Wikipedia gives 3 x 7 m, Stadscuratorium 2.5 x 6.5 m; sizes left out. Where they hang inside the garage is unknown (check: Location).
- **Bike garage opening**: no exact opening date found; "in use since early 2023" rests on the Fietsflat closing on 30 Jan 2023 and Commons photos of the garage in use in February 2023.
- **Walk of Pride**: 52 tiles (ANP, NH Nieuws) vs 53 (NL Times at the unveiling). The scout's list of initiators (IHLIA, Homomonument foundation, GALA) is only partly confirmed: GALA laid it, Richard Keldoulis is named as an initiator.
- **Sint Luciënsteeg wall**: VVAB archive documents (2021 advice, Ronnes article) disagree on whether the older alley fronts with the stones are kept; the museum's architect speaks of a new window at the corner. Hence outlook Major change ahead and check Still there?.
- **Weiner on the Spui**: the 1996 date in the scout paragraph was not confirmed in the Kunstwacht text read; left out.
- **Kromelleboogsteeg closed**: scout BC-07 says the alley is closed; no source read confirms it (nl.wikipedia has no article), so the enrich line only names it.
- **Rokin 64 and Bartholomeus de Moor**: left out (see S-093).
- **Militiegebouw**: round-1 text said it is still part of the University Library; corrected (see enrich edits).
- **Maison de Bonneterie building today**: CBRE (undated) says H&M leased the Amsterdam store; no dated source, so the current tenant is not named.
- **Hotel Polen**: Andere Tijden gives 105 guests and alarm at 06:39; Wikipedia "over 100" and first engine 06:42. Body uses neither guest count nor alarm time.
- nl.wikipedia returned errors for several raw fetches late in the session (Sint Luciënsteeg, Kromelleboogsteeg); those facts come from other sources.
