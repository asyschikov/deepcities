## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Beurs van Berlage (437)** is not in A-LIST.md but is a top-list sight in every guidebook. Parked as an A-list candidate; please add it to the Candidates list (I may not edit A-LIST.md). Its lanterns and fountains (518418) went into the Beursplein 5 place, pitched on the stock traders leaving Berlage's building.
- **Compagnietheater (3035)**: sold in 2021; compagnietheater.nl now redirects to an unrelated gambling site. Current use unknown: `check: Facts` set. Search engines could not be reached by curl.
- **Allard Pierson front stone**: the register says Oberkirchner sandstone below, nl.wikipedia says Bremer. Left the stone type out.
- **Klein Trippenhuis width**: nl.wikipedia gives 2.40 m on one page and 2.50 m on another; text says "roughly two and a half metres". The infobox there says monument 3027, the slice and the register link say 3026; used 3026.
- **Makelaarsbruggetje**: the register says "probably" by F. Olie; nl.wikipedia says tobacco broker Frits Olie built it at his own initiative. Kept the wiki account, which is detailed (rent paid for the water).
- **Accijnshuis**: register attributes it to Jacob van Campen; nl.wikipedia says unknown, Van Campen or Pieter de Keyser. Text gives both. Current café not named (Café Heffer since 1997 per wiki; heffer domain is parked, so the name may have changed).
- **Ship Chandlers Warehouse**: nl.wikipedia claims "oldest shop in Amsterdam", built 1623, and 17th-century leaded glass on every floor, all unsourced (one flagged "Bron?"); left out. Register dates the front to about 1750 (with a question mark), the BMZ photo caption to about 1775.
- **Binnengasthuis**: the Commons photo "Amsterdam - Binnengasthuistraat 9a.JPG" is tagged as monument 518303, not 518310; not used.
- **Bethaniënklooster**: nl.wikipedia still describes it as a concert venue with music-student rooms; the owners' site says it has been a B&B since 2017. Text follows the B&B site.
- **Tot Heil des Volks**: nl.wikipedia tells that the director escaped a raid by pointing at the word "Heil" on the society's sign and giving the Hitler salute; that was at its building on the Oudezijds Achterburgwal, not at Barndesteeg 21-25, and rests on one unsourced passage. Left out (see Leads).
- **Rederij Kooij**: still sails from "Rokin t/o nr 125" per its site; the booth itself was not checked in person.
- **Oudemanhuispoort**: the book market's opening hours were not found from a source I could fetch; the text gives only the university building's hours for the courtyard.
- **Oude Hoogstraat 6**: the register (2006) mentions a school bag hanging above the shop since 1956. Could be a nice Look-for or small curiosity if someone confirms it is still there.
- Records 902 (Universiteitstheater) and 3994 (house against the Oude Kerk) might have stories that a book source (e.g. Vis, De Poort, or Oude Kerk histories) would give; nothing found online.

## Leads

- Tot Heil des Volks wartime story (the "Heil" sign and a raid at its building on the Oudezijds Achterburgwal): https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tot_Heil_des_Volks
- Rijksmuseum holds painted wall hangings and a ceiling from "the room at Nieuwe Doelenstraat 22" (Commons files KA 2913, KA 2914, KA 2915): a lead for the De Jaren building or its neighbour.
- Huis aan de Drie Grachten (Grimburgwal), seen from the Sleutelbrug: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleutelbrug_(Amsterdam)
- The Bureau voor Handelsinlichtingen beside the Accijnshuis, now coffeeshop The Grasshopper: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accijnshuis_(Amsterdam)
- Nes 17, The Empire, one of the earliest gay cafés, until the 1930s: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nes_(Amsterdam)
- Herberg of Elisabeth Bas on the corner of the Pieter Jacobszstraat, where Frederik Hendrik stayed; VOC founded in 1602 in the house of Dirck van Os in the Nes: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nes_(Amsterdam)
- Belle, the statue for sex workers on the Oudekerksplein (2007, Els Rijerse), and the bronze hand on a breast in the pavement (1993): nl.wikipedia search results.
- The Strümphler organ of the Compagnietheater, now in the Eusebiuskerk in Arnhem (outside Amsterdam).
