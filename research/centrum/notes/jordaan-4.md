# jordaan-4: notes

44 records. 9 places: 7 with a story of their own and 2 groups, holding 14 records in all.
No A-list records. 5 records belong with places in other slices: 2 with the Willemsstraat
housing group (jordaan-6), 2 with the city masons' house (jordaan-3) and 1 with Eelke van
Houten's second-hand gables (jordaan-1). 25 records: no story found.

How the research went: I read the full city and national register texts for every record, the
raw nl.wikipedia wikitext for all 27 records with an article plus the street articles
(Passeerdersgracht, Raamplein, Raamdwarsstraat) and the people (Willem Drees, Jac. P. Thijsse),
amsterdamopdekaart.nl for Prinsengracht 12, 108, 400-406, Passeerdersgracht 23-25 and 28-32,
Raamplein 1 and Egelantiersgracht 2-6, and stadsherstel.nl for Prinsengracht 206 and
Passeerdersgracht 6. No WebSearch (budget used up), no browser, no Commons API. For the "no
story found" records the check was the full register text plus Wikipedia where there is one, and
amsterdamopdekaart where it had a page; no separate web search per record was possible.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Mixed group status.** "Two schools on the old Leidse cemetery" holds three national (518264-6)
  and one municipal monument (202055). heritage_status is set to National monument, as jordaan-3
  did for the Noorderkerk group.
- **Raamplein 1 current use.** Eyeworks (TV production) from 2012 (wiki) or 2013
  (amsterdamopdekaart) to 2017/18; text says 2013 to about 2018. Use since then not found:
  `check: Facts`. The flats on the former playground rest on amsterdamopdekaart alone (page with a
  November 2026 photo): set as `outlook: Major change ahead`, owner not named.
- **Handelsschool architect.** Wikipedia gives Leguyt and Springer, the national register only
  Springer, amsterdamopdekaart Leguyt (Springer's role unclear). Field gives both. The text over
  the door (HOGESCHOOL VOOR ECONOMISCHE STUDIES, in the register) has been removed (wiki), so it is
  not in Look for. The cog ship and owl by Wilhelm Rädecker on the front are Wikipedia only: left out.
- **Thijsse.** Wikipedia (Thijsse) says he became head of "the public school on the
  Passeerdersgracht" in 1892, before this building (1896). amsterdamopdekaart names him head of
  school 32 in this building in 1897, until 1902 (wiki Thijsse: kweekschool from end 1901, moved to
  Bloemendaal 1902). Text says "from 1897". amsterdamopdekaart also writes "Scholen 3 en 32" once,
  a typo for 5 and 32.
- **Double school dates and class.** Register and amsterdamopdekaart: 1896 design; amsterdamopdekaart
  dates it 1899; Wikipedia says the cemetery was cleared in 1897. Field says 1896–1901 for the group.
  The register calls it a school "of the first class, fully paid by the city and meant for the
  poorest"; the text says "free schools for the poorest" and avoids the class label.
- **Arbeidsbeurs "first".** amsterdamopdekaart calls the 1887 Arbeidsbeurs the first labour bureau
  in the Netherlands; the national register says the building is the earliest purpose-designed
  municipal office for labour mediation. The text uses the register's claim only. Kramer's
  authorship is "probably" (amsterdamopdekaart) and "later attributed" (wiki): text says "usually
  credited". The Maatschappij voor den Werkenden Stand also has a place in nieuwmarkt-1; worth a
  cross-link.
- **Rood paleis year.** Passeerdersgracht 19 article says 1938, the novel's article 1936: no year
  in the text.
- **Prinsengracht 70 mural.** Painted 2009 (amsterdamcentraal.nl, July 2010: "last year"). The Oost
  draft of the Zeeburgerdijk mural says the Prinsengracht wall was painted without permission;
  not confirmed here, left out. Last confirmation of the mural: Het Parool 8 March 2025 (cited on
  Wikipedia). Not flagged. Wikipedia's "the artists probably appear as the cherry-picker crew"
  softened to "a painted cherry picker with its crew".
- **Leidsegracht 70 barber.** Wikipedia says first woman with the men's barber diploma in the
  Netherlands; the Nationaal Archief photo caption (1969) says first in Amsterdam. Text says
  Amsterdam. The daughter is not named (private person). Whether a barber still works there is
  not known; the text keeps it in the past tense. jordaan-3's ledger has 3379 as "1666 neck gable
  for Jan Adriaansz Buys, leans on no. 70": consistent with Buys owning land here, not checked further.
- **Saturnus date.** Gevelstenen van Amsterdam: second half 17th c.; Amsterdam Monumentenstad: 1705.
  Text gives the society's date.
- **J.J. van Noortschool opening.** Wikipedia: opened 3 October 1925; register: taken into use 1926.
  No opening date in the text. Current use (British Language Training Centre) rests on Wikipedia,
  2026; the text says "a language school".
- **Gunters & Meuser.** amsterdamopdekaart (last edited 2021) calls it still existing, taken over by
  Isero in 2016; guntersenmeuser.nl now shows a suspended-domain page and Isero's store list did
  not render with curl. `check: Still there?`.
- **Prinsengracht 12 (204049)**: the register praises the "completely intact" 19th-century shop
  interior as very rare. If the shop is open to the public and the interior survives, it could be
  an Okay place; a browser check could settle it.

## Leads

- Walenhoek street sign and the alley itself (Passeerdersgracht 11-17): photographed 2026,
  Wikipedia gallery "2026 Straatbord Walenhoek-Walengang, Asd.jpg".
- Passeerdersgracht 21, built 1910 for the city cleansing department (Stadsreiniging), not listed
  (https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Passeerdersgracht/21).
- Raamplein 6: from 1744 a farrier's and carriage smithy (later Gebr. Schous Autoveren), demolished
  late 1980s; Marnixstraat 360, Paul Huf's photo studio after the war (nl.wikipedia Raamplein).
- Raampleintje: the widened quay where the Raamstraat meets the Prinsengracht, a folk name
  (nl.wikipedia Raamplein).
- Lijnbaansgracht 211-218: odd building outline follows the old kink of the Lijnbaansgracht
  (nl.wikipedia Passeerdersgracht).
- Tile tableaux by Rozenburg inside Raamplein 1 (Huis met de Hoofden and the school): only if the
  building ever opens (open days).

## Route ideas

- **Passeerdersgracht loop** (10 minutes): Prinsengracht 394 (Saturnus) and 400 (Van Noortschool),
  round into the Passeerdersgracht for the Walenhoek refuge, the Arbeidsbeurs across the water,
  the two schools at the Raamplein end, and back along the Prinsengracht to In de Buis at the
  Leidsegracht corner, with the city masons' house (jordaan-3) a few steps on.
- **Western canal-side curiosities**: Prinsengracht 70 (mural), Gunters & Meuser at the
  Egelantiersgracht corner, Prinsengracht 206 (Batenburg stone), to link with the Van Houten
  gables (jordaan-1) and the Zeeburgerdijk mural in Oost as a London Police pair.
