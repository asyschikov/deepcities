# jordaan-2: notes

71 records. 8 places: 3 with a story of their own (Turksche Keizerspoort, Platanenhof, Roothaan)
and 5 groups, holding 22 records in all. No A-list records. 11 records belong with places already
drafted in jordaan-1 (Hofje Venetiae, Eelke van Houten's second-hand gables); their numbers
need adding there (see Conflicts). 38 records: no story found.

How the research went: I read the full register texts (city and national) for every record and the
raw nl.wikipedia wikitext for all 55 records that have an article, plus the street articles
(Lauriergracht, Laurierstraat (Amsterdam), Hazenstraat (Amsterdam), Van Houten-monumenten) and
the people articles (Jan Roothaan, Lauriergracht 37). I also used hofjesinamsterdam.nl,
jordaanweb.nl, broersma.nl and WebSearch for current status. Commons rate-limited hard (429s
with 50-second waits), so photo search was limited to checking that the Wikidata images exist
and show the right building. Almost every house article on the Lauriergracht and Laurierstraat
comes from one Wikipedia author working from Stadsarchief photos and Amsterdam Monumentenstad.
These articles are the only source for many small facts, so I used them only for things that can
be seen or that a second source supports.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Merges into jordaan-1 (needs a decision).** 213048 (Lauriergracht 97, the back entrance of Hofje
  Venetiae) and ten Van Houten houses on the Lauriergracht and Laurierstraat (3223, 3225, 3240,
  3244, 3246, 3247, 3248, 3249, 3262, 3233) tell the same story as places already in
  jordaan-1.yaml. I did not edit that file. Adding the ten numbers to "Eelke van Houten's
  second-hand gables" keeps its monument_number under 200 characters (about 115). A Look for
  line for the Laurierstraat (for example nos. 27–29 and 36–38) could go there too. Laurierstraat
  62 (3250) is also a Van Houten house, but it got its own place for the Roothaan stone.
- **De Voorzienigheid split.** jordaan-1 has the Elandsstraat side (213030, 205009, 200534), and
  its text already mentions the chapel. My place covers the Lauriergracht side and is pitched on the
  chapel's rescue and KesselsKramer. The two could be merged, or linked in a route.
- **KesselsKramer / chapel outlook.** Broersma's article is undated. It says the agency has
  decided to leave after more than 28 years, and Broersma lists the property. The Funda page
  (seen only in search results, blocked for curl) shows it for sale. Set `outlook: Major
  change ahead` with no date. Broersma puts the wafer bakery at no. 37, but nl.wikipedia says no. 37
  is a girls' boarding school of 1929, so the bakery is left out.
- **Founding date of De Voorzienigheid:** the city register says 1852 (213057), jordaan-1 says
  founded here in 1856 (213030). My text gives no year.
- **Karthuizer monastery:** wiki says it was destroyed by the Geuzen in 1566, the register says
  it ended with the Reformation of 1578. The text uses both (wrecked 1566, graveyard after 1578).
  The peat ration differs (25 "manden" on wiki, 25 "ton" on hofjesinamsterdam), so the text just says
  "a yearly ration of peat". Opening hours (10:00–20:00, no groups) come only from Amsterdam
  Oude Stad. Worth a `check` on site if the user wants it.
- **Breitner's dates:** RKD (via wiki) says 1893–1899, en.wikipedia says 1893–1898, Christie's says
  1894. The text says "in the 1890s". I could not confirm which houses appear in the Stedelijk
  painting, so the text says only that he photographed and painted the row opposite.
- **Platanenhof:** jordaanweb says the theatre is in a former "schuilkerk" (hidden church), and
  that the oldest part is a wooden house of about 1550. Both claims rest on single sources and are
  left out. The war-time weapons in the tunnels (wiki) are also left out (disputed in the
  article itself). Main-wing date: register 1706, wiki 1705, so the text says "early 1700s".
- **WWI blocks:** the register names the designer as both "Joh. G. IJzendoorn" and
  "Joh. G. IJzerman", so the name is left out.
- **Rooie Nelis (3245):** the café closed (wiki says in 2019; NH Nieuws reports the closure after
  the operator's death, the city council debating its future). No later news on a buyer or new
  use. A strong local story (one family from 1937, model for the set of 't Schaep met de 5
  pooten, per wiki), but the current state is unknown, so there is no draft. Could become a place with
  `status: Gone` once it is clear what the corner is now.
- **Lauriergracht 116:** wiki says the Lutheran orphanage was there "between 1784 and 1811",
  but its own print of the 1770s already shows the orphanage name over the gate. The text puts it
  there before 1784 and has it rebuilt in 1784.
