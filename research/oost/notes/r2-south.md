Decisions:
- **115 is its own place, not a merge.** The statue's story (1967 unveiling, pulled off its plinth by squatters at the 1982 De Blaaskop eviction, a mock Schaefer statue on the empty plinth, moves in 2007, 2010 and 2018, the new Van Houwelingen plinth) is too strong for one line in the newspaper street place. The "lost Wibauthuis" is no longer lost: the Hogeschool van Amsterdam built a new Wibauthuis on the site in 2015. The body says so.
- **136**: no plaque or object about the etching exists. The place stands on the Rembrandttoren as the named landmark. It is pitched as hidden history, not architecture.
- **138**: I added the Wolkerstuin (Jan and Karina Wolkers' garden 1972 to 1980, a writers' residence since 2018) to the scout item. It is the best hook. The park is open to the public (amstelglorie.nl).
- **121** stands alone as Okay because no place of mine is close enough to carry it as a Look for line. The Wingerdweg school in Noord with the same reliefs is on the map already, so the body mentions it.

## Left out

- **134 Firefighters' war memorial, Ringdijk 98.** A 4en5mei photo from February 2024 shows it **indoors**, against a red wall inside the Kazerne Willem fire station. It is a sandstone relief of two firemen and a child with a flame on top. You cannot see it from the street, and I found no public opening, so I did not write a place. If the coordinator wants it anyway, the facts are on 4en5mei (monument 1475). Its text reads "De heilige vlam geloofd, het kwade vuur gedoofd". The last name is H.J. van Leersum, who died on 15 October 1940. Kunstwacht has it wrong as "gedoofd ... gedoofd", "Van Leeuwen" and "13.10.1940". Two men died at Schiphol on 10 May 1940, and four died at the Planetenplein on 11 October 1940 during the RAF raid on Tuindorp Oostzaan. The Weesperzijde 99 liberation plaque was not checked.

## Source conflicts

- Wibauthuis: Kunstwacht says it "stood from 1961 to 1997". nl.wikipedia says it was built 1961 to 1968, left by the city in the 1990s and demolished in 2006 and 2007. I used Wikipedia.
- Wibaut unveiling: the statue article says two grandchildren unveiled it, the Floor Wibaut article says two great-grandchildren. I wrote "two of his descendants". The return was placed in July 2018 (nl.wikipedia) and unveiled in June 2019 (AT5). I kept both dates. Kunstwacht's "since 2019" refers to the plinth.
- Krop reliefs: Kunstwacht counts 43 and the Hildo Krop Museum counts 41. The text gives both. The school year is 1926 (Kenniscentrum, blog). Commons dates a photo 1927.
- Les Grandes Formes: Kunstwacht says pupils of the Florens College did the pruning. A former teacher writes it was the Lagere Tuinbouwschool Linnaeus. The text says "a nearby horticultural school". The masts refer to the De Meer floodlights (nl.wikipedia) or to the power pylons near the Diemen power station (the teacher). The English text mentions both.
- Alma's Compositie: dated "around 1955" by nl.wikipedia and Kunstwacht, 1958 by Spoorbeeld. The text says "the 1950s".
- Opland's metro art: Kunstwacht gives 1985. nl.wikipedia (citing 1974 to 1977 newspapers) says 1974 to 1977. I used Wikipedia.
- Omval etching: the Met and the Rijksmuseum say the far bank is the Watergraafsmeer dike. Ons Amsterdam cites Lugt for a viewpoint on the Amstel bank. The text describes what the print shows and does not say where Rembrandt stood.
- Rembrandttoren height: 135 m to the roof, 150 m with the antenna (nl.wikipedia).
- Amstelglorie: its own history page says 80 extra hectares were added in 1986, which clashes with the 21 ha total given everywhere. I used 21 ha and left out the 1986 extension.
