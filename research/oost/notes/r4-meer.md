## Site checks and status

- **311**: converted and finished (Arcam July 2025, BNA Gebouw van het Jaar 2026 entry, gebouwdin 2024). Not a listed monument as far as the registers in `slices/` show; sources speak loosely of its "monumental character". No Commons photo found.
- **312**: ferry confirmed running by Het Parool (2 September 2024, reposted by Vrienden van Watergraafsmeer) and a 2020 crossing; the family planned to renew the boat store. No `check` set. No timetable is published anywhere; the text says it is a private family ferry with no timetable. Pin moved from the scout's reedbed point (52.357656, 4.961153) to the ferry route in OSM (52.356849, 4.962131), about 100 m south-east.
- **313**: entrance confirmed by Ommetje met Tom (2026 site, describes the gate and stair) and GvO (2015/2020). **Source conflict**: GvO and Vrienden van Watergraafsmeer treat it as a wartime shelter (a 1945 sign pointed to it; the owner says Germans used it but it never served as a shelter); Ommetje met Tom places it among Cold War shelters. Hence `check: Facts`. No photo (Commons `2020 Middenweg 253-325` shows the whole row and was not used).
- **314 left out**: the only evidence is GvO 2015 and Vrienden van Watergraafsmeer 2017; nothing later, no photos, no pins for the five urinals. The brief says to leave out what can't be confirmed. Could become a Look for line in "Football clubs' war memorials, Middenmeer" after a site visit.
- **315**: pin fixed on the garden (OSM way 1301270874, opposite Darwinplantsoen 14), so no `check: Location`. Opening hours from the Friends' site, checked 23 November 2026. The scout's "about 260 kinds" is 320 in oost-online (June 2026); text uses 320. The empty plinth of the stolen statuette is not mentioned (no evidence it is still there).
- **316**: building and address settled: Duivendrechtsekade 85 (Historisch Amstelland, Parool 2023, PDOK); scout pin matches the address point. No `check` set. Club Cinetone and Grand Café named in GvO August 2025.
- **326**: settled without a flag. Ons Amsterdam (2004) says Ringdijk 5 was *built* in 1832 as a cholera hospital; the council minutes summarised by Vrienden van Watergraafsmeer (2019) say the council *rented* Mr Graan's little house at no. 5 from 7 August to 1 May for 45 guilders, and it was never needed. That fits the city's monument text (two low buildings on a 1780 drawing). Text says rented.
- **Kop van Guus (331)**: Pépé Grégoire's bronze head by August Willemsen's grave was unveiled 29 November 2022 and sawn off its plinth and stolen on 21–22 January 2023 (oost-online, Het Parool). No report of a replacement found, so it is not mentioned.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **312 the wiel and 1672**: the 1725 map shows a breach pool by the ferry, but its date is a local historian's guess: Jan Dijk (2020) writes he could not find whether it came from 1651 or the deliberate flooding of 1672, and "guesses" 1672; his 2021 piece states 1672. The 1672 inundation of the polder itself is confirmed (19th-century Teylers encyclopedia). The text says "Local historians tie the pool to 1672". The scout's "stayed wet for eight years" (Jan Dijk) is not used.
- **312 dates**: GvO 2006 says the family took over "around 1900"; Het Parool 2024 says Gerrit van den Brink bought it in 1914 (110 years in 2024). Text uses 1914.
- **312 wartime story** (Jews hidden in the ferry house, taken to Frisian apple boats) comes only from the family via GvO 2006; told as the family's account. Rififi (filmed here, per GvO) left out: not clear which film.
- **311 Kamerlingh Onneslaan**: divisare says the 1960s; GvO (Klein Dantzig) says the lane took garden land in 1959. Text: "around 1960". Frida Vogels' childhood in the director's house rests on one neighbour's GvO story (2016), who reads it from her diaries.
- **316**: nl.wikipedia spells Isodoor, Historisch Amstelland Isidoor; text uses Isidoor. Eye counts the UFA films loosely; text follows nl.wikipedia (21 Dutch features to 1940, 17 under UFA). The scout's "18 German features" and "the young Paul Verhoeven worked there" (a literary GvO piece) are not used; Soldaat van Oranje is in nl.wikipedia's list of films shot there.
- **331 Oud-Roosenburgh**: nl.wikipedia says the city added the grounds to the cemetery in 1904, GvO says 1914; text gives no year.
- **331 Kitarō**: nl.wikipedia says "would be" the first Japanese to die in the Netherlands; GvO says the first in Europe. Text: "said to be the first Japanese to die in the Netherlands". The Kaiyō Maru's yard is not named (nl.wikipedia implies Amsterdam; it was built at Dordrecht).
- **327 Hibsekrib**: GvO 2006 says J. Ibsen; Ons Amsterdam 2004 and GvO 2020 say J. Ipsen. Text: Ipsen.
- **328 numbering**: the "missing" Middenweg numbers are from one resident's GvO account (2021), consistent and detailed; Brinkstraat 13–23 and Ploegstraat 27–49 also vanished (in sources, not in text).
