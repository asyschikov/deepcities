## Status checks (November 2026)

- Gemeenlandshuis: owned by Hendrick de Keyser since 2008, restored 2013–2014, rented for events and weddings, with occasional public open days (HdK page, live). Nothing gone.
- Diemerpark: open. De Brug (4 November 2026) reports works this month: three animal tunnels under the dike, a new stair at the Diemerparklaan entrance (bridge and path closed 12–27 November), benches and bins replaced until 9 December. NH Nieuws (18 November 2026): otter with a cub on a wildlife camera in August 2026, plus a planned otter pontoon. Both are in the text.
- Space to Take Place: still in OpenStreetMap as a named bench; Stadscuratorium featured it recently as a standing work. The project website (spacetotakeplace.nl) is gone, which the text says. No news of removal.
- Nijlpaard: in place in August 2024 (oost-online visit). Nothing newer found, no news of removal.
- Floating Poetry Room: Commons photos of 2021 show it at the Paul Hufkade; Stadscuratorium still lists it. I found no 2025–2026 news either way. Worth a quick look on site.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- Gemeenlandshuis date: the national register says 1726; nl.wikipedia, Hendrick de Keyser and AGV say built or completed 1727. The attribute says 1726–1727, the text says 1727.
- Carver's name: register "Pieter le Normand", Hendrick de Keyser and nl.wikipedia "le Normant". Used le Normant.
- Water board founding: nl.wikipedia gives 1437 in the text and 1438–1970 in the lead. The English text says 1437.
- Ownership length: nl.wikipedia and AGV "ruim 250 jaar", Hendrick de Keyser "bijna 275 jaar" (counting from 1727 to 2008 gives 281). English says "about 250 years"; the Dutch follows HdK's sentence. Minor, left as is.
- Diemerpark opening: nl.wikipedia Diemerpark says 2003, Diemerzeedijk article says 2004, de Brug gives 3 March 2004. Used 3 March 2004.
- Dumping end: nl.wikipedia says chemical waste was burned until 1973 and the area was used "in the sixties and seventies"; NEMO Kennislink says dumping went on until 1982. Text gives burning to 1973 and dumping to 1982.
- Living layer: Kennislink about 1 m, nl.wikipedia at least 80 cm. Text says "about a metre".
- Scout claims not used: "Amsterdam's biggest park" (not true with the Amsterdamse Bos, and unsourced) and the waste list "waste oil, pesticides, hospital waste" (could not confirm; Het Parool of 14 November 2026 mentions arsenic barrels in its headline but is paywalled).
- Nijlpaard year: Stadscuratorium and the plaque (per oost-online) say 2005; the Commons caption says 2004. Used 2005. "First artwork on IJburg" comes only from the plaque as reported by oost-online, so the text says "a plaque says".
- Nijlpaard lock name: nl.wikipedia calls it the Nijlpaardenschutsluis (sluis 121); thestorybehind.it says "Sluis 2008". Used the Wikipedia name and gave no number.
- Space to Take Place maker: Tribar's own site says Tribar; amsterdamhv.nl says "Tribas". Not named in the text; Tribar is linked.
- Number of deed holders: amsterdamhv.nl "ongeveer duizend", Droog "an edition of thousand", Stadscuratorium "duizenden". Used about a thousand.

## Walking or cycling route idea

"From toxic dump to floating poems" (about 6 km by bike): Gemeenlandshuis van Diemen, then east along the Diemerzeedijk to the Diemerpark and De Ontmoeting, across the bridge at the Diemerparklaan to Haveneiland, the Nijlpaard, the Floating Poetry Room and Space to Take Place.
