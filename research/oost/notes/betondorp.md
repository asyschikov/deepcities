## Decisions and status checks

- Betondorp ranked Amazing (a whole 1920s concrete garden village, protected cityscape, Reve and Cruijff). Downgrade to Nice if the Amazing budget is tight.
- Museum Tot Zover: its own place in odd-museums (it is a destination with its own hours), not folded into the cemetery. Open Tuesday to Sunday 11-17 per totzover.nl (programme running in November 2026).
- Cruijff's childhood home: a social rental home (Ymere, let again in 2022); the text says to look from the street only. No plaque found; the tiled no. 14 shirt and window stickers are confirmed by NOS (2022) and the I amsterdam Cruijff walk (updated March 2026).
- Cruijff mural (2017): still listed on the I amsterdam walk in March 2026. Pin from OpenStreetMap, about 20 m south of the Anfieldroad/Wembleylaan corner, matching the press description.
- Heilige Familiekerk: the English-speaking Parish of the Blessed Trinity is listed at Zaaiersweg 180 by I amsterdam and GCatholic; I could not find a current parish website with Mass times, so none are given.
- De Nieuwe Ooster: park hours and the 2026 aula refurbishment from denieuweooster.nl. The Van Heutsz tomb is told factually (Aceh War deaths, 1927 communist protest, Parool 2023 "mausoleum of a dictator"); the Dutch text keeps the protest and the Parool quote but not the casualty sentence.
- Possible extra (not written, outside the brief): "Rue des Rêves" by Steffen Maas at Ploegstraat 50, a wall tribute to Gerard and Karel van het Reve; mentioned in the Betondorp "Look for" list. Could be its own small curiosities place if wanted.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- Bunker 200488: the city register gives architect G. Versteeg and year 1924, which cannot be right for a German WWII post (Versteeg co-planned Betondorp; the data looks copied). `built` and `architect` are left out. No Commons photo found.
- De Nieuwe Ooster dates: the RCE text says first layout 1889 and Springer extension 1917, extended in 1930, 1942 and 1959; nl.wikipedia says designed 1892, opened 1 May 1894, extended 1904 and 1927. Text uses the 1894 opening and 33 ha; `built` says 1892–1894, extended to 1959.
- De Nieuwe Ooster listing: city designation date 31 October 2003 (bier shed 31 January 2003), RCE register inscription 12 January 2004. `listed` = 2003.
- Van Heutsz tomb: nl.wikipedia says it "left its original place" in 2003 and got a new spot in 2008; unclear whether the tomb itself was moved. Left out.
- Brink restoration: nl.wikipedia says 1987–1989, a DBNL monument guide says 1983–1987. Text uses 1987–1989.
- Verenigingsgebouw: an NAi archive photo calls it Volksgebouw "Het Meerhuis"; not confirmed elsewhere, so the name is not used. Its current use was not found.
- Brink library (335336): no Commons category photos; current use unknown.

## Walking route idea

"Concrete, Cruijff and the cemetery" (about 3 km): Betondorp and the Brink, Johan Cruijff's childhood home, Akkerstraat, Heilige Familiekerk, Zaaiersweg, De Nieuwe Ooster, Museum Tot Zover, then back across the Middenweg to German bunker, Middenweg, Johan Cruijff mural, Anfieldroad, Stadion De Meer (gone).
