# Oost scout, round 4 (items 301–334)

Scouted 2026-11-23. A finishing round: the full Geheugen van Oost archive for sweep 4 (hidden history) in all four gebieden, every miss from the blind recall of 2026-11-23, a sweep of the kinds that recall showed were thin (small one-off curiosities, writers' and artists' houses and studios, art spaces, recent IJburg and Zeeburgereiland landmarks), the partial cells in COVERAGE.md, and the 500 m grid of test 4 (results in COVERAGE.md). Nothing is written yet: the user picks.

I have 34 candidates, numbered 301 to 334: **21 possible new places and 13 merges**. Each was checked against the live map (921 places, 185 in Oost, read-only), all `places/*.yaml` and the "Checked and dropped" lists of SCOUT.md, SCOUT-2.md and SCOUT-3.md. Every pin was point-checked with `scripts/areas.py` and is in borough Oost. Nothing in Zuid or Centrum; the A-list stays parked (Muziekgebouw and Bimhuis untouched).

Each item: title; location (address, lat,lng, gebied/wijk); hook; why it fits; lead source; caveats; suggested rank; new place or merge. Homes are private: for houses, only the outside can be seen.

**Sources gone through this round:**
- **Geheugen van Oost (geheugenvanoost.amsterdam), the whole archive.** The site runs on Zotonic, and its open JSON API (`/api/model/search/get/query?cat=article`, `/api/model/rsc/get/<id>`) returns every published text. All **7,527 articles** were downloaded (6,053 stories, 859 newspaper clippings, 615 news and editorial pages), about 13 million characters. Every title and opening was read, in four slices of about 1,880, and the full text of about 330 flagged stories. This replaces round 3's web-search-only use of the site. The site's own search box is a JavaScript front end, which is why round 3 got nothing from it.
- **Atlas Obscura** (browser, one tab, closed): all six pages of the Amsterdam list, filtered by coordinates. In Oost only Museum Tot Zover and Betondorp (both on the map) and Mark Bischof's studio (dropped below). Brouwerij 't IJ is at De Gooyer, in Centrum.
- **gebouwdin.amsterdam.nl** JSON API: all **899 Oost projects 1988–2025** listed by name and architect; about 15 read in full (telephone exchange, OAT terminal, Bio-Theater site, De Nieuwe Meent, Witte Kaap).
- **Blind-recall leads:** frankwandelt.nl (Oost-Watergraafsmeer walk), lexandthecity.nl ("28 verborgen pareltjes" and the IJburg tour), Ons Amsterdam "Langs de gulden Middenweg" (2004), iamsterdam (Frankey, IJburg walk), oost-online.
- **Other:** nl.wikipedia (Transvaalplein, Olifant met kalf, Oranjesluizen, Framer Framed, Berlagebrug), NH Nieuws (Baggerbeest opening, Minco squat 2025), Amsterdam Alternative (Minco, November 2025), archiweb and Wicona (Witte Kaap), EYE film database (Cinetone), the city monument texts in `slices/` (Ringdijk 5 and 15), OSM (water, wetland, marinas, artworks, named features in every empty grid cell), PDOK locatieserver for addresses.

## Sweep 4: Hidden history (Geheugen van Oost)

### Oud-Oost

301. **Villa Mattern, Renate Rubinstein's bungalow**; Nicolaas de Roeverstraat 3, on the corner of the Polderweg, 52.358764, 4.933045 (Oud-Oost, Dapperbuurt/Oostpoort); a glass-and-steel bungalow of 1967 (F.J.E. Dekeukeleire, for Harry van der Meijden), an odd single-storey survivor between the railway and the new Oostpoort blocks; the columnist Renate Rubinstein ("Tamar" in Vrij Nederland) bought it in 1985 because MS had left her unable to climb stairs, and here she received Simon Carmiggelt, the secret love she wrote about in "Mijn beter ik" (1991); a writer's house of the kind the blind recall missed; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/87970 (Polderweg walk) and /page/101351 (Dapperbuurt walk, architect, date and Rubinstein); private house, outside only; **Nice**; new place.

302. **Marga Minco's house, Tweede Oosterparkstraat 265**; 52.359416, 4.924935 (Oud-Oost, Oosterparkbuurt), 56 m from the Witsenhuis; the neo-Renaissance house with a tall gable where the author of "Het bittere kruid" and "Een leeg huis" lived from 1969 until her death in 2023 at 103; sold, left to decay, squatted on 12 July 2025 by "De Panters" in her honour (readings, films), evicted that autumn after the owner went to court; a writer's house with a live fight over it; lead: https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/350626 and https://amsterdamalternative.nl/articles/20030 ; also GvO /page/115976; **Outlook: Major change ahead** (owner's renovation plans, details unknown); private, outside only; **Nice**; new place.

303. **The child's gravestone in the Tropentuin**; herb garden between the Wereldmuseum (ex-Tropenmuseum) and the Oosterpark, pin about 52.362100, 4.921300 (Oud-Oost, Oosterparkbuurt); a small stone reading "Mijn Zoon 3-augustus-1877 27-mei-1886 RIP" lies at the back of a community herb garden among the celery; it surfaced in 2001 when old trees were cleared, the only visible trace of the Oosterbegraafplaats that was emptied for the museum and park; the Stadsarchief identified the boy as Johannes Christiaan Karel Kesler, born in Banjarmasin; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/105543 and /page/105975 (2023); **check: Location** (pin approximate; the stone is easily covered by leaves); **Nice**; new place (could also be a paragraph in "Oosterpark and its music dome").

304. **Framer Framed in the old gasworks**; Oranje-Vrijstaatkade 71, 52.356891, 4.932080 (Oud-Oost, Dapperbuurt/Oostpoort); a free contemporary art space on decolonial and social themes, founded 2010, opened in the Tolhuistuin in 2014, moved here in 2019; a 35 m community tapestry on the slavery past is being stitched there (GvO /page/117148, 2026); one of the art spaces the blind recall missed; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framer_Framed ; open Tue–Sun; **Okay**; new place, or a paragraph in "Oostergasfabriek remains and U Bevindt Zich Hier" (108 m away) if the user prefers fewer venue places.

305. **Stefan Glerum's stained-glass fronts, Heren 5 block**; entrance hall of Polderweg 502-646, along the railway, 52.358194, 4.934297 (Oud-Oost, Dapperbuurt/Oostpoort); two lead-light facades about 18 m high tell the site's history from the bottom up: coal for the gasworks, a stoker who turns into a swimmer (the bathhouses), a milk bottle (the dairy), bandaged animals (the animal shelter), Spitfires strafing the gasholders, Anton Rooskens at Don Bosco, and Ernst Laqueur, who isolated a female hormone in his lab here; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/88580 (artist's own description, 2020); visible from the street, best when lit; **Okay**; new place.

306. **Former synagogue Benei Berith Beth Jaäkov, Commelinstraat 16**; 52.363207, 4.924558 (Oud-Oost, Dapperbuurt), 69 m from "Dapperbuurt survivors"; under a plain 19th-century front an Amsterdam School ground floor (Jac. S. Baars, 1922) with a separate door for the women's gallery, one of the last surviving society synagogues of the Jewish east; the interior was stripped in 2014; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/81648 ; **check: Facts** (name, architect and date from one source); street front only; **Okay**; new place, or a Look for line in "Dapperbuurt survivors".

### Indische Buurt, Oostelijk Havengebied

307. **Fré Cohen's level-crossing plaques in the 1930s viaducts**; east face of the Molukkenstraat railway viaduct, pin about 52.358600, 4.944200 (Indische Buurt-Oost, on the Watergraafsmeer edge); when the railway was raised in 1935–1939 to end half-hour waits at the barriers, each new viaduct got a bronze plaque; the first, at the Molukkenstraat (1937), shows lowered barriers between the arms of Amsterdam and Watergraafsmeer and was designed by Fré Cohen, the Jewish graphic artist who took her life in 1943 to avoid arrest; at the Linnaeusstraat and Wibautstraat the plaques were torn out and only their outlines are left; a board for her went up at the Fré Cohenbrug in March 2025; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/97977 (2022), /page/82175, /page/89556 ; **check: Location** (pin approximate, east face); **Nice**; new place (the Javastraat plaque could also be a Look for line in "101 Namen, railway viaduct").

308. **Man & Schaap (Merijn Bolink, 2003)**; pond between the Flevoparkweg, Zeeburgerdijk and Zuiderzeeweg, 52.361383, 4.947662 (Indische Buurt-Oost); a bronze man's head and a sheep's head stick out of the water eye to eye, and jets from the man's eyes squirt at the sheep; both are "up to their necks", a nod to the old Zeeburgerdijk breaches that flooded the polder; public art that sweep 2 did not pick up; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/110758 (2024); **Okay**; new place.

309. **"De Kus" (Maarten Loonen, 1983), Azartplein**; in front of Azartplein 1-47, KNSM-eiland, 52.377465, 4.936800 (Oostelijk Havengebied); a corten-steel sculpture made by a squatter-artist living in the Havenarbeidsreserve building, placed outside it in 1983 and the only thing left from the squat years after the roof blew off and the 1990s conversion; the city register lists it as "artist unknown"; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/117010 (September 2026); **Okay**; new place.

310. **The wandering Fröger column, Flevopark**; in the bushes by the westernmost entrance and the footbridge over the Ringvaart, 52.361704, 4.948624 (Indische Buurt-Oost); a sandstone column "for W.A. Froger 1812–1883, who showed Amsterdam the way to the sea" (the engineer who first proposed digging through the dunes), set up in 1953 at the Spaarndammerdijk, moved to the passenger terminal, lost in a park storage yard and set upright here around 2016 with no path to it; this settles the "worth a later look" item in SCOUT-3's dropped list; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/80207 ; **check: Still there?** (last report 2019); **Okay**; new place, or a Look for line in "Hekkepoort, Flevopark" (112 m).

### Watergraafsmeer

311. **The Watergraafsmeer telephone exchange (Boeken, 1923), now De Telefoondienst**; Kamerlingh Onneslaan 1 / Middenweg 56-58, 52.353355, 4.930194 (Watergraafsmeer, Frankendael); J.A.G. Boeken's Amsterdam School exchange, whose original front faces a side lane off the Middenweg with Art Deco letters on the gate; the back became the front, and on it went Hildo Krop's four-continents sculpture, moved from the old telephone head office on the Herengracht; the diarist Frida Vogels grew up in the director's house behind it; KPN left and Ronald Janssen turned it into 33 homes with workshops in 2024–2025, keeping the Krop work; lead: https://gebouwdin.amsterdam.nl/api/projectdetail/4105 and https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/20578 ; listed status not checked (the GvO author calls it a monument); **Nice**; new place. (Blind-recall miss.)

312. **The 1672 wiel and the Nooit Gedacht pull ferry**; Ringvaart corner at the end of the Kruislaan past Station Science Park, pin on the reed marsh at 52.357656, 4.961153 (Watergraafsmeer, Middenmeer), the ferry landing about 60 m east; the reedy pool is a "wiel", the scour hole left where the polder dike was cut on purpose in the Rampjaar 1672 to flood the Meer against Louis XIV's army (it stayed wet for eight years, and the pool shows on the 1725 map); beside it, a hand-pulled cable ferry across the Ringvaart has been run since about 1900 by one family, who hid Jews in the ferry house and took them across at night to Frisian apple boats on the Merwedekanaal; Rififi was filmed here and Drs. P promoted "Heen en weer" on it; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/95032 (wiel, 2021) and /page/9322 (ferry, 2006); OSM marks the reedbed and "Botenstalling Nooitgedacht"; **check: Still there?** (no ferry report after 2006); **Nice**; new place.

313. **The air-raid shelter under Middenweg 325**; corner of the Kruislaan, running through to the Finsenstraat, 52.347979, 4.940185 (Watergraafsmeer, Middenmeer); a big concrete room with pillars under a corner block, still reached by a little stair down from the pavement and now let as workshops; a neighbour's brother unscrewed the wartime arrow sign pointing to it in 1945 and the family kept it; not the German guard bunker opposite Middenweg 460A already on the map; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/83534 (2020); private, only the stair visible; **Okay**; new place.

314. **The last concrete urinals of Sportpark Middenmeer**; five on the sports park (by WV-HEDW, sc Overamstel, between Real Sranang and TOS-Actief, at Fortius and at Zeeburgia), pin on the park around 52.349000, 4.950400 (Watergraafsmeer, Middenmeer) to be set on one of them; concrete troughs from before club canteens had toilets, always next to a ditch, kept on purpose by the park manager who likes them ("they give the park its own character"); lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/4735 (2015, after Het Parool); **check: Still there?**; **Okay**; new place.

315. **Park Darwin and its flower garden**; Darwinplantsoen, between the Hugo de Vrieslaan and the Gooiseweg, pin 52.344400, 4.928800 (Watergraafsmeer, Frankendael); a hidden strip park by Hans Warnau (designed 1952, opened 1963) with a raised perennial garden of about 260 kinds, saved from house-building by neighbours in 2016 and kept by volunteers, and a rose garden with the empty plinth of a horse-and-rider statuette stolen in 1985; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/74620 and /page/117074 ; **check: Location** (pin on the garden to be fixed); **Okay**; new place.

316. **Cinetone, "Holland's Hollywood"**; Duivendrechtsekade, pin 52.337377, 4.935785 (Watergraafsmeer, Omval/Overamstel); in 1933 the Biedermann brothers built a sound-film studio in an old factory here, where more than 20 features of the 1930s were shot (Bleeke Bet, the Jantjes); in the war it was UFA Filmstadt Amsterdam and made 18 German features; after 1947 the Dutch film industry ran it, and Bert Haanstra and the young Paul Verhoeven worked there; now an events venue; lead: https://filmdatabase.eyefilm.nl/en/collection/film-history/article/film-studios and GvO /page/114063 (2025); **check: Location** (pin from GvO; the building's address is not confirmed); outside only unless at an event; **Nice**; new place.

317. **The Van Deudekom ghost sign**; corner of Van 't Hofflaan 19 and the Pasteurstraat, about 52.348869, 4.937945 (Watergraafsmeer, Frankendael); a painted grocer's ad ("Kruidenierswaren... Comestibles... Telef. 52987") that reappeared when a fitness club's board came off, then restored with borough money by the Werkgroep Historische Gevelreclames; the family's shops go back to the Ringdijk in 1888; two more on the Weesperzijde (Goudeket radio at no. 24, Café Hesp at no. 130) could be Look for lines; lead: https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/3129 and /page/44310 ; **check: Still there?**; **Okay**; new place, or drop if ghost signs feel too slight.

### IJburg, Zeeburgereiland

318. **Oranjesluizen, south side**; Zuidersluis and the Prins Willem-Alexandersluis at the northern tip of Zeeburgereiland (the old Paardenhoek), pin 52.381667, 4.960556 (IJburg, Zeeburgereiland, Bovendiep; the northern locks are in Noord); the locks of 1870–1872 (Justus Dirks; first stone laid by King Willem III, first ship through on 25 September 1872) closed the IJ off from the Zuiderzee so the new North Sea Canal could be kept at level, and turned the IJ into an inland water; about 120,000 boats a year still pass, and walkers and cyclists cross on the lock gates; Noord's round C proposes the lock-keepers' row on the Noorder IJdijk (Noord 314), so the two should cross-refer; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranjesluizen ; monument status not found in `slices/` (the round 1 monument pass did not include them), **check: Facts**; **Nice**; new place.

319. **De Witte Kaap (Claus en Kaan, 2010)**; Wim Noordhoekkade 106-132, pin on the prow 52.350300, 5.012500 (IJburg-Oost, Haveneiland); a snow-white block on a triangular plot, three storeys in the middle and seven at the ends, with a rounded "bow" and stacked decks that critics compare to a cruise liner; Het Parool called it the handsomest block in a monotonous IJburg; youth and neighbourhood centre in the plinth; lead: https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/bily-mys-de-witte-kaap and https://gebouwdin.amsterdam.nl (project 736); **Okay**; new place. (Blind-recall miss.)

320. **Baggerbeest, the Sluisbuurt broedplaats**; by the Piet Heintunnel on the sand plain of the future Sluisbuurt, about 52.371300, 4.959700 (Zeeburgereiland); an incubator of more than 70 studios with a bar, a youth centre and a give-away shop, opened as the "cultural heart" of a district of 5,500 homes still to be built; named after an imaginary amphibian that crawled out of the dredged IJ mud the island was made of; lead: https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/332834 ; **check: Location** (pin from OSM "Baggerbeest" bar); probably temporary until the Sluisbuurt is built, but no end date found, so no Outlook yet; **Okay**; new place. (Blind-recall miss.)

321. **Vondice, a one-room hotel in a sludge pumping station**; Roepie Kruizestraat 4, 52.374034, 4.968330 (Zeeburgereiland); a 52 m² former sludge pumping station, a last piece of the island's sewage-works past, turned into a single-suite hotel; lead: https://fundainbusiness.nl (sale brochure, Roepie Kruizestraat 4) and booking listings; the brochure says it may be converted to housing, **check: Still there?**; **Okay**; new place, or a Look for line in "RI-Oost silos and Rialto Silo". (Blind-recall miss.)

## Merges (blind-recall misses and Geheugen van Oost details)

323. **Bolgewas (Paul Koning, 1969) and "Spelende kinderen" (Gerda Rubinstein, 1957)** → merge into "Oosterpark and its music dome": a grinning bronze head that pops out of the lawn "like a flower greeting a sunny day", easy to miss; and a playing-children group whose plaque named the wrong sculptor until Rubinstein proved it was hers in 2010. 324. **The Transvaalplein elephants, and the Van Eyck playground that is gone** → merge into "Jewish memorials of the Transvaalbuurt" (pinned 44 m away): a stone elephant cow and calf from a neighbourhood project with a children's poem set in the paving; the square was a Van Eyck playground in the 1950s, but its equipment went in 1983, so the walk's "Van Eyck playground with elephants" is a mix-up. Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olifant_met_kalf , https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaalplein . (Blind-recall miss.)

325. **Frankey's "Tram 14 / Saint Johan"** → merge into "Badhuis Javaplein and the colonial street names" (37 m): Streetart Frankey (2019) turned the glass of the tram 14 shelter into a stained-glass window of Johan Cruijff, the number 14, as Amsterdam's patron saint; check: Still there? Sources: https://www.iamsterdam.com/zien-en-doen/attracties-en-bezienswaardigheden/streetart-frankey , https://amsterdamexperiences.nl/streetart-frankey/javaplein/ . (Blind-recall miss.)

326. **Ringdijk 3 and 5: the smithy and the "cholera house"** → merge into "Ringdijk and the Watergraafsmeer polder": Ons Amsterdam (2004) says Ringdijk 3 was a smithy and the little house at no. 5 was built in 1832 as an emergency hospital in a cholera epidemic; the city's monument text instead shows two low buildings on the spot on a drawing of 1780, so **check: Facts**; the "Huis op de Dijk" of the hidden-gems list is the former smithy already told in that place (Het Witte Dijkhuis, Ringdijk 15). 327. **The Hibsekrib** → merge into "Linnaeushof and the Hofkerk": a wooden stair leads down to the Linnaeusdwarsstraat, a dead-end lane of low houses of about 1860, nicknamed after a J. Ipsen who lived on the corner; it was once a slum ("krotjesstraat"). Sources: Ons Amsterdam 2004, GvO /page/3249, /page/89573. Look for line.

328. **The Van het Reve court, Ploegstraat 89** → merge into "Betondorp and the Brink": the brothers Karel and Gerard van het Reve spent most of their childhood in the court at Ploegstraat 89 (then 57), the setting of Gerard's "Werther Nieland"; add Dick Greiner's hand-drawn letters on the Brink doors and the numbering slip of 1980 that left Middenweg 170-322 missing (GvO /page/5733, /page/55969, /page/91644). Café De Avonden itself is left out (food). Source: Ons Amsterdam 2004. (Blind-recall miss.)

329. **The 1891 first stone, Tweede van Swindenstraat** → merge into "Het begon met een dubbeltje": the same Bouwmaatschappij tot verkrijging van eigen woningen built 60 more houses here; the stone was laid by Agnes, aged 4, daughter of the ex-mayor and foreign minister Van Tienhoven. GvO /page/11814. Look for line.

330. **Pretoriusstraat 37** → merge into "Berlage's gateway blocks, Pretoriusstraat": a 1937 shopfront by Th.J. Lammers, the only projecting front in the street; the Jewish shopkeepers were deported (Stolpersteine), and a girl hid for months under the shop-window floor. GvO /page/49607. Look for line.

331. **Oud-Roosenburgh's name stones; the Japanese grave; "Kop van Guus"** → merge into "Museum Tot Zover" and "De Nieuwe Ooster": stones of the 1642 country house set into a low wall by the museum (its café is named after it); a grave and monument in Japanese granite for an early Japanese who died in the Netherlands; Pépé Grégoire's open-work head (2022) by the grave of August Willemsen, Pessoa's translator. GvO /page/89829, /page/93341, /page/102087. Look for lines.

332. **The row over the Tugelaweg text** → merge into "Doe je ogen even dicht, Tugelaweg text monument": the text drew on GvO interviews with Jewish survivors without credit, and Maurice Ferares protested that it leaves out what happened to the residents. GvO /page/74281, /page/74286. One sentence.

333. **The bubble screen in the Amsterdam-Rijnkanaal** → merge into "Leeuwen van Zeeburg": since 2018 a perforated pipe on the canal bed blows a curtain of bubbles (a stripe of foam shows on the surface) to stop salt water creeping inland in droughts, an idea of Johan van Veen from 1940; a board nearby marks the vanished lock island. GvO /page/85848. Look for line.

334. **Heiman Barnstein's stone (1926)** → merge into "Nieuw Uilenburg, Handwerkers Vriendenkring blocks": a memorial stone over the gate to the HWV inner garden, Tugelaweg 57-58, for the co-founder of the building fund that moved Uilenburg slum families here; **check: Still there?** GvO /page/46915. Look for line.
