# Oost scout, round 2 (items 101–157)

User said "go" on all items, with the merges listed at the end (2026-11-23).

I have 58 new candidates for Oost, numbered 101 to 158. None of them is in SCOUT.md or the monument slices; seven sit next to a monument and are flagged as possible merges. I checked the live map for 37 keywords (Oosterpark, Omval, IJburg, Steigereiland, Krop, Cruijff, Bajes, Middenweg and others), and nothing from Oost is on it yet. Every location below was point-checked against the city's borough boundaries and is in Oost. I wrote nothing to the repo or any website, and the browser tab I opened is closed.

**Best sources this round:**
- **City public-art database (Kunstwacht / buitenkunstamsterdam.nl):** it has 249 works in Oost, and I went through all of them. Most items below come from there.
- **City wall-art register (maps.amsterdam.nl, "Wandkunst"):** 34 works in Oost.
- **Kunstwacht links:** they are written as `buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/<id>`. That pattern is taken from the site's code and was not opened in a browser, so if a link fails, search the site by title.
- **Status:** most texts in that database are undated, so any item without a separate check should be confirmed on site.

**Public art with a story**
101. Banpaal 1624 (top of a banishment post); Oosterpark, in a bed by the skate area; the sign calls it a "limit post", but it is the top of one of the six 1624 posts on the Spaarndammerdijk, about 7.5 km out, that banished criminals could not pass; a stray piece of the West's ban-post story, dumped in an eastern park; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1211 ; how and when it got here is not documented; Nice
102. Memorial to police dog Albert; Oosterpark, at the spot where his handler trained him; Albert (1916–1923) found a murderer's hat in a pile of hats in 1919, which led to Amsterdam's dog brigade (1921); he died after swallowing his sick master's handkerchief; an odd, touching curiosity; https://www.begraafplaats.nl/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/DB-2019_2-Gestikt-in-zakdoek-baasje.pdf ; one source says Albert is buried here, so check the stone's text on site; Nice
103. Hommage aan Nescio (Hans Bayens, 1971); Oosterpark; Nescio's three "Titaantjes" lounging on a bench in the park where his characters "leaned on the fence all summer night"; stolen in 1985, recast in 1988; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1217 ; Nice
104. Monument voor de Tachtigers (Jan Wolkers); Oosterpark, opposite Oosterpark 82; a steel flame in the reeds, quoting Marsman on the poet Gorter; it faces Willem Witsen's house, where the 1880s poets met; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1218 ; Oosterpark 82 is monument 526984 in the oosterpark slice, so this could be merged with it; Okay
105. Justus van Maurik bench; Oosterpark; a double bench carved with characters from the cigar-maker's sentimental Amsterdam novels, which nobody reads any more; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1212 ; small, could be a "look for" in another Oosterpark place; Okay
106. Surinamemonument (Guillaume Lo-A-Njoe); 's-Gravesandeplein; for the 7 June 1989 SLM crash at Zanderij, which killed most of the Kleurrijk Elftal footballers (176 dead); placed near Café De Draver (Ruyschstraat 377), the regular café of several victims; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1254 ; the NOS said in 2014 the yearly commemoration would end, a 2019 report says it still took place here, so check current practice; Nice
107. Muziek en Vreugde (Bart van Hove, 1889); garden behind The Manor hotel (old Burgerziekenhuis), Linnaeusstraat 89; two putti saved from the roof of the Stadsschouwburg that burned down in 1890, later lost on a city yard and found again; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1203 ; hotel garden, so access is unclear; Nice
108. Geloof, Hoop en Liefde (Nicholas Pope); KIT annex, Eerste van Swindenstraat by the Muiderkerk tower; three plaster-saint figures (two on the roof corners, one behind glass, on wheels) answering the colonial allegories on the Tropen building opposite; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1390 ; good counterpoint to round-one item 14; Okay
109. De Vrijheid van de Gedachte (Hildo Krop, 1951); Oranje-Vrijstaatplein; made for the PTT telephone exchange in Zuid, moved with KPN to Sloterdijk, sold to a private firm, bought by the borough in 2018 and placed by the borough office because of free speech; a wandering statue with Boutens' poem on its rings; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1828 ; Nice
110. Rotsvast (Rob Ligtvoet); Flevopark, lawn by Het Nieuwe Diep; the artist and friends secretly put it there one night around 1982, and for 25 years nobody knew who made it, until a Parool and RTV NH search found him; the waves on it are casts of IJmuiden beach; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1726 ; Amazing rare
111. Fata Morgana (Iris le Rütte); above the cycle tunnel at the Pauwenpad, on the rail embankment; three dromedary silhouettes walking east along the railway, best against a sunset; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1235 ; Nice
112. Les Grandes Formes (Jacques Vieille, 1995); A10 verge near the Gooise Knoop, Watergraafsmeer side; 15 steel pylons ("the cathedral") with espaliered pears, apples and vines, recalling the polder's 17th-century country-house gardens and the old De Meer stadium floodlights; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Grandes_Formes ; upkeep depended on a school, so check the state of the plants; Nice
113. Wokkel (W.F. Snieder); halfway along the Zeeburgerbrug (A10); a 15 m twisted concrete column, made of the same elements as the bridge, by a Rijkswaterstaat engineer; moved from the south to the north side of the road in 1993; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1785 ; seen only from the road; Okay
114. Kantoorgangers (Elisa van Schie and Martin Takken, 2002); facades and balconies of the Schalk Burgerstraat; twelve 35 cm bronze commuters in grey suits march toward town over the houses; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1849 ; check that all twelve are still there; Nice
115. Wibaut statue (Han Wezelaar) and the lost Wibauthuis; Wibautstraat by the Tilanusstraat; the housing alderman stands near where the municipal housing office, the Wibauthuis (1961–1997), stood; new bronze pedestal by Hans van Houwelingen (2019); https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1256 ; the street itself runs on the old railway line, a context line for round-one item 37; Okay
116. Beursmannetje / De krantenlezende man (Pieter d'Hont, 1966); Prins Bernhardplein 173; a gift from the Financieele Dagblad that stood on the Damrak facing the Exchange for decades, was stored in 2006 for the metro works and came back to the paper's door; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1808 ; I found no news of the FD leaving this address, but the statue's presence is not confirmed; Okay
117. Vrouwe Fortuna (Pieter Starreveld, 1950); field next to club Panama, Oostelijke Handelskade; given by the staff of the shipping company SMN for its 80th birthday, it moved from the Java-eiland headquarters to the quay head and into storage, and has been back since 2007; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1750 ; Okay
118. Katsenarie (Berend Strik, 1994); daycare in the former harbour customs office, Cruquiusweg 25; a blue cloud with copper onion domes sprouting from the facade, like an Efteling castle; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1778 ; check that the daycare and facade are unchanged; Nice
119. Trafohuisjes (Huibert Groenendijk); courtyards of the Java-eiland; five transformer houses and two gas stations by the designer of the Maxi-Cosi, who called them "chapels" for the blessings of technology; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1734 ; inside courtyards; Okay
120. Oute Waal retaining wall (Ingrid Pasmans); Oetewalerstraat by numbers 26–34; screen-printed plates with an old map naming Houtewaal, the hamlet (first mentioned in 1387) at the present Muiderpoort; the Linnaeusstraat was the Oetewalerweg until 1878; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1205 ; Okay
121. Hildo Krop's 43 gable stones; Watergraafsmeerschool, Huismanshof 11–13; 43 different terracotta reliefs (Don Quixote, a faun, a child on a goat); https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1311 ; the same series is on the Wingerd school, which is already on the map ("Former Wingerdschool and Resedaschool"); Okay

**Words on walls and wall art**
122. "Domweg gelukkig, in de Dapperstraat" (Ootje Oxenaar); senior housing by Hans van Heeswijk on the Dapperstraat; J.C. Bloem's most quoted line is on the facade in eight languages, and more Bloem poems are on the lift glass; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1336 ; Nice
123. Karel Appel Was Hier (Martijn Sandberg); Dapperstraat 7; a bronze bitten half-apple on Appel's birth house, now a guest home for Rijksakademie artists; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1807 ; tiny (17 cm); Nice
124. Stenen gedicht (Gerrit Kouwenaar and Kees Nieuwenhuijzen); building Batavia, Panamalaan 11; a 12 by 15 m poem laid in brick: "Ik lig als een schip op de rede..."; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1769 ; Nice
125. "De krant in vijf kunstwerken" (Opland); Wibautstraat metro station; cartoonist Rob Wout turned the station into a newspaper: falling letters, 1977 headlines, press drawings seen through peepholes, and a concrete hut with ghost front pages; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1185 ; could be folded into round-one item 37 (newspaper street); Nice
126. Peter Alma's station murals and the Marnixbad tableau; Amstelstation hall, Julianaplein 1; two 1939 murals (the locomotive's history over Empire State and Eiffel Tower silhouettes, and the world of technology), plus a 1958 Alma tile tableau rescued from the demolished Marnixbad in 2003, and Theo van Reijn's facade reliefs, wrapped in netting against pigeons; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1847 ; Amstelstation is monument 526988 in the frankendael slice, so most likely a merge; ticket machines now hide part of one mural; Nice
127. 128. Aart Roos brick relief; former fashion school, Zeeburgerdijk 112; a metres-high abstract brick relief (1969) on the street facade; https://maps.amsterdam.nl/wandkunst/ ; could pair with 127; Okay
129. "Daarom moet ik wenen" (Wessel Couzijn, c. 1950); over the door of President Brandtstraat 330; a small relief of a family in a gateway with a Hebrew line from Lamentations ("for these things I weep"), in the old Jewish Transvaalbuurt; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1283 ; Nice

**WWII and the Jewish east**
130. Tugelaweg text monument (K. Michel and Hansje van Halem, c. 2017); along the Tugelaweg; a 350 m text by poet K. Michel, set by type designer Van Halem, for the Jewish neighbourhood as it was before the war; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansje_van_Halem ; the year and the line "Doe je ogen even dicht" (the database title) still need checking on site; may merge with round-one item 2; Nice
131. Het Buikschot (Adam Jansma, 1962); foot of the rail embankment by Tugelaweg 110; a figure doubled up in pain where the Germans shot three random prisoners from the Weteringschans jail on 15 December 1944 in reprisal for a rail attack; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1233 ; 4 May ceremony every year; Nice
132. Keesje Brijde's cross; Keesje Brijdeplantsoen, Sporenburg; a plain wooden cross for a 13-year-old shot on 13 December 1944 while picking coal on the railway yard; two resistance men put it up, and Indische Buurt residents and railway staff kept it for decades; https://www.tracesofwar.com/sights/56894/Monument-Keesje-Brijde.htm ; still the site of a 4 May gathering (2023 confirmed); Nice
133. Eli van Tijn memorial (Steffen Maas); Kraaipanstraat 58–60, at the former Jewish Kraaipanschool; for the head teacher and resistance man, "lived as a socialist, died as a Jew"; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1405 ; could fold into round-one item 2 or 5; Okay
134. Firefighters' war memorial; Ringdijk 98 fire station; names of six firemen killed between 10 May and 13 October 1940 (bombing raids), by Nico Onkenhout; the Weesperzijde 99 fire station has a liberation plaque too; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1333 ; small; Okay
135. Betondorp Brink memorials; Brink; a memorial for Johan Cruijff (Steffen Maas) and a plaque for the Jewish Betondorpers, side by side; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1860 ; texts not checked; may merge with round-one item 38 or the Betondorp ensemble; Okay

**Hidden history and green corners**
136. Rembrandt's Omval and the Rembrandttoren; Omval peninsula, Amstelplein; Rembrandt etched this bend of the Amstel in 1645, and on the same spot stands Amsterdam's tallest building, the Rembrandt Tower (135 m), with a Mondriaan Tower beside it; https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/359969 ; I found no plaque about the etching; Nice
137. Oosterbegraafplaats under the Oosterpark; north part of the park, by the Tropenmuseum; the park's northern half was laid over the old Oosterbegraafplaats, cleared for De Nieuwe Ooster; a park marker says so; https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=274045 ; nothing to see except the marker, so better as a line in another place; Okay
138. Natuurtuinpark Amstelglorie; along the Amstel off the Spaklerweg; a large allotment park run as a nature garden, a green surprise between offices and water; https://iamsterdam.com/en/whats-on/calendar/attractions-and-sights/sights/amstelglorie-allotments ; founding year not found; "Nut en Genoegen" turned out to be in West; Okay
139. Drie scheefgetrokken kubussen (André Volten, 1985) and Bella Vistapark; Spaklerweg, former GEB/Nuon site; Volten's tilted cubes, made for the energy company's headquarters, were moved into the new Bella Vistapark (2020); https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1364 ; the water tower beside it is a monument (526948); Okay
140. Gyroscoop and the Veemarkt animal paving; Entrepotkade and Veemarkt; Bastienne Kramer's brick "garden" with animal figures recalls the cattle market; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1744 ; better as a "look for" in round-one item 34 (Museum Perron Oost and the cattle market); Okay

**IJburg and Zeeburgereiland**
141. Enneüs Heerma Bridge (Grimshaw, 2001); IJburglaan, Zeeburgereiland to Steigereiland; IJburg's gateway, with a steel arch that seems to dip under the road, so it reads as a smile in the water; National Steel Prize 2002; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enne%C3%BCs_Heerma_Bridge ; I could not confirm the "smiling bridge" nickname; Nice
142. Nesciobrug (Wilkinson Eyre, 2006); over the Amsterdam-Rijnkanaal, Diemerpark to Science Park; about 780 m long, the country's first suspension bridge for bikes and walkers, named after Nescio, who walked the Diemerzeedijk where it lands; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nescio_Bridge ; Nice
143. Waterbuurt West floating homes; Steigereiland, along the IJburglaan; about 75 floating and dike homes by Marlies Rohmer (2011), with jetties as streets; https://divisare.com/projects/274416-architectenbureau-marlies-rohmer-luuk-kramer-floating-houses ; Nice
144. Vrijburcht and its fish wall; Jan Olphert Vaillantlaan / Vaillantkade, Steigereiland; a co-housing block that residents started in 2000 (52 homes, theatre, café, crèche), with Suzanne Willems' wall of 274 aluminium fish and a giant paper boat on the quay; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1765 ; Okay
145. Nautilus (Barbara Nanning); gateway ceiling of the Theo Frenkelhof, Erich Salomonstraat, IJburg; a Jules Verne undersea world in red, green and gold overhead; https://www.buitenkunstamsterdam.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/1784 ; Okay
146. Urban Sport Zone and skatepark Zeeburgereiland; Zeeburgereiland sports park; one of the largest skateparks in the Netherlands, on the initiative of an Amsterdam skater, with skater-drawn tiles glazed like Delft blue, in a 3.8 ha BMX and free-running zone; https://www.amsterdamnow.com/en/lifestyle/grootste-skatepark-van-nederland-op-zeeburgereiland/ ; Nice
147. RI-Oost silos; former sewage works, Zeeburgereiland; sewage was treated in these towers until the early 2000s; a contest drew plans up to a chocolate swimming pool, and the west silo opens as cinema Rialto Silo from November 2026, with a gym and food to follow; https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/363794/vanaf-november-is-het-echt-zover-naar-de-bios-in-silo-op-zeeburgereiland ; the article is from 28 Sept 2026, so check that it actually opened; Nice
