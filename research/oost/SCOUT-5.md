## New places

401. **The Kruislaan flood doors (Kruislaancompartimentering)**; in the Kruislaan where it passes under the railway yard by Station Science Park, 52.353931, 4.950681 (Middenmeer, cell G8); two huge blue steel doors stand parked beside the road and the cycle path, ready to be slid across the gap in the railway embankment. Decided in 1955 under the post-war law for protecting waterworks in wartime and built in 1960–1961, they turn the railway dike into a second, inner dike, so that a breach in the Ooster Ringdijk (or a bomb on it) cannot flood the whole polder, about 5 m below sea level. A steel sliding shutter was made for the Molenwetering culvert under the same embankment, and a small extra dike was thrown up by the Molukkenstraat. The city could not get its story straight: one spokesman said it was to seal off the "FOM polder" with the nuclear research lab next door, then the city said it was to protect the 40,000 people of the Meer after the 1960 flood of Tuindorp Oostzaan, while the province said it had all been decided in 1955. The structure only got its name, as a public space, in 2019; why: Cold War waterworks hiding in plain sight, the kind of "behind the gate" curiosity the C-list is for, and the only visible sign of how deep the polder lies on this side; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruislaancompartimentering , two newspaper reports of about 1960–1961 on Delpher (one in NRC; via https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:010371298:mpeg21:a0100 and https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=KBNRC01:000035604:mpeg21:a0121 ), photo of August 2024 on Commons ("2024 Kruislaancompartimentering, Asd (A).jpg"), https://www.bruggenvanamsterdam.nl/sluis215.htm (structure 215), Gemeenteblad 2019, 316210; caveats: the doors' exact mechanism (rails or hinges) and whether they have ever been closed in earnest are not described; the first newspaper's title and both exact dates were not read (only the OCR text), so check them when drafting; **Nice**; new place.

402. **The rooftop observatory of Science Park 904**; two domes on the roof of the UvA science faculty, Science Park 904, 52.354192, 4.954926 (Middenmeer, cell G9); two 6-metre domes, hoisted up in April 2008 as the highest point of the new campus, one telescope for the Sun and one for the night sky, used by astronomy students of the Anton Pannekoek Institute. On free public evenings in the dark half of the year, students take small groups up into the domes to look through the telescopes (on cloudy nights it becomes a tour with images); why: a working observatory in a polder 5 m below sea level that anyone can visit for free, and the first sight in the empty Science Park cell G9; lead: https://www.astronomie.nl/nieuws/observatorium-in-science-park-amsterdam-457 (2008) and https://api.uva.nl/stargazing/stargazing-nights ; caveats: evenings are irregular (none in summer; check the UvA agenda), registration advised, not wheelchair accessible; **Okay**; new place, or a paragraph in "CWI and the Science Park internet hub", which already sends readers to the Computermuseum in the same building.

403. **Leeuwenburg, Zanstra's unloved concrete colossus**; Weesperzijde 190, by the Amstelstation, 52.345763, 4.916142 (Omval/Overamstel, cell I3); a 56-metre office block of in-situ concrete with small recessed windows (Zanstra, Gmelig Meyling and De Clercq Zubli, built 1973–1977 on the site of the Blooker cocoa factory), first the Rijkspostspaarbank and Postgiro, then from 1998 to 2022 a big site of the Hogeschool van Amsterdam. It is one of the few real brutalist buildings in the country: the 2023 atlas "Bruut" put it in its top 50 as "Zanstra's onbeminde kantoorkolos". In 2018 the city planned to demolish the low wing for a tower; Bond Heemschut tried to get it listed and failed. In November 2024 the demolition was called off because the foundations could not carry new building, and a renovation (2025–2030) is replacing all the façade panels; why: modern and contested, a fight over whether ugly can be heritage, at the gebied's edge where the map has only the towers; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeuwenburg_(Amsterdam) ; also NH Nieuws on Amsterdam brutalism (https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/346633/brutalisme-amsterdam-wibautstraat ) and Arcam (2025, ZZDP transformation); caveats: **Outlook: Major change ahead** (new façade panels may change the look the atlas praised); the state of the work in late 2026 needs checking; it stands 90 m from the Rembrandttoren, so it could also be a paragraph in "Rembrandt's Omval and the Rembrandttoren"; **Okay**; new place.

404. **The thermometer streets of De Wetbuurt (Amsteldorp)**; Fahrenheitstraat, Celsiusstraat and Réaumurstraat, pin at Réaumurstraat 10, 52.342219, 4.924013 (Frankendael, cell J4); in 1901 the still independent village of Watergraafsmeer named three streets of a builder's scheme by the Weespertrekvaart after Boer leaders who were then alive (De Wet, Steyn, Botha, plus Wolmarans and Leyds), its own little Transvaal quarter. When Amsterdam annexed the village in 1921 it already had those names, so in March 1922 they became the scientists of temperature and pressure: Fahrenheit, Celsius, Réaumur, Von Guericke and Torricelli, three thermometer scales side by side. North of it lies Tuindorp Amstelstation, 484 identical duplex homes of 1947–1948 for elderly people giving up big flats to families, with streets named after vanished inns and country houses of the polder (Belvedere); the whole of "Amsteldorp" sits about 5.5 m below NAP, so from the station dike you look over its roofs. Piet Kramer bridges cross its ring ditch; why: a renaming story with a joke in it, in a corner (cell J4/J5) the map does not reach; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Wetbuurt and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuindorp_Amstelstation ; GvO /page/99728 and /page/116884 for the street's life; caveats: nothing on the spot tells the story (the street signs do); OSM has an "Iglo" climbing frame by Aldo van Eyck tagged as city heritage at about 52.344231, 4.921485 in Tuindorp Amstelstation, not confirmed by any other source (**check: Facts**, could be a Look for line if it holds); **Okay**; new place.

405. **Kijker (Barbara Kletter, 1993), the turntable you can stand on**; in the Molenwetering by the Nobelweg and the Hugo de Vrieslaan, 52.347560, 4.928522 (Frankendael, cell H5); asked for a work about science, because the streets around are named after scientists, Kletter made a combined telescope and microscope on a round platform 4.6 m across over the water, reached by a little bridge. You step onto it and turn it with your foot while holding the rail, so you are the one looking and the one being looked at, and the ground moves under you; why: a playful, interactive piece of public art that sweep 2 did not pick up, in an empty grid cell (H5); lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kijker_(beeld) and https://artatsite.com/Amsterdam/details/Kletter_Barbara_Kijker_Nobelweg_beelden_statues_sculptures_kunst_Art_at_Site_Amsterdam.html ; OSM node 9060690341; caveats: **check: Still there?** (no dated photo found; whether the platform still turns is unknown); **Okay**; new place, or a Look for line in "Bloementuin van Darwin (Darwinplantsoen)" (round 4 draft in `places/r4-meer.yaml`, about 350 m south).

## Merges

406. **The nuclear lab in the old village gasworks** → merge into "CWI and the Science Park internet hub": the campus did not start in 1963. In 1946 FOM, Philips and the city set up the Instituut voor Kernphysisch Onderzoek (IKO) in the empty gasworks of the former municipality of Watergraafsmeer on the Ooster Ringdijk (closed when Amsterdam annexed the village in 1921; the gasmaster's house became part of the lab). Its Philips synchrocyclotron, opened in November 1949, was called the largest in Western Europe; it ran until 1977. IKO became part of NIKHEF in 1975, and the 1961 flood doors (401) were said to protect this "FOM polder". One paragraph and a Look for line. Sources: https://www.nikhef.nl/timeline-nikhef-50 , the IKO history by Hazewindus (https://hagenbeuk.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/IKO-TEKST-DEF.pdf ), GvO /page/62079 (the gasworks, the house); check the 1949 date between the sources when drafting.

407. **The Oetewalerbrug's sea monsters** → merge into "Rechthuis Watergraafsmeer": the bridge that brings the Middenweg over the Ringvaart beside the Rechthuis is a Piet Kramer bridge of 1924–1925 (opened 31 January 1925), with sea-monster sculptures by Adrianus Remiëns (not Hildo Krop) and its name cut into the stone parapet (1958) instead of on the usual metal plate; it replaced a dangerous drawbridge and the little toll house went. Named after Oetewaal, the village by the Muiderpoort that Amsterdam swallowed in 1658; locals call it the "Hemabrug". One Look for line. Source: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oetewalerbrug ; the bridge is also on the Amsterdam School map.

408. **"Vrede" (Hans Reicher, 1962)** → merge into "Piet Zanstra's flats on the Radioweg": a bronze nude kneeling on a tall pillar of Swedish granite, letting a dove go, at the south end of the green strip on the northern Radioweg (at the Johannes van der Waalsstraat, 52.352096, 4.941418, about 330 m north of the pin). Not a war memorial, though many took it for one; the nude caused a stir, and pranksters now and then painted a bra on her. Locals called it simply "Het Beeld". One of Reicher's last works; he died in 1963. One Look for line. Source: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrede_(Hans_Reicher) .
