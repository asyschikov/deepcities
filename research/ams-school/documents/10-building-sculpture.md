An Amsterdam School building is meant to be seen up close. Look at a corner, a doorway or a bridge pier and you will often find a carved animal, a worker or a mythical figure. The stone is set into the brick, and it was designed along with the building rather than added afterwards. One sculptor made more of these figures than anyone else: Hildo Krop (1884–1970), Amsterdam's city sculptor (*stadsbeeldhouwer*).

## From pastry cook to city sculptor

Krop was born in Steenwijk, the son of a baker. He trained as a pastry cook and worked in Belgium, France and Italy. By 1906 he was a cook for a couple in England, where his talent for drawing was spotted. He then studied in Paris at the Académie Julian and in 1908 at the Rijksakademie in Amsterdam, where he learned stone carving from the sculptor John Rädecker. After a winter with Georg Kolbe in Berlin (1911–1912), he took up *direct carving* in Paris with Ossip Zadkine. This means cutting straight into the stone instead of having a craftsman copy a clay model, and it suited building sculpture well.

## The Scheepvaarthuis workshop

![The Scheepvaarthuis, where Amsterdam's revival of building sculpture began](commons:File:Amsterdam Scheepvaarthuis 002.JPG)

From 1913 to 1916 Krop was one of the assistants in the workshop of **Hendrik van den Eijnde** on the [Scheepvaarthuis](place:), alongside **Joop van Lunteren** and **Anton Rädecker**, John's brother. English Wikipedia says the workshop "gave a great impulse to the revival of stone sculpture in the Netherlands, where stone carving could be learned again". Van den Eijnde made the *Four World Seas* at the entrance (1916) in porphyry and terracotta. The building's many sea and trade motifs refer to the shipping lines' global empire. Its personifications of the oceans are, in the words of en.wikipedia, "exotic mysterious women".

The glazier **Willem Bogtman** made almost all of the building's stained glass, including the large skylight over the stairwell. He went on to make the glass for the [Raw Aron Schuster Synagogue](place:), where six windows show the twelve tribes of Israel, and for architect Harry Elte's own house ([Stadionweg 44 (Harry Elte's house)](place:)).

## The city's sculptor

After the Scheepvaarthuis, Krop was appointed city sculptor. English Wikipedia says he joined the Public Works department's staff in 1916. The register text for the [Zocherstraat HBS](place:) (c. 1917) describes him as the city's then *unofficial* sculptor, receiving his first commissions from A.R. Hulshoff of Public Works (see [The city builds: Public Works schools, bathhouses and utilities](doc:The city builds: Public Works schools, bathhouses and utilities)). From then on, his figures appear on municipal buildings across the city:

- **Telephones and trade.** On the [Telephone exchange (Singel 340)](place:) (1916–1918) the capitals are carved as telephone poles with wires.
- **Schools.** At the Zocherstraat HBS Krop carved a serpent biting its tail and Egyptian-style eagles. At [Het Sieraad](place:) the figures refer to the trades taught there, and at the [Wingerdweg schools](place:) he made small fairy-tale figures. The four groups on the [Tweede Openbare Handelsschool (Berlage Lyceum)](place:) (1921–1924) were carved in tufa. Ton Mooy recut them in granite in 2004.
- **Transport.** He made *Abundance* and *Traffic* for the [Spaarndammerspoorbrug](place:) (1922), sculpture for the [Gemeentetram headquarters](place:), and a wooden relief for the [Remise Lekstraat](place:).
- **Later municipal work.** Krop's sculpture also appears on the [Smaragdplein school](place:) (1921–1922), relief tiles in the [Europaschool (Hygiëaplein)](place:), the [Gemeentelijk Meisjeslyceum (Reijnier Vinkeleskade)](place:), the [Wilhelmina Gasthuis surgical clinic](place:) (1929) and the [Geological and Chemistry Institute (Roeterseiland)](place:) (c. 1930). The city register calls the last one remarkably anecdotal late work.
- **Wit.** On the [Urinal on the Oudezijds Voorburgwal](place:) (1926) a ranting orator faces the council chamber.
- **Private clients.** The insurer's office [De Friesche (Rokin 115)](place:) shows the blessings of a well-insured life. At [Villa den Tex (Apollolaan 1)](place:) he carved dozens of small limestone figures on site.

## Bridges with Piet Kramer

Krop's longest partnership was with the bridge architect Piet Kramer. Public Works engineers did the steel and concrete. Kramer gave the bridges their brick, granite and iron, and Krop added the figures. The [P.L. Kramerbrug](place:) (1917–1921) has seals, sea lions and Gijsbrecht van Amstel. The Leidsestraat trio of [Koningssluis (Brug 29)](place:), [Brug 43 (Leidsestraat, Keizersgracht)](place:) and [Aalmoezeniersbrug (Brug 68)](place:) has fabulous beasts that Krop designed in 1916 and that the firm of Johan Polet carved. The [Barbiersbrug](place:) (1927–1928) on the Amstelkanaal is another of their showpieces. There is a faun on the [Niek Engelschmanbrug (Brug 106)](place:) (1924–1925), and Jonah and the whale on [Brug 382 (Jan van Galenbrug)](place:). The [Berlagebrug and Schollenbrug (Brug 423, 340)](place:) has a ceramic relief; nl.wikipedia calls it *Genius van Amsterdam* (1932), while en.wikipedia lists it as *Stedemaagd* (1933). On the Muzenplein, the [Kinderbrug and Hildo Kropbrug (Muzenplein)](place:) carry his name.

## The Vierwindstrekenbrug

![One of Krop's four figures on the Vierwindstrekenbrug](commons:File:Krop Vierwindstreken.JPG)

On the [Vierwindstrekenbrug](place:) (1932) Krop represented the four points of the compass with a man at each corner. The north has an Inuit man with a weapon, an igloo and walruses. The south has an African hunter with spear and shield, a hut and lions. The east has a Chinese dock worker with a pagoda and steamships. The west has an American stockbroker on the telephone, with bowler hat, briefcase, office building, share prices and cars.

These figures are stereotypes of their time. Only the West appears as a modern man of business. The other three peoples are shown through huts, animals and manual labour, in the way 1930s Europe tended to see them. The Dutch Wikipedia article says the bridge's picture of the world belongs to a period that "lies behind us". It also stresses that the bridge is an outstanding, intact example of Amsterdam's expressionist bridges. The bridge carried only its number, 381, until it was named after the sculptures in 1996.

## Krop beyond buildings: the grave of Albert Hahn

Krop's politics shaped his work. He belonged to socialist and later communist circles, and his father-in-law chaired the Amsterdam branch of the SDAP. When the socialist cartoonist Albert Hahn died in 1918, a committee raised money for his grave at De Nieuwe Ooster cemetery. Krop's monument, unveiled on 28 September 1919, shows a working-class family driven forward by a genius, above a stylised lotus. Its inscription reads *De arbeiders aan Albert Hahn* ("The workers to Albert Hahn"). It is not a place on this map, but it is a national monument and is worth the trip to the cemetery.

![Krop's grave monument for Albert Hahn at De Nieuwe Ooster (1919)](commons:File:Graf Albert Hahn.JPG)

## Other sculptors of the movement

- **John Rädecker** (1885–1956) taught Krop to carve stone. His three wooden figures, *Stedenmaagd* (1926), were made for the renewed council chamber of the former city hall on the Oudezijds Voorburgwal, whose 1920s wing is the [City hall extension (Prinsenhof)](place:). He also contributed, with Willem Rädecker, to the [Vossius Gymnasium](place:). He is best known for the National Monument on the Dam.
- **Joseph Mendes da Costa** (1863–1939) belonged to the generation before. He was a Portuguese-Jewish stonemason's son who carved for Berlage's Exchange, and Michel de Klerk drew his portrait in 1919. One of his pupils, Adrianus Remiëns, carved for the Prinsenhof extension.
- **Mari Andriessen** (1897–1979) made the putti and four seasons on a villa among the [Apollolaan villas](place:), long before his *Dokwerker* (1952).
- **L.S.W. van der Noordaa** (1894–1945) made the ceramics of [Atlanta (Stadhouderskade 5-6)](place:) and the sculpture of the [AMVJ building](place:), both of 1928.
- **Jan Heijens** made the Pied Piper at the [Vijfde Montessorischool Watergraafsmeer](place:) and worked on the Vossius.
- Others on this map include **Theo Vos** at the [Carlton Hotel (Grand Hotel Centraal)](place:), and **Jan Trapman**, **Jaap Kaas** and **Hubert van Lith**, whose animals and figures stand beside Krop's on the Vierwindstrekenbrug.

Krop kept working into his eighties. One of his last works is the statue of Berlage (1966) on the Victorieplein, in front of the [12-verdiepingenhuis (De Wolkenkrabber)](place:). He died in 1970 and is buried at Zorgvlied.
