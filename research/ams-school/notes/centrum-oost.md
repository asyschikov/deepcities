## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **Scheepvaarthuis dates.** The RCE text says construction ran 1912–1916 (its short description says 1911–1916). Wikipedia nl/en say 1913–1916, with the commission in 1912. I used 1912–1916 from the register. The listing year 1974 comes from nl Wikipedia, not the register. Sculptors: the en Wikipedia article on Van den Eijnde names Krop, Anton Rädecker and Joop van Lunteren in his workshop, and W.C. Brouwer led the team before him. I did not find a verified list of individual façade figures, so the body stays general.
- **Rembrandtplein 44.** The city record gives 1927–1930, but the register text describes Van Schaik's 1918 remodelling as the AS façade. I used 1918. The architect's first name is not given.
- **Tuschinski (included, flag).** The RCE calls it "uitbundige art deco". The en Wikipedia says the façade blends Art Deco, Art Nouveau, Jugendstil and Amsterdam School. I included it because it is the defining building of the period on the Rembrandtplein, and the body states the style mix honestly. The coordinator should decide whether it belongs. Architect: H.L. de Jong, with Willem Kromhout taking over after a dispute (nl Wikipedia).
- **Amsterdamsche Bank (included, flag).** Wikidata and nl Wikipedia class it as AS; English sources say Art Deco. A web search suggested it is a municipal monument, but I did not verify that in the city register (it is not among the city candidates), so heritage_status is omitted.
- **Lijnbaansgracht 259-260.** The city record says 1935–1940 and Wikidata says 1932. nl Wikipedia says the work was tendered in October 1930 ("gymnastiekgebouw met woning op een gedeeltelijk bestaande fundering"). I used 1930–1932. The register name field is "Costerschool ?", which was the previous school on the site.
- **Nieuwe Achtergracht 100 (GGD building).** The city register says Publieke Werken completed it in 1923 for the municipal health service (GGD). Commons/Stadsarchief images and Joods Monument call "Nieuwe Achtergracht 100" the Joodsche Invalide, inaugurated in 1925. The present GGD headquarters is J.F. Staal's 1938 Joodse Invalide on the Weesperplein corner. The register point is on the Lepelkruisstraat axis, so it is probably the symmetrical 1923/1925 building, but it is unclear whether that was the GGD or the first Joodse Invalide. I followed the register and wrote "according to the city monument register" in the body. This needs checking against the Stadsarchief.
- **Uilenburg housing client.** The city record says Stichting Handwerkers Vriendenkring. The register text speaks of complexes built by the Gemeentelijke Woningdienst. nl Wikipedia mentions both organisations in the clearance. I used the record's client.
- **Badhuis Uilenburg.** Designed in 1919 (city); Wikidata says 1923. I used 1919–1923.
- **Double school Nieuwe Uilenburgerstraat 96.** The register name field says "Jonas Daniël Meijerschool", but the text does not use the name. The title avoids it and the body mentions it as the register name.
- **Geology/Chemistry institute (209057).** The architect is "vermoedelijk A.R. Hulshoff" (probably). The city gives 1930–1931; RCE photo titles date the Krop sculpture 1930–1932.
- **Desmet.** The register calls the 1927 front a mix of Art Deco and AS. Its plaster is not original.
- **Bridge listing years** (518384, 518385, 518392–518395) were not available, so `listed` is omitted.
- **Weteringschans 193-195.** nl Wikipedia says it is a municipal monument, but I found no city record number (it is not in the city candidates; possibly a recent designation), so monument_number is omitted.
- **Flesseman.** Not listed. The original façade is largely hidden under 1986–1988 external insulation (nl Wikipedia). The coordinator may consider whether there is enough left to see.

## Material for map-level documents

- **The Scheepvaarthuis as the starting point.** Van Gendt engineered the concrete frame; Van der Mey brought in De Klerk and Kramer from Eduard Cuypers' office. The sculpture workshop was led by Van den Eijnde, with Krop, Anton Rädecker and Van Lunteren; Krop was later appointed city sculptor. Bogtman made the glass and Theo Nieuwenhuis the boardroom. RCE: "het eerste volledig in de stijl van de Amsterdamse School gerealiseerde bouwwerk". Sources: https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/4158, https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheepvaarthuis, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_van_den_Eijnde. A 2025 monograph exists: Louise de Blécourt, *Het Scheepvaarthuis. Eerste gebouw van de Amsterdamse School* (W Books, 2025).
- **Van der Mey before and after.** His first building is the rationalist Hugo de Vries lab (1910–1915). He designed Brug 283 (1913–1914) behind the Scheepvaarthuis, one of the very first AS bridges, and later the third part of Amstelstein on the Vijzelstraat (1926–1928, demolished 1962–1963).
- **Kramer's bridges and the Leidsestraat trio.** Bridges 29, 43 and 68 (1921) share one design, with Krop's fabulous beasts designed in 1916 and carved by Johan Polet. The Leidsebrug (1923–1925) was the first Amsterdam bridge with built-in benches; it has Polet's hippos and lions. Brug 41 has SPQA pylons (1922). Brug 70 (1934–1935) and the Dageraadsbrug (designed 1931, built 1934–1935, no Krop sculpture) show the leaner 1930s. The Bridges section was housed in the Werf Uilenburg (1925–1926), "where Kramer designed his bridges" (nl Wikipedia).
- **The Vijzelstraat widening (1914–1927)** was the old centre's first large-scale urban project, under De Bazel's supervision. Rutgers' Carlton Hotel (1925–1929, sculpture by Theo Vos) and his block at Herengracht 513-515 date from it, as do Kruyswijk's Vijzelstraat 81, Amstelstein and Kramer's Vijzelstraat bridges. Source: city:207009, rce:518381.
- **Uilenburg slum clearance** produced a "rare concentration of AS architecture" in the old centre: Mulder's housing (1926), the double school (1920), the bathhouse (1919, now Splendor), and the PW yard (1925). Source: city:202037 text.
- **Publieke Werken under A.R. Hulshoff** (head of the Buildings section from 1916, city architect from 1924). Designers were not named. Examples: the gym halls on the Lijnbaansgracht, the GGD building, and the Roeterseiland institute with Krop sculpture. Source: city:200494, city:205026, city:209057.
- **AS in the old centre is rare.** Several city register texts stress this: Rembrandtplein 44, Plantage Kerklaan 37, Prinsengracht 688. The small infill buildings by lesser-known architects (Th. Kint, Joh. Visser, Kleinhout & Van der Steur, Westerman, Van Schaik) are a theme of their own. Kleinhout & Van der Steur also built Kriterion (1924). AS shopfronts were a commercial fashion (city:201042, Vijzelgracht 15).
- **Jan Gratama, who coined "Amsterdamsche School" (1916),** finished the Incassobank archive (1928–1929). Source: city:202047.
- **Wijdeveld (editor of *Wendingen*)** designed the Flesseman façades on the Nieuwmarkt (1925–1927). Source: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flesseman.
