## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **De Klerk's blocks.** The coordinator note says "three blocks for Eigen Haard". The first block (north side, rce 5500) was built for the contractor **Klaas Hille**, not Eigen Haard (en.wikipedia Michel_de_Klerk; MIT Dome). The register dates 5500 to "1913" and 5499 to "1914". The literature gives 1913–1915 for block 1, and 1915–1916 (design) with completion around 1916–1918 for block 2. I used 1913–1915 and 1915–1916. The phases are Precursor and Expressive; the first block could arguably be "Expressive".
- **Het Schip.** The register date is 23 July 1974, so I set `listed: 1974`; the BRIEF example shows 1970. The register says "1917"; usual dating is 1917–1920 (meeting hall 1920). The museum has been in the post office since 2001 and in the former school since 2016, at Oostzaanstraat 45, open Tue–Sun 11–17. I took the "102 dwellings (now 82)" figure from en.wikipedia and did not verify it elsewhere.
- **Gulden & Geldmaker.** City record fields say 1917–1921; the text says 1916–1917. I used 1916–1921.
- **Nassaukerk.** City fields say 1926–1930; nl.wikipedia says built 1925–1926 (opened 18 June 1926). I used 1925–1926.
- **Bethlehemkerk.** nl.wikipedia says listed 28 January 1988, but the 5085xx numbers were registered on 28 January 1998 (Zonnehuis, Nieuwendam gate building). This is probably a 1988/1998 typo, so I left `listed` out.
- **Brug 604.** The city says 1953–1954; nl.wikipedia says 1953–1955. I used 1953–1955 in the attribute; the Dutch body mentions both.
- **Brug 382.** The register says designed 1931–33 and built 1935; nl.wikipedia says built 1936 and opened mid-1937. I used 1935–1937.
- **City of Wesopa.** The nl.wikipedia infobox is inconsistent (start 1948, finished 1947); it opened on 14 October 1948. I used 1947–1948.
- **Heritage status not verified:** Spaarndammerspoorbrug, Gemaal Mercatorstraat and Brug 605 (left out); Astoria is set to "Not listed" (nothing found). The monument numbers for Wingerdweg 28-34, Kromme Distelstraat 17 and the Vierwindstrekenbrug (all municipal according to nl.wikipedia) are unknown.
- **Weesp numbers** (450xxxxxx) are the former Weesp municipal register ids from the city dataset. The "Gemeentelijk monument" links point to the generic city monument map.
- **Address for rce:508535** came from a PDOK geocode plus nl.wikipedia (Wognumerplantsoen 2-4).
- **English sources.** Only Het Schip and De Klerk have real English sources. All other English text is written natively from the register facts, not translated sentence by sentence.

## Material for map-level documents

- **Cradle of the style.** De Klerk's three Spaarndammerbuurt blocks show the progression from the "burchtachtig" (castle-like) block 1 for the private builder Klaas Hille (1913–15), to block 2 for Eigen Haard (1915–16), to Het Schip (1917–20). The third block moved to the triangular railway site once block 2 took the south side of the plantsoen. Neighbouring architects had to "fit in": the Schoonheidscommissie debated the Gulden & Geldmaker carré (city:200764, citing Stieber pp. 225–226). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_de_Klerk, https://dome.mit.edu/handle/1721.3/167399, rce 3961/5499/5500.
- **Who named it.** Jan Gratama coined "Amsterdamsche School" in 1916. He also built Eigen Haard housing in the Vogelbuurt, Noord (city:200714).
- **Rural AS in the garden villages of Noord.** Keppler's Gemeentelijke Woningdienst, Wibaut and De Miranda built Tuindorp Oostzaan (1919–24, for NSM shipyard workers, under the 1917 Noodwoningenwet, meant to last only 35 years) and Tuindorp Nieuwendam (1924–34, Boeyinga plan, inspired by Ebenezer Howard). Each village got its own facilities: reading room, bathhouse, fire shed, community hall, schools. Concrete experiments (Winget, Schütz & Bangert) answered the post-WWI brick shortage. Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuindorp_Oostzaan, https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuindorp_Nieuwendam, rce 508378–81.
- **Public bathhouses.** These existed because new homes had no bathrooms: Kromme Distelstraat 17 (1919, eight showers, shaped like a stolpboerderij), Meteorenweg (1927, separate men's, women's and children's sections), and the Vogeldorp bathhouse.
- **Kramer's bridges and Krop's sculpture**, from the 1930s to the 1950s:
  - Brug 382: warehouses and dockers, Jonah in the whale.
  - Vierwindstrekenbrug (1932): the "four winds" as ethnic stereotypes of their time, worth a sensitive essay paragraph.
  - Bruggen 604/605 (1953–55): Kramer working with Van Eesteren's modernist plan. Brug 604 has sculpture by Meefout instead of Krop.
  - Source: Baggelaar & Van Schaik, *Piet Kramer, bruggenbouwer van de Amsterdamse School* (2016), cited on the nl.wikipedia bridge pages.
- **Public Works schools** with sculpture for children: Krop's fairy-tale figures at Wingerdweg 28-34 (Lansdorp 1926); Marnette's school with Krop sculptures in Nieuwendam (lead).
- **Social program in brick.** Het Schip's post office gave the poor a phone and postal service. The Zonnehuis was built through a job-creation scheme because club life was meant to "educate the working class" (nl.wikipedia Zonnehuis).
- **The style spreads to small towns, late.** Weesp has an AS shopfront, a villa (1922), a park bridge and a cinema opened in 1948 that the municipality calls "a late example of the Amsterdam School".
- Walking guide for Noord: https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-noord/nieuws/501479/amsterdamse-schoolarchitectuur-in-amsterdam-noord-xq03e5
