## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **Het Sieraad dates:** RCE says 1921–1923, Wikidata 1924, and the city register's metadata 1926. I used RCE and noted the alternative in `built`.
- **Wijdeveld Hoofdweg:** the city register gives 1925–1926; architectuur.org/Wikidata give 1922–1927 (design to completion). I used the register.
- **Cabralschool:** the city text (2013) says it is in use by basisschool De Wissel; nl.wikipedia says it became the MidWest business centre after a renovation from 2013. I used Wikipedia (more recent). The register also names the client as "Christelijke School Vereeniging Aeneas Mackay", which is odd for a PW design. I left the client out.
- **Overtoom 103-105:** the register's architect field says "Klint, Th." but its text says "Theo Kint". I used Kint.
- **WG pathology lab:** RCE says designed 1927 by Hulshoff; the city metadata says 1930–1939 and Wikidata 1930. I gave "1927–1930".
- **Gemeentetram:** Wikipedia says 1922–1923, Wikidata and the city metadata 1923.
- **Mercatorplein sculptors:** nl.wikipedia lists Anton Rädecker, "Roorda", Johan Polet, Theo Vos, Adrianus Remiëns and Jan Havermans as 1920s sculptors around the square. This is not confirmed by the RCE texts (which mention only "figuratief bouwbeeldhouwwerk"), so I named them only in the body, not in `artists`.
- **Kramer bridges 358/359:** the granite carvings are only "vermoedelijk" (probably) by Hildo Krop per Wikipedia, marked "(attributed)". The bridges' heritage status is unknown, so it was left blank.
- **Pesthuysbrug (1938):** it is borderline. The bridge is very sober, and Kramer's role is described loosely ("een grote vinger in de pap", a big hand in it).
- **Westerwijk / Bethelkerk** are filed as `religious`; Westerwijk is a church community hall rather than a church.
- **Rijksmonument designation years** for the 5271xx series: Jeruzalemkerk and Het Sieraad are 2003 per Wikipedia, and Jan Maijenschool "since 2003". I did not set `listed` where unsure (Staal, Van der Mey, Vespucci, Mercatorplein, Corantijn, school island, X-ray building, lab, Gemeentetram).
- Wikimedia APIs (Wikipedia, Wikidata, Commons) rate-limited curl from this machine for most of the session. Commons file names were gathered with Commons search via WebFetch. The `Category:Rijksmonument_<nr>` lookups returned empty, so photos were not taken from rijksmonument categories.

## Material for map-level documents

- **Plan West and "schortjesarchitectuur":** the 1922 Commissie van Drie (Gratama, Versteeg, Hulshoff) reused Sloten's 1909/1916 plans. Gulden & Geldmaker drew all the dwelling plans, and famous architects only designed the façades, hence the joke *schortjesarchitectuur* (apron architecture). The developer H. van der Schaar (Amstels Bouwvereniging) built about 6,000 homes, mostly in 1925–1927, the same contractor role he had in Plan Zuid. A whole *Wendingen* issue (1927 no. 6/7) was devoted to Plan West. The policy of giving both sides of a street to the same architect turned the Hoofdweg into a sequence of "rooms": Kramer, then Rutgers, then Berlage's square, then Wijdeveld's gate. Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_West, city register texts 200725/200726.
- **Berlage's late urban role:** the Mercatorplein is the AS-era square Berlage designed in full: the façades, two towers and arcades. The demolished north tower (1961) and the 1995–1998 rebuild of the north wall "to the original" are a good preservation story. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercatorplein_(Amsterdam)
- **Public Works schools:**
  - Hulshoff steered the aesthetics, and many PW architects had been taught by AS leaders (register text 200681).
  - In Plan West, schools are slotted into the blocks: the Cabralschool bridging two streets, the 1928 "school island" with its sculptural wall, Frantzen's quadruple Jan Maijenschool, the nursery schools De Klimop and Overtoom 103-105.
  - Source for a schools essay: T. van Toorn, *De Amsterdamsche School-schoolgebouwen in de westelijke stadsuitbreiding* (UvA thesis 1991). The city's "Inventarisatie van Amsterdamse schoolgebouwen 1850-1965" (Spoelstra 2003–04) is the source of many false keyword matches.
- **Hildo Krop as city sculptor:** his early work is the Zocherstraat HBS (c. 1917, Egyptian-inspired, among his largest school sculptures). There is mature work on Het Sieraad (trade-school crafts plus the admirals' voyages, motto "zonder moeite niets") and the Gemeentetram HQ ceramics. Register texts 224056 and 527153 say Hulshoff gave Krop commissions from 1916 onward.
- **Kramer's bridges, from expressive to sober:** the Machinesloot/Molenslootbrug pair (1929–1930, balustrades ending in sea anemones, funded after residents' lobbying and a neighbourhood "feestweek") and the bare 1938 Pesthuysbrug show the arc of the style. Sources: nl.wikipedia Machineslootbrug, Molenslootbrug, Pesthuysbrug.
- **Churches built into housing:** Jantzen's Jeruzalemkerk (1929) has seven flats built into the church as theology, plus number symbolism and Wright influence. His earlier Westerwijk hall (1926) and Roest's Bethelkerk "little town" show how denominations claimed space in a planned district (Reformed, Dutch Reformed and Catholic churches each placed in Plan West).
- **AS beyond housing in Oud-West:** the city machinery of the 1920s is all in AS dress: the tram HQ, the traffic police station (first traffic lights c. 1932), the GEB substation, and the WG hospital pavilions. So are early commercial high-rises (Warners' Atlanta, Kuipers' AMVJ), described as "American style with AS influence".
- **First AS social-housing commission:** the city register claims G.F. la Croix's Bellamystraat block for Rochdale (designed 1917) was the first Woningwet complex given to an AS architect (text 224006). This is worth checking against the usual narrative (De Klerk / Eigen Haard 1913–1915) before using it in an essay.

## Bilingual content

- Every place has an `nl` block, written directly in Dutch from the Dutch sources listed in its `nl.sources`: the gemeentelijk register (redengevende omschrijving), the RCE omschrijving and nl.wikipedia. The English versions were written first, from the same facts.
- Almost none of these buildings have English-language sources: no en.wikipedia articles exist for them, and only general pages such as en.wikipedia "Piet Kramer" and "Amsterdam School" cover the background. So the English is written from the facts in English rather than taken from English sources. The top-level `sources` mostly list register IDs and Dutch URLs for that reason.
- Dates, names and numbers were cross-checked between the two versions. The Dutch `built` values carry the same ranges as the English, with Dutch wording for the qualifiers (Mercatorplein, Het Sieraad, Surinameplein, Zocherstraat).
- The Dutch titles differ from the English where a Dutch name is more natural, e.g. "Woonblokken Hoofdweg (Piet Kramer)", "Scholencomplex Cabotstraat", "Politiebureau Verkeerswezen (Overtoom)".
