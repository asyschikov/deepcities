# Notes — slice Oost

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Badhuis Boerhaaveplein dates:** the city register dump gives 1919, RCE 1920–1921, and nl.wikipedia opening on 6 July 1921. I used RCE.
- **Batjanstraat school:** the register only suggests Westerman ("mogelijk"), so the attribute says "possibly".
- **Kraaipanbuurt dates:** the Woningdienst ring-block experiment is dated 1916 in the Linnaeushof register text, and the in-procedure entries give 1920–1924. A funda listing cites Gratama's elaboration of Berlage's plan in 1916. No monument number yet, so heritage_status is omitted. The coordinate is the register point of Albert Luthulistraat 1-41 / Schalk Burgerstraat 2-140.
- **Krugerhof:** nl.wikipedia (2025) says provisional protection; the city dump (2026) has it as municipal monument 230013, designated 9 June 2026. There is no register text yet.
- **Rinus Michelsbrug:** nl.wikipedia calls it a 1950s traffic bridge and also says both bridges come from Kramer's office "in the 1920s". The text says sources differ.
- **Berlagebrug and Schollenbrug (rce:530055):** the ensemble straddles Zuid (Berlagebrug, Brug 423) and Oost (Schollenbrug, Brug 340). The Wikidata point falls in Oost, so I included it here. **Coordinator:** check that the Zuid slice doesn't also include it.
- **Brink, Betondorp:** the register describes it as a mix of AS and Nieuwe Bouwen. Monument numbers list the main Brink units (335310, 335325, 335330, 335336, 335347, 335357); gallery 335342 and the garages are left out.
- **Galileïplantsoen bridges:** one place for five bridges. The coordinate is the Jaap Kruizingabrug (Brug 335), the middle of the group.

## Material for map-level documents

- **Kramer's Watergraafsmeer bridges, from showpiece to sober:** the 1928 Molenwetering set has brick-and-granite "draughts-stack" piers. Then come the wartime Brug 427 (1941), the post-war Drieburg bridges designed around 1940 and built 1950–1951, and the Omvalbrug (1953), one of his last. Kramer's own career arc shows clearly in one district. Sources: nl.wikipedia pages Brug 337, Brug 254, Brug 441, Omvalbrug.
- **Bridge names as local memory:** Ajax players and coach (2005, near the old De Meer stadium), local historians Jaap Kruizinga (2015) and Henk van Laar (2017), and the graphic designer Fré Cohen (2016, Zaaiersweg). See https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinus_Michelsbrug and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henk_van_Laarbrug.
- **Hygiene and housing:** the Boerhaaveplein bathhouse (1921) as the city's first free-standing bathhouse, built because the new association blocks lacked baths. It links to the theme of Westerman and the Public Works bathhouses. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerhaaveplein_28_(Amsterdam)
- **Jan Gratama** coined the term "Amsterdamsche School" (1916). In Oost his office built the Kraaipanbuurt and planned Betondorp (with Versteeg), and there he is a builder rather than a critic. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Gratama
- **Betondorp:** concrete instead of brick (brick shortage after the First World War). It is an AS-adjacent experiment, with Greiner's Brink as the hinge between AS ornament and the Nieuwe Bouwen. It is Gerard Reve's "Cementwijk" and a protected townscape since 2022. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betondorp
- **Jewish Oost:** the Talmud Tora school (Baars) and the Tugelaweg blocks (Handwerkers Vriendenkring), with the 1943 closure and deportations. A possible thread for an essay on who the "workers' palaces" were for.
- **Commercial vs. social AS:** Krugerhof (1929–1930) was built by a private developer, and Het Volk criticised it as cramped and expensive next to municipal housing. A useful counterpoint to the housing-association story. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krugerhof
- **The school as Gesamtkunstwerk:** Kruyswijk's Newtonschool reliefs (Theo van Reijn, "arbeid overwint 1929") and the Montessori school with Hildo Krop's carved roof beams and Jan Heijens' Pied Piper. Source: city register texts 200642 and 200638.
- **Berlage's frame:** the Transvaalbuurt plan (1903), the Berlageblokken (1911–1915) and the Berlagebrug (1932) are all in this slice. Rationalist precursor, urban framework, and a late "businesslike expressionist" gateway.
