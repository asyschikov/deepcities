## Material for map-level documents

- **Kramer's Raadhuisstraat bridges**: the Rozengracht–Raadhuisstraat radial, opened in the 1890s as the road to Amsterdam-West, became so busy that by 1921–1923 Kramer designed replacements for all its bridges. They are brug 8 (Singel), 22 (Herengracht), 106 (Keizersgracht, with Hildo Krop's fauns, crows and frogs and Winkelman's ironwork) and 63 (Prinsengracht). Wartime price rises delayed construction to 1925. This is a good walking sequence from Magna Plaza to the Westerkerk. Sources: RCE 518387–518390; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmoesbrug_(Amsterdam), https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niek_Engelschmanbrug
- **Van der Mey before Kramer**: before Kramer, the Scheepvaarthuis architect Jo van der Mey ran bridge design at Public Works (Zaagpoort 1913, Muntsluis 1915, Hilletjesbrug 1921 "from the office of"). His granite projecting piers are a precursor motif. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilletjesbrug, https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaagpoort
- **Public Works schools in the Jordaan**: slum clearance (*sloppen* such as the Wijde Gang) freed sites for schools and health posts. The city register describes the change from traditional (1914 Lindengracht, 1915 Anjeliersstraat 35) to Amsterdam School detailing (1927 Lindengracht 146, Theo Thijssen) and the "corner school" type. Religious schools by Kruyswijk ('t Smallepad, Van Noort) and Duncker (Vincentius) followed the 1920 Primary Education Act, which equalised funding for public and religious schools.
- **Hildo Krop in this slice**: the telephone exchange capitals (telephone poles and city arms; the Herengracht entrance was moved to the PTT building in the Watergraafsmeer), brug 106, the urinal's soapbox orator facing the council chamber (1926), and the four "Prosperity, Labour, Care, Rest" figures on De Friesche (Rokin 115, 1928).
- **The office building and "cityvorming"**: the canal ring and Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal filled with purpose-built offices in 1915–1930. Examples are Van Gendt's Deli-Batavia, Drie Koningen and Harkema, the Stam office, Huize Labor, Gebouw De Kolk and the Telegraaf. Huize Labor's demolition of a pair of 17th-century step gables (1919) triggered the founding of the Vereniging Hendrick de Keyser (city register 205048). This is a good story for an essay on the tension between the style and the historic city.
- **The Nes tobacco quarter**: the Harkema office (Van Gendt 1923) shows the tobacco trade's sample rooms. Tobacco auctions were held at today's Frascati from 1879 (city register 201007/209058).
- **Two Mart Stams**: Mart J. Stam of Wormer (Herengracht 303, 1919; the Mercurius rice mill in Wormer) is not the modernist Mart Stam of Purmerend (city register 203018).
- **Adriaan Moen and Gerzon**: a forgotten architect (died 1950) whose Gerzon buildings (1915, 1923–1926) and Singel 46-48 were published by Wattjes in *Nieuw Nederlandsche Bouwkunst* (1926). The 1926 Gerzon façade carries carvings of Indonesian women with peacocks (city register 203063, 203048).
- **Colonial trade imagery**: Gebouw Batavia (arrack; monkeys and elephant heads), Deli-Batavia (tobacco), Gerzon (Indonesian women) and the Van Noort school's "Indian heads".
