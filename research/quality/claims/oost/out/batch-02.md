## For the user

- Zeeburg: the live text called it the cemetery of the poor and gave 70,000 to 100,000 graves. Joods Amsterdam says recent research overturned the 'proletariaat' label, and no source gives 70,000 (both say about 94,000 to 100,000; the 70 is probably the share of children under 13). Rewritten around what the sources say.
- Flowers by Karel Appel: Buitenkunst gives 2002 for the placement; oost-online (Karel Appel was hier) says the district bought it and opened it in 2001, and nl.wikipedia says it has stood there in restored form since 2014. Kept 2002 (it is what the cited source says); worth a look. The physical description (one flower head and planks) differs a little from oost-online (one standing and three lying flowers, beams and plough blades); left as is, since I could not check photos.
- Leeuwenburg: the 2,500 façade panels came only from a search snippet of a paywalled Bouwwereld article; cut until a readable source turns up.
- Vettewinkel: the steel frame for the two new floors rests on an IAA search snippet; the page did not load, so it was cut.
- Former synagogue: the summary now says the last surviving Baars synagogue in Amsterdam (the source's own limit). Linnaeusstraat demolition 1962 sourced to amsterdamopdekaart.
- Glass fronts, Polderweg: Laqueur's lab is now said to have discovered oestrogen in 1925 (source wording), not 'isolated a female sex hormone'.
- Observational lines with no source were cut where they stated facts (no plaque at the Kruislaan doors, no path at Man & Schaap, no ferry timetable, bike racks and passers-by at Flowers). Look-for lines drawn from Commons photos were left alone.
