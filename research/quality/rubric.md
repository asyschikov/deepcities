# Rubric (score every criterion 1-5 with these anchors; don't drift)

**Story**: 5 the summary and first paragraph lead with a specific odd, disputed or surprising fact that is the point of the place. 3 there is a hook but it comes late or is generic ("striking", "notable"). 1 register or catalogue description only.

**Accuracy** (fetch the linked sources): 5 every checkable claim is supported, EN and NL agree. 4 one minor slip or unsupported detail. 3 one claim contradicted by a source, or EN/NL disagree on a fact. 2 several. 1 the core claim is wrong. Unreachable sources: unverified, not wrong.

**Pin** (Nominatim reverse, 1 per second, or Overpass): 5 on the object itself. 4 within about 30 m or on a sensible point of an area. 3 on a neighbour building or street. 1 wrong place.

**Use**: 5 says what can be seen from public ground, current use, how and when to get in (or that it's private), and a concrete Look for list. 3 two of these. 1 none.

**Dutch**: measure it. For each source you fetch, compare the NL body with the source text: count sentences in the NL that share a run of 8 or more consecutive words with a source (ignore names, titles and addresses). 5 none, and it reads native. 4 one such sentence, or a close paraphrase of one passage. 3 two or three such sentences, or the NL follows one source's order and wording throughout. 2 about half the NL. 1 most of the NL. Also -1 (floor 1) if it reads as a translation of the English. Record the count.

**Writing**: 5 plain, concrete, wry where apt, 150-250 words, no padding. 4 one weakness (length 250-320, a padded paragraph, one spaced en dash or em dash). 3 two weaknesses. 2 over 350 words or mostly padding. 1 unreadable.
