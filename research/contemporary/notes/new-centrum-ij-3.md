## Tandwielenfabriek (centrum-ij-46)
- Built 1920 as a bedstead factory, gear wheels until the 1980s, vacancy, demolition plans,
  neighbours' protest, bought by Buro Amsterdam 2013, Janssen with Osborne, 2015, 12 lofts,
  133-180 m2, two shed roofs each, up to 7 m, casco, back to back, own front door, steel sliding
  gates, AAP nom. 2016: Arcam.
- Bought from a housing corporation; over a decade of new-build plans never carried out; plan made
  with Stadsdeel Centrum and residents; addresses Nieuwe Ridderstraat 43-53 and Nieuwe
  Jonkerstraat 50-60: Buro Amsterdam (developer). Arcam gives Nieuwe Ridderstraat 45; the
  Buro range is used, pin between the two streets (BAG NR 45 / NJ 50).
- Janssen quotes (no particular architectural or historical value; factories in the centre being
  demolished), 5 existing openings + 7 new, perforated gates from pixelated photos, trusses
  brushed clean, cement "scars", planters after the ivy was removed: Dezeen 2015; Archello
  (architect text) agrees.
- Geurt Brinkgreve Bokaal 2015: architect's site, confirmed by the Binnenstad article title (UvA
  DARE record "Geurt Brinkgreve Bokaal 2015 naar Tandwielenfabriek in de Nieuwmarktbuurt").
  Architect's site also lists "Zuiderkerk Award 2015 nominated" and "Arie Keppler Award 2016
  winner": single-source, left out.
- `original_use` set; work = Conversion; completed "2015 (conversion)". Not checked for monument
  status (a Gebouwd in Amsterdam snippet says the building had no monumental value; not set).

## Foeliestraat 2-4 (centrum-ij-47)
- Six flats, commercial space and bike storage; between a listed VOC warehouse and a housing block;
  plinth for the height difference; sloping top floor; concrete beams echo the beams in the gable of
  the monument on the Prins Hendrikkade; narrow rear patio; brick façade as noise screen near the
  IJ tunnel entrance, glass behind, frames hidden behind the brick grid; headers and stretchers;
  house numbers in the formwork; client Buro Amsterdam; AAP nom. 2021: Arcam.
- Grid continues over the roof to the apex; sliding windows for fresh air; patio about 2 m wide;
  bedrooms face it: Archello / ArchDaily (architect text). 760 m2: architect's site.
- The scout hook was right. Architect's site also claims an ARC21 Detail Award 2021 and other
  nominations: not confirmed elsewhere, left out.
- The ground floor held a beauty clinic in the 2023 Commons photo; text says "commercial unit".

## Simonsz (centrum-ij-48)
- 19 flats, Prosperity, former school and homeless shelter on the site, central entrance, bronze
  glass/frames/letterboxes/intercoms, piers deep below and shallow above so the façade seems to
  lean forward (nod to old houses), more distance between street and ground floor, gas-free, WKO,
  solar panels and sedum roof, light partitions: Arcam.
- 44 m front, top storey steps back to follow narrow houses: Archello (architect text).
- Architect's site claims Architectenweb runner-up 2020: single-source, left out.

## De Keyzer (centrum-ij-49)
- Dubbeltjespanden at the start of Czaar Peterstraat and Blankenstraat for over a century; block
  follows the old building lines; prefab brick-and-concrete elements; bays separating roof terraces;
  large windows and deep piers; light courts with the old wall anchors; Tom Frantzen; client De Key;
  2011; Commercieel/Voorzieningen/Winkels/Woningbouw; not public; Czaar Peterstraat 2; AAP nom.
  2012: Arcam.
- 130 x 24 m, 68 homes round four light courts, care functions and commercial ground floor,
  81 parking places, 2008-2011, Zuiderkerkprijs 2011: architectuur.org.
- 68 free-sector flats and the garage sold to Altera (Dec 2012); 14 homes for Amsta, care and
  services centre and ground-floor business units stay with De Key; "last year" won the
  Zuiderkerkprijs: NUL20 (7 Dec 2012) and PropertyNL (82 parking places). nl Wikipedia's
  Zuiderkerkprijs list also gives De Keyzer for 2011.
- **Correction:** arkitectureonweb says the Zuiderkerk award was 2012 and calls the 68 flats "for
  the elderly"; three sources say 2011, and the flats are free-sector rentals, so both are not used.
- The Dubbeltjespanden detail (built 1878-1880 by the BVEW, the weekly dime, a 2001 gas explosion,
  demolition in 2006) is from Wikipedia only and left out; the text just says nineteenth-century
  homes, as Arcam does.
- Scout's "Frantzen et al." is right (FRANTZEN et al architecten, Tom Frantzen). No usable
  architect's web page (frameset site from 2002).

## Lariks (centrum-ij-50)
- Revaleiland, Houthaven, M3H for Lieven de Key, for young people, Amsterdam School inspired by the
  nearby Spaarndammerbuurt, name from the timber once traded here, washed-gravel concrete, Friends
  concept, two-room flats for young people in social professions, 253 euros basic rent per month,
  flexible plans, two main entrances, raised shared garden over the garage, business units, laundry:
  Arcam. Borough West (Arcam).
- Built by Hillen & Roosen from late 2020, finished 2023; four fronts; businesses on two sides,
  homes on two; garage in the middle; homes ringing the first-floor garden; four Houthaven buildings
  named after woods; Reval = Tallinn; light wood for a project with a light brick; 85 homes for
  three, 4 for two, 15 two-room flats: Lieven de Key.
- Gouden Piramide 2024 special mention, jury doubts about temporary contracts and moving on after
  five years: M3H news page quoting the jury (I did not read the jury report itself).
- **Unit count:** M3H and Architectenweb say 102 homes; Lieven de Key's list adds up to 104. The
  text gives no total. e-architect's "300 residential units" (likely rooms) not used.
- Address: Revaleiland 121-286 (Gebouwd in Amsterdam snippet) / 125-286 (Lieven de Key snippet);
  pin is the BAG point for Revaleiland 176 (commercial unit in Lariks per a Broersma listing seen
  only as a search summary). Slightly uncertain which corner is best; the pin is inside the block.

## Tussen de Lakens (centrum-ij-56)
- Closing piece of the Blaauwlakenblok restoration; name from the Blaauwlaken- and Zwartlakensteeg
  and the cloth dyers; atelier home for the first tenants, a creative family from a demolished part
  of the block; veiled cube, three connected floors and roof terrace; social housing with flexible
  plans; Chris Kabel; white aluminium, about 48,000 punched hexagons bent up or down by hand; panels
  anchored to white masonry and rising as the roof balustrade; Abbink X Co (Angie Abbink); De Key;
  2012; AAP nom. 2013: Arcam.
- Punching as an affordable alternative to laser cutting; pattern recalls the textile trade:
  Archello (Abbink x Co text) and Chris Kabel's own page. Kabel's page credits "Abbink X de Haas";
  Arcam and Archello say Abbink X Co: I use Abbink X Co.
- Alleys gated, residents and visitors only: nl Wikipedia Blaauwlakenblok and the Commons photo
  descriptions ("niet openbaar toegankelijke Blaauwlakensteeg").
- **Uncertain:** whether any of the house can be seen from a public street. I found nothing to say
  so, so the text says it cannot be visited and the only Look-for is the alley gates. The
  coordinator may want to keep it off the list for that reason. Wikipedia's claim that it was in
  the NAi yearbook's 30 best projects is single-source, left out.

## Elandshof / Woningen Elandsstraat (centrum-ij-57)
- 2004 three couples; plot between Elandsstraat and Lijnbaansstraat; city sold with polluted soil
  and buildings to be demolished to avoid a tender; three more families; CPO; six apartment rights
  with a sixth of the garden each; Jongerius architect and resident; courtyard; 133 glass and wood;
  135 and 137 bluestone; door with the children's names; three pavilion houses with wooden steps,
  front doors and gardens on the dead-end Lijnbaansstraat; Bastiaan Jongerius and Merel Martens;
  Vereniging Elzes; 2011; AAP nom. 2012: Arcam. DASH (TU Delft, 2018) confirms the story and the
  name Elandshof.
- **Date:** Arcam says 2011, DASH says early 2012; completed field uses 2011 (AAP nomination year
  2012 fits).
- **Discrepancy:** Dezeen says the two Elandsstraat buildings were "retained and renovated"; Arcam
  and DASH describe them as fitted into the street front, and DASH says the plot came with
  buildings to be demolished. The text avoids saying new or renovated. Work set to New building.
- Title: Arcam's name with the name used by DASH and ArchDaily (Elandshof).
