Westpoort now covers only Havens-West (BA) and Coenhaven/Minervahaven (BB). I checked every item below against the city's wijk boundaries. Only two sit on the edge: the All Aboard roundabout is on the BA/FA line, and Thuishaven is likely in FA, so it may count as already-covered Nieuw-West.

**Monuments and heritage**
1. Houtveemloods; Nieuwe Hemweg 10 (52.3964, 4.8649, wijk Coenhaven/Minervahaven); a very long timber-storage shed, about 1950, by the city's Dienst der Publieke Werken, beside the Houtveemkanaal that was filled in around 2019; the only municipal monument in Westpoort, designated 8 July 2025; https://api.data.amsterdam.nl/v1/monumenten/monumenten/69612699-f2c9-4976-aa85-7e8c6efcb61c; caveats: business premises (about 17,000 m² advertised for rent), so exterior only; no published description of why it was listed; Nice.
2. Ruigoord village and Sint-Gertrudiskerk; Ruigoord 76/78 (52.4103, 4.7491); a former island village, emptied for a port that never came and squatted by poets on 23–24 July 1973; the church is now the arts venue; Amsterdam's oldest free space, home of the Landjuweel festival (next edition 23–27 July 2026 per festival listings); https://ruigoord.nl and https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/355398/; caveats: a 25-year lease from the port runs from 1 January 2026; the church is used less now because of safety rules; nobody may live there; church built 1892 per Wikipedia, 1894 per OSM/Wikidata; Amazing.
3. Eendenkooi Ruigoord; Bauduinlaan, southern tip of the former island (52.4030, 4.7473), with a bird hide just south (52.3984, 4.7511); a duck decoy documented since 1652, rebuilt from 2012 by the port and Stichting Het Eiland with young people who left school early; a rare surviving decoy inside a heavy-industry port; https://eendenkooi.wordpress.com/; caveats: access hours unclear and the blog has been quiet since 2021, so it needs a site visit; Nice.

**Hidden history (war, vanished, disputed)**
5. Hembrug site, Hempontplein (52.4197, 4.8267); here stood the Hembrug railway swing bridge (1907–1983), once the swing bridge with the widest openings in the world; on 26–27 September 1944 two resistance swimmers, Remmert Aten and Jaap Boll, cut hundreds of German demolition charges off its middle pier and sank them; on 7 August 1928 the Olympic cycling road race (a 168 km time trial) started here; three stories on one spot; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hembrug_(brug) and https://www.olympedia.org/results/153420; caveats: the bridge is gone (Hemtunnel since 1983), nothing is left to see; Amazing.
6. Petroleumhaven; Petroleumhavenweg (about 52.411, 4.855); a U-shaped oil harbour from 1887, built "an hour's walk" from the city so it could be sealed off, with a 70 m middle island where a watchman lived with his family; British commandos destroyed almost all of it in May 1940; a short-lived railway halt served it 1891–1902; https://www.stelling-amsterdam.nl/kazernes/amsterdam-petroleumhaven/index.php; caveats: the terminals are fenced, so you see it from the road; not a monument; Nice.
7. Lancaster memorial Houtrakpolder; Machineweg, Ruigoord (52.4185, 4.7355); on 30 January 1944 Lancaster JB659, on a raid to Berlin, crashed onto the Van der Bijl farm, killing six family members and all seven crew; a propeller unit with plaques, unveiled 2001; https://tracesofwar.com/sights/17758/Memorial-Lancaster-JB659.htm; caveats: the farm itself is gone under port land; Nice.
8. Vrijstaat Groenoord and the Afrikahaven fight; edge of the Afrikahaven next to Ruigoord (about 52.415, 4.75); the summer 1997 eco-camp of tree huts, tunnels and concrete lock-ons was evicted on 7 October 1997 by about 200 riot police, and the harbour was dug anyway (partly as a sludge depot); a disputed and long underused basin; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruigoord; caveats: the camp is gone and the exact spot is approximate; Nice.
9. ADM terrain; Hornweg, Westhaven (52.418, 4.796); a shipyard from 1877, then from 1997 to 2019 one of Europe's largest squats (Robodock festival, about 130 artists, more than 2,500 trees planted), evicted January 2019 and returned to shipbuilding; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woon-_en_werkgemeenschap_ADM; caveats: gone, now a fenced yard; the community moved to Amsterdam-Noord; Nice.
10. Ford Amsterdam Assembly ("Fordhaven"); Hemweg/Westhaven near the Hempont (52.4187, 4.8188); Henry Ford's plant, 1932–1981, which even assembled Ford Mustangs in 1965–67, killed off by the flop of the Transcontinental truck; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Nederland; caveats: I could not find what, if anything, survives on the site; Nice.

Hemweg power station; Nieuwe Hemweg (52.4052, 4.8451); 1950s coal plant with a row of eight stacks; unit 8 was shut by government order in December 2019; its 175 m "wolkenfabriek" chimney was cut down about a metre a day from June 2025; https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/349451/; caveats: Hemweg 9 (gas) still runs; the chimney's completion date is unconfirmed (September 2025 was expected); fenced; Nice.

Ceres Paragon "ship-in-a-slip" terminal; Amerikahaven (about 52.413, 4.775); a radical 2001 container terminal with a cut-in dock so ships could be worked from both sides, barely ever used ("white elephant"), closed 1 January 2013; all nine giant cranes were shipped off to Spain and Poland; https://worldcargonews.com/?p=6147; caveats: the cranes are gone; Grimaldi's Amsterdam Multipurpose Terminal has run the Amerikahaven site since 2023; fenced; Okay.
