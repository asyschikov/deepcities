## Decisions and judgement calls

- **Marbon ranked Nice although gone.** The brief says gone things are Okay unless the story is exceptional; the 1971 explosion (nine dead, permit signed off the same day, a turning point for industrial-safety policy and the fire brigade) is treated as exceptional. Coordinator may downgrade to Okay.
- **Ford ranked Okay** (gone, nothing to see). The Mustang/occupation story is good, so Nice is defensible.
- **ADM ranked Nice** (gone, but the country's biggest free state for 21 years).
- **Ceres is not marked Gone**: the indented berth still exists inside Holland Cargo Terminal; only the cranes and the operator are gone.
- **All Aboard is in Nieuw-West**, not Westpoort: the city wijk lookup (`areas.py`) puts the OSM pin in Sloterdijk Nieuw-West (stadsdeel Nieuw-West). Included as asked; the body says it lies just outside Westpoort.
- **Ford overlaps the hemweg slice's "Coal and bulk mountains (HES Bulk Terminal)"**: the bulk terminal took over the Ford land. The Ford pin (52.4158, 4.8220) is about 700 m from that pin and 300 m from the hemweg bank swallow wall. Consider linking them in a route.
- Categories for sculptures follow the sibling drafts (curiosities).

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **Marbon pin**: I could not find the exact plot. GE Plastics' address was Cyprusweg 2; today the Cyprusweg (a short road off the Hornweg) has no BAG addresses. The pin is the PDOK road centroid, and the body says so. Ons Amsterdam (2005) described the site as a vacant field; I could not confirm what is on the plot in 2026, so the text only says nothing recalls the plant. No memorial found.
- **Marbon victims**: nl.wikipedia says five city firefighters and four members of the company brigade; Ons Amsterdam says five firefighters and three Marbon staff died on the spot plus one later (NRC 16 Aug 1971: the ninth was Mr Boom of the company brigade). Consistent; text uses the Ons Amsterdam phrasing.
- **Marbon restart**: nl.wikipedia says 1972; Andere Tijden describes a 14-day "test run" after the disaster that simply carried on. Text says 1972.
- **Marbon, 1968**: Nationaal Archief/Anefo has photos of an earlier explosion at Marbon on 26 November 1968 (Bestanddeelnr 921-8882 to 8885). Not used in the text; could be added.
- **Ford pin is approximate**: the plant was at Hemweg 201 (Stadsarchief, IISG), 16 ha just west of the Hembrug beside the Westhaven; the old Hemweg no longer exists. Wikimapia puts the former plant area at 52.4133, 4.8203; the IISG photo geotag is 52.4174, 4.8259.
- **Ford job numbers**: Wikipedia gives 1,480 employees in 1981 and 1,650 jobs threatened; Conam says "ruim elfhonderd" were laid off. Left out of the text.
- **Ford Mustang dates**: Wikipedia says 1965–1967; Vervoor says assembly started September 1965 and stopped mid-1967, 397 built in 1966 (Kevin Marti), 400 to 600 in total.
- **ADM land history**: nl.wikipedia says the ADM bought the Westhaven land in 1960; Het Parool (2025) says the city sold it to the ADM "in 1970" with a shipbuilding-only covenant. Year dropped from the text.
- **ADM area**: 42 ha (nl.wikipedia, EenVandaag), 43 ha land and water (Parool, Allington), 45 ha (en.wikipedia), 66 ha (NOS/ANP). Text uses 42 ha for the yard and 43 ha for the 2025 sale.
- **ADM residents**: 125 (nl.wikipedia), 130 (en.wikipedia, EenVandaag 2015). Summary says about 130.
- **ADM today**: since the 2025 purchase I found no news on current use, so the text doesn't describe it.
- **Ceres cranes**: Loadstar (2012) says some went to Spanish ports; Parool (2014) says four of nine were sold at the end of 2012; Noordhollands Dagblad (2015) says the last five went to Tenerife (3) and Gdańsk (2). The scout's Grimaldi/AMT lead concerns another Amerikahaven terminal (bought from EMA in 2023), not the Ceres site, so it is left out.
- **Ceres crane reach**: Liftech says 22 containers across; NHD quotes 24. Text uses Liftech.
- **Heliport origin**: nl.wikipedia says it opened in 2005 "op initiatief van Adam Curry"; nu.nl (2005) says his company Rotorjet went bankrupt in March 2003; an Amsterdam Centraal snippet (Dec 2003) mentions "Rotorjet Amsterdam Heliport BV" (page unreachable, certificate error). Text keeps to the first two.
- **Lifeliner 1 move date**: traumaheli-mmt.nl says 19 July 2023, MMT Nederland says from 1 September 2023. Text says 2023.
- **All Aboard location wording**: Stadscuratorium, Rodi and Amports say the Seineweg/Radarweg roundabout; NH Nieuws/AT5 say Basisweg/Seineweg. Same roundabout; the address uses Seineweg / Radarweg.
- **All Aboard dates**: lifted into place 27 September 2025 (NH Nieuws); official unveiling 6 November 2025 (Amports).

## Route idea

"Port of lost causes" (bike, about 15 km): Hempontplein (Hembrug, Hempont, Kissing Couple XXXL) → Ford assembly plant (gone) and the coal mountains → Amsterdam Heliport → ADM squat (gone) → Marbon chemical plant (gone) → All Aboard. Ceres is too far west and too invisible for a route; it suits a Ruigoord/Afrikahaven loop.
