## Merges

- Ruigoord village and its church are one place, pinned at the church (the venue and office address).
- The 1997 Groenoord camp is its own place, as SCOUT listed it, and the Ruigoord body only mentions the Afrikahaven briefly. It could also go inside Ruigoord instead.
- The Stopplaats Amsterdam Petroleumhaven halt (1891–1902) is in the Petroleumhaven body, not a place of its own: it was about 1.6 km away on the Den Helder line and nothing is left of it.

## Corrections to SCOUT and source conflicts

- **Groenoord eviction:** SCOUT says it began on 7 October with "about 200 riot police". The contemporary ANP/NT and Trouw reports say it started at 07:30 on **6 October 1997**. On day 1, 200 ME guarded the fencing, and about **850** police took part overall. nl.wikipedia (Ruigoord) says 7 October and 800 ME on the first day, and nl.wikipedia (Afrikahaven) says 7–8 October. I went with the 1997 newspapers. The camp started "medio juli" (NT). nl.wikipedia gives two different dates (16 July and 22 July), so I left out an exact day. Two more camps followed, and harbour work started on 1 December 1997 (nl.wikipedia, Afrikahaven).
- **Church date:** nl.wikipedia and ONH say 1892. Amsterdam op de kaart cites contemporary papers (Maasbode and Nieuws van den Dag, 21-6-1894): first stone June 1894, in use December 1894, architect C.P.W. Dessing. OSM also gives 1894. I used **1894**.
- **Squat date:** 24 July 1973 (nl.wikipedia, Cruquius Museum); SCOUT said 23–24 July. The last mass was on 22 July 1973 (Anefo photo caption, Nationaal Archief).
- **Ruigoord lease:** signed 17 December 2025, runs from 1 January 2026 until at least 2051 (NH Nieuws). The Port of Amsterdam route page still says "zeker tot 2027", which is outdated.
- **Houtveemloods size:** the city ("Erfgoed van de Week") gives 100 by 150 m and 15,000 m². Arcam and De Westkrant give 160 by 160 m. I used the city's figure. Arcam gives the address as Nieuwe Hemweg 70; the register says Nieuwe Hemweg 10. Opened by Queen Juliana on 6 July 1950. The register's "oorspronkelijke functie: wonen" is clearly a data error, so I used "Timber warehouse (veem)".
- **Eendenkooi date:** everyone says 1652, except Historisch Halfweg, which says 1672 (probably a typo).
- **Gemaal Houtrakpolder:** OSM tags the building start_date 2002, but Rijnland and the Gemalenstichting give 1936, and the 2009–2014 photos show a 1930s building. I used 1936. The Gemalenstichting names the architect as "J. Lok, ontwerp Tauw". Some sources give the address as "Halfweg", but the point is inside Amsterdam.
- **Petroleumhaven 1940:** the Stelling site says British commandos destroyed "vrijwel de gehele" harbour in May 1940. nl.wikipedia says the tanks were set on fire on 14 May 1940 and about half the capacity was destroyed or badly damaged. The body says "a large part".
- **Lancaster:** the plaque, ASN and Dodenakkers say 30 January 1944, around 22:10 (one listing says 31 January). The unveiling date is 28 November 2001 per 4en5mei and the morning of 29 November per Dodenakkers, so the body says "November 2001". ASN puts the 2001 burial in "an Amsterdam civilian cemetery". Dodenakkers says it was Zwanenburg, next to the two crew already buried there, and I followed that.

## Uncertainties

- **Eendenkooi access:** the official blog has had no posts since June 2021, and I found no published opening hours. The body only says you see the decoy wood from the Bauduinlaan (Port of Amsterdam Ruigoord cycle route). It needs a site visit to confirm the pond is still kept up. OSM has a bird hide about 500 m south (52.3984, 4.7511), but I couldn't confirm what it overlooks, so I left it out.
- **Groenoord pin:** it is approximate (the south tip of the Afrikahaven next to the village). No source pins the camp.
- **Houtveemloods visibility:** Arcam says it is not open to the public. I assumed the rear flank can be seen from the Nieuwe Hemweg, as in De Westkrant's photo of the back. Worth checking on site.
- **Ruigoord category:** I chose `religious` because the pin is the church. The place is really about a squatters' and artists' colony, which none of the map's categories fits well.

## Walking / cycling route ideas

1. **Ruigoord island loop (by bike, from bus 382 or Sloterdijk):** Ruigoord and the Sint-Gertrudiskerk, then Vrijstaat Groenoord (gone), Eendenkooi Ruigoord, Lancaster memorial Houtrakpolder and Gemaal Houtrakpolder. This is the island's story: decoy, polder, squat, war, harbour.
2. **Port heritage ride (east):** Houtveemloods, then Petroleumhaven. Two bold 19th- and 20th-century answers to how Amsterdam stored dangerous and bulky cargo, which fits with the hemweg and port slices.
