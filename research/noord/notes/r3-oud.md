- 203 Kromwijk bow: **left out**. On 20 July 2026 Street Art Cities set its marker at Ms. van Riemsdijkweg 75 to "removed". The last sightings are from January 2025, and the bow is otherwise sourced only by a 2008 archive photo caption. It is unclear whether the graffiti or the bow itself has gone. A site visit would settle it.
- 238 Helling X and 255 the two trams went into "NDSM slipways and Kraan 13 (Faralda Crane Hotel)" (ndsm.yaml) as two body paragraphs, two Look for lines and sources. OSM names the western slipway X-Helling and the eastern one Y-Helling, so Helling X is the small western slipway in the place. I set `check: Still there?` for the trams (OSM still maps both, but the latest dated photos are from 2015 and 2018).
  - Dropped from the scout: the "1918 tram" (not found; AD only says another old Amsterdam tram was donated) and the 2017 council motion. The motion was not found. The Kunstraad's October 2017 advice only shows that the X-helling tenants were uncertain about rents.
- 236 Kunststad went into "NDSM Loods (Scheepsbouwloods)": Dynamo Architecten, built 2005–2006, the black steel frame, and the lease since 2014. One link was added (architectuur.nl). The scout's "50-year" lease could not be confirmed, so the text only says "on a long lease". architectuurgids.nl returned 403.
- 206 went into "Hotel de Goudfazant": a paragraph on the Noordergasfabriek (1913–1924) and the Ponthaven (1930, still the GVB ferry harbour), plus a Look for line and a link. The scout's "retired ferries leave for heritage trips" was not sourced and is left out. The text says "this corner of the Hamerkwartier" was the gasworks: Wikipedia puts the Ponthaven and the GVB ferry office on the Aambeeldstraat, on the former gasworks site.
- 204 went into "Volewijck gallows field (gone)": a body paragraph on the kindertjesboom and Hooft's Warenar (1617), from HCAN.
- 207 went into "Tolhuistuin and the Shell canteen": a paragraph on the IJ-paviljoen and its mooring posts (Rodi, Dirk Roos). The attribution to Piet Kramer is the author's inference from the Johanna Borskibrug, so the text says "probably". The factory-side part of 213 went in as one clause: the ELTA halls on the Papaverweg became the Fokker factory.
- 213 went into "War memorials at De Nieuwe Noorder (Fokker bombing, Draka)": two sentences (ELTA halls in 1919, aircraft taken to Schiphol by barge, moved to Schiphol in 1951), from fokker-history.com. The scout's "nothing of the factory survives" was not verified and is left out.
- 209 went into "Mosveld's stone floodlights": a new Look for list with Astoria (Mosplein 18, 1929–1966, twelve flats since 2012/2013) and De Bult (1967). Two links were added. De Bult survived the 2016 rebuild: the nl.wikipedia street article still describes it, with a 2016 photo.
- 210 went into "Friendly Fire manhole covers": two Look for lines, the Latherusstraat synagogue and the Mospleinkerk (now the Coptic Orthodox church), from HCAN. Sources only, since the place already has 6 links.
- 212 went into "Disteldorp": the bathhouse Look for line was expanded (1919, eight showers, closed 1977, houses got showers in 1983) and a source added.
- 254 went into "Noorderklankpark" as a paragraph plus a Look for line, with two links added. The Tanker and the Pomp are 70–150 m from the instruments.
  - Dropped from the scout: the flamingo mural, yoga and the Ghanaian church services (no source found).
  - The LED CLOUD canopy (2013) is described in the past tense, because no recent evidence was found that it still runs.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- Van der Pekbuurt:
  - End date of the northern renovation: Wikipedia says 2033, NUL20 (May 2025) about twelve years from 2023, and Ymere's undated response to the documentary "2030 rather than 2036". The text uses Ymere's "around 2030".
  - The scout's Gentiaanbuurt (Ibelings van Tilburg, AAP 2025 nominee) is **not** part of the Van der Pekbuurt. Arcam says it was built for the Stoomvaartmaatschappij Nederland by Van Gendt and Kleinhout. It is left out of this place and could be a separate lead.
- Norderney: RadioFreak (2023) gives "18 April 1964" as the start, which is wrong. Wikipedia (nl/en) and offshoreradio.info agree on November 1964. Current use is confirmed by ADE 2026 event listings (search snippet).
- Iepenarboretum:
  - Planting date: Boomzorg's 2010 article says the elms were planted in autumn 2009, a later Boomzorg item says 2010. The text says 2009–2010 and "autumn 2009".
  - Designer: Boomzorg names LANDLAB, Bureau Oslo also lists the park. No architect field is set.
  - The count (70 trees, 32 kinds) comes from the headline of a paywalled Parool article (2023).
- Johan van Hasseltkanaal: the lift-out middle span of the Ridderspoorweg bridge rests on nl.wikipedia alone.

## Route idea

"The canal that never was", Oud-Noord, about 3 km:

Iepenarboretum → Tolhuistuin and the Shell canteen → Volewijck gallows field (gone) → Van der Pekbuurt → Friendly Fire manhole covers → Disteldorp → Johan van Hasseltkanaal → Mosveld's stone floodlights → Noorderklankpark (with the Tanker and the Pomp) → Noorderpark station.
