## Site checks the scout asked for

- **306, which houses were the guardhouse:** settled without a visit. TracesOfWar gives Landsmeerderdijk 191; nl.wikipedia says three joined houses on the corner of Landsmeerderdijk and Kadoelenweg; BAG has exactly three adjoining houses, nos. 189, 191 and 193, on that corner. Pinned on 191, no `check`. (BAG build year says 1969 for all three, a placeholder value common in BAG for old buildings.)
- **311, does the 2012 Marktplaats bar still stand:** probably yes. BAG has a building of 2012, status in use, at 52.39489, 4.920252, beside the pavilion; a 2014 Commons photo shows it; Pompet (the previous operator) was described with "an outdoor bar". Whether it still has the second-hand windows and charred shutters after the 2017 repairs and later café refits is not known, so `check: Still there?` with a note in both languages. The scout's "red chalet" is the Meili Peter pavilion itself, already painted red in 2014.

## Sources and conflicts

- **305:** gebouwdin calls the Gentiaanbuurt "rijksbeschermd"; Arcam says part of the protected cityscape and tags it "Rijksmonument". The 1990s register text only names it as a candidate for a protected cityscape. The text says "protected as part of a listed cityscape" and sets no heritage fields. Both renovation projects (block 5, 2019, and blocks 6-10, 2023) claim to be "the first gas-free" in Amsterdam's existing housing; the text says both teams claim it. The AAP 2025 nomination in SCOUT-4 was not confirmed on the Arcam page; left out. Not part of the Van der Pekbuurt (as r3-oud already noted).
- **311:** Hoogvliet is inferred from the client "Vestia Hoogvliet" on Meili Peter's page (the 2009 Noorderparkkamer site only says "a Rotterdam park"); kept in the text, since Vestia Hoogvliet is that satellite town's housing association. The scout's "wood from a crate that shipped a milk-carton machine from China" was not in any source I read; left out. Year of the move: the 2009 site says five years in Rotterdam, and the foundation programmed it from 2007. Current use: July 2026 alcohol licence for "Noorderpark Café" at Floraparkweg 1; previous operator Pompet. Not written as a food place: architecture first, current use one line.
- **306:** Kiekens dates differ: nl.wikipedia has production moving to Almelo in 1947 and the building closing in 2001; TracesOfWar has casemate parts in the 1930s and the magazine gone in the 1980s. The text uses only what doesn't clash. The squat story rests on NL Times (English, quoting NU.nl); its start year (about 2002) is derived from "13 years". The lane name Groene Ridderhof (OSM) is linked to the 1631 inn De Groene Ridder (nl.wikipedia); the scout's "'t Haasje" lane was not found and is left out. Klein Kadoelen build years 2019-2020 from BAG.
- **307:** Single source (Arcam, Heemschut text, updated May 2026). No permit, sale or plan found for Schoenerstraat 7 in 2026 (officielebekendmakingen.nl). `outlook: Major change ahead` with a note; no `outlook_when`. Cross-reference to Van Stigt's "Groenhoven and Gouden Leeuw" in Zuidoost (already on the map).
- **309:** Arcam names the architects as Herman Zeinstra and Gianni Cito of Dok Architecten, 2012.
- **315:** Single source for the history (the farm's own English site). The scout's "new goat barn from December 2026" was not found; the farm's news page (October 2026) reports only a fundraiser (August 2026) for a new barn for the sheep and goats, which is what the text says. Opening hours from iamsterdam (weekdays 10:00-16:00, Thursday to 15:00; weekend hours cut off), written as "most days from 10:00 to 16:00". An Anefo photo of 8 June 1972 shows the farm being set up.
