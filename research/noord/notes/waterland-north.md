## Status checks (November 2026)

- Witte Kerkje Holysloot: roof timbers being renovated November 2026 to March 2027; not for hire, services moved to the Wijkgebouw opposite (holysloot.nl). Stated in the place.
- Former Reformed church Holysloot: a private home since after 1996; street view only.
- Holysloot school: closed 1979; the schoolmaster's house is café-restaurant Het Schoolhuis (open, 2026 site). Mentioned in one sentence only; not a separate food place.
- Milk tap: Disseldorp organic dairy, Broekergouw 5; the farm's own page (2026) gives every day 8:00 to 21:00. Pinned on the address point of Broekergouw 5 (PDOK), not on the tap itself.
- Volgermeerpolder: open; active Volgermeer Vereniging and Burgerkomitee in 2026; 2024 water report found no further spread; 2025 nature day found 400+ grass snake eggs. Pinned on the bird screen (vogelkijkhut.nl GPS 52.42205, 4.99181), not the scout's point (52.417013, 4.980448), which I could not confirm.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- Zunderdorp church date: the register and nl.wikipedia say the hall church was built in 1711 against the old tower and repaired in 1824; Reliwiki says the church was demolished and replaced in 1854 (and gives G. Bronke as architect, the Holysloot architect). I followed the register; the 1854 claim and architect are left out.
- 1925 tower fire: Het Centrum of 16 April 1925 says "last night" (15 April); Reliwiki's summary says 25 April. The texts just say "April 1925".
- Volgermeer barrel count: nl.wikipedia says "tens of thousands", ONH says about 10,000. Texts say "thousands" (EN) and leave the number out (NL).
- Holysloot school: register says first stone 18 October 1875; holysloot.nl says the master moved into the stone building in 1874. Register followed.
- Former Reformed church: city register year 1915, register text says built 1916 (demolition of the wooden church approved August 1916); ONH says opened February 1917. Used 1916–1917.
- Holysloot population: en.wikipedia about 160 (2004 figure), holysloot.nl about 290 (2020). No number in the texts.
- "Smallest churchyard in North Holland" (en.wikipedia, citing De Nieuwe Noorder) is written as "often called".
- Broekergouw 12 is "one of the two reclamation farms in this polder" per the register; which polder and which reclamation is not stated, so I kept the register wording.
- The 1916 flood: no flood marker found in Holysloot. "Whole village fled, down to the last resident" is from the Noord-Hollands Dagblad summary of the book *Van Hoolesloot tot Holysloot*.
