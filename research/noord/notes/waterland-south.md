## Ranks

Amazing: Ransdorp tower and church. Nice: former town hall, School met den Bijbel,
Schellingwouderkerk, Kinselmeer, Vuurtoreneiland. Okay: the rest.

## Status checks (November 2026)

- Vuurtoreneiland: restaurant runs (summer glasshouse mid-May to September, winter in the fort);
  chef Benny Blisto (Restaurant Bak) took over the kitchen from summer 2026 (Amsterdam Now).
  The light has been off since the end of 2016 (nl.wikipedia).
- De Durgerdam: open as a 14-room hotel with restaurant De Mark since 2023 (Michelin, Aedes).
- Finse school: no longer a school; part of it was sold as a family home (funda listing for
  no. 68-B). Not a listed monument.
- Schellingwouderbrug: in use; no 2026 works found. Malfunction story from NH Nieuws is undated.
- Ransdorp tower: I found no regular opening hours for 2026, only Open Toren Dag (June 2025).
  Text says it opens on occasional days. Worth confirming (Facebook page RansdorperToren).
- De Wegwijzer (Ransdorp): I could not confirm whether it still works as a school; the text avoids
  saying.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- Raadhuis Ransdorp: national register and nl.wikipedia say 1652; the city text on the church
  (200463) says "uit 1625". Used 1652. The architect (Pieter Pietersz van Saerdam) comes only from
  the city text and a secondary page, so it is marked "attributed".
- De Wegwijzer: city text 200702 (and year_from in the slice) say 1915; the national register text
  says 1897 (teacher's house) and 1921–1922 (school). Used the national register.
- Bandstands: national register dates Ransdorp's to about 1920; the city text for Schellingwoude's
  says about 1910, possibly a decade later. Used "about 1920" and "about 1910–1925".
- Durgerdam's Kapel: DBNL says the former town hall (no. 101) dates from 1678 (gable stone);
  nl.wikipedia says 1687. The text names it without a year.
- Kinselmeer: nl.wikipedia says it formed in the 1421 flood and grew in 1825 when the Stenen Beer
  washed away. The scout could not confirm the 1421 origin, so I left it out; the 1825 enlargement
  appears in two nl.wikipedia articles (Kinselmeer, Ransdorp) but I found no independent source.
  Consider cutting it if a stricter standard is wanted.
- Vuurtoreneiland lighthouse: a rijksmonument since 1981 per nl.wikipedia, but I could not find its
  register number, so no heritage fields were set. Coordinates are the island address
  (Vuurtoreneiland 2A), not the tower itself.
- De Durgerdam "since 1664" and "Prince Hendrik was a regular" come from the owner's (Aedes) page.

## Notable gaps in the input

- Durgerdam has many more rijksmonuments (church 1840, the Kapel/former town hall no. 101,
  17th-century timber-frame houses, the bandstand) that are not in this slice or any other Noord
  slice. Only nos. 195 and 206 came through. Worth a separate Durgerdam ensemble pass.
- The Oranjesluizen (1870–1872, by Schellingwoude) are not in the slice; only mentioned in the
  bridge text.
