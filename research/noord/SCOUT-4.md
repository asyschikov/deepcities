## Sweep 4: Hidden history

### Noord-Oost (with the Waterland villages)

301. **Goudriaankanaal, the king's unfinished ship canal**; a pair of parallel ditches west of the Kinselmeer along the cycle path past bus stop "Ransdorp, Kinselmeer", pin on the ditch at 52.401764, 5.017974 (Noord-Oost, Waterland; also checked at bridge 2462 "Kanaalpost" on the Bloemendalergouw, 52.396175, 5.010690); in 1823 the engineer A.F. Goudriaan won King Willem I's contest with a plan to dam the IJ between Durgerdam and Diemen and dig a 150 m wide sea canal through Waterland, the Gouwzee and Marken, bypassing the Pampus shallows; the Tweede Kamer rejected the budget almost unanimously, so the king started digging anyway from secret funds (the Amortisatiesyndicaat), a crisis between king and parliament; Amsterdam fought it, offered to build the Westerdok and Oosterdok instead, and showed that the water off Marken was only 3.30 m deep, as shallow as the Pampus bar; a royal decree stopped canal and dam on 10 March 1828; the Bloemendalergouw, the first road between Ransdorp and Holysloot, was laid on the canal's dike, which is why it curves the way it does; the ditches got sloping nature banks in the 1990s and the line still shows on aerial photos; a failed national megaproject you can still read in the polder, mentioned nowhere on the map; https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/een-onzichtbaar-kanaal-door-landelijk-noord (April 2026), https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goudriaankanaal , https://www.hollandroute.nl/?p=1468 , https://holysloot.nl/verkeer-en-vervoer/ , MIP Wijk 15 https://020apps.nl/mip/beschrijvingen/Wijk%2015%20Amsterdam.pdf ; little to see beyond two ditches and a reed edge, no path along them (glimpsed from the cycle path, the Durgerdammergouw, the Bloemendalergouw and Dorpsstraat Holysloot); start of digging 1825 (Hollandroute) or 1826 (Wikipedia, Ons Amsterdam); the secret funds and the Westerdok trade are in nl.wikipedia only; the 150 m width is Hollandroute only; **Nice**; new place (alternative: a paragraph in "Kinselmeer and the Durgerdam dike"). Found independently by two agents in two sweeps.

302. **Schellingwoude's church stones sunk in the IJ, 1573**; in May 1573 the rebels tried a second time to cut Amsterdam off from the sea by sinking ships just west of Schellingwoude, ballasted, as H.M. de Frémery showed (Jaarboek Amstelodamum 1925), largely with gravestones and blocks broken out of the Schellingwoude church; the Spanish fleet sailed over the wrecks anyway while the rebels watched from the Schellingwouder Schans; in 2022 archaeologists sonar-scanned the IJ between the KNSM-eiland and Schellingwoude for the blockade, nothing found yet; https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/hier-gebeurde-het-het-ij-begin-mei-1573 and https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/309938/zoektocht-naar-scheepswrakken-uit-1572-in-het-ij-machtig-mooi-als-we-deze-stukken-vinden (19 October 2022); nothing visible on land; the existing text dates the burning of the village to 1572, check the two fit; status of the search after 2022 unknown; **Okay**; merge into "Schellingwouderkerk and bandstand".

303. **Durgerdam's two bells and the war**; the church bell by Pieter Hemony (1672) was spared because on 20 April 1940 the heritage inspector Jan Kalf hung a notice beside it in four languages declaring it a monument, and a large letter M was put on it; the Kapel's bell (Gerard Koster, 1689, paid for by the lottery after the 1687 fire) was taken by the Germans on 13 January 1943, found in Groningen after the war and brought back; https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/getuigen-van-bewogen-dorpsgeschiedenis (village historian Dick Reedijk, 2016); the 1689 inscription is confirmed by a Stadsarchief drawing caption; the wartime story is single-source; the same article dates the Kapel to 1499, which the notes already treat as unconfirmed (leave out); **Okay**; merge into "Dorpskerk Durgerdam" (the Hemony bell) and "De Kapel, former town hall of Durgerdam" (1943 and the return).

304. **"Ons Genoegen", the Kuinre fisherman's house**; Durgerdammerdijk 30-31; a three-door house built in 1919 by Cor Harder, a fisherman who moved here when Kuinre ended up inland behind the new polder; his Durgerdam-built schouw (flat-bottomed fishing boat) was bought back by the family around 2020 and lies at the jetty again; https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/de-vaste-route-van-hennie-koopman-door-durgerdam (September 2021); single source, private house, boat status after 2021 unknown; **Okay** at most; merge into "Durgerdammerdijk, west end (nos. 37–63)" or "Durgerdam" (one sentence), or drop.

## Sweep 6: Modern and contested

### Oud-Noord

305. **Gentiaanbuurt, the shipping line's garden village**; Resedastraat 26, 52.389697, 4.914116 (Oud-Noord, Volewijck); about ten closed blocks (some 340 homes) around the Gentiaanplein, wedged between the Van der Pekbuurt and the Mosveld, designed by A.D.N. van Gendt and G.H. Kleinhout (block 5 dated 1928) for the Stoomvaartmaatschappij Nederland; the flats don't stack but interlock, so up to three stairwells pass through one home, a fire risk that drove the renovation; block 5 (Hooyschuur, 2019) became the first gas-free block in Amsterdam's existing housing, and blocks 6-10 (Ibelings van Tilburg, finished November 2023) got inside insulation and their 1920s colours back, with most tenants returning (AAP 2025 nominee); a shipping company's housing hidden behind a better-known neighbour, and the lead COVERAGE.md asked for; https://gebouwdin.amsterdam.nl/api/projectdetail/3366 and https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/gentiaanbuurt-blok-6-10/ ; private homes; "first gas-free" is the project team's own claim; Arcam calls it a rijksmonument, other sources a protected cityscape (treat as cityscape); pin is 76 m from "Former Wingerdschool and Resedaschool"; **Okay**; new place (alternative: a paragraph in "Van der Pekbuurt, the garden village that was not demolished" as its neighbour; round B's draft wrongly put it inside the Van der Pekbuurt, the live text does not).

### Noord-West

306. **The navy's powder-magazine guardhouse and Villa Friekens, Kadoelen**; corner of Landsmeerderdijk and Kadoelenweg, about Landsmeerderdijk 189-193, 52.410222, 4.906680 (Noord-West, Kadoelen); in 1879 the navy put a gunpowder magazine of the Stelling van Amsterdam at the foot of the dike, and its brick guardroom of 1891 on the dike survives as three joined houses; the depot was sold in 1925, the magazine pulled down around 1980; the fan maker H.T. Kiekens worked on the site until 2001, after which it was squatted for 13 years as "Villa Friekens", a live-work place for artists and musicians, until the squatters left without a fight in November 2015; the Klein Kadoelen houses (2019-2020) now stand there, their lanes named after the 1631 inn Groene Ridder and the polder mill 't Haasje; a military relic nobody notices plus the squat story that round B left as a lead; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadoelen_(Amsterdam) and https://nltimes.nl/2015/08/07/amsterdam-noord-artist-squat-close-november ; TracesOfWar has an entry (seen as a snippet only); the house numbers of the guardhouse are not confirmed (`check: Location`); the squat is gone, the guardhouse is what you see; **Nice**; new place (200 m from "Gemaal Kadoelen and the Concertgemaal").

307. **Sint-Rosaschool, Joop van Stigt's double cross**; Schoenerstraat 7, 52.404015, 4.909643 (Noord-West, Banne Buiksloot); a former girls' domestic-science school (designed 1969, built 1971-1972), textbook structuralism: two crosses set against each other around an octagonal entrance court, a figure-of-eight route between two skylit courts, bare concrete columns with stylised capitals, sweeping concrete spiral stairs; Arcam (Heemschut text) says it "is weinig onder de aandacht gekomen"; its last user, Xplore Agora, left in summer 2025 and its future is unknown; the map already has Van Stigt's work in Zuidoost; https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/sint-rosaschool-noord/ ; single source; closed, vacancy after 2025 per Arcam only; not to be confused with the 1929-1930 St. Rosaschool in "Sint Rita complex (BUNK)"; **Okay** (Nice if a demolition fight starts); new place.

310. **Diereneiland's buurtkamer, Molenwijk**; Molenwijk 11, 52.416415, 4.887631 (Noord-West, Oostzanerwerf); a neighbourhood hut (Archivolt, 2022) between the children's farm and the playground, designed with Molenwijk's children's council: a slide comes out of the building and there is a lookout on the roof, built of recycled concrete; https://gebouwdin.amsterdam.nl/api/projectdetail/3924 ; **Okay**; merge into "Molenwijk and its art route" (Look for line).

### Noord-Oost

308. **IJdoornlaanbrug, the "Bridge of Sighs"**; IJdoornlaan over the Noordhollandsch Kanaal by the BovenIJ hospital, 52.404839, 4.925074 (Noord-Oost, Elzenhagen; the west end is in Banne Buiksloot); steel bascule bridge 970, designed in 1975 by D.L. Sterenberg, the last staff bridge designer of Publieke Werken; it crosses the canal at 68 degrees, so deck, portal, counterweight and lifting rods are all lopsided, unlike any other moving bridge in Amsterdam (Heemschut); balance problems delayed its opening until September 1983 and earned it the nickname "Brug der Zuchten"; colour-coded steel (blue railings, red lifting arms, brown and yellow), and the parallelogram bridge-keeper's house on the pier is now a SWEETS hotel suite; the peak of a bridge designer whose other work is on the map in West and Nieuw-West, and a passer-by sees the skew; https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/ijdoornlaanbrug/ (Heemschut text) ; design history single-source; SWEETS suite in use in 2026 not confirmed; on a busy road; **Nice**; new place (182 m from "War memorials at De Nieuwe Noorder").

309. **De Nieuwe Noorder's mourning pavilions**; Buikslotermeerdijk 83 (Noord-Oost, Elzenhagen); Dok Architecten (2012) added small timber pavilions with lopsided pointed roofs beside the 1930s reception building, linked by a dead-straight glass-roofed route; the aula stands in a pond and a glass strip at the top of its 7 m roof keeps the sky in view; after the service the doors open and mourners follow the coffin straight to the grave; https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/kantoor-en-rouwcentrum-de-nieuwe-noorder/ (AAP 2013 nominee); **Okay**; merge into "War memorials at De Nieuwe Noorder (Fokker bombing, Draka)".

## Sweep 7: Curiosities, green corners and odd museums

### Oud-Noord

311. **Noorderparkkamer, the Swiss "parasite" pavilion, and its Marktplaats bar**; Floraparkweg 1, Noorderpark, pin on the timber pavilion 52.395058, 4.920506 (Oud-Noord, Volewijck); the wooden pavilion with a sliding wall that opens it into a stage is by the Zurich architects Marcel Meili and Markus Peter: first designed around 1991 as a craftsman's workshop ("Haus Hodel"), shown about ten years later as a readymade at the Dutch "Parasite" exhibition, built in 2001-2002 as a community pavilion in a Rotterdam satellite town due for redevelopment, and later moved to the Florapark, where from 2007 it was Noord's "cultural living room" (neighbours married in it); in 2012 Overtreders W and bureau SLA added a crowdfunded bar billed as the first building in the Netherlands made entirely from Marktplaats purchases: three stripped temporary-hospital units, 42 second-hand windows, wood partly from a crate that shipped a milk-carton machine from China, shutters from a bankrupt formwork factory charred the Japanese way (Dutch Design Awards and DOEN Materiaalprijs nominations, Dutch Architecture Yearbook 2013); the foundation stopped in 2017, and after repairs it has had a run of café operators, now Noorderpark Café (February 2026); travelling Swiss architecture and crisis-era DIY building hiding in plain sight as a park café; https://www.meilipeterpartner.ch/projekte/parasite-house-rotterdam , https://arcam.nl/architectuur-gids/noorderpark/ , https://www.overtreders-w.nl/noorderparkbar , https://bureausla.nl/nl/projects/noorderparkbar/ , https://noorderpark.nl/parkberichten/stichting-noorderparkkamer-stopt-einde-tijdperk/ ; ### Noord-Oost

312. **Noord's allotment belt: parks pushed out of Oost**; the six parks on the Volkstuinwandelroute Boven IJ (Tuinwijck, De Molen, Rust en Vreugd, Wijkergouw, Kweeklust, Buikslotermeer, about 12 km, posts with blue tops) are largely refugees from Oost: De Molen was founded on 11 January 1939 on the Oostelijke Ringdijk by a demolished windmill (hence the name), moved here in 1980 and was opened in 1981 by alderman Jan Schaefer; Rust en Vreugd, founded in 1930 in the Watergraafsmeer, moved here 1973-1978; Wijkergouw's clubhouse "Arbeid Adelt" is a stolp farm that burned down in 1993 and was rebuilt by members; Kweeklust (since 1936) is open April to October with a kingfisher wall and a path of tree stumps; https://tuinparkdemolen.nl/ , https://wijkergouw.nl/organisatie/ , https://www.tuinparkkweeklust.nl/wandelroute/nederlands , https://vrijetijdamsterdam.nl/listings/volkstuinen/tuinpark-rust-en-vreugd/ ; each park's history rests on its own club site; Rust en Vreugd's site has lapsed; pins for reference only: De Molen 52.389678, 4.971157, Rust en Vreugd 52.384333, 4.966713, Wijkergouw 52.387084, 4.963312 (all Noord-Oost, Waterland); **Okay**; merge into "Tuinpark Tuinwijck, the allotments that kept moving" (turns its single story into a pattern).

### Noord-Oost

313. **Pontje Holysloot, the ring-the-bell ferry**; Holysloter Die, Holysloot landing by the ferry house at Dorpsstraat 6, 52.413936, 5.022095 (Noord-Oost, Waterland; north landing 52.415009, 5.022356, also in Noord); Holysloot is a dead end except when the ferry runs; four generations of the Fockens/John family ran it from Dorpsstraat 6-8: Cornelis Fockens rowed people over for 2.5 cents, from 1925 with 250 guilders a year from the city; his son Willem rowed all year from 7:00 to 22:00 and quit in 1960 when Amsterdam refused to raise his subsidy from 2,000 to 3,000 guilders, leaving the village with a detour of at least 8 km to church, school and the Broek market for years; Staatsbosbeheer revived it in the late 1960s, a motorboat replaced the rowboat in 1976, the present pontje dates from 1994; Bart John was ferryman from age 16 in 1986 until the new neighbours at no. 6 took over in 2025 and began restoring the old "overhaal" (crossing) sign seen on a 1922 Stadsarchief photo; you still ring a bell on a rope; it carries about 8,000 people a year to the old farm path to the Poppendammergouw and Broek in Waterland; a living village institution and the only way north out of Holysloot on foot; today one closing sentence in "Holysloot and the 1916 flood (former Reformed church)"; https://www.pontjeholysloot.nl/geschiedenis , https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-noord/nieuws/442215/pontje-van-holysloot-terug-van-stapel-met-nieuwe-veerman (18 April 2025), https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-noord/nieuws/432862/het-kan-spoken-op-het-holysloter-die (20 February 2025), https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holysloter_Die ; seasonal: weekends and holidays 9:00-17:00, daily in July and August, from 1 April (ferry site, 2026) or 15 April (Rodi, 2025) to 1 October; fares differ (site 2026: 1 euro on foot, 2 with a bike; Rodi 2025: 1.50); sources disagree on Queen Beatrix in 1991 (ferry site: carried free; Rodi: made to pay), so `check: Facts` or leave the anecdote out; not a hand ferry, and "smallest ferry in the world" appears only in a 2016 travel blog (leave out); 46 m from "Hay barn farm, Dorpsstraat Holysloot 12"; **Nice**; new place (alternative: merge into "Holysloot and the 1916 flood (former Reformed church)").

314. **Schellingwouderpark's pull ferries and the lock-keepers' row**; west ferry 52.384404, 4.953615, east ferry 52.383296, 4.960817 (both Noord-Oost, Waterland); the walk along the IJ meadows to Schellingwoude crosses two small self-service pull ferries ("trekpontjes"); the east one lands at the Noorder IJdijk, a row built to house the Oranjesluizen staff (five detached houses and two workers' rows) and one of only two streets still called "IJdijk"; the park (2010, about 10 ha) has kingfisher walls and winter spots for grass snakes, northern voles and dragonflies; a do-it-yourself crossing on a corner most Amsterdammers never find; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-noord/201220/-wandelen-in-noord-naar-de-oranjesluizen (12 April 2021), https://www.komoot.com/highlight/5663598 (June 2023), https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schellingwouderpark , https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noorder_IJdijk ; latest evidence both ferries work is June 2023 (`check: Still there?`); the Oranjesluizen themselves are in borough Oost; round B dropped the park as a whole, the ferries and the row are new; **Okay**; new place (pin the west ferry, which fills an empty grid cell).

### Noord-West

315. **Kinderboerderij De Buiktuin on the concrete works' rubble**; Noordhollandschkanaaldijk 19, 52.402060, 4.922860 (Noord-West, Banne Buiksloot), 72 m from the mill; residents started it in 1972 as Noord's last farms disappeared, on the site of the Van Baarsen concrete works: the city spread earth over the rubble, which is why the pasture is still lumpy; Aart Staartjes opened it in 1975; Harry Slinger of the band Drukwerk donated a pig someone had given him at a gig; Van Baarsen also links it to "Paard van Noord"; https://www.kinderboerderijdebuiktuin.com/history/ (its own site, single source) and iamsterdam (location, opening); open daily 10:00-16:00, a new goat barn is planned from December 2026 (possible temporary closures); **Okay**; merge into "Krijtmolen d'Admiraal" (a paragraph and a Look for line: the lumpy pasture).
