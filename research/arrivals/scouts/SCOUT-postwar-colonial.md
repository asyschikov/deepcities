# Scout: City of Arrivals, slice `postwar-colonial` (1945 to now, from the former colonies)

Scope: Indische Nederlanders and repatriates, Moluccans, Chinese-Indonesians, Surinamese (the 1975 wave, the Bijlmer), Antilleans and Arubans, postwar Chinese, slavery remembrance (Keti Koti, NiNsee). Existence checked with `pnpm -s dc places sights --all --q <word>`. Nothing written to the live site, nothing committed.

Key authoritative sources used throughout: Stadsarchief themasite [Amsterdam Migratiestad](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/), Amsterdam Museum [Samangat! Moluks in Amsterdam](https://www.amsterdammuseum.nl/verhalen-en-collecties/themes/samangat-moluks-in-amsterdam/verhalen), the city's [75 jaar Molukkers](https://www.amsterdam.nl/sociaaldomein/diversiteit/75-jaar-molukkers/) page, Ons Amsterdam (read via BrowserOS, targeted).

Coordination: the 1800-1945 scout explicitly left Ons Suriname / Suriname Museum, the Cotton Club and the Bijlmer De Kom monument to this slice; it keeps Reestraat 6, the Binnen Bantammerstraat and the Colonial Institute facade.

## Shortlist (by strength)

1. **National Slavery Monument and Keti Koti** (Oosterpark, south-west corner; 2002) (E: "National Slavery Monument and Keti Koti", rnukdpl6s0wlpxykmtk08d2q): the national monument to the abolition of 1863 and, every 1 July, the place where Surinamese and Antillean Amsterdammers lead the Keti Koti commemoration; at the 2002 unveiling descendants were kept outside the fences. Who: Afro-Surinamese and Afro-Caribbean Amsterdammers. Era: 2002 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; [Ons Amsterdam, Oosterpark 1 juli 2002](https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/oosterpark-1-juli-2002-onthulling-slavernijmonument); [Koninklijke Verzamelingen, moments of the monument](https://www.koninklijkeverzamelingen.nl/verhalen/belangrijke-momenten-van-het-nationaal-monument-slavernijverleden). Caveats: existing text is strong; a short arrival paragraph only (who carries the day, why Amsterdam).

2. **Gliphoeve, the Bijlmer flats Surinamese activists squatted** (Geldershoofd and Gravestein; 1974 to 1975) (E: "Gliphoeve, the Bijlmer flats Surinamese activists squatted", bltlk8q7ofxahychj8fo1vh8): when housing quotas kept Surinamese out of whole neighbourhoods and families were stuck in pensions, activists squatted empty honeycomb flats in 1974; by the mid-1970s about 2,100 people lived in Gliphoeve I, nearly 90% Surinamese, and the Bijlmer became the arrival district of the 1975 wave. Who: Surinamese. Era: 1974 to now. Status: changed (Gliphoeve I demolished; Gliphoeve II renamed Geldershoofd). Evidence: [Stadsarchief, 1975 Uittocht na Surinaamse onafhankelijkheid](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1975-uittocht-surinaamse/); [AT5, Na 1975 werd de Bijlmer het nieuwe thuis voor Surinamers](https://www.at5.nl/artikelen/235661/na-1975-werd-de-bijlmer-het-nieuwe-thuis-voor-surinamers-we-hebben-het-zelf-gedaan); [Volkskrant, Hoe de Bijlmer de Bijlmer werd](https://www.volkskrant.nl/nieuws-achtergrond/hoe-de-bijlmer-de-bijlmer-werd-het-verhaal-van-de-voormalige-gliphoeve-bewoners~bff251d4/). Caveats: the "Bijlmermeer" place (et0awp08hhog3xeyhfwv3crm) could carry the general 1975 arrival paragraph instead; pick one as the anchor.

3. **Suriname Museum and Ons Suriname** (Zeeburgerdijk 19-23; association 1919, building since 1970s) (E: "Suriname Museum in the 1882 double school", nmmp3r0a58nbohv69ztzjvjq): home of the oldest Surinamese association in the Netherlands; here the Surinaams Historisch Museum opened in 1997 (closed 2005), The Black Archives grew from 2016, and Otto and Hermina Huiswoud have a prominent place at the entrance. Who: Surinamese. Era: 1919 to now. Status: open (museum; Black Archives back from Bijlmerplein 140 in 2026). Evidence: [Stadsarchief, 1997 Surinaams Historisch Museum](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1997-surinaams-historisch-museum/); [DutchCulture, Society Our Suriname](https://dutchculture.nl/en/location/society-our-suriname); [Parool, Otto en Hermina Huiswoud](https://www.parool.nl/nieuws/otto-en-hermina-huiswoud-vroege-strijders-tegen-kolonialisme~b65a2eb0/); [The Black Archives returns to Oost](https://www.theblackarchives.nl/the-black-archives-keert-terug-naar-oost-in-april-2026.html). Caveats: sources differ on when the association moved in (1973 in a UvA chapter, 1975 in our text).

4. **Javakade arrival tiles** (Javakade, Java-eiland; ships of April and May 1951, tiles May 2026) (N; could also hang on E "Zeeman op de uitkijk and the head of the Java-eiland", nk0m8e7ajdmdq0vht0rpaivf): four ships (New Australia, Groote Beer, Somersetshire, Asturias) brought Moluccan ex-KNIL soldiers and their families to this quay by the Stoomvaart Maatschappij Nederland office; in May 2026 four memorial tiles with the ships and arrival dates, designed by Lars Bogaers (SNARS), were set in the pavement. Who: Moluccans. Era: 1951. Status: open (tiles; a permanent monument is planned inside the future Slavernijmuseum). Evidence: [Gemeente Amsterdam, 75 jaar Molukkers (Gedenktegels)](https://www.amsterdam.nl/sociaaldomein/diversiteit/75-jaar-molukkers/); [Amsterdam Museum, Javakade: Aankomst in Amsterdam](https://www.amsterdammuseum.nl/verhalen-en-collecties/themes/samangat-moluks-in-amsterdam/javakade). Caveats: the Amsterdam Museum page totals "about 4,600" but its ship figures add to roughly 4,100; quote per ship, not the total. Exact tile spot to confirm on site.

5. **Beta berlayar jauh, the Moluccan marines' monument** (Marineterrein, Kattenburgerstraat 5; 2026) (E: "Beta berlayar jauh, the Moluccan marines' monument", xfz0lp41wyazu32abhv8il7y): unveiled on 29 April 2026, 75 years after the first ship, for the Moluccan marines stationed at the Kattenburg barracks in 1951; the soldiers were discharged soon after arrival and their families sent to camps. Who: Moluccans. Era: 1951 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; [Gemeente Amsterdam, 75 jaar Molukkers](https://www.amsterdam.nl/sociaaldomein/diversiteit/75-jaar-molukkers/); [Amsterdam Museum, Ton Louhenapessy en Marine Molukkers](https://www.amsterdammuseum.nl/verhalen-en-collecties/themes/samangat-moluks-in-amsterdam/ton-louhenapessy). Caveats: existing text already has the arrival story.

6. **Hotel Jakarta, on the old Indies passenger quay** (Javakade 766; quay used to 1960s, hotel 2018) (E: "Hotel Jakarta", pui8gmz7znjzym8jscmd8zgo): the tip of the Java-eiland is where the SMN liners left for and returned from the Indies, so repatriates and the 1951 Moluccans landed alongside; the Kop van Java is now the chosen site for the Nationaal Slavernijmuseum (opening delayed to 2033), which already runs a small Moluccan presentation in the hotel. Who: Indische repatriates, Moluccans; slavery remembrance. Era: 1946 to now. Status: changed (now hotel). Evidence: [Amsterdam750, Java-eiland en Hotel Jakarta](https://amsterdam750.nl/verhaal/van-golfbreker-tot-ontmoetingsplek-het-verhaal-van-java-eiland-en-hotel-jakarta/); [Gemeente Amsterdam, Nationaal Slavernijmuseum](https://www.amsterdam.nl/nationaal-slavernijmuseum/); [Parool, opening delayed to 2033](https://www.parool.nl/amsterdam/opening-nationaal-slavernijmuseum-vertraagd-tot-2033-gemeente-zoekt-naar-tijdelijke-locatie~b46513e8/). Caveats: existing text is about the garden; needs the arrival paragraph. Don't double-count with no. 4: tiles = Moluccans, hotel = the quay and the museum to come.

7. **Anton de Kom monument** (Anton de Komplein; 2006) (E: "Anton de Kom monument", gpqpa6wg4twhxhjy8fsvaq77): the Surinamese writer of Wij slaven van Suriname, honoured in the heart of Surinamese Amsterdam; at the 2006 unveiling opponents covered it with the Surinamese flag, and the debate about how he is shown still runs. Who: Surinamese. Era: 2006 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; [4 en 5 mei, monument voor Anton de Kom](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/zoeken/939/amsterdam-monument-voor-anton-de-kom); [NPO Blend, de langlopende strijd om Anton de Kom](https://www.npoblend.nl/nieuws/een-waardig-beeld-de-langlopende-strijd-om-anton-de-kom). Caveats: pair with Reestraat 6 (1800-1945 slice) for the before/after.

8. **Levensboom, Monument van Besef** (Surinameplein; 2003) (E: "Levensboom, Monument van Besef", mznh96x29f5wuldgqmlm6qqy): the community's own slavery memorial, where Surinamese Amsterdammers have marked the night before Keti Koti on 30 June since 1993. Who: Afro-Surinamese. Era: 1993 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; Stadscuratorium entry for the Levensboom (to add); [Koninklijke Verzamelingen](https://www.koninklijkeverzamelingen.nl/verhalen/belangrijke-momenten-van-het-nationaal-monument-slavernijverleden). Caveats: need one more authoritative link at write time.

9. **Nelson Mandelapark and Kwaku** (Nelson Mandelapark; 1975 to 2026) (E: "Nelson Mandelapark and Kwaku", ixmzhd415cly8a0avk7utsdo): Kwaku began in 1975, the year of independence, as a football tournament for Surinamese kids who could not afford a holiday and became the summer festival of Surinamese Amsterdam; 2026 was its last edition in this form. Who: Surinamese, later all diasporas of Zuidoost. Era: 1975 to now. Status: changed. Evidence: existing text; Kwaku coverage in Parool/AT5 (add at write time). Caveats: existing text already strong.

10. **Cotton Club, the Nieuwmarkt's Surinamese jazz café** (Nieuwmarkt 5; postwar) (E: "Cotton Club, the Nieuwmarkt's Surinamese jazz café", ztxusocyqxritry12i9dtpgw): the first haunt of the small postwar Surinamese community and of Black American soldiers, named after the Surinamese trumpeter Teddy Cotton. Who: Surinamese. Era: late 1940s onward. Status: open (café). Evidence: existing text; [Stadsarchief, Jazz-jaren](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/agenda/jazz-jaren-1/) (for Cotton's 1930s presence). Caveats: the cluster "Around the Nieuwmarkt, migrant Amsterdam" (q1ogooimbt808nocosyfc1nu) duplicates; list single places, not both.

11. **La Cubana, Max Woiski's club** (Leidsestraat 52; 1942 to 1958) (N): the Surinamese singer Max Woiski Sr. opened his club in May 1942 and brought calypso and Caribbean music to the Leidseplein crowd, with a painted starry dome "as if on the Waterkant in Paramaribo" and his own brown beans and rice on busy nights; his son, who arrived on the quay in 1950, later ran La Tropicana. Who: Surinamese. Era: 1942 to 1958. Status: gone (building there; current use to check). Evidence: [Ons Amsterdam, Bruine bonen met rijst in de Leidsestraat](https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/bruine-bonen-met-rijst-in-de-leidsestraat); [Parool, Max Woiski](https://www.parool.nl/kunst-media/max-woiski-van-de-muziek-naar-de-showbusiness~bfc9d91d/); [Data Amsterdam, Woiskistraat](https://data.amsterdam.nl/openbare-ruimten/0363300011952042). Caveats: opened in wartime; straddles the 1800-1945 slice (not on their list). Data Amsterdam says he played a club "in the thirties", Ons Amsterdam says opened 1942: use Ons Amsterdam's date.

12. **Gunung Batu, the Moluccan Evangelical church** (Jacques Perkstraat 1, Slotermeer; since 1993) (N): Amsterdam has no Moluccan neighbourhood, so the church is the community's fixed point; the Geredja Indjili Maluku congregation took over the 1960s New Apostolic church here in 1993. Who: Moluccans. Era: 1993 to now. Status: open. Evidence: [Gemeente Amsterdam, 75 jaar Molukse geschiedenis](https://www.amsterdam.nl/nieuws/achtergrond/75-jaar-molukse-geschiedenis/); [nl.wikipedia, Lijst van kerken in Amsterdam](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_kerken_in_Amsterdam); [Nederlands Dagblad, Amsterdamse kerk houdt Molukse cultuur levend](https://www.nd.nl/geloof/geloof/1167099/kerk-houdt-molukse-cultuur-levend-we-mogen-trots-zijn-op-onze). Caveats: the church's own Facebook marks 25 years "in March", consistent with 1993; build year of the church not yet found.

13. **Tjalie Robinson's Slotermeer: Hugo Verriesthof** (Hugo Verriesthof 4; 1954 to 1962) (N): after Jakarta and two pensions, the Indo writer Tjalie Robinson (Jan Boon) settled in a still-unfinished Slotermeer street where many Indische repatriates were housed; he wrote about one coal stove for five rooms and the toko round the corner, and argued for keeping Indisch culture against the demand to assimilate. Who: Indische Nederlanders. Era: 1954 to 1962. Status: changed (ordinary housing). Evidence: [Stadsarchief, 1954 Van Jakarta naar Nieuw-West](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1954-jakarta-nieuw-west/); [Ons Amsterdam, Indisch Amsterdam: Teruggekeerd (Lucassen)](https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/indisch-amsterdam-teruggekeerd); [Indische Letteren via DBNL](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_ind004201301_01/_ind004201301_01.pdf) (gives no. 4). Caveats: no plaque known; a house-of-a-famous-newcomer item. Could become the Slotermeer anchor for "tens of thousands of repatriates in Nieuw-West".

14. **Toko Ramee** (Ferdinand Bolstraat 74; August 1961) (N): Oen Poo Kwie and Oei Hwie Nio, a Chinese-Indonesian family whose relatives ran big restaurants on Java, opened this toko opposite the Albert Cuyp in 1961, naming it after her parents' shop in Salatiga; the grandmother of another family had run restaurant Djokja in the same street since 1954. Who: Chinese-Indonesians, Indische Nederlanders. Era: 1961 to now. Status: open. Evidence: [Chinese Indonesian Heritage Center, Van Toko Oen naar Toko Ramee](https://cihc.nl/foto-met-verhaal/74-van-toko-oen-naar-toko-ramee-deel-2-2/); [ONH, 'Toen die toko kwam...' Caveats: new owner since 1998; Djokja is from a family blog (keasberry.com), weak.

15. **Toko Dun Yong** (Stormsteeg 9; 1950s) (E: "Toko Dun Yong, the Chinese shop that grew out of a button shop", qv461jmu4hj8ffcmy2hvlscm): a former ship's stoker from Guangdong and his Rotterdam wife started the city's oldest Chinese toko by the Zeedijk; it grew into a five-storey store as Chinatown moved here from the Binnen Bantammerstraat. Who: Surinamese Hindustani Hindus. Era: 1997 to now. Status: open (temporary space). Evidence: [Ons Amsterdam, Het huis dat Sebo bouwde](https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/het-huis-dat-sebo-bouwde-vijftig-jaar-indonesisch-restaurant-sama-sebo); [Parool, Rieka Wattimena (1937-2018)](https://www.parool.nl/nieuws/rieka-wattimena-1937-2018-was-altijd-druk-in-de-keuken~b91f3ca8/); [Parool 2019](https://www.parool.nl/ps/gejuich-in-sama-sebo-want-ze-gaan-niets-veranderen~b185ac49/). Caveats: Ons Amsterdam spells Wattinema, Parool Wattimena: use Parool. Founder is Dutch-born: the arrival angle is the Indies kitchen and its Moluccan staff.

30. **Monumentale Voorouders Boom** (H-buurt, Zuidoost; 2000, site reopened 8 May 2026) (N): a living slavery monument, planted in 2000 by Stichting Memre Den to honour the ancestors who resisted; the stadsdeel calls it Zuidoost's first slavery monument; mosaic tiles made by schoolchildren name freedom fighters, and a plaque was added in 2026. Who: Afro-Surinamese and Afro-Caribbean Zuidoost. Era: 2000 to now. Status: open. Evidence: [Memre Den](https://www.memreden.com/); [Zuidoost&Meer, opening heringericht terrein](https://www.zuidoostenmeer.nl/nieuws/algemeen/16575/opening-heringericht-terrein-rondom-monumentale-voorouders-boom); [Stadsdeel Zuidoost on Facebook (official)](https://www.facebook.com/amsterdamzuidoost/posts/-wist-je-dat-er-een-bijzondere-boom-staat-in-de-h-buurtde-monumentale-voorouders/1158430889649346/). Caveats: exact spot in the H-buurt not found; must locate before pinning.

31. **Centrumeiland's anti-colonial street names** (Srefidensiplein and around, IJburg; named 2019) (N; Srefidensiplein has E "CPO Srefi Bow", yvotvixnz1htulngzttmr0ph, as a building only): 27 streets named after people from Suriname, the Antilles, Aruba and Indonesia who resisted colonialism and slavery, among them Otto and Hermina Huiswoud, Max Woiski Sr. and Jr., Virginia Dementricia of Aruba and Diponegoro; announced by the mayor at Keti Koti after KITLV research and NiNsee advice. Who: Surinamese, Antillean, Aruban, Indonesian heroes. Era: 2019. Status: open. Evidence: [Gemeenteblad 2019-173244](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2019-173244.pdf); [AT5, Wie zijn de verzetshelden achter de straatnamen](https://www.at5.nl/artikelen/196074/wie-zijn-de-verzetshelden-achter-de-straatnamen-van-het-nieuwe-centrumeiland); [Werkgroep Caraïbische Letteren](https://werkgroepcaraibischeletteren.nl/27-straten-vernoemd-naar-strijders-tegen-koloniale-overheersing/). Caveats: a "remembered" item, not a place newcomers used; borderline-strong.

32. **Albert Cuyp market, where Surinamese Amsterdam shopped** (Albert Cuypstraat) (E: "Albert Cuyp market", mwfaf5jchs4htezhpu4y09ue): after 1975 the market and its tokos were where you found Surinamese produce; Tjin's, a Surinamese-Chinese family shop, has been on the corner since 1981. Who: Surinamese, Surinamese-Chinese. Era: 1970s to now. Status: open. Evidence: [Geheugen van Oost, Surinaams koken in Nederland](https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/10192/surinaams-koken-in-nederland); [Flevolands Geheugen, Gezelligheid in De Pijp](https://www.flevolandsgeheugen.nl/page/5299/gezelligheid-in-de-pijp); [Tjin's Toko, about (official)](https://www.tjinstoko.com/pages/about). Caveats: memory sites plus a shop page: medium; an AT5 video exists on the same theme. Extra paragraph only.

33. **Warung Spang Makandra** (Gerard Doustraat 39; 1978) (N): a Javanese-Surinamese warung, "strong together" in Sranan, in De Pijp since 1978, one of the few places that tells the Javanese chapter of Surinamese migration. Who: Javanese Surinamese. Era: 1978 to now. Status: open. Evidence: [Spang Makandra (official)](https://spangmakandra.nl/); [De Buik](https://www.debuik.nl/amsterdam/restaurant/warung-spang-makandra); [Volkshotel blog 2016](https://www.volkshotel.nl/en/blog/hotspots/londons-tip-warung-spang-makandra/). Caveats: weak (no authoritative source; Instagram gives Gerard Doustraat 33). Food round rule: worth visiting without eating? Marginal.

34. **Oey Tjeng Sit's pharmacy** (Prinsengracht 128; 1958 to 1980) (N): a Chinese-Javanese pharmacist from Purwokerto, friend of W.F. Hermans, who showed his abstract collages in the shop window to puzzled Jordaan customers; work in the Stedelijk. Who: Chinese-Indonesian. Era: 1958 to 1980. Status: gone (now other use). Evidence: [Stadsarchief, 1953 Kunstenaar en apotheker Oey Tjeng Sit](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1953-kunstenaar-apotheker-oey-tjeng-sit/). Caveats: single (authoritative) source; came to study in 1938, so the arrival is prewar.

35. **The Indische HBS** (Karel du Jardinstraat, De Pijp; from 1946) (N): an "overbruggings-HBS" where Indische children who had spent the war in Japanese camps caught up after arrival; the building is now a Montessori primary school. Who: Indische Nederlanders. Era: 1946 to 1950s. Status: changed (now a school). Evidence: [Ons Amsterdam, Indisch Amsterdam: Teruggekeerd (Lucassen)](https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/indisch-amsterdam-teruggekeerd); [Java Post, Indisch Amsterdam](https://javapost.nl/2014/11/20/indisch-amsterdam/); [Canon LO, 'Indische HBS'](https://www.canonlo.nl/biografie/verdieping/51746). Caveats: house number unknown (Java Post says now "6de Montessori De Scholekster"; today's school is the 9e Montessori at no. 76); confirm before pinning.

## Report

- Counts: 35 items, 23 (E) and 12 (N). Borderline 17, dropped 9.
- Top ten: Slavery Monument and Keti Koti; Gliphoeve; Suriname Museum and Ons Suriname; Javakade arrival tiles; Beta berlayar jauh; Hotel Jakarta on the old Indies quay; Anton de Kom monument; La Cubana (Leidsestraat 52); Gunung Batu Moluccan church; Toko Ramee.
- Odd: the pool already has most of the Surinamese Bijlmer and Moluccan monuments, so the work is mostly extra paragraphs. Antilleans and Arubans are nearly invisible as places (only the empty urns on the Dam, the Centrumeiland street names and two gone youth/women's centres). Amsterdam has no Moluccan neighbourhood, so the church, the quay and the 2026 tiles carry the Moluccan story. The Devi Dhaam story is one of a temple that never got a building. The Indies repatriates (tens of thousands, more than The Hague by 2000 per Lucassen) left almost no marked places: Hugo Verriesthof, the Indische HBS and the tokos are the best I found.
