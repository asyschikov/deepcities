# SCOUT: jewish-arrivals (City of Arrivals)

Slice: Sephardic arrivals from Portugal and Spain, Ashkenazi arrivals from Germany and Poland, Eastern
European arrivals and transmigrants around 1900, German and Austrian refugees of the 1930s, and the
1945 return. Arrival stories only; the Jewish Heritage map already holds the rest.
Checked against `pnpm -s dc places sights --all` (full dump, 2,533 places) on 2026-11-25.
Sources: research YAMLs already cite registers and joodsamsterdam.nl for the (E) places. New checks done
through WebSearch and BrowserOS (nl.wikipedia, research.annefrank.org, DuckDuckGo).

## Shortlist (ordered by strength)

1. **Portuguese Synagogue (Esnoga) and the Ets Haim library** (Mr. Visserplein 3; 1671 to 1675) (E: "Portuguese Synagogue (Esnoga)", mgbfdpu4kchmnsvxlbpoc56l): the descendants of Jews forced to convert in Portugal and Spain, who came to Amsterdam from about 1600 and returned to Judaism openly here, built the largest synagogue in the world in their day. Their library, Ets Haim, is still in the complex. Who: Sephardic (Portuguese) Jews. Era: 1600 to 1700. Status: open (synagogue and museum, part of the Jewish Cultural Quarter). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 3716](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/3716), [JCK Portugese Synagoge](https://jck.nl/locatie/portugese-synagoge), [RCE book "The Portuguese Synagogue in Amsterdam"](https://english.cultureelerfgoed.nl/binaries/cultureelerfgoed-en/documenten/publications/2013/01/01/the-portuguese-synagogue-in-amsterdam/Portuguese-synagogue_EN_LR_web.pdf). Caveats: the live summary is all candles and wallpaper. The arrival paragraph is the main addition.

2. **Vlooienburg, the first Jewish island, and Spinoza's statue** (Amstel 7 / Zwanenburgwal, round the Stopera; from about 1600) (E: "Vlooienburg under the Stopera, the plaque that took thirty years, and Spinoza", qs04c1uh8vx5juadbuq9a6kf; cluster alt: "Jonas Daniël Meijerplein and Vlooienburg...", fh0mc9q8q9xkj6yovtkgia65): a timber-yard island made around 1593 where the first Portuguese newcomers rented houses. Their first prayer rooms were here: Beth Jacob, on the first floor of a house called Antwerpen on the Houtgracht, about 1602, then Neve Shalom and Beth Israel. Spinoza, son of a Portuguese merchant, was born at its edge. Who: Sephardic Jews, later the poor Ashkenazim. Era: 1600 to 1943. Status: gone (under the Stopera; plaque, model and Spinoza statue of 2008). Evidence: [Spinozaweb: Vlooienburg](https://spinozaweb.org/locations/3), [4en5mei Vlooienburgmonument](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/zoeken/4633/amsterdam-vlooienburgmonument), [Kunstwacht Spinoza Monument](https://amsterdam.kunstwacht.nl/kunstwerken/bekijk/546), [Wikipedia (nl) Vlooienburg](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlooienburg). Caveats: the founding date of Beth Jacob differs by source (1597 in older encyclopedias, about 1602 in Spinozaweb and recent scholarship). Use "around 1600".

3. **The Great Synagogue (Jewish Museum)** (Nieuwe Amstelstraat 1; 1671) (E: "Jewish Museum (Joods Museum), four synagogues in one", py728mskln2x7j728twnd5pg): Jews from German lands and then Poland came after the Sephardim, poorer and in larger numbers. By 1674 there were about 5,000 Ashkenazim against 2,500 Sephardim. Their Great Synagogue of 1671 stands opposite the Esnoga, and three more were added as the community grew. Who: Ashkenazi Jews from Germany and Poland. Era: 1635 to 1800. Status: changed (now the Jewish Museum). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 265](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/265), [JCK Joods Museum](https://jck.nl/locatie/joods-museum), [Wikipedia (nl) Grote Synagoge](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grote_Synagoge_(Amsterdam)), [Jewish Virtual Library (Amsterdam)](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/history-of-jews-in-amsterdam). Caveats: the separate Polish congregation (1660, forced to merge in 1673) can go in this text; its prayer house has no known site.

4. **Lloyd Hotel, the emigrants' hotel** (Oostelijke Handelskade 34; 1921) (E: "Lloyd Hotel and the Lloyd complex", qv0a05zf47i06yulvlmtq4dd): built by the Koninklijke Hollandsche Lloyd for emigrants bound for South America, mostly Jews leaving Eastern Europe, who were deloused next door before they could check in. In the 1930s it took in refugees. Who: Eastern European Jewish transmigrants, 1930s refugees. Era: 1921 to 1935. Status: changed (hotel again). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 523289](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/523289), [Wikipedia (nl) Lloyd Hotel](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Hotel_(hotel)), [Wikipedia (nl) KHL](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijke_Hollandsche_Lloyd). Caveats: the live summary already tells it. The emigrants passed through rather than settled, so frame it as transit.

5. **Merwedeplein, the refugees' square** (Merwedeplein 37; 1933 to 1942) (E: "Merwedeplein, the Frank family's square", gy73jsn1kntbnvgiwh1djw2o): the Franks came from Frankfurt in 1933 and lived here among other German refugees. A 1941 list counts 163 foreign Jews on this one square, and the Rivierenbuurt became the refugees' neighbourhood. Who: German and Austrian Jewish refugees. Era: 1933 to 1942. Status: open (no. 37 is now a refuge flat for persecuted writers). Evidence: [Joods Amsterdam: Merwedeplein](https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/merwedeplein/), [4en5mei Anne Frank statue](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/zoeken/2680/amsterdam-anne-frank-2), [Anne Frank House: Otto goes to the Netherlands](https://www.annefrank.org/annes-wereld/en/anne-frank/1933-emigration-netherlands/otto-goes-netherlands). Caveats: Anne's and Margot's schools are already their own places (see Borderline). Say so in the text and don't list them.

6. **Mozes en Aäronkerk, on the site of Spinoza's family home** (Waterlooplein 207) (E: "Mozes en Aäronkerk, the church named after two houses", u36dpf41xyomrrpcuh06gug5): Michael d'Espinosa, a Portuguese merchant, rented a house at the corner of the Houtgracht and Leprozengracht in the early 1650s. Baruch grew up there, round the corner from the synagogue that banned him in 1656. The church stands on the spot. It also links to the guest workers' slice: the 1975 Moroccan hunger strike was held inside. Who: Sephardic Jews. Era: 1600 to 1700. Status: changed (church, now an events venue). Evidence: [Spinozaweb: Houtgracht](https://spinozaweb.org/locations/5), [Rijksmonument 6305](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/6305). Caveats: Spinozaweb says his actual birth house is unknown. Say "family home", not "birthplace".

7. **Huis De Pinto** (Sint Antoniesbreestraat 69; bought 1651) (E: "Huis De Pinto, the house that stopped a motorway", iod6ugp3yos61fyy3gy0sdjf): the De Pinto family came from Lisbon by way of Antwerp and Rotterdam. Isaack de Pinto bought this house in 1651, and his son gave it its Italianate front. It is the grandest surviving trace of the Portuguese Jews' rise on the Breestraat. Who: Sephardic Jews. Era: 1600 to 1800. Status: changed (public library and reading room). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 5431](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/5431), [Wikipedia (en) Huis de Pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huis_de_Pinto), [Huis De Pinto](https://huisdepinto.nl/). Caveats: no source says they "fled" in 1651. Keep it as Lisbon via Antwerp, with the New Christian background in general terms.

8. **Rembrandthuis and his Portuguese neighbours** (Jodenbreestraat 4; 1639 to 1658) (E: "Rembrandthuis (Rembrandt House Museum)", y853cvdfiqbh4zi5qwky1nog): well-off Sephardim bought houses in Rembrandt's block on the Breestraat and became his direct neighbours. He rented cellar space to them, painted Dr. Ephraim Bueno, quarrelled with Daniel Pinto next door, and the Talmud Tora synagogue stood behind his house. Who: Sephardic Jews. Era: 1600 to 1700. Status: open (museum). Evidence: [Mirjam Knotter, "Rembrandt's Jewish neighbors" (open access)](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/oa-edit/10.5117/9789463728188-4/rembrandt-jewish-neighbors-stroll-neighborhood-mirjam-knotter), [Rijksmonument 2029](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/2029), [Jerusalem Post: Rembrandt and the Jews of Holland](https://m.jpost.com/jerusalem-report/rembrandt-and-the-jews-of-holland-578486). Caveats: avoid the 19th-century myth of Rembrandt as a friend of the Jews (curators dispute it). The claim that he sketched Menasseh ben Israel is weak. Stick to neighbours, leases and the Bueno portrait.

9. **Uilenburgersjoel and the Jewish island of the poor** (Nieuwe Uilenburgerstraat 91; 1766) (E: "Uilenburgersjoel, the synagogue behind the wall", e95bjuggykyeq3819ysm1y8o; cluster alt: "Uilenburg, the Jewish island...", ef47x04a03yndqqtiht5s5pl): poor Ashkenazi newcomers from Germany and Eastern Europe crowded onto Uilenburg. Their synagogue of 1766 is reached only through an alley, in a court the High German community owned. Who: Ashkenazi Jews. Era: 1700 to 1943. Status: open (events and a congregation). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 5798](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/5798), [Stichting Uilenburgersjoel](https://www.uilenburgersjoel.nl/herinneringen-van-morgen/), [Wikipedia (nl) Uilenburger Synagoge](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uilenburger_Synagoge). Caveats: list one of the two, not both.

10. **The Russensjoel, Nieuwe Kerkstraat 149** (1889) (E: "The Russensjoel and the old \"Jodenkerkstraat\" (Nieuwe Kerkstraat 149)", qgebug2xadhtttz8fvvzlgbl): Jews fleeing the Russian pogroms, some of whom stopped in Amsterdam on the way to America, opened their own prayer house here in 1889. Who: Eastern European Jews. Era: 1880 to 1914. Status: changed (house). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 2960](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/2960), [Joods Amsterdam: Nieuwe Kerkstraat](https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/nieuwe-kerkstraat/). Caveats: none; the live text already tells the arrival story.

11. **Zeeburg quarantine station, the refugee camp** (Zeeburgerdijk 633-641; camp 1938 to 1940) (E: "Herberg Zeeburg, quarantine station and refugee camp", az70tfshn2sculd3t521tiwc): after Kristallnacht the empty quarantine sheds became a guarded camp for Jewish refugees from Germany, among them Edith Frank's brother Walter Holländer. A camp order dated January 1940 still survives. Who: German and Austrian Jewish refugees. Era: 1938 to 1940. Status: changed (the inn survives; the sheds are gone). Evidence: [Anne Frank House research: Jewish refugee camp Zeeburg](https://research.annefrank.org/en/locaties/63a9ac86-dcbe-449c-85de-be2e3561d374/), [Rijksmonument 6499](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/6499). Caveats: the refugees were confined rather than welcomed, and the text should say so plainly.

12. **Pathé Tuschinski** (Reguliersbreestraat 26-34; 1921) (E: "Pathé Tuschinski", hx7dwu71gosab9zsoiknjyt1): Abraham Tuschinski, a young Polish Jew from Brzeziny, was on his way to America when he stayed in Rotterdam (1903 or 1904). There he started a kosher restaurant for transmigrants, then cinemas, and in 1921 he built this palace. Who: Eastern European Jewish (trans)migrant. Era: 1900 to 1942. Status: open (cinema). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 4828](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/4828), [Jewish Currents: The uncivil servant](https://jewishcurrents.org/the-uncivil-servant-abraham-tuschinski-and-his-theater/), [IISG ondernemers: Tuschinski](https://www.iisg.nl/ondernemers/pdf/pers-1485-01.pdf). Caveats: sources give 1903 or 1904 for the stay in Rotterdam. The live summary already says "Polish Jewish emigrant".

13. **Waterlooplein, over the Houtgracht synagogues** (Waterlooplein; Houtgracht filled 1882) (E: "Waterlooplein market, from Jewish market to flea market", hfisq9jg2z8hguxong5ppm4s): under the square lies the Houtgracht, where the three Portuguese congregations merged as Talmud Tora in 1639. Spinoza was banned in their synagogue in 1656. In 1882 the canals were filled and the Jewish street traders of the Jodenbreestraat were moved onto the new square. Who: Sephardic, then Ashkenazi Jews. Era: 1600 to 1940. Status: changed (flea market). Evidence: [Joods Amsterdam: Waterlooplein](https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/waterlooplein/), [Spinozaweb: Vlooienburg (Talmud Tora at the Houtgracht)](https://spinozaweb.org/locations/3), [Wikipedia (en) Houtgracht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houtgracht). Caveats: the 1639 synagogue's exact plot (Houtgracht 208, later Waterlooplein 33-39, demolished 1931) needs a check against a map before it is pinned. I found no plaque.

14. **Jan van Eijckstraat 16, where the Wiener Library began** (1933 to 1939) (E: "Jan van Eijckstraat 16: where the Wiener Library began", tppsxewme1azq9zi0zkuqa63): Alfred Wiener fled Berlin in 1933 and set up the Jewish Central Information Office here, collecting Nazi material. It moved to London in 1939; his wife and daughters, left here, were deported. Who: German Jewish refugee. Era: 1933 to 1943. Status: changed (house). Evidence: [Wikipedia (en) Wiener Holocaust Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Holocaust_Library), [Joods Monument: Expositur](https://www.joodsmonument.nl/en/page/700/expositur), [AD on the tribute](https://www.ad.nl/buitenland/eerbetoon-aan-afred-wiener-de-verzetsheld-die-de-gruweldaden-van-nazi-s-in-kaart-bracht~aad968b9b/). Caveats: none.

15. **Querido Verlag, the exile publisher** (Keizersgracht 333; 1933 to 1940) (N): in April 1933 Emanuel Querido, an Amsterdam Jewish publisher, gave the refugee Fritz Landshoff a German-language imprint here. It published Klaus Mann's Die Sammlung and well over a hundred banned exile writers until the invasion. Who: German (largely Jewish) exile writers and publishers. Era: 1933 to 1940. Status: changed (canal house; no plaque found). Evidence: [Deutsche Nationalbibliothek: Querido Verlag 1933-1940](https://mediengeschichte.dnb.de/KIE/Content/EN/Topics/querido-en.html), [DNB: Hell and paradise, Amsterdam, Querido and German exile literature](https://dnb.de/EN/Kulturell/QueridoExilDbsm/querido.html), [Leo Baeck Institute: Querido Verlag](https://www.lbi.org/german-exile-publishers/querido-verlag/). Caveats: Emanuel Querido himself was Amsterdam-born (murdered in Sobibor, 1943). The newcomers were Landshoff and the authors. Check that no. 333 is the same building (Landshoff's memoir is titled "Amsterdam, Keizersgracht 333"). Not all the writers were Jewish. Overlaps the 1800-1945 slice.

16. **Amsterdam Museum, the orphanage that sheltered refugee children** (Kalverstraat 92; 1938 to 1940) (E: "Amsterdam Museum (the Burgerweeshuis, Kalverstraat)", tb18ar3l6vpng68y1wjv1x8c): Jewish children from Germany and Austria, brought out by Truus Wijsmuller and others, stayed in the city orphanage. On 14 May 1940, 74 of them left Amsterdam and sailed from IJmuiden on the last boat to England. Who: German and Austrian Jewish refugee children. Era: 1938 to 1940. Status: changed (museum, closed for renovation). Evidence: [Anne Frank House timeline: Jewish children taken to England at the last minute](https://www.annefrank.org/en/timeline/63/jewish-children-taken-to-england-at-the-last-minute/), [Britannica: Kindertransport](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Kindertransport), [kindertransport-17uhr13.de: the last Kindertransport](https://kindertransport-17uhr13.de/der-letzte-kindertransport/?lang=en). Caveats: the count is 74 for the ship, with 66 sometimes given for the orphanage group. Some children lived with foster families.

17. **Truus Wijsmuller's bust** (Bachplein) (E: "Truus Wijsmuller's bust, Bachplein", c713iaaetg58mpsy5i5t1l4z): the Amsterdammer who met Eichmann in Vienna in December 1938 and got 600 children out, the start of the Dutch Kindertransports. Who: German and Austrian Jewish refugee children, and their rescuer. Era: 1938 to 1940. Status: open (monument). Evidence: [4en5mei Bachplein](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/monumenten_zoeken/oorlogsmonument/1438), [Stadscuratorium](https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/gedenkteken-truus-wijsmuller/), [Anne Frank House timeline](https://www.annefrank.org/en/timeline/63/jewish-children-taken-to-england-at-the-last-minute/). Caveats: this is a rescuer's monument, not a place the newcomers used. It pairs naturally with item 16.

18. **Beethovenstraat, a street of German refugees** (Beethovenstraat at the Corellistraat) (E: "Beethovenstraat raids and tram stop", mhthdzsjj6sab85nkewzaobi): many German Jewish refugees found flats in the new Beethovenstraat, and the raids of 1943 came for them here. A pavement plate and a 2025 threshold mark the tram stop. Who: German Jewish refugees. Era: 1933 to 1943. Status: open (memorials). Evidence: [4en5mei (4570)](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/monumenten_zoeken/oorlogsmonument/4570), [Joods Amsterdam: Beethovenstraat](https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/beethovenstraat/), [NOS](https://nos.nl/l/2514671). Caveats: the memorials are about the deportation. The arrival paragraph has to carry the 1930s.

19. **IJssalon Koco** (Van Woustraat 149; 1941) (E: "IJssalon Koco, where the road to the February Strike began", egatu1m4gzff5z1dhwthzfmv): an ice-cream parlour run by two German Jewish refugees, Ernst Cahn and Alfred Kohn. Cahn became the first person executed by firing squad in the occupied Netherlands. Who: German Jewish refugees. Era: 1933 to 1941. Status: changed (shop; plaque). Evidence: [4en5mei (1471)](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/zoeken/1471/amsterdam-plaquette-op-de-gevel-van-de-voormalige-ijssalon-koco), [Wikipedia (nl) Ernst Cahn](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Cahn), [NH Nieuws](https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/318293/ijssalon-koco-joodse-knokploeg-februaristaking/). Caveats: none.

20. **Blasiussjoel** (Swammerdamstraat 70; about 1900s) (E: "Blasiussjoel, Swammerdamstraat 70", sznqovapkl7ekg0jj1dd6x0m): the synagogue of Jews who had fled the Russian pogroms and settled in the Weesperzijde streets. Who: Eastern European Jews. Era: 1890 to 1943, reopened after the war. Status: changed (homes). Evidence: [Joods Amsterdam: synagoge Swammerdamstraat](https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/synagoge-swammerdamstraat/), [Reliwiki](https://www.reliwiki.nl/index.php/Amsterdam,_Swammerdamstraat_70_-_Synagoge), [AHV wiki](https://www.amsterdamhv.nl/wiki/swammerdamstraat-70.html). Caveats: find the founding year before the text is written (not in my notes).

21. **Lekstraat synagogue** (Lekstraat 61-63; 1937) (E: "Lekstraat synagogue", baktmd71by5dx5i3uikjobb9): the last synagogue built before the war, for a Rivierenbuurt whose Jewish population grew from 4,000 to 17,000 in ten years, partly with refugees from Germany. Who: Dutch Jews moving south and German refugees. Era: 1930s. Status: changed (now a liberal-traditional congregation and Jewish centre). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 526707](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/526707), [Joods Amsterdam: synagoge Lekstraat](https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/synagoge-lekstraat/), [Wikipedia (nl) Lekstraatsynagoge](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekstraatsynagoge). Caveats: most of the growth was Amsterdam Jews leaving the old quarter. Don't credit it all to refugees.

22. **Refugee house plaque, Zuideinde 443** (Oostzanerwerf, Noord; 1933 to 1939) (E: "Refugee house plaque, Zuideinde 443", refa3obwkah8361e9reb3uvt): 30 of the 75 Jewish refugees who came to this area between 1933 and 1939 lived in this one house. A rare memorial to arrival itself, in Noord. Who: German and Eastern European Jewish refugees. Era: 1933 to 1942. Status: open (plaque). Evidence: [4en5mei (2673)](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/monumenten_zoeken/oorlogsmonument/2673), [TracesOfWar](https://www.tracesofwar.nl/sights/24481/), [Joods Monument: Leo Mozes Rozenszajn](https://www.joodsmonument.nl/en/page/129325/leo-mozes-rozenszajn). Caveats: check whether the house is on the Amsterdam side of the Oostzaan border (the live record says Noord).

After a year in the Candida building on the Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal it moved here, before Prinsengracht 263. Miep Gies started here. Who: German Jewish refugee entrepreneur. Era: 1933 to 1940. Status: changed (canal house, offices or shops; no plaque known). Evidence: [Anne Frank House research: Opekta, Singel](https://research.annefrank.org/en/locaties/5c893854-c6f4-4ec1-8bc7-3f53d91174cc), [Anne Frank House timeline: Otto starts Opekta](https://www.annefrank.org/en/timeline/30/otto-starts-opekta-in-the-netherlands/), [Wikipedia (en) Opekta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opekta). Caveats: could instead be a paragraph on the Anne Frank House (E: "Anne Frank House", n0ntsglbwukufqsfzdygtwcu), which would then carry the refugee-business story. Choose one.

24. **Comité voor Joodsche Vluchtelingen, Lijnbaansgracht 366** (1939 to 1941) (N): the committee founded in 1933 to house, feed and place Jewish refugees. It first worked from 's-Gravenhekje 7 and moved here in 1939 when that building came down; after 1941 the Jewish Council used the address. Who: German and Austrian Jewish refugees, and the Amsterdam Jews who received them. Era: 1933 to 1941. Status: changed (present use not checked). Evidence: [Anne Frank House research: Committee for Jewish Refugees](https://research.annefrank.org/en/locaties/ee99471c-eda3-4963-a448-a980cb61c5f2/), [EHRI: CJV records](https://portal.ehri-project.eu/units/il-002798-6257542), [Wikipedia (en) Committee for Jewish Refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_Jewish_Refugees_(Netherlands)). Caveats: the building's survival and look still need a street check (coordinates 52.360008, 4.894254, near the Vijzelgracht end). If it is gone, it becomes a paragraph elsewhere.

25. **Liberal Jewish synagogue, Zuidelijke Wandelweg** (2010; congregation 1932) (E: "The Liberal Jewish synagogue on Jacob Soetendorp's island", nrgi6zgog3xjlyaryea20c1r): Amsterdam's liberal congregation began in 1932, and between 1933 and 1940 its new members were mostly recent German Jewish immigrants, who brought the Reform tradition with them. Evidence: [Wikipedia (nl) Liberaal Joodse Gemeente Amsterdam](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberaal_Joodse_Gemeente_Amsterdam), [Joods Monument: Liberaal Joodse Gemeente](https://www.joodsmonument.nl/en/page/533/liberaal-joodse-gemeente), [Wikipedia (en) NVPJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlands_Verbond_voor_Progressief_Jodendom). 26. **Huize Avondrood** (Nieuwe Herengracht 143; 1720) (E: "Huize Avondrood, a merchant's house with nine lives (Nieuwe Herengracht 143)", v2afj88x7n1qyna20hab9rwv): built for a Sephardic merchant, it later sheltered exiles from the Boer War. Caveats: the Sephardic merchant was established here, not a newcomer. A weaker arrival hook; the Boer exiles also fit the 1800-1945 slice.

27. **Jewish cemetery Zeeburg** (Valentijnkade, Flevopark; 1714 to 1942) (E: "Jewish cemetery Zeeburg", o0mpgfjnaymw9nhjzjwfpbcm): the burial ground of the poor Ashkenazi majority, about 100,000 people, most of them children. 28. **Twelve stumbling stones at Nieuwe Prinsengracht 18** (E: "Twelve stumbling stones at one door (Nieuwe Prinsengracht 18)", v4dfz50jf57ixhkh46pcjnp6): twelve stones at one door, many for refugees from Germany and Poland who had found rooms in one house. Caveats: lower priority. It duplicates the Beethovenstraat story in a different district.

29. **Gerhard Badrian's stone** (Rubensstraat 26; 1944) (E: "Gerhard Badrian's stone, Rubensstraat 26", cemiwv2z5qoml4p8tkr9rmz0): a German Jewish refugee who became a daring resistance man, freeing prisoners in SD uniform; shot here in 1944. Who: German Jewish refugee. Era: 1933 to 1944. Status: open (memorial stone). Evidence: [4en5mei (2509)](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/monumenten_zoeken/oorlogsmonument/2509), [Wikipedia (nl) Gerhard Badrian](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Badrian). Caveats: a refugee's story, but a resistance memorial. Optional.

30. **Michelangelostraat 36, the Hidden Camera** (1944 to 1945) (E: "Michelangelostraat 36, hideout of the Hidden Camera", c0l0ko0pn1um38r9zrmkeijw): the German Jewish filmmaker Fritz Kahlenberg, in hiding in this attic, ran the photographers of the Ondergedoken Camera with Tonny van Renterghem. Who: German Jewish refugee. Era: 1940 to 1945. Status: changed (house). Evidence: [NIOD: De Ondergedoken Camera](https://www.niod.nl/en/projects/underground-camera), [UNESCO NL Memory of the World](https://www.unesco.nl/nl/nl-memory-world-register/de-ondergedoken-camera). Caveats: when and why Kahlenberg came to the Netherlands is not confirmed in my sources. Verify before saying "refugee".

31. **Centraal Station, the cold welcome plaque** (Oosttunnel, IJ side; 1945) (E: "Centraal Station's two plaques: the cold welcome and the sergeant", negoeq5xe2uuuvdqy81q3695): the plaque states that the government failed the camp survivors it received here in 1945. It marks the second arrival, the return. Who: Jewish survivors returning. Era: 1945. Status: open (plaque). Evidence: [4en5mei Herinnering (4962)](https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/zoeken/4962/amsterdam-herinnering). Caveats: one authoritative source only so far. Add a news report of the 2019 unveiling before using it.

32. **The Lloyd building, Prins Hendrikkade 33** (1920) (E: "The Lloyd building, the \"gilded coffin\" on the Prins Hendrikkade", jnr5senvcx9anonwf0ijqybk): the head office the Koninklijke Hollandsche Lloyd built from the profits of carrying emigrants to South America, the same trade as item 4. Who: emigrants (largely Eastern European Jews). Era: 1914 to 1922. Status: changed (offices). Evidence: [Rijksmonument 518433](https://monumentenregister.cultureelerfgoed.nl/monumenten/518433), [Amsterdam op de kaart](https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Prins_Hendrikkade/33). Caveats: the emigrant trade of the First World War years was not only Jewish. A companion to item 4 rather than a strong item on its own.

## Checked and dropped

- Counts: 32 shortlisted, 29 (E) and 3 (N) (Querido Verlag, Opekta Singel 400, Comité voor Joodsche Vluchtelingen). The Borderline list adds one more (N), Allert de Lange.
