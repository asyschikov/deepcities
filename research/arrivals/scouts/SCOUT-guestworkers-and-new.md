# Scout: guestworkers-and-new (1960 to now)

Slice: Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Yugoslav, Turkish and Moroccan guest workers, family reunification, West African churches, refugees and the undocumented, Eastern Europeans, expats.
Checked against the pool with `pnpm -s dc places sights --all --q ...` on 2026-11-25.

## Shortlist (ordered by strength)

1. **Mozes en Aäronkerk** (Waterlooplein 207; hunger strike 7 to 15 Nov 1975) (E: "Mozes en Aäronkerk, the church named after two houses", u36dpf41xyomrrpcuh06gug5): about a hundred Moroccan workers facing deportation went on hunger strike in the church; it was the first time the legalisation of migrant workers became national news, and copycat strikes followed in Utrecht and The Hague. Today a cultural venue, Sant'Egidio services. Who: Moroccan guest workers (with KMAN, unions, churches). Era: 1975. Status: open (changed use). Evidence: Moslim Archief guide p. 50 (city PDF above); ONH "Wereldplek in Amsterdam: Mozes- en Aaronkerk verleende kerkasiel" https://onh.nl/verhaal/wereldplek-in-amsterdam-mozes-en-aaronkerk-verleende-kerkasiel ; Amsterdam750 "Wie waren de Kerkmarokkanen?" https://amsterdam750.nl/verhaal/kerkmarokkanen/ . Caveats: existing text already has a sentence on it; it deserves its own paragraph. Count of permits differs: "all 182" (Moslim Archief guide, existing text) vs "120 of the 182" (Zaans Museum https://zaansmuseum.nl/verhaal/de-lange-adem-van-de-182-kerk-marokkanen/ ); safest wording: "most of them".

2. **Afrikahuis, Van Ostadestraat 268-270: the first Moroccan mosque and Casa Migrante** (Van Ostadestraat 268-270; mosque 1974 to 1982, African congregation 1994 to 2008, Casa Migrante now) (E: "Afrikahuis, Joop van Stigt's limestone church", vcugfm97az6grdhaubw4f1zs): in September 1974 Moroccan workers led by bookbinder Mohamed Echarrouti opened Amsterdam's first Moroccan mosque (later Al Kabir) in the basement the priest of this new Sint Willibrordus church offered them; later an African migrant church gave the building its name, and today the Spanish-speakers' drop-in house Casa Migrante (founded for Spanish guest workers) has its address at no. 268. Who: Moroccans, West Africans, Spanish and Latin Americans. Era: 1974 to now. Status: changed (temporary tenants; demolition threatened). Evidence: Stadsarchief canon window 41 (caption: "begon in de kelder van de ... Sint-Willibrorduskerk buiten de Veste in de Van Ostadestraat"); Amsterdam750 "Waar stond de eerste Marokkaanse moskee" https://amsterdam750.nl/verhaal/de-eerste-marokkaanse-moskee-van-amsterdam/ ; Moslim Archief guide (D4 "Van Ostadestraat 268 eerste locatie Al Kabir Moskee"); Casa Migrante history page https://casamigrante.com/nl/onze-geschiedenis/ (address Van Ostadestraat 268). Caveats: the strongest single building in this slice, three migrant layers in one. Casa Migrante founding year differs: 1961 (Casa Migrante, Kansfonds https://www.kansfonds.nl/artikelen/verhalen/de-mystiek-van-casa-migrante/ ) vs 1969 (Amsterdam750). Parool obituary of founder Theo Beusink (2019) says it long sat in the Spaarndammerbuurt: https://www.parool.nl/amsterdam/theo-beusink-1932-2019-beschermheer-van-spaanstalig-amsterdam~baf6291d/ . Check whether Casa Migrante is in this building or the neighbouring part before writing.

3. **Gedenkteken Atatürk and the site of woonoord Atatürk** (Atatürk, off the Klaprozenweg, Noord; hostel c. 1965/66 to 1978, monument 1978) (E: "Gedenkteken Atatürk", n55jul3695dglbhlg2rx9ky9): 34 white wooden barracks for 272 Turkish men, most of them NDSM shipyard workers recruited from 1967; residents founded Ataspor, struck over the food in 1972-73, and the hostel became the cradle of Turkish Amsterdam. Only the street name and the memorial remain. Who: Turkish guest workers. Era: 1965 to 1978. Status: gone (memorial and street name). Evidence: Stadsarchief Migratiestad "1972-1973 Bewoners Atatürk komen in opstand" https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1972-1973-bewoners-ataturk-komen-opstand/ ; Amsterdam750 "Wat was woonoord Atatürk?" https://amsterdam750.nl/verhaal/woonoord-ataturk/ ; canon window 41 (names "Atatürk Noord" and a second woonoord, Tussenhaven on the IJdijk). Caveats: existing text is already a full immigration story; list-add as is. Start year 1965 or 1966 depending on source.

4. **Moskee Al Kabir, Weesperzijde 76** (Weesperzijde 76; here since 1982) (N): Amsterdam's first Moroccan mosque, founded by guest workers in 1972-74 in the Van Ostadestraat basement (item 2), moved here in 1982 and still serves the community; it calls itself "a living monument of the Moroccan community". Who: Moroccans. Era: 1972 to now. Status: open. Evidence: Moslim Archief guide (D5/C5 "Weesperzijde 76, vorige locatie Van Ostadestraat 268"); canon window 41; mosque's own site https://moskeealkabir.nl/ . Caveats: could be a paragraph on item 2 instead of its own place; building history of no. 76 not checked.

5. **De Duif, the second church of the "kerkmarokkanen"** (Prinsengracht 756; summer 1978) (E: "De Duif, the first Catholic church back on the street", jyvfr45sqhf2azxnzlcok9hx): after Kamer debates and demonstrations, the Moroccan hunger strikers struck again here in summer 1978; on 17 October 1978 they got residence permits, and the regularisation was later widened to thousands more. Who: Moroccan workers. Era: 1978. Status: open (venue and church). Evidence: Moslim Archief guide p. 50; Amsterdam750 kerkmarokkanen; nl.wikipedia Mozes en Aäronkerk (citing Andere Tijden ep. 684 https://anderetijden.nl/aflevering/684/In-de-ban-van-koning-Hassan ); Leidsch Dagblad 17 July 1978 https://leiden.courant.nu/issue/LD/1978-07-17/edition/0/page/1 . Caveats: existing De Duif text has no hunger-strike mention; needs a paragraph.

6. **Fatih mosque, Rozengracht** (Rozengracht 150; mosque since 1981) (E: "Fatih mosque, the new De Zaaier church the beauty committee lost to", w6ii28yzh82qznhc6q6z7zz3): the oldest Turkish place of worship in Amsterdam, bought by the Islamic Foundation Netherlands in 1981; the mihrab sits in the old main doorway. Who: Turkish guest workers. Era: 1977 to now. Status: open. Evidence: canon window 41 ("Het oudste Turkse gebedshuis, de Fatih-moskee"); Moslim Archief guide (B4/B5); NH Nieuws https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/338314/van-kerk-naar-moskee-de-succesvolle-metamorfose-van-de-zaaier-in-amsterdam . Caveats: existing text covers the conversion; add the first-Turkish-mosque framing and the Rokin start (item 7).

7. **Nieuwezijds Kapel, where the Fatih mosque began** (Rokin 78, now the Amsterdam Dungeon; mosque 1970s to 1980/81) (E: "Nieuwezijds Kapel, the Holy Place inside a shopping block", yvri4l2pqtmpvy3pwbvl5iuq): the hidden octagonal church behind the Rokin shops became the first home of the Turkish Fatih congregation before it moved to the Rozengracht. Who: Turkish workers. Era: 1970s. Status: changed (now Amsterdam Dungeon). Evidence: canon window 41 ("krijgt in 1977 onderdak in de Nieuwezijds Kapel en verhuist vier jaar later"); Moslim Archief guide ("in haar beginjaren ... Rokin 78, waar nu de Amsterdam Dungeon is"). Caveats: existing text says "a Turkish mosque followed (1972-1980)"; the canon says 1977 to 1981. Reconcile before writing.

8. **Site of Pension Johnny** (corner Amstelstraat / Paardenstraat; fire night of 4-5 Dec 1970) (N): an arson fire in a crowded boarding house for guest workers killed eight Moroccan residents and a Tanzanian visitor; there was one staircase and no fire escape, and four of the dead were not even registered. It exposed the pension trade and led to protests. Who: Moroccan guest workers. Era: 1970. Status: gone (building with the burnt floor stood until about 1976). Evidence: Ons Amsterdam "Brand in pension Johnny: één uitgang, geen brandtrap" https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/brand-in-pension-johnny-een-uitgang-geen-brandtrap (read via BrowserOS, targeted); Amsterdam750 "Wanneer kwamen de eerste Marokkaanse arbeiders" https://amsterdam750.nl/verhaal/wanneer-kwamen-de-eerste-marokkaanse-arbeiders-naar-amsterdam/ ; de.wikipedia Amstelstraat (building until 1976). Caveats: nothing marks the spot; exact current building and number not checked. The best single place for the boarding-house story.

9. **Sint-Josephkerk, the Vluchtkerk** (Erik Roodenstraat / Robert Scottstraat, Bos en Lommer; squat Dec 2012 to 31 May 2013) (E: "Sint-Josephkerk", xpau1hizqyb46l4qkkkrr7x7): the empty concrete church where the rejected asylum seekers of We Are Here spent the winter of 2012-13, the first of many "Vlucht-" squats that gave the group its naming habit. Now a children's indoor playground. Who: refugees from Somalia, Eritrea, Sudan and elsewhere (We Are Here). Era: 2012-13. Status: changed. Evidence: Binnenlands Bestuur https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/sociaal/ondermijning/asielzoekers-bivakkeren-in-gekraakte-kerk ; DutchNews https://www.dutchnews.nl/2012/12/greg_shapiro_sinterklaas_with-2 ; Stadsarchief Migratiestad "2013 Wij Zijn Hier" https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/2013-wij-we-are-here/ . Caveats: existing text mentions the Vluchtkerk in one sentence; worth a paragraph.

10. **Corvershof and the Diaconie garden, where We Are Here began** (Nieuwe Herengracht 18-20; camp from 4 Sept 2012) (E: "Corvershof and the couples' homes behind H'ART", uxgxywibm91l8g6i1jguv8yv): a tent camp of rejected asylum seekers in the Protestant Diaconie's garden in September 2012 started We Are Here; the Diaconie's Wereldhuis for undocumented people works from the cellars here. Who: undocumented refugees. Era: 2012 to now. Status: open. Evidence: en.wikipedia We Are Here (citing Parool/Trouw) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Here_(collective) ; Stadsarchief Migratiestad 2013 page; Oxford COMPAS/CMISE paper https://cmise.site.ox.ac.uk/article/deserving-or-undeserving-governing-migrant-irregularity-and-squatting-in-amsterdam . Caveats: existing text says "later came students and refugees" but not this; Wereldhuis founding year not found (Diaconie pages only, mirror site).

11. **Huis van Bewaring II, the Vluchthaven** (Havenstraat 6; Vluchthaven Dec 2013 to mid-2014, asylum shelter 2015-16) (E: "Huis van Bewaring II, the prison that became the British School", yjoq70az9qtckl1pr91ogfro): the emptied remand prison where the city housed We Are Here members ("Vluchthaven"), then asylum seekers, before it became the British School of Amsterdam in 2021: refugees and expats in one building. Who: refugees; British and international expats. Era: 2013 to now. Status: changed (school). Evidence: en.wikipedia We Are Here (Parool 6 Dec 2013 "Havenstraat biedt iedereen hoop"; NRC 13 Dec 2013); existing text for 2015-16 and the British School; nltimes https://nltimes.nl/node/23836 (lead). Caveats: existing text lacks the 2013-14 Vluchthaven; confirm dates in Parool.

12. **Bajeskwartier, the Bijlmerbajes as an asylum centre** (H.J.E. Wenckebachweg / Bijdorpstraat; AZC 2016 to 2018) (E: "Bajeskwartier and Bajesdorp", nrx2dgcndexsweb2agamfjtl): after the prison closed in 2016 its towers housed asylum seekers, many Syrians and Eritreans, until 2018; the "Wenckebachweg" of the brief is this site. Who: asylum seekers (2015 wave). Era: 2016-18. Status: gone (towers demolished, new district). Evidence: existing text; Vice https://www.vice.com/ro/article/refugiaiti-africani-duc-un-trai-deprimant-intr-o-fosta-inchisoare-olandeza (lead); COA/Parool needed. Caveats: one sentence now; needs a sourced paragraph (residents' origins not yet verified, do not state nationalities without a source).

13. **Westermoskee (Ayasofya Camii)** (Piri Reisplein 101; opened 1 April 2016) (E: "Westermoskee", n38rv2y627vrt24y1d101pae): the Turkish Milli Görüş congregation bought the site in 1993-94; 22 years of fights later Amsterdam's largest mosque opened, in Amsterdam School brick with a 42 m minaret. Who: Turkish community. Era: 1994 to now. Status: open. Evidence: Moslim Archief guide (essay by Emin Batman); NRC 2005 https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2005/05/07/het-bijzondere-van-de-baarsjes-10497086-a710231 ; Parool 2014 https://www.parool.nl/nieuws/na-jaren-gesteggel-is-westermoskee-bijna-af~b1a65d43/ . Caveats: existing text is good; add one line on who built it and why.

14. **Moskee El Oumma** (Postjesweg 179A, Overtoomse Veld; built 1992) (N): Moroccan mosque paid for by a neighbourhood collection, described by UvA research as the first purpose-designed mosque in Amsterdam, beside the police station that came after the 1998 riot in the area. Who: Moroccan families of Nieuw-West. Era: 1992 to now. Status: open. Evidence: UvA press release 2026 https://www.uva.nl/en/content/news/press-releases/2026/03/purpose-built-mosques-tell-a-primarily-dutch-story.html ; Moslim Archief guide (A4, essay by Abdelkader Benali); mosque site https://eloumma.nl/contact/ . Caveats: "first purpose-built" is contested: the same city guide calls Taibah (1985) the first building built as a mosque; say "among the first".

15. **Islamic burial ground at Westgaarde** (Ookmeerweg 275, Osdorp; opened 1978) (N; the Tenerife monument in the same park is E, o5l8lm90go85kz7p8x50dmi8): Amsterdam's first Islamic graves, on the initiative of Turkish and Moroccan organisations in 1978, for those who could not or would not be repatriated; graves are never cleared. Who: Turkish, Moroccan, Surinamese Muslims. Era: 1978 to now. Status: open. Evidence: Moslim Archief guide p. 40-41 (with 1978 inauguration photo by Hans van den Boogaard, Stadsarchief); De Nieuwe Ooster/Westgaarde pages needed. Caveats: marks the moment migrants decided to stay; a sensitive place, keep text sober.

16. **De Kandelaar** (Bijlmerdreef 1239; opened 2007) (E: "De Kandelaar", cz3wra0vbel3fdvf00rnyaug): a shared building for some fifteen migrant churches, designed with Ghanaian pastors, for congregations driven out of the demolished Bijlmer parking garages where they had met since the 1980s. Who: Ghanaian and other West African Christians. Era: 1980s to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; NH Nieuws https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/315601/in-amsterdam-zuidoost-zitten-meer-kerken-dan-cafes-maar-veel-daarvan-zitten-zonder-ruimte ; Streven 1996 on garage churches https://dbnl.org/tekst/_str010199601_01/_str010199601_01_0093.php . Caveats: good as is.

17. **Nossa Senhora de Fátima, church behind a doorway** (Jacob Catskade 11; Portuguese-speaking parish since 1989) (E: "Church behind a doorway, Jacob Catskade 11", v7t4g2hg66ixsiqom0j8j1pf): a hidden 1919 church inside a housing block, home since 1989 to the Portuguese-speaking Catholics (Portuguese, Cape Verdean, Brazilian). Who: Portuguese and Lusophone migrants. Era: 1989 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; Geheugen van West (cited in existing text); KDC Radboud database of Catholic migrant communities https://www.ru.nl/kdc/weten/katholieke-migrantengemeenschappen-nederland/ (lead). Caveats: the only Portuguese/Southern-European place I could confirm; composition of the parish not verified.

18. **Moeder Godskerk, the Syriac Orthodox church** (Keizersgracht 220; Syriac since 1985) (E: "Moeder Godskerk (Church of Our Lady), Syriac and Catholic", vwo6pkwzauz5odrly8amwtnl): when the Redemptorists left, the Syriac Orthodox Church bought the canal church; it is shared with Catholic congregations, including a Surinamese one. Who: Syriac (Aramean) Christians, largely from south-east Turkey, who came as workers and refugees from the 1970s. Era: 1985 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; en.wikipedia Church of Our Lady, Amsterdam https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady,_Amsterdam . Caveats: the origin of the congregation needs an authoritative source before writing it.

19. **Dragers van verre (Nelson Carrilho)** (Westerpark; 1989) (E: "Dragers van verre (Nelson Carrilho)", svo2lgh22gzjlfc2qnj079g8): two bronze figures carrying loads on their heads, a monument residents asked for to the multicultural city, by a Curaçao-born sculptor. Who: all newcomers. Era: 1989. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; Kunstwacht/buitenkunstamsterdam (lead). Caveats: Amsterdam has no actual guest-worker monument (see Borderline); this is the nearest.

20. **El Hizjra, the first Arabic bookshop** (Frans Halsstraat 1985, then Singel 300 from 1992 to 2013) (N): Moroccan-born Abderazak Sbaiti and Simone IJben opened the first Arabic bookshop in the Netherlands; its foundation's writing prize launched Abdelkader Benali, Mustafa Stitou and Murat Isik. Who: Arabic-speaking (mainly Moroccan) migrants and their children. Era: 1985 to 2013. Status: gone (foundation continues). Evidence: Stadsarchief Migratiestad "1985 Arabische boekhandel El Hizjra" https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1985-arabische-boekhandel-el-hizjra/ (archive 1987-2013 at Stadsarchief). Caveats: no marker at Singel 300; current use not checked.

21. **HTIB, the Turkish workers' association** (Eerste Weteringplantsoen 2C; founded 7 June 1974 in Amsterdam) (N): left-wing association founded by Turkish workers and political refugees, still active; its women's sister HTKB (1975) was the first national Turkish women's group. Who: Turkish workers. Era: 1974 to now. Status: open. Evidence: nl.wikipedia Turkse Arbeidersvereniging https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkse_Arbeidersvereniging ; HTIB "50 jaar" https://htib.nl/htib-50-jaar/ ; IISG holds its archive (ARCH00589); Atria on HTKB https://atria.nl/en/kennis/artikelen/alles-over-de-turkse-vrouwenbeweging-in-nederland . Caveats: since when at this address unknown; office, not a sight; politically neutral wording needed.

22. **Startblok Riekerhaven** (Riekerhaven, Nieuw-West; opened 1 July 2016) (N): 565 young people in temporary container blocks, half Dutch and half young refugees with status, an experiment in living together after the 2015 arrivals; copied at Startblok Elzenhagen in Noord. Who: young refugees (Syria, Eritrea and others) and Dutch youth. Era: 2016 to about 2027. Status: open (temporary). Evidence: gemeenteblad 2015 https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2015-116797.pdf ; World Habitat https://world-habitat.org/?p=12702 ; NH Nieuws https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/311348/amsterdam-wil-andere-verdeling-jongeren-en-statushouders-in-grote-gemengd-wooncomplexen . Caveats: temporary permits (block 1 extended to 30 June 2027); residents only, view from outside.

23. **Kapel en Convict, Ukrainian refugees' home and Circus Odesa** (Prinses Irenestraat 19; since 2025) (E: "Kapel en Convict: the high-rise monastery in the Beatrixpark", txtz9yb0h7h192isp9g7g7i2): about 150 young Ukrainian refugees live in the stacked monastery; the round chapel became a Ukrainian meeting place and café. Who: Ukrainians (2022 onward). Era: 2025 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text (sources there); Amsterdam750 "Van Kiev tot Weesp" for the wider Ukrainian story https://amsterdam750.nl/verhaal/van-kiev-tot-weesp/ . Caveats: very recent; good as the Ukrainian example.

24. **Former Buurthuis Transvaal, now BOOST** (Danie Theronstraat 2-8; BOOST since 2017) (E: "Former Buurthuis Transvaal, Danie Theronstraat", nay21dj17ngqd8bux3eop03i): a listed 1970s community centre where refugees and other newcomers learn Dutch and find their feet. Who: refugees and newcomers. Era: 2017 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; BOOST site (lead). Caveats: the immigration angle is current use only; short paragraph.

25. **WOW Amsterdam** (Wiltzanghlaan 60; AZC from 2000 to the mid-2000s) (E: "WOW Amsterdam: technical school, asylum centre, art house", qmf435ep77ddd034uqcscc6r): a technical college turned asylum seekers' centre in 2000, now studios and a hostel. Who: asylum seekers (1990s-2000s). Era: 2000s. Status: changed. Evidence: existing text; COA/Parool needed. Caveats: little is known on the asylum years; medium.

26. **Blauwe Moskee** (Henri Dunantstraat 10; 2008) (E: "Blauwe Moskee, Henri Dunantstraat", va0zbmykdts05phe9rqvyr7k): mosque with Dutch-language services in Slotervaart, the first in Amsterdam to broadcast the Friday call (2019). Who: Moroccan-Dutch and other Muslims of Nieuw-West. Era: 2008 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; en.wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mosque,_Amsterdam ; NOS https://nos.nl/artikel/2309517 . 27. **De Olijftak, El Hijra mosque** (Arthur van Schendelstraat 17-18; mosque since about 1998) (E: "De Olijftak (El Hijra mosque)", uuu4k7ugqximblpgvc2vhx67): a Mennonite church that became a Moroccan mosque named after the Prophet's migration. 28. **Moskee Al-Ihsane** (Mendes da Costahof 32; 1993, new building 2017) (E: "Moskee Al-Ihsane", ftaatjtntkp7bfct7wa170h9): congregation that started in low business units and rebuilt in Dudok's style with a pattern from Fes. Who: Moroccans of Geuzenveld. Era: 1993 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text (Dudok society). Caveats: cluster item.

29. **Moskee Nour, former Apostolic church** (Witte de Withstraat 96; mosque since 1996-97) (E: "Former Apostolic church, Witte de Withstraat (now a mosque)", wb7nc43ioj44mvkl908j2ach): a 1921 Greek-cross church converted into a mosque and community centre. Who: Muslim community of De Baarsjes. Era: 1997 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text (register). Caveats: which community founded Nour not verified; cluster item.

30. **Broederhuis, Rembrandtpark** (Orteliuskade 2-4 / Postjesweg 124; mosque 2003 to 2016) (E: "Broederhuis, Rembrandtpark (monastery, mosque, studios)", hauwk3osq4hjs7dv3bfc5tz4): a religious brothers' house that became a mosque, now studios partly for asylum-status holders. 31. **Javastraat, a street of newcomers' shops** (Javastraat, Indische Buurt) (N; only "101 Namen, railway viaduct" exists nearby): Turkish and Moroccan shops, the big Turkish coffeehouse Cinar Kardesler (run by Mustafa Cinar, who came in 1975), Turkish bakery Kapadokya, the Syrian-Lebanese grocer Tigris & Eufraat (2015) and Nadia Zerouali's Couscousbar (2017). Who: Turkish, Moroccan, Syrian. Era: 1970s to now. Status: open (changing). Evidence: Moslim Archief guide (C3/C4 Javastraat 20H and 30); Geheugen van Oost https://geheugenvanoost.amsterdam/page/93766/koffiehuis-cinar-kardesler-%C3%A9%C3%A9n-van-de-grootste-turkse-koffiehuizen-in . Caveats: a street, not a building; gentrifying fast; coffeehouse may have closed (interview 2011).

32. **Dappermarkt** (Dapperstraat) (E: "Dappermarkt", x72c3w4tdoi9rtzkylhdvkdf): a 1910 market that became one of the most mixed in the city, with Turkish, Moroccan, Surinamese and Ghanaian traders. Who: all. Era: 1960s to now. Status: open. Evidence: Canon van Amsterdam window 48 (existing text); Moslim Archief guide on migrant market culture (Plein '40-'45 example). Caveats: existing text has no migrant angle and I found no sourced history of migrant stall-holders here; needs a source before a paragraph. Plein '40-'45 (Nieuw-West) is the guide's own example and may be stronger.

33. **Respect monument No War, Balboaplein** (Balboaplein; 2005) (E: "Respect monument No War, Balboaplein", qkqstiwd9vbnh5e29bmmhsps): a 4 May memorial from Moroccan zellij to Delft blue, made from local faces after a disrupted ceremony. Who: Moroccan-Dutch youth and neighbours. Era: 2005. Status: open. Evidence: existing text. Caveats: about belonging rather than arrival; medium.

34. **Bevrijde vogel, Sierplein** (Sierplein; cube added 2024) (E: "Bevrijde vogel, Sierplein", l1hdxidz2vtt8euo0agirndh): war memorial whose new cube tells the war from Moluccan, Surinamese, Moroccan and Sinti angles. Who: newcomers' wartime histories. Era: 2024. Status: open. Evidence: existing text. Caveats: medium; overlaps postwar-colonial.

35. **Imagine IC** (Bijlmerplein 393; since 1999) (N): migrant-heritage archive-museum in the Bijlmer that began as a "Beeldverzamelgebouw" collecting first-generation migrants' life stories and images. Who: all migrant Amsterdammers. Era: 1999 to now. Status: open.
