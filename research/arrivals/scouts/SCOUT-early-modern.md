# Scout: City of Arrivals, slice `early-modern` (1580 to 1800, all groups except Jews)

Scouted 2026-11-25. Shortlist only; nothing written to the live site. Pool checked with
`pnpm -s dc places sights --all --q ...` and `dc place <id>` (existing text read for every (E)).
Key authoritative source found: the Stadsarchief theme site **Amsterdam Migratiestad**
(https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/), whose pre-1800
entries give several new, place-bound stories (Scandinavian boarding house, Huguenot publisher,
poor migrants at the Regulierspoort, the Lutheran church as a migrants' church, Armenians).

"Add paragraph" means: the existing text lacks the immigration story; the list view should add it.

## Shortlist (ordered by strength)

1. **Waalse Kerk** (Walenpleintje 159 / Oudezijds Achterburgwal; refugees' church since 1586) (E: "Walloon Church and its gate of skulls", dhwxocbmhj09f1trc7yd828h): a friary chapel handed in 1586 to French-speaking Protestant refugees from the Southern Netherlands; galleries were added when Huguenots poured in after 1685, and the congregation still preaches in French. Who: Walloons, Huguenots. Era: 1586 to now. Status: open (services in French, concerts). Evidence: existing place text; [Wikipedia: Walloon Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloon_Church,_Amsterdam) (lead); [De Gids 1985 on the Huguenot refuge, 50,000 to 60,000 to the Republic, Walloon poor relief took them in first](https://dbnl.org/tekst/_gid001198501_01/_gid001198501_01_0035.php). Caveats: existing text already tells the story well; maybe add a line that the Walloon deaconry was the first reception for Huguenots in 1685.

2. **English Reformed Church in the Begijnhof** (Begijnhof 48; English since 1607) (E: "Begijnhof", vn6ztawt9z1koc564tgmx9ub; no separate place for the church): the beguines' church given in 1607 to English- and later Scottish-speaking Protestants; a 1927 tablet by Reformed Church in America clergy honours "the Pilgrim Fathers who settled first in the city of Amsterdam", and since March 2024 a bust by Fabio Pravisani remembers the Bergamo violinist Pietro Antonio Locatelli, who lived here from 1729 and is buried in the church. Who: English and Scottish Protestants, Italians. Era: 1607 to now. Status: open (Presbyterian services). Evidence: [ERC Amsterdam church guide (NL)](https://www.ercadam.nl/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/NLD.pdf); [HMDB: Pilgrim Fathers tablet](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=291658); [HMDB: Locatelli monument, erected 2024](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=311751). Caveats: the Begijnhof place mentions the English church in one line only; consider a separate place "English Church" for the list view rather than listing the whole Begijnhof. Pilgrim link: the Scrooby group passed through Amsterdam 1608 to 1609 before Leiden; do not claim they worshipped in this building (only a genealogy blog says so).

3. **Oude Lutherse Kerk** (Singel 411 / Spui; 1633) (E: "Oude Lutherse Kerk, the church behind the warehouse name", bd9zus4cc3pfaefl5qadav2s): the church of a congregation of German and Scandinavian migrants, who met in a warehouse from about 1600; services were in High German far into the 17th century, and sailors and labourers sat beside very rich merchants. Who: Germans, Danes, Norwegians, Swedes. Era: 1588/1633 to now. Status: changed (now UvA aula, still occasional services). Evidence: existing text; [Stadsarchief, 1734 Lutheranen helpen Salzburgse emigranten ("Migrantenkerk")](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1734-lutheranen-helpen-salzburgse/); [Stadsarchief, 1663 Rel rond Deense dominee](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1663-rel-rond-deense-dominee/). Caveats: add paragraph (the existing text says "many of them immigrants" only in passing). The 1734 story (Amsterdam Lutherans raising almost 23,000 guilders for Salzburg Protestant refugees stuck in Zeeland) is a strong extra hook.

4. **Armenian church Surp Hoki** (Krom Boomssloot 22; 1714) (E: "Armenian church in a warehouse (Surp Hoki)", y7r5f2euzhumkr6rzltq205s): Armenian silk and jewel merchants, many from New Julfa in Isfahan and often called "Persians", made a warehouse their church in 1714; a new Armenian community bought it back in 1985. Who: Armenians. Era: 1714 to now. Status: open (Sunday liturgy). Evidence: existing text; [Stadsarchief, 1743 Armeniër trouwt met Amsterdamse](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1743-armenier-trouwt-amsterdamse/); [Wikipedia: Voskan Yerevantsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskan_Yerevantsi) (first printed Armenian Bible, Amsterdam 1666 to 1668; lead only). Caveats: existing text is complete; could add the Isfahan origin. The Armenian press of the 1660s has no known address.

5. **Walenweeshuis** (Vijzelgracht 2A / Prinsengracht; 1669 to 1671) (E: "Walenweeshuis, the Walloon orphanage waiting for a future", ps47axwum9uk96gvgg6xqliv): newcomers' orphans were not taken by the city orphanages, so the Walloon church built its own, first in the Laurierstraat (1631) and then this Dortsman block; from 1971 to 2016 it was the French consulate and Maison Descartes. Who: Walloons, Huguenots (later the French institute). Era: 1631 to 1967. Status: changed (empty, restoration announced 2025). Evidence: existing text; [NH Nieuws on its condition](https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/341809/zorgen-om-walenweeshuis-vijzelgracht-amsterdam); [Amsterdam Museum, "Doorgegeven Zorg", paintings of the Stichting Hospice Wallon](https://assets.amsterdammuseum.nl/downloads/Doorgegeven-Zorg_De-schilderijen-van-Stichting-Hospice-Wallon-1753805402.pdf). Caveats: overlaps with "Vijzelgracht corner, Walloon orphans..." (nu9fdr9m85u45ybyrzwehfke); list only this one.

6. **Ronde Lutherse Kerk** (Singel 11; 1668 to 1671) (E: "Ronde Lutherse Kerk, the dome that was not allowed to be a tower", jwu1v9y082kenwqqwx5tat5n): built because the Spui church was too small "for the many Lutheran immigrants"; a dome because the migrants' church was not allowed a tower. Who: Germans, Scandinavians. Era: 1671 to 1935. Status: changed (hotel event hall). Evidence: existing text; [Stadsarchief, 1663 Danish minister: by 1650 Danes and Norwegians were nearly a quarter of the members](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1663-rel-rond-deense-dominee/); [Wikipedia: Ronde Lutherse Kerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronde_Lutherse_Kerk) (lead). Caveats: list this and no. 3 together or pick one if space is tight.

7. **Descartes's house** (Westermarkt 6; 1634) (E: "Westermarkt 6, where Descartes fathered a daughter", ozzqbokq676xfhwmw5yk7vni): the French philosopher lived in the Republic for some twenty years; the plaque of 1920 (Alliance Française) quotes his praise of Amsterdam's freedom, the classic refugee-thinker's testimony. Who: French. Era: 1634. Status: private house, plaque and pavement tile. Evidence: existing text; [HMDB: Descartes plaque, Alliance Française 1920](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=131437); [Open Plaques: Descartes](https://openplaques.org/people/5402). Caveats: Descartes was a voluntary exile, not a persecuted refugee; say "came for the freedom" rather than "fled". Also "Westermarkt beyond the tower" (a1ludnk9q5hvxvgrj5rsnk1f) shares the address: avoid double listing.

8. **Huis met de Hoofden** (Keizersgracht 123; 1622, De Geer from 1634) (E: "Huis met de Hoofden (House with the Heads)", ps4js53ffz8e85afl4tuahvc): bought in 1634 by Louis de Geer, son of a Walloon family from Liège, who recruited Walloon smiths to march to Amsterdam and ship out to his Swedish ironworks; his son Laurens housed the Czech refugee bishop Comenius here (marker: stayed 1656). Arrival and onward emigration in one house. Who: Walloons, Czech Brethren. Era: 1634 to 1670. Status: open (Embassy of the Free Mind museum). Evidence: existing text; [DBNL, Erflaters van onze beschaving on De Geer's Walloons walking to Amsterdam](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/rome002erfl01_01/rome002erfl01_01_0013.php); [HMDB: Comenius marker, Keizersgracht 123](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=227769); [Wikipedia: Walloon immigration to Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloon_immigration_to_Sweden) (lead). Caveats: add paragraph on De Geer's Liège origin and the Swedish Walloons. The marker text is from HMDB, not checked on site.

9. **Nieuwe Waalse Kerk** (Keizersgracht 676; congregation's second church 1719, present building 1856) (E: "Nieuwe Waalse Kerk, now a museum of illusions", rn7e0etakka9ta3b2ufe43fq): the Walloon congregation, swollen by Huguenots, opened a second church in 1719 in an old bell foundry on the Prinsengracht; this replacement of 1856 kept the Huguenot congregation going into the 20th century. Who: Huguenots. Era: 1719 to 1959. Status: changed (Museum of Illusions since 2025). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia: Nieuwe Waalse Kerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Waalse_Kerk) (lead); [Stadsarchief, 1862-1867 De hugenootse familie Boissevain](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1862-1867-hugenootse-familie-boissevain/) (shows the Huguenot families' continuity; not read in full). Caveats: building is 19th century; the early-modern story is the 1719 church (gone, site on the Prinsengracht not pinned).

10. **Christ Church, the Anglican church** (Groenburgwal 42; congregation 1698, here since 1771) (E: "Christ Church, the English church in a cloth hall", i9d2m7kfxk0r01n9ddltr8h7): the English Episcopal congregation of 1698, for English merchants and soldiers who came after William III; windows show William and Mary and two English regiments. Who: English. Era: 1698 to now. Status: open. Evidence: existing text; [Stadsherstel: Groenburgwal 42 Christ Church](https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/groenburgwal-42-christchurch/). Caveats: overlaps the walk "Groenburgwal and Raamgracht" (e8fhx7glygq7g7jizf9c7mkz); list Christ Church.

11. **The Bruinistenkerk, church of the English Separatists (gone)** (Barndesteeg, behind nos. 13-15, on the vanished Bruinistengang; built 1668) (E: "Barndesteeg, the penitents' convent and the mission hostel", wx8zwvbosc6wy8p6u7oygsh3, add paragraph; or N as its own gone place): English "Brownists" fled to Amsterdam from 1593; their church on the Lange Houtstraat (Vlooienburg, 1606) burned in 1662 and the remnant built this hidden church in 1668. By 1701 five members were left and joined the Begijnhof Presbyterians; the building became a notorious tenement, demolished 1910, and Tot Heil des Volks bought the site in 1915. Who: English Separatists (Pilgrim-era dissenters). Era: 1593 to 1701. Status: gone (Tot Heil des Volks hostel opposite). Evidence: [Ons Amsterdam, "Zelfs de politie waagde zich niet in de Bruinistenkerk" (Apr 2023), read via BrowserOS](https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/zelfs-de-politie-waagde-zich-niet-in-de-bruinistenkerk); [Het Parool, 8 Apr 2023](https://www.parool.nl/amsterdam/van-godshuis-tot-rovershol-de-bruinistenkerk-was-verzamelplaats-van-zakkenrollers-en-ander-gespuis~bf8bda732/); [Wikipedia: Francis Johnson (Brownist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Johnson_(Brownist)) (lead); literature: H. de la Fontaine Verwey, "Van kerk tot dievenhol", Amstelodamum 1949. Caveats: Ons Amsterdam names Ainsworth as founder in 1593, Wikipedia names Johnson; write "English Separatists led by Francis Johnson and Henry Ainsworth". Its 1593 meeting place "Engelse Pelgrimsteeg bij de Bakkerstraat" is unverified, leave out.

12. **Vondel's shop, child of Antwerp refugees** (Warmoesstraat 39, plaque; second plaque at Warmoesstraat 108; 1597 to 1650) (N for the plaque; E for his grave: "Nieuwe Kerk, the church without a tower", trm070oydav7jweh28oersju): the poet was born in Cologne in 1587 to Mennonite parents who had fled Antwerp in 1582; the family settled in the Warmoesstraat in 1597 and ran a silk and stocking shop; a plaque says he lived and traded at no. 39 from 1631 to 1650 and wrote the Gijsbrecht there. Who: Flemish (Antwerp) refugees. Era: 1597 to 1679. Status: plaques on later buildings; grave in the Nieuwe Kerk. Evidence: [HMDB: Vondel plaque, Warmoesstraat 39](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=131301); [HMDB: "Hier woonde Vondel", Warmoesstraat 108](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=218584); [DBNL, Biographisch woordenboek: parents fled Antwerp, Cologne birth](https://dbnl.org/tekst/bran038biog01_01/bran038biog01_01_4367.php); [Nieuwe Kerk handout on Vondel](https://www.nieuwekerk.nl/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/DNK-2024-12-19-Joost-van-den-Vondel-handout-spreads.pdf). Caveats: an 1879 study says the exact house cannot be identified; the two plaques disagree. Best as one N place "Vondel's Warmoesstraat" at no. 39 with the caveat, rather than list-adding the whole Nieuwe Kerk. Pool coordinates for no. 39 (HMDB): 52.37559, 4.89854.

13. **Noordse Bos weavers' houses** (Spiegelgracht 27; Vijzelgracht 10 and 14-16; 1670) (E: "Noordse Bos, the weavers' houses of 1670", aapv5y7n49dc05n4lzbswjbg): the city gave free land for 400 houses that only cloth workers from outside the city could rent at first, many of them Walloons: an early state-sponsored scheme to attract skilled migrants. Who: Walloon and other immigrant weavers. Era: 1670 to 1672. Status: one corner house survives; others rebuilt 1870s. Evidence: existing text; [KNOB Bulletin (Bosman) on Amsterdam orphanages and the 1670s extension](https://bulletin.knob.nl/index.php/knob/article/download/Bosman141/250/295) (context, not read in full). Caveats: authoritative source for "many of them Walloons" still wanted (Abrahamse, De grote uitleg van Amsterdam, is the standard work). Medium strength.

14. **Our Lord in the Attic** (Oudezijds Voorburgwal 38-40; 1661 to 1663) (E: "Our Lord in the Attic (Ons' Lieve Heer op Solder)", q459if11mofdctbi93ajiglz): the attic church was built by Jan Hartman (1619 to 1668), a Catholic merchant born in Coesfeld, Westphalia, who made his fortune in Amsterdam. Who: Germans (Westphalian Catholics). Era: 1661 to 1888. Status: open (museum). Evidence: existing text; [Getty Conservation Institute, Our Lord in the Attic case study](https://www.getty.edu/conservation/publications_resources/teaching/case/olita/building/sael.html). Caveats: add paragraph on Hartman's origin; the existing text calls him a "stocking merchant", which I could not confirm.

15. **Rembrandthuis and the Black neighbours of the Jodenbreestraat** (Jodenbreestraat 4; 1630 to 1660) (E: "Rembrandthuis (Rembrandt House Museum)", y853cvdfiqbh4zi5qwky1nog): archive research by Mark Ponte traced about a hundred Black men and women, many brought as servants or sailors through the Atlantic trade, living as free people around this street in Rembrandt's time; he probably used them as models. Who: free Black Amsterdammers (Africa, Brazil, Caribbean). Era: 1630 to 1660. Status: open (museum; 2020 exhibition "HERE: Black in Rembrandt's Time"). Evidence: [Rembrandthuis press release, HERE: Black in Rembrandt's Time](https://www.rembrandthuis.nl/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/press-release.-here-black-in-rembrandts-time.-the-rembrandt-house-museum-amsterdam.pdf); [DutchNews 2020](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2020/03/black-in-rembrandts-time-a-myth-shattering-exhibition-on-identity-and-truth/); [UvA (dare) record on the research](https://dare.uva.nl/id/d34f2ede-9e22-45eb-a216-382728b7916c). Caveats: add paragraph. Coordinate with `postwar-colonial` and `jewish-arrivals` (many worked in Portuguese-Jewish households): this is the place for the forced-migration side in this era.

16. **Oost-Indisch Huis, where foreign crews signed on** (Oude Hoogstraat 24; 1606 to 1798) (E: "Oost-Indisch Huis (East India House)", tbe0rj2l8it8w85ucvr0f9p5): crews were recruited here; for most of the VOC's life more than half its soldiers and close to half its common sailors came from abroad, above all from the German lands and Scandinavia, drawn to Amsterdam to sign on. Who: German, Scandinavian and other foreign sailors and soldiers. Era: 1606 to 1798. Status: changed (University of Amsterdam). Evidence: existing text; [UvA: Roelof van Gelder, Het Oost-Indisch avontuur. Duitsers in dienst van de VOC](https://dare.uva.nl/id/0465297a-7f69-4415-a686-737c65a5bcbe); [Big Think on Jelle van Lottum's figures](https://bigthink.com/strange-maps/dutch-golden-age-wasnt-all-that-dutch) (secondary). Caveats: add paragraph; percentages from a secondary summary, phrase as "more than half of the soldiers".

17. **Coymanshuis, an Antwerp family's house** (Keizersgracht 177; 1625) (E: "Coymanshuis, Jacob van Campen's first known design", d8v11d37ehamcpmmyt32pxdr): Balthasar Coymans the elder (born Antwerp 1555) settled in Amsterdam in 1592 after a stay in Hamburg; his sons built this house, the very image of how the Antwerp merchant exodus made Amsterdam's fortune. Who: Flemish/Brabant merchants. Era: 1592 to 1759. Status: changed (offices). Evidence: existing text; [KNOB Bulletin article on the Coymans family and house](https://bulletin.knob.nl/index.php/knob/article/download/672/680/749); [Wikipedia: Coymanshuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coymanshuis) (lead). Caveats: add paragraph; the existing text also carries the family's later slave-trade role, which fits the forced-migration thread.

18. **Rey and Bernard's bookshop, the Huguenot publishers of Rousseau** (Pijpenmarkt, now Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal 296; mid-18th century) (N): Marc-Michel Rey, son of Huguenots from the Dauphiné who had fled to Geneva, and his wife Elisabeth Bernard, daughter of the Huguenot writer-publisher Jean Frédéric Bernard, ran the bookshop that printed Rousseau and smuggled the French Enlightenment past censorship; Rousseau became godfather to their daughter in 1762. Who: Huguenots, French-language printers. Era: 1740s to 1780. Status: gone (shop); present building at no. 296 not checked. Evidence: [Stadsarchief, 1747 Uitgeverij/boekhandel Rey-Bernard](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1747-uitgeverij-boekhandel-rey-bernard/); [Wikipedia: Marc-Michel Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Michel_Rey) (lead, not opened). Caveats: the "printers who fled censorship" item of the brief; needs a look at what stands at NZV 296 now and whether the street number has stayed the same.

19. **Het Gekroonde Hof van Denemarken, the Scandinavian boarding house (gone)** (Ridderstraat, between the Oude Schans and the Eerste dwarsstraat, now Nieuwe Ridderstraat; 1748 to 1793) (N): the Norwegian Rachel Theunis from Kristiansand, married in turn to two Norwegians, a Dane and a man from Szczecin, ran a cheap lodging house in the Lastage where migrant sailors from Germany and Scandinavia stayed; notarial records show her helping other newcomers. Who: Norwegians, Danes, Germans, Poles. Era: 1748 to 1793. Status: gone (street rebuilt). Evidence: [Stadsarchief, 1748 'Het Gekroonde Hof van Denemarken'](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1748-'-gekroonde-hof-denemarken'/). Caveats: one authoritative source only (the archive itself), but it is a primary-record story; exact house unknown, place it on the street. Best "sailors' quarter" item for the slice.

20. **The Lutheran orphanage** (Lauriergracht 116; 1678, front of 1784) (E: "The Lutheran orphanage on the Lauriergracht", z3csxegpxxglvahescux3ibv): the migrant church's own orphanage, needed because the city orphanage took only citizens' children; the swan of the Lutherans still sits in the pediment. Who: German and Scandinavian Lutherans. Era: 1678 to 1811. Status: changed (offices). Evidence: existing text; [Rijksmuseum: print of the new front, 1784](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Nieuwe-voorgevel-van-het-Lutherse-Diaconie-Weeshuis-te-Amsterdam-1784--0346edf70487c72af50c637454b2d853); [Luthermuseum: Lutheran congregations and institutions of Amsterdam](https://luthermuseum.nl/en/tokens/lutheran-congregations-and-institutions-of-amsterdam). Caveats: add paragraph on why the Lutherans needed their own orphanage (the citizens-only rule: source it before writing; the Walloon text in the pool already states it for the Walloons).

21. **Van Brants Rus Hofje** (Nieuwe Keizersgracht 28-44; 1732 to 1733) (E: "Van Brants Rus Hofje", qe1b2e08dtg739m19t7q4jfy): founded for Lutheran women by Christoffel Brants, son of a cooper from Wittmund in East Frisia, who traded grain from Archangelsk, befriended Peter the Great and became Russia's man in Amsterdam. Who: East Frisian Germans, the Russia trade. Era: 1732. Status: changed (students' hofje). Evidence: existing text; [Luthermuseum, Lutheran institutions of Amsterdam](https://luthermuseum.nl/en/tokens/lutheran-congregations-and-institutions-of-amsterdam). Caveats: I could not confirm Brants's own birthplace (Amsterdam or East Frisia) in the archive index; keep to "son of a cooper from Wittmund" as the existing text has it.

22. **Munttoren, the old Regulierspoort where poor migrants slept** (Muntplein; 1597) (E: "Munttoren (the Mint Tower)", wu8x1ee699pypv0gvq1a3rzq): in October 1597 two brothers from Lyon, aged twelve and eight, were found begging and sleeping with many other poor newcomers in the arches of the town wall by the Regulierspoort, boarded up with planks and rags; they were given a pass to Zeeland. A plain picture of the hundreds of thousands who came hoping for work. Who: French, German, Danish, English labour migrants. Era: 1597. Status: the gate's tower stands. Evidence: [Stadsarchief, 1597 De broertjes Topin aan de bedelstaf](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/themasites/amsterdam-migratiestad/1597-broertjes-topin-bedelstaf/); existing text for the tower. Caveats: add paragraph; the arches were in the wall near the gate, not in the surviving tower itself.

23. **West-Indisch Huis and the Walloons of New Netherland** (Herenmarkt 99 / Haarlemmerstraat 75; 1623 to 1647) (E: "West-Indisch Huis (West India House)", c6jx4cj6mvvx6l95xrzgs15x): the WIC's board met here; in 1624 its ship Nieu Nederlandt carried some thirty families, mostly Walloon refugees, to the Hudson, the first permanent settlers of what became New York: refugees who had arrived in the Republic moving on. Who: Walloons (emigrating), WIC colonists. Era: 1624. Status: changed (offices, John Adams Institute). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia: Jessé de Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jess%C3%A9_de_Forest) (lead); [Columbia Univ. Libraries: The De Forests and the Walloon founding of New Amsterdam](https://dlc.library.columbia.edu/durst/cul:prr4xgxd41). Caveats: add paragraph; numbers vary (30 to 34 families); de Forest himself did not sail. Also the house's link to the slave trade belongs to the forced-migration thread (coordinate).

24. **Huis Bartolotti** (Herengracht 170-172; around 1617 to 1620) (E: "Huis Bartolotti and the White House next door", istrimkx7o8g1yox4fm23k9f): the Bolognese merchant Giovanni Battista Bartolotti left his fortune to his wife's nephew Willem van den Heuvel on condition that he take the Italian name: an Italian immigrant's name written into the canal. Who: Italians. Era: early 17th century. Status: open (museum since 2026). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia: Huis Bartolotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huis_Bartolotti) (lead, not opened). Caveats: weak as an arrival story unless an authoritative source on Bartolotti's own settlement is found; the Van den Heuvel family's Flemish origin unverified.

25. **Huis Marseille** (Keizersgracht 401; around 1665) (E: "Huis Marseille, a map of Marseille on a canal house", wgwo1e1v7iw7n4u00rgti7yi): built for Isaac Focquier, a French merchant whose ship from Marseille made his fortune; his ties to the Walloon church suggest a French-speaking Protestant family. Who: French. Era: 1665 to 1676. Status: open (photography museum). Evidence: existing text; [Huis Marseille: the name](https://huismarseille.nl/en/history/the-name-huis-marseille/); [Caroline Hanken book fragment (2019) on the Fouquier family](https://download.boekhuis.nl/9789462085183_fragm-docb.pdf). Caveats: his birthplace is unknown; do not call him a Huguenot refugee.

26. **Lutherse Diaconiehofje (Konijnenhofje), gone** (Konijnenstraat 16-48; around 1670 to 1909) (E: "Lutherse Diaconiehofje (Konijnenhofje) (gone)", fpmqosheztmndaoxhywua3g9): the Lutheran deaconry's almshouse for poor women of the migrant congregation; its gable stones went to the Lutherhof. Who: German and Scandinavian Lutherans. Era: 1670 to 1909. Status: gone. Evidence: existing text; [Luthermuseum, Lutheran institutions of Amsterdam](https://luthermuseum.nl/en/tokens/lutheran-congregations-and-institutions-of-amsterdam). Caveats: weaker; useful only if the list wants a Lutheran care cluster (orphanage, hofjes).

27. **The Comenius stone** (Valeriusplein; stone 1920, Comenius in Amsterdam 1656 to 1670) (E: "The Comenius stone: first stone of a monument that never came", luch8lsv1dnvg5j9ivccgxfz): the exiled bishop of the Czech Brethren spent his last fourteen years as a refugee in Amsterdam; the Czechoslovak state sent this stone in 1920 for a monument that never came. Who: Czech (Bohemian) religious refugees. Era: 1656 to 1670 (memorial 1920). Status: stone in the pavement.
