# Scout: City of Arrivals, slice 1800-1945

Scope: German entrepreneurs, Italians, the first Chinese, Belgian refugees of 1914, the colonial connection (Surinamese, Indonesians, the 1883 exhibition), emigrants passing through, and the 1930s refugees who were not only a Jewish-slice story (exile publishers, artists, archives). Existence checked with `pnpm -s dc places sights --all --q`. Nothing written to the live site.

## Shortlist (ordered by strength)

1. **Lloyd Hotel** (Oostelijke Handelskade 34; 1921) (E: "Lloyd Hotel and the Lloyd complex", qv0a05zf47i06yulvlmtq4dd): built by the Koninklijke Hollandsche Lloyd as a hotel for emigrants bound for South America, many of them Eastern European Jews fleeing pogroms, deloused next door before check-in; from 1938 the state housed refugees from Nazi Germany here; a hotel again. Who: Eastern European emigrants in transit, German refugees. Era: 1921-1940. Status: changed (now hotel). Evidence: existing place text; [nl.wikipedia, Lloyd Hotel](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Hotel); [nl.wikipedia, Koninklijke Hollandsche Lloyd](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijke_Hollandsche_Lloyd) (authoritative source still to add: Stadsarchief or Rijksmonumentenregister). Caveats: Jewish slice may also claim it; agree who writes the paragraph. Existing text already tells the story well.

2. **Binnen Bantammerstraat, the first Chinatown** (Binnen Bantammerstraat; from c. 1911) (E: "Binnen Bantammerstraat, Amsterdam's first Chinatown", j5ynez7kwp0ww2j5z9gw3wo7): Chinese stokers brought in by Dutch shipping lines (kept in reserve as strike-breakers in the 1911 seamen's strike) lodged here between contracts; boarding-house keepers acted as shipping masters; in 1928 Kong Hing opened here, the first Chinese restaurant to draw Dutch diners (Josephine Baker posed in its door in 1932); in the 1930s slump laid-off seamen sold peanut biscuits in the streets and about 1,000 were repatriated. Who: Chinese seamen (mostly Guangdong and Zhejiang). Era: 1911-1940. Status: changed (ordinary street, bilingual street sign since 2005). Evidence: [IISG, The Chinese of Amsterdam](https://iisg.nl/collections/chinezen-zeedijk/chinezen.php); existing place text; [UvA Public History on Chinese-Indonesian restaurants](https://publichistory.humanities.uva.nl/blog/babi-pangang-or-foe-yong-hai-chinese-indian-restaurants-in-the-netherlands). Caveats: Kong Hing's house number not found. The postwar slice owns the Zeedijk temple; the cluster "Around the Nieuwmarkt, migrant Amsterdam" (q1ogooimbt808nocosyfc1nu) overlaps, pick one.

3. **Pathé Tuschinski** (Reguliersbreestraat 26-34; 1921) (E: "Pathé Tuschinski", hx7dwu71gosab9zsoiknjyt1): Abraham Tuschinski, a Polish Jew who left in 1904 for America and got no further than Rotterdam, built Amsterdam's most famous cinema palace; in the 1930s its La Gaîté stage was one of the jazz rooms where Surinamese, Caribbean and American musicians played night after night. Who: Polish Jewish emigrant; Black musicians. Era: 1904-1940. Status: open (cinema). Evidence: existing text; [Stadsarchief, Jazz-jaren. Mensen, migratie en muziek in Amsterdam 1930-1939](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/agenda/jazz-jaren-1/); [Wikipedia, Tuschinski Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuschinski_Theatre). Caveats: the La Gaîté jazz angle is new and could be an extra paragraph.

4. **Max Beckmann's exile studio** (Rokin 85; 1937-1947) (E: "Max Beckmann's exile studio in a tobacco attic (Rokin 85)", mer1sei0s134fwgjvf9t02k8): the German painter fled the Reich the day after Hitler's "degenerate art" speech in 1937 and painted about a third of his life's work under this tobacco attic's glass roof, stuck here through the occupation. Who: German political/artistic exile. Era: 1937-1947. Status: changed (offices). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia, Max Beckmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Beckmann). Caveats: none; exile story is already the place's story.

5. **Herberg Zeeburg, quarantine station and refugee camp** (Zeeburgerdijk 633-641) (E: "Herberg Zeeburg, quarantine station and refugee camp", az70tfshn2sculd3t521tiwc): the city's quarantine grounds, whose empty barracks became a camp for Jewish refugees from Germany from December 1938 (Anne Frank's uncle Walter Holländer was held here a year). Who: German and Austrian refugees. Era: 1938-1940. Status: changed (the inn stands; barracks gone). Evidence: existing text; [Anne Frank House research site](https://research.annefrank.org/en/); [Wikipedia, Herberg Zeeburg (nl)](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeburg_(Amsterdam)). Caveats: Jewish slice overlap.

6. **Querido Verlag, the exile publisher** (Keizersgracht 333; 1933-1940) (N): after the Berlin book burning of May 1933 Emanuel Querido set up a German-language house run by Fritz Landshoff, ex-Kiepenheuer; it printed 124 books by banned writers (Heinrich Mann, Feuchtwanger, Döblin, Joseph Roth, Toller) and Klaus Mann's anti-Nazi monthly Die Sammlung. Dissolved May 1940; Querido was murdered in Sobibor in 1943. Who: German exile writers. Era: 1933-1940. Status: changed (canal house; no plaque found). Evidence: [Stadsarchief, Exil literatuur](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/stukken/tweede-wereldoorlog/exil-literatuur/); [Deutsche Nationalbibliothek, Querido Verlag 1933-1940](https://mediengeschichte.dnb.de/KIE/Content/EN/Topics/querido-en.html); Landshoff's memoir title "Amsterdam, Keizersgracht 333. Querido Verlag" ([bibliotheek.nl](https://www.bibliotheek.nl/catalogus/titel.066003490.html/fritz-h--landshoff)). Caveats: Keizersgracht 333 is the parent firm's seat (shared with Van Holkema & Warendorf); a check in the Stadsarchief address books would nail the Verlag's own rooms. A Parool piece says Querido got a stumbling stone: worth finding which address.

7. **The Colonial Institute's facade** (Mauritskade 62-64; 1915-1926) (E: "Read the facade of the Colonial Institute", y9cdoekn5uaiwedh0xanjzl6): the Koloniaal Instituut (now KIT and Wereldmuseum) was built for colonial trade and knowledge; its stone tells the colonial relationship that brought Indonesian and Surinamese students, servants and sailors to the city. Who: colonial connection (Indonesia, Suriname). Era: 1910-1945. Status: open (museum). Evidence: existing text; [Wereldmuseum Amsterdam history](https://amsterdam.wereldmuseum.nl/en); [Wikipedia, Tropenmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropenmuseum). Caveats: it is the institution "that dealt with them", not a migrants' place; the extra paragraph should say so plainly.

8. **Reestraat 6, where Anton de Kom lived** (Reestraat 6; 1935) (E: "Reestraat 6, where Anton de Kom lived", rnfnkpvexon65qv8jlg3e9t1): the Surinamese writer, who came to the Netherlands in 1920, was deported back from Suriname in 1933, wrote Wij slaven van Suriname (1934) and died after resistance work in 1945, lived here in 1935. Who: Surinamese. Era: 1920-1945. Status: changed (only the ground floor of the old house survived). Evidence: existing text; [Canon van Nederland (window Anton de Kom)](https://www.canonvannederland.nl/); [Wikipedia, Anton de Kom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_de_Kom). Caveats: postwar slice may claim the Bijlmer monument (gpqpa6wg4twhxhjy8fsvaq77); this house is the pre-war one.

9. **Museumplein, the 1883 colonial exhibition** (Museumplein; 1883) (E: "Museumplein", akzx2lcqjzqw8sxum6qcavfl): at the International Colonial and Export Exhibition 28 people from Suriname were shown in a "West Indian" tent and Javanese villagers lived in a mock kampong for the summer, photographed by ethnic "type"; it is the first large group of colonial subjects many Amsterdammers ever saw. Who: Surinamese and Javanese (forced display). Era: 1883. Status: gone (the ground is the square). Evidence: [Rijksmuseum, group portrait of Surinamese exhibited 1883](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Groepsportret-van-Surinamers-tentoongesteld-op-de-Koloniale-tentoonstelling-Amsterdam-1883--2579b63e2783ce0a51822b1ea0cb8f61); [Bovenkerk in De Gids, DBNL](https://dbnl.org/tekst/_gid001200101_01/_gid001200101_01_0013.php); [Wikipedia, International Colonial and Export Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Colonial_and_Export_Exhibition). Caveats: the existing text mentions the exhibition only in passing; needs a careful, unsensational paragraph.

10. **Koninklijk Theater Carré** (Amstel 115-125; 1887) (E: "Koninklijk Theater Carré", ti17h50ut2dgk0eevx7vhflo): a German circus family (Oscar Carré, born in Halberstadt) came to the Netherlands in 1863, played winters in Amsterdam and built this stone circus. Who: German. Era: 1863-1911. Status: open (theatre). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia, Oscar Carré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Carr%C3%A9); [Androom, grave at Zorgvlied](https://androom.home.xs4all.nl/biography/p023807.htm). Caveats: birth year 1845/1846 differs by source.

11. **Krasnapolsky, the tailor's hotel** (Dam 9 / Warmoesstraat 169; 1866) (E: "Krasnapolsky's Winter Garden and the power station behind it", wanthsf989hpp4s401xp9iq9): Adolph Wilhelm Krasnapolsky, born 1834 in Peine near Hanover to a family from Kiev, came to Amsterdam at 22 as a tailor, worked at the Winkel van Sinkel and in 1865/66 took over the Nieuwe Poolsche Koffiehuis, a café much used by German speakers, which he grew into the grand hotel. Who: German/Polish-origin. Era: 1856-1912. Status: open (hotel). Evidence: [nl.wikipedia, A.W. Krasnapolsky](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_Wilhelm_Krasnapolsky); [Anantara Krasnapolsky history (nl)](https://www.anantara.com/nl/grand-hotel-krasnapolsky-amsterdam/history); existing text. Caveats: takeover year 1865 vs 1866; "Polish" origin is family lore via Kiev; existing title is about the Winter Garden, the immigrant angle needs a paragraph.

12. **Peek & Cloppenburg, Nieuwendijk to the Dam** (Dam 20, Nieuwendijk 168-170; 1885-2027) (E: "Peek & Cloppenburg: from the Nieuwendijk to the Dam", uuisrd9acyvij4c8q238znen): two clothiers from the Cloppenburg area of Oldenburg started in Rotterdam in 1869, opened on the Nieuwendijk by 1885 and moved into their limestone palace on the Dam in 1917; one of the German textile dynasties (with C&A, V&D, Sinkel) that built Dutch retail. Who: German (Oldenburg/Westphalia). Era: 1869-1917. Status: open (leaving the Dam in early 2027). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia, Peek & Cloppenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peek_%26_Cloppenburg); [DUIC, Peek & Cloppenburg Utrecht](https://www.duic.nl/cultuur/verdwenen-winkels-peek-cloppenburg-aan-de-oudegracht/). Caveats: some sources call the founders Dutch merchants; their origin around Cloppenburg is the majority view.

13. **IJssalon Koco** (Van Woustraat 149; 1941) (E: "IJssalon Koco, where the road to the February Strike began", egatu1m4gzff5z1dhwthzfmv): ice-cream parlour of two German-Jewish refugees, Ernst Cahn and Alfred Kohn; the fight there on 19 February 1941 led to Cahn being the first person executed by firing squad in the occupied Netherlands. Who: German-Jewish refugees. Era: 1930s-1941. Status: changed (stone by the door). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia, Ernst Cahn](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Cahn); [Joods Monument](https://www.joodsmonument.nl/). Caveats: Jewish slice overlap.

14. **International Institute of Social History** (founded at Keizersgracht 264 in 1935; now Cruquiusweg 31) (E: "International Institute of Social History (IISG)", qqrvdnqxlfk1cilbdfbyputg): N.W. Posthumus set it up in 1935 to rescue the archives of movements persecuted in Germany, Austria and Russia, with exiles such as the Russian Menshevik Boris Nikolaevsky; in 1938 it acquired the exiled German SPD's archive with the Marx and Engels papers; it also holds the archive of the exile publisher Allert de Lange. Who: German, Austrian and Russian political exiles (and their papers). Era: 1935-1940. Status: open (at the later site). Evidence: [IISG, detailed history](https://iisg.amsterdam/en/about/history/detailed-history-iish); existing text; [IISG, Allert de Lange archive](https://iisg.nl/collections/allertdelange). Caveats: the live place is the Cruquiusweg building, not the 1935-1937 Keizersgracht house; the paragraph has to say where it began.

15. **Jan van Eijckstraat 16, where the Wiener Library began** (E: "Jan van Eijckstraat 16: where the Wiener Library began", tppsxewme1azq9zi0zkuqa63): Alfred Wiener, a German-Jewish refugee, ran his documentation office against Nazism from here in the 1930s; the archive reached London in 1939. Who: German-Jewish refugee. Era: 1933-1939. Status: changed (flats). Evidence: existing text; [Wiener Holocaust Library](https://wienerholocaustlibrary.org/). Caveats: Jewish slice overlap.

16. **Allert de Lange, the second exile publisher** (Damrak 62; 1933-1940) (N): the Amsterdam firm's German department, led by Walter Landauer and Hermann Kesten, printed 91 books by writers banned in Germany (Joseph Roth, Stefan Zweig, Brecht among them); the Nazis seized its archive in 1940, which came back via Moscow in 1991. Landauer died in Bergen-Belsen. Who: German exile writers. Era: 1933-1940. Status: changed (Damrak shop building; check current use). Evidence: [Stadsarchief, Exil literatuur](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/stukken/tweede-wereldoorlog/exil-literatuur/); [IISG, Allert de Lange archive (Damrak 62)](https://iisg.nl/collections/allertdelange); [Leo Baeck Institute, Allert de Lange](https://lbi.org/german-exile-publishers/allert-de-lange). Caveats: could be one combined place with Querido ("the exile publishers") with Querido as the pin. The existing "Damrak walk" cluster (adqipies5fphb49b9bd6601m) is next door.

17. **Castrum Peregrini** (Herengracht 401; 1942-1945) (N): the German poet and anti-Nazi exile Wolfgang Frommel lived from 1942 with the artist Gisèle van Waterschoot van der Gracht in her flat, where Jewish youths hid; it became a publishing house and foundation (now H401). Who: German exile, German-Jewish refugees in hiding. Era: 1940-1945. Status: open (H401 cultural centre). Evidence: [H401, Dealing with a complex past](https://h401.org/2018/10/dealing-with-a-complex-past/11103/); [Stadsarchief, Exil literatuur (Castrum Peregrini as clandestine press)](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/stukken/tweede-wereldoorlog/exil-literatuur/); [Kenan Malik, In the fortress of the pilgrims](https://kenanmalik.com/2016/05/08/in-the-fortress-of-the-pilgrims/). Caveats: the later abuse revelations around Frommel's circle are well documented; any text must mention them soberly or the place should go to Borderline.

18. **Diamond Exchange, Belgian refugees of 1914** (Weesperplein 4) (E: "Diamond Exchange on the Weesperplein (Diamantbeurs)", sg5x5jc3sih4rbjqsfsd6iny): when Antwerp fell in October 1914 about a million Belgians crossed into the Netherlands; with the diamond trade at a standstill, Belgian refugees were lodged in the exchange. Who: Belgian refugees. Era: 1914-1918. Status: changed (offices/hotel). Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia, Belgian refugees in the Netherlands during WWI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_refugees_in_the_Netherlands_during_the_First_World_War); [1914-1918-online, Exile and migration (Netherlands)](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/exile-and-migration-the-netherlands/). Caveats: the best Amsterdam site I found for 1914; "Alida Jacobsdorp" (refugee houses) could not be located.

19. **Hirschgebouw** (Leidseplein 23-31; 1882/1912) (E: "Hirschgebouw, the fashion palace opened for men only", owooq6lk1d1u4nf3ix7lguw6): Hirsch & Cie, a Brussels fashion house led by Leo Hirsch, a Jewish entrepreneur from Altena in Westphalia, opened on the Leidseplein in 1882; Nazi expropriation closed it in 1943. Who: German-Jewish/Belgian. Era: 1882-1943. Status: changed (Apple store). Evidence: [nl.wikipedia via Wikiwand, Hirsch & Cie](https://www.wikiwand.com/nl/Hirsch_%26_Cie); [Leiden, Depth of Field](https://depthoffield.universiteitleiden.nl/0501a02/); existing text. Caveats: an incoming firm rather than an immigrant family settling; middle strength.

20. **Maison de Bonneterie** (Kalverstraat 181-183 / Olieslagerssteeg; 1889) (E: "Olieslagerssteeg, the lepers' house, the oil pressers and Maison de Bonneterie", rwae30mkxpyvv5tefp4hv8bv): founded in 1889 by Rosa Cohen-Wittgenstein, born in Warburg (Westphalia) in 1867, and her husband, selling woollen socks; a department store by 1909. Who: German-Jewish. Era: 1889-1909. Status: open (store on Rokin/Kalverstraat). Evidence: [Amsterdam Museum, The founder of Maison de Bonneterie](https://amsterdammuseum.nl/en/topic/women-of-amsterdam/contribution/147745-the-founder-of-maison-bonneterie); [Wikipedia, Maison de Bonneterie](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_de_Bonneterie). Caveats: the original shop at 181 later housed H&M; existing place is a lane cluster.

21. **C&A on the Nieuwendijk and the Damrak** (Nieuwendijk 193 in 1893; Damrak 70-79) (E: "Beurspassage, the 'Amsterdam Oersoep'", lt6ywkmufnv86538dkwd63hx): the Brenninkmeyer brothers were "Tödden", travelling linen traders from Mettingen in Westphalia; after Sneek they opened in Amsterdam in 1893 and from 1906 Bernard Joseph Brenninkmeyer built the cheap ready-to-wear formula here; their big Damrak store burned in 1963. Who: German (Westphalian). Era: 1893-1945. Status: changed (former C&A complex now shops, passage art). Evidence: [nl.wikipedia, C&A](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%26A); [Cofra Holding, heritage](https://www.cofraholding.com/en/about/heritage/beginnings/); existing text. Caveats: link to the Beurspassage is via the 1963 C&A fire only; might better be a new pin at Nieuwendijk 193 (current use unknown).

22. **Vroom & Dreesmann's first shop** (Weesperstraat 70; 1887) (N, gone): Anton Dreesmann, born 1854 in Haselünne (Emsland), and his brother-in-law Willem Vroom opened Magazijn De Zon here on 21 May 1887, the first of the V&D department stores. Who: German (Emsland). Era: 1887. Status: gone (Weesperstraat rebuilt as a through road). Evidence: [nl.wikipedia, Vroom & Dreesmann](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vroom_%26_Dreesmann); [Wikipedia (en), V&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vroom_%26_Dreesmann); [Amsterdam.info on Dreesmann](https://www.amsterdam.info/shopping/vd). Caveats: site gone; could be told inside the existing Weesperstraat place (k36tgrxl35wzoa60fgl3shcr) or via the Dreesmannhuis (jl4tppxctwoj569r7xx37jgx, the son's house; weaker). The 1912 Kalverstraat store (F.M.J. Caron) is an alternative pin; number not confirmed.

23. **Winkel van Sinkel** (Nieuwendijk 174-176; 1822-1903) (N, gone): Anton Sinkel, born 1785 in Cloppenburg, opened his drapery here in 1822, the start of the store whose name became the Dutch byword for a shop that sells everything; young Krasnapolsky worked here. Who: German (Oldenburg). Era: 1822-1903. Status: gone (building demolished 1903; site later HEMA per Wikipedia). Evidence: [Rijksmuseum, drawing of the Winkel van Sinkel on the Nieuwendijk](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Gezicht-op-de-Winkel-van-Sinkel-op-de-Nieuwendijk--c5f20132ead2f8aa2bdedb8d387e466f); [Wikipedia, Winkel van Sinkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winkel_van_Sinkel); [nl.wikipedia, A.W. Krasnapolsky](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_Wilhelm_Krasnapolsky). Caveats: opening year 1820/1821/1822 and house numbers vary; the famous building is the Utrecht branch. Better as one "German shopkeepers of the Nieuwendijk" cluster with P&C and C&A.

24. **Kaiser Wilhelm Schule** (Vijzelgracht 9; 1908) (E: "Vijzelgracht corner, Walloon orphans, Holtkamp's cakes and a German school", nu9fdr9m85u45ybyrzwehfke): Jan Stuyt's school built for Amsterdam's German community; by 1934 De Tribune called it a Nazi school, and from 1942 the Dutch Nazi youth trained here. Who: German residents. Era: 1908-1945. Status: changed. Evidence: existing text (cites the 1934 Tribune); [Anne Frank House research on German schools in Amsterdam](https://research.annefrank.org/en/onderwerpen/7eaef6db-5d69-466c-a948-9d51fa897c9b). Caveats: only one independent source found; needs Stadsarchief confirmation. Shows the settled German community as well as the refugees.

25. **Gerhard Badrian's stone** (Rubensstraat 26; 1944) (E: "Gerhard Badrian's stone, Rubensstraat 26", cemiwv2z5qoml4p8tkr9rmz0): a German-Jewish refugee turned resistance fighter, shot here in 1944. Who: German-Jewish refugee. Era: 1933-1944. Status: memorial stone. Evidence: existing text; [Joods Monument, Gerhard Badrian](https://www.joodsmonument.nl/). Caveats: Jewish slice overlap.

26. **Truus Wijsmuller's bust** (Bachplein) (E: "Truus Wijsmuller's bust, Bachplein", c713iaaetg58mpsy5i5t1l4z): the Amsterdam woman who organised the Kindertransports of 1938-1940 that brought thousands of children out of Germany and Austria. Who: refugee children. Era: 1938-1940. Status: memorial. Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia, Truus Wijsmuller-Meijer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truus_Wijsmuller-Meijer). Caveats: Jewish slice overlap; she is the helper, not the arrival.

27. **Refugee house plaque, Zuideinde 443** (E: "Refugee house plaque, Zuideinde 443", refa3obwkah8361e9reb3uvt): 30 of the 75 Jewish refugees from Germany and Eastern Europe sheltered around Oostzaan in 1933-1939 lived in this one house. Who: German and Polish Jewish refugees. Era: 1933-1939. Status: plaque. Evidence: existing text; [Joods Monument](https://www.joodsmonument.nl/). Caveats: Jewish slice overlap; one of few Noord items.

28. **The Lloyd building on the Prins Hendrikkade** (Prins Hendrikkade 33; c. 1920) (E: "The Lloyd building, the 'gilded coffin' on the Prins Hendrikkade", jnr5senvcx9anonwf0ijqybk): the head office the Koninklijke Hollandsche Lloyd paid for from its wartime emigrant trade to South America. Who: emigrants (company side). Era: 1915-1922. Status: changed. Evidence: existing text; [Wikipedia, Koninklijke Hollandsche Lloyd](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijke_Hollandsche_Lloyd). 29. **Tonia Stieltjesplein** (Centrum; named 2025) (N): new square for Tonia Stieltjes (1881-1932), born in the Czaar Peterstraat to Christiaan Milgens, an Afro-Surinamese man born into slavery who bought his freedom, went to sea and settled in Amsterdam in 1879; she became the first Black woman trade unionist in the Netherlands and was painted a dozen times by Jan Sluijters. Who: Afro-Surinamese. Era: 1879-1932. Status: open (street sign with explanatory plate). Evidence: [Gemeenteblad 2025 nr. 203782](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2025-203782.pdf); [Amsterdam Museum, Activist, painter's model and willpower doctor](https://www.amsterdammuseum.nl/en/topic/women-of-amsterdam/contribution/106055-activist-painters-model-and-willpower-doctor). Caveats: exact location of the square not found (situation drawing only); must check on the map before pinning. Strong story, weak place until located.

30. **Ons Suriname, founded 1919** (Zeeburgerdijk 19-23) (E: "Suriname Museum in the 1882 double school", nmmp3r0a58nbohv69ztzjvjq): the oldest Surinamese association in the Netherlands, founded in Amsterdam on 18 January 1919 as a Bond van Surinamers for the few hundred Surinamese then in the country; in this building since 1975, now the Suriname Museum. Who: Surinamese. Era: 1919 onward. Status: open (museum). Evidence: [DutchCulture, Society Our Suriname](https://dutchculture.nl/en/location/society-our-suriname); [NOS on the Suriname Museum](https://nos.nl/l/2527684); existing text. Caveats: building is postwar; coordinate with postwar-colonial slice, which will likely take it.

31. **Old Rijksakademie and Heinrich Campendonk** (Stadhouderskade 86) (E: "Old Rijksakademie, now De Ateliers", qdjeb50akdgdykge8i2sosrn): the German expressionist Campendonk, dismissed as "degenerate" in 1933, became professor at the Rijksakademie in 1935 and stayed in Amsterdam until his death in 1957. Who: German exile. Era: 1935-1957. Status: changed (De Ateliers). Evidence: [Wikipedia, Heinrich Campendonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Campendonk); existing church place (t1y45gzdmjeeo6vsbmxhks9e) names him. Caveats: dates from Wikipedia/RKD not re-read in this pass; verify before writing. Existing text does not mention him.

32. **Carlton Hotel site, Vijzelstraat** (Vijzelstraat 4-8) (E: "The Carlton crash and the gap it left", hg3xo96hfhmusbpegw2sui6c): the Stadsarchief's 1930s jazz research puts the Vijzelstraat at the heart of a migrant music scene: musicians from Suriname, the Caribbean, the US and Eastern Europe lived on the street, and the Carlton's stage was where Louis Armstrong (1933) and Cab Calloway (1934) first played for Dutch audiences. Who: Afro-Surinamese, Caribbean, American musicians. Era: 1930s. Status: changed. Evidence: [Stadsarchief, Jazz-jaren](https://www.amsterdam.nl/stadsarchief/agenda/jazz-jaren-1/); [VPRO OVT on Jazz-jaren](https://www.vpro.nl/ovt/artikelen/jazzjaren-de-vergeten-soundtrack-van-jaren-dertig-amsterdam). Caveats: visiting stars are not immigrants; only worth it if a resident musician's address on the Vijzelstraat can be pinned (book: Mark Ponte, Jazz-jaren, WBooks 2026).

- **Italian ice-cream makers**: Tuscan (Bagni di Lucca) families such as Tofani came to Amsterdam ([Openluchtmuseum, Venezia](https://www.openluchtmuseum.nl/nl/verdiep/venezia)), and a 1935 protest by Dutch ice-makers led to curbs on Italians; but no surviving pre-1945 Amsterdam parlour found (Monte Pelmo dates from 1957, Tofani has no founding date). Could be told in the guestworkers slice.
- **Italian terrazzo workers (Friulians)**: established in Amsterdam and The Hague from the late 19th century ([IISG](https://archief.socialhistory.org/en/today/08-28/italian-terrazzo-workers)), but no Amsterdam workshop or signed floor found.
- **Perhimpunan Indonesia**: Indonesian students' nationalist society (Hatta), based in Leiden and The Hague; no Amsterdam premises found. A Leiden thesis ("Fighting against Empire in Mokum") may name venues; worth a second pass.

## Report

- Counts: 32 shortlist items, 26 (E) and 6 (N) (Querido, Allert de Lange, Castrum Peregrini, V&D first shop (gone), Winkel van Sinkel (gone), Tonia Stieltjesplein).
- Top ten: Lloyd Hotel; Binnen Bantammerstraat; Tuschinski; Beckmann's studio; Herberg Zeeburg; Querido Verlag; Colonial Institute facade; Reestraat 6 (De Kom); Museumplein 1883 exhibition; Carré.
- Odd things: the Deep Amsterdam pool already holds most of this era's arrival places, often with the immigrant story only in passing (Krasnapolsky, Museumplein, Diamond Exchange, IISG, Beurspassage/C&A), so the work is mostly extra paragraphs. The German retail dynasties (Sinkel, C&A, P&C, V&D, Hirsch, Maison de Bonneterie, Hunkemöller) would read best as one Nieuwendijk/Kalverstraat cluster. The Italian, Belgian and Indonesian strands have strong history but no good surviving Amsterdam place. The Stadsarchief's 2026 "Jazz-jaren" research (Mark Ponte) is a fresh, authoritative source for 1930s Black and Caribbean Amsterdam; its book should be mined for addresses. Tonia Stieltjesplein needs its location confirmed.
