## New places

### De Aker

401. **The Valutabuurt: streets named after money that vanished**; Ecuplein, pin at Marinus Boezem's *Tectona Grandis* discs, 52.348503, 4.785514 (De Aker, cell L5). In 1997 the city named the square at the heart of De Aker the Ecuplein, after the ECU, the European Community's unit of account, and the streets around it after the currencies of Europe: Guldenpromenade, Marksingel, Frankenstraat, Lirestraat, Pesetalaan, Drachmesingel, Escudolaan, Schillingdijk, Forintplantsoen, Zlotylaan, Litasweg, Roebelkade, Kroonweg, Pond Sterlinglaan, with small courts for the coins (Centimehof, Centavoshof, Pennyhof, Örehof) and the quay by the entrance gate called Eurokade, which the city register still explains as "name of the future European currency". The ECU was replaced by the euro on 1 January 1999, and within a few years most of the rest were gone too: a neighbourhood of dead money. The theme kept growing after the euro: in 2017 a resident proposed the Lirabrug, named for the Turkish lira, and in 2022 the cycle path along the Guldenpromenade became the Dirhampad, after the Moroccan dirham. Around the square: the 400-metre Dukaat block (Tangram, 1995 to 2001), its shopping centre named after an old gold coin; the 54-metre Zorro tower (1999), which its architects named after the masked hero's straight back and swirling cape, the windows climbing in a spiral; Marijke de Goey's *Kubuspoort*, a city gate of stacked cubes on the Eurokade; Boezem's 33 polished granite discs that mirror the sky. Fits: a curiosity of naming with a joke the planners could not know in 1997, in an empty grid cell (L5) of a district the recall found "thin on documented curiosities". Leads: BAG register (beschrijvingNaam for Ecuplein, Eurokade, Guldenpromenade, Valutaboulevard, Dirhampad and the rest); https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuplein ; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorro_(gebouw) ; https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2017-52651.pdf (Lirabrug: "de straten in deze buurt zijn vernoemd naar Europese valuta"); https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/gmb-2022-64181.html (Dirhampad, 8 Feb 2022); buitenkunstamsterdam.nl records 618 (Kubuspoort), 619 (Tectona Grandis), 925 (John Blake). Look for: the street signs; the Zorro's spiral of windows; the Kubuspoort; the granite discs; in the walkway through the Dukaat (Pieter Calandlaan), John Blake's light box of seven photos of the Akerpolder's market-garden past and the ceiling panels in pink, apple green, lime, cornflower blue and deep red at the block's gates. Caveats: the naming year 1997 rests on nl.wikipedia (the register dates are 2014 administrative entries); nl.wikipedia's claim of a hydro power plant in the Zorro tower has no second source, leave it out; the Blake light box and the art have no dated photo found (`check: Still there?` for the light box only); round A dropped "De Aker's gateway art" as having no hook, the money theme is what is new. Rank: **Okay** (Nice if the user likes naming jokes). New place.

### Sloten/Nieuw-Sloten

402. **Velodrome Amsterdam and the Sloten cycling circuit**; Sportpark Sloten, entrance Sloterweg 1045, pin on the velodrome hall, 52.339599, 4.807689 (Sloten/Nieuw-Sloten, cell N8). One of only three covered cycling tracks in the Netherlands: a 200-metre track of wooden boards banked at up to 47 degrees, built in the open air in 1972 to a design by the German track builders Schürmann and roofed over in 1998 so it would stop wearing out. It hosted the Six Days of Amsterdam from 2001 and national track championships; ASC Olympia, founded in 1898 and the oldest cycling club in the country, trains here. In 2021 the surface was relaid in Siberian pine ("no more splinters when you fall", the riders told NH Nieuws), with a section that lifts to let ambulances into the infield and LED pacing lights; in 2024 the Dutch 3x3 basketball women trained in the infield for their Olympic qualifier. Around the whole sports park (1956) runs an asphalt road-racing circuit, which the city's 2026 plan wants to extend with a mountain-bike course along the A4. Fits: a rare, steep wooden track where top riders and clubs train, at the polder edge of the village; cell O7's reason in COVERAGE.md named the sports park as having nothing. Leads: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velodrome_Amsterdam ; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportpark_Sloten ; https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/293275/wielerbaan-velodrome-vernieuwd-geen-splinters-meer-als-je-valt (16 Oct 2021); https://nos.nl/artikel/2516197 (3x3 basketball, 2024); https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/groter-aanbod-ook-buiten-verenigingsverband-op-sportpark-sloten/ (21 Aug 2026). Look for: Joseph Ongenae's painted *Betonplastiek* (1973) in front of the hall (round 1 item 20). Caveats: a working sports venue, open to the public only at races and events (no fixed open sessions found); the sources disagree on the last Six Days here (2014 or 2016) and on the circuit's length (2.5 km in nl.wikipedia, 5 km in the 2026 plan), `check: Facts`; **Outlook: Major change ahead** (the 2026 sports-park plan moves the car entrance to the Anderlechtlaan and lifts the circuit over it; plan not fixed, no dates); round B dropped sports parks elsewhere as leisure, so this is the user's call. Rank: **Okay**. New place, or drop.

## Merges into existing places

403. **"Verzetsheldenbuurt memorial, Joop Woortmanplein"**: the place counts 25 resistance names around the square, but De Aker's naming reaches much further. The western streets honour people who stood up to dictatorships worldwide: Gandhi (M. Gandhilaan), Victor Jara, Orlando Letelier (Allende's foreign minister), Archbishop Óscar Romero and Marianella García Villas of El Salvador, Eduardo Mondlane of Frelimo, Ruth First, Dietrich Bonhoeffer, Mordechai Anielewicz of the Warsaw ghetto rising, Jaroslav Seifert, James Baldwin. The 1990 bridges over the Ernst Cahnsingel, unnamed for years, carry the names of Truus Wijsmuller (who got Jewish children out of Central Europe), Titus Brandsma, and three people of the resistance in the Dutch East Indies: Louis Welter, Wil Meelhuysen and Mother Alacoque, the nun who ran the Charitas hospital in Palembang under Japanese occupation. One paragraph and a Look for line. Sources: BAG register (beschrijvingNaam for each street and bridge); nl.wikipedia Truus Wijsmullerbrug, Titus Brandsmabrug, Louis J. Welterbrug, W.A. Meelhuysenbrug (bridges of about 1990, named by 2007, designed by N. Wijnmaalen of Publieke Werken). Caveat: the streets are 300 to 600 m west of the pin (Gandhilaan 52.354753, 4.777515, cell K4), so it is a "Look for" walk, not a view from the square.

404. **"Noorderakerweg and Zuiderakerweg, the polder roads De Aker kept"**: the double house at Noorderakerweg 21 and 23 (52.354026, 4.782054, cell K4), with its high roof, is one of the few tuinder houses on the road that were not demolished, and the setting of Jeroen Wielaert's novella in *Nieuw West Side Stories* 8 (Boekenweek 2025): his mother Minnie (born 1928) grew up at no. 21 with her parents Rob and Geertje Frederiksen, who hid a young man, Ferdinand Roskam, who refused to sign the loyalty declaration and work in Germany; the family saw the bombing of Schiphol and a German bomber crash in the polder, far enough from the house. The book is based on fact filled out with the author's imagination, so the body should say so. One Look for line. Source: https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/oorlog-en-overspel-een-onderduiker-in-de-akerpolder/ (14 Mar 2025, with a 1934 family photo of the house). Caveat: private homes, outside only.

405. **"Molen van Sloten"**, three updates:
- since March 2025 a glass ceiling under the cap lets visitors watch the whole drive, from the sails through the upright shaft (*koningsspil*) down to the scoop wheel, after the climb to the top loft was closed as unsafe; the display was renewed after 30 years (water management and mill technique below, a miller's life in the 1847 part above);

408. **"Sloterweg village houses"**: Sloterweg 1236, on the corner with the Osdorperweg (52.341620, 4.797484), was the *Electrische banket- en broodbakkerij Huizinga*, one of four bakers in a village where Catholics and Protestants each shopped at their own; Harrie Holla and Truus de Waal ran a grocer's there from 1956 to 1975 and left the baker's painted advertisement ("BAKKERIJ BROOD KOEK BANKET CHOCOLADE SUIKERWERKEN") on the side wall, which the next owners painted out in the late 1970s. The shop window survives because the village is a protected townscape; the history group is still looking for a colour photo of the sign (Oct 2026). One Look for line. Source: https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/welke-kleuren-had-de-muurschildering-van-bakkerij-huizinga/ (18 Oct 2026). Caveat: private house, outside only; do not invite readers to send photos.
