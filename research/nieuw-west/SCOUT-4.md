## New places

### De Aker/Sloten/Nieuw-Sloten

301. **Nieuw Sloten, the Olympic village that wasn't**; pin on Rik van Dolderen's *Baldakijn* between the Kasterleepark and the Belgiëplein, Antwerpenbaan, 52.346271, 4.810838 (Sloten/Nieuw-Sloten, cell M8). From 1960 this was the Sloten market-garden area, itself the refuge of growers pushed out by the Overtoomse Veld. Amsterdam bid for the 1992 Olympics in 1984 and pencilled its Olympic village in here; from 1985 the city negotiated with the growers, and around 1986 it bought out 32 of them (18 rebuilt near Aalsmeer, the rest went to the Flevopolder and Noordoostpolder). In October 1986 the Games went to Barcelona, but the empty land sped up the housing plan: politicians planted a chestnut in the former greenhouse area in October 1990, the first homes were ready in 1991, tram 2 was extended over the Antwerpenbaan the same year, and the district was finished by 1996. It was the "compact city" answer to the exodus to Purmerend and Almere: low-rise at high density (50 to 80 homes a hectare), fourteen sectors each given to a different architect (Sjoerd Soeters's water villas on the Blankenbergestraat, "teetering between art and kitsch" per the architecture guide; Koen van Velsen; Groos and De Jong), streets named after Belgian towns, and the BNA's environmental architecture prize in 1996. The Vrije Geer referendum of 1995 stopped the last field next to the old village. Fits: the recall's biggest miss; the Olympic origin and the bought-out growers give a whole 1990s district a story a visitor can read on the street. Leads: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuw_Sloten ; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/nieuws/410063/jarig-winkelcentrum-nieuw-sloten-over-verkassende-tuinders-en (Sept 2024); https://www.architectureguide.nl/project/item/prj_id/486/alfa/N ; https://edepot.wur.nl/261226 ; buitenkunstamsterdam.nl records 715 (Baldakijn) and 714 (Lamellenhek); Geheugen van West 794. Look for: the *Baldakijn* and the *Lamellenhek* fence in the Kasterleepark (Van Dolderen, mid-1990s); Soeters's houses on the Blankenbergestraat; in the jazz quarter east of the Plesmanlaan (Ben Websterstraat, Duke Ellingtonstraat, Bessie Smithbrug), Irini Schrijer's untitled 1993 stone plateau with a tilted steel frame, locally "Plateau met lijst", 52.344390, 4.824223 (cell M10). Caveats: the shopping centre's renovation (ten months from Sept 2024) should be finished, check; the Rodi article gives two completion dates (1994, 1996): use "mid-1990s" or 1996 (nl.wikipedia: "halverwege de jaren negentig"); the chestnut tree not located; round B dropped "Nieuw-Sloten as a whole" as architecture without a story, the Olympic and buy-out story is what is new. Rank: **Nice**. New place.

302. **The Rooie Reus, Sloten's champion copper beech**; garden of the former pastorie behind the Sloterkerk, Osdorperweg 28, pin approx. 52.342300, 4.797900 (Sloten/Nieuw-Sloten, cell M6). A copper beech planted around 1865, with a trunk over 5 m round, voted Amsterdam's most beautiful tree by Het Parool readers in 2011 and now one of the city's 119 "kampioenbomen" (champion trees). It stands in a private garden; its owners have it scanned every few years, and in June 2023 tree surgeons fitted slack crown anchors so two long limbs cannot fall on people on the path to the Vink en Boer building; a saddle fungus on one limb is harmless and may give bats a roost. Fits: a named, celebrated tree in the village with a living story; Sloten has five of the borough's champion trees. Leads: Geheugen van West 18124 "Rooie Reus van Sloten" (2012); https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/de-rode-beuk-van-sloten-krijgt-veiligheidslijnen/ (30 Jun 2023); https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/bijzondere-bomen-in-sloten-en-oud-osdorp/ (21 Jul 2025); https://maps.amsterdam.nl/bomen_bijzonder_nieuwwest/ . Look for: the other champion trees, the Oriental plane of 1910 opposite the Sint-Pancratiuskerk (Sloterweg 1186) and, at Tuinpark Ons Buiten, an Osage orange of 1950. Caveats: private garden, seen from the path beside the church; pin to be set on site (`check: Location`); the Parool vote year rests on Geheugen van West. Rank: **Nice**. New place, or merge into **"Sloterkerk"** (pin within 25 m).

303. **Noorder- and Zuiderakerweg, the polder roads De Aker kept**; Noorderakerweg 47, 52.353023, 4.780966 (De Aker, cell K4); Zuiderakerweg 41, 52.348444, 4.791553 (cell L6). Two narrow roads of the Middelveldsche Akerpolder, a peat polder dug out for peat in 1876 to 1896 and then market gardens and greenhouses, survive inside the 1990s to 2004 new town of De Aker because the Dorpsraad and a Werkgroep Akerwegen fought to keep them; a few tuinder houses still line them, while the farmland, cows and greenhouses behind went under the estate. Nel Harkema, born at Noorderakerweg 47 in 1955 and living there again, filmed her memories for the Sloten history group in August 2026: milkman and baker coming by horse and cart from Oud Osdorp, and the Akerbad, an open-air pool on the Ringvaart dike at the old Akersluisweg, closed by decision of January 1974 when the dike line moved. Fits: the visible remnant of the polder the recall remembered as greenhouses; a lane of old houses wedged into a new town. Leads: https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/nel-harkema-waar-koeien-graasden-staan-nu-huizen/ (8 Sep 2026); https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middelveldsche_Akerpolder ; Geheugen van West 14726, 41674, 34102. Caveats: private homes, outside only; "partly kept" is the source's word, so the walk needs a check of which stretches survive. Rank: **Okay**. New place.

### Osdorp

304. **The Wereldgroente greenhouses, Osdorperweg 937**; 52.379345, 4.761352 (Lutkemeer/Ookmeer, cell E1). The last working greenhouse complex at the west end of the Osdorperweg rents out 25 to 35 m² plots under glass, with a climate computer and bees for pollination, as part of a Living Lab of growers, seed breeders and universities in a neighbouring greenhouse (first "Stadslandgoed" initiative, opened spring 2024). Most tenants grow Surinamese and other "world vegetables" that are dear or no longer imported, such as Surinamese okra (also used as a hair wash); tens of Amsterdammers come every week and it has turned into a social meeting place nobody planned. Fits: the recall's "Akerpolder greenhouses", the living end of Sloten-Osdorp's market-garden past. Leads: https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/353491/tuinders-kweken-surinaamse-groenten-in-osdorp-ik-krijg-energie-hiervan (19 Oct 2025); https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/droog-en-slakvrij-eigen-biologische-groenten-verbouwen/ (7 Mar 2024). Caveats: a commercial rental, no visitor access beyond what tenants allow; the complex's own history not found; the NH piece gives no house number (the 2024 post does). Rank: **Okay**. New place, or drop (user's call).

305. **Haci Bayram Mosque, the former School met de Bijbel**; Osdorperweg 386, 52.358485, 4.789289 (Osdorp-Midden, cell J5). Built around 1931 as the Protestant Mr. J. Terpstra school. In the war German soldiers slept in its classrooms and gym while headmaster Marinus Willemsen lived next door; from September 1944 he commanded the Binnenlandse Strijdkrachten (the united resistance) of Sloten and Osdorp, with a German officer billeted in his house while he distributed illegal papers and hid people in hiding, among them a Jewish girl. The school closed in the late 1970s; a Turkish cultural centre moved in in 1982, a prayer room in 1993, and since 1997 the Haci Bayram association owns it; the gym is now the prayer hall. In 2003 someone threw a hand grenade inside (no arrests). In 2014 the mosque hosted a 5 May freedom meal where old pupils told the war story. Fits: a Christian village school turned mosque that remembers its resistance headmaster. Leads: https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/5-mei-viering-aan-de-osdorperweg-386/ (May 2014, from 4en5meiamsterdam.nl/53558); https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/5-mei-in-de-haci-bayram-moskee-en-de-serre/ (May 2014); Geheugen van West 15886, 15874. Caveats: the 4en5mei page and a 2025 to 2026 sign of activity were not reached (the 4en5mei URL answers 301); visitors only at open days. Rank: **Nice**. New place.

### Geuzenveld-Slotermeer

306. **The Light Kite (Tijdmakers)**; Sloterpark shore past the Sloterparkbad, 52.365117, 4.815195 (Slotermeer-Zuidoost, cell H9). A large steel kite of light by Saskia Hoogendoorn and Lieuwe Martijn Wijnands, made for the Amsterdam Light Festival of 2016 and hung on a tall pole by the Sloterplas since January 2019, lit every evening; the makers wanted the feeling of endless summer kite-flying in winter, and most Dutch viewers hear André Hazes singing "De vlieger". A resident who stumbled on it in 2018 had to search the internet to find out what it was. Fits: a festival piece that found a permanent home in a garden-city park. Leads: buitenkunstamsterdam.nl record 1030; https://amsterdamlightfestival.com/en/artworks/the-light-kite ; Geheugen van West 28483 (Oct 2018). Caveats: Geheugen van West's 2018 date predates the 2019 placement given by Commons (check: Facts, which year it arrived); who owns it is not stated. Rank: **Okay**. New place, or a Look for line in "Sloterplas: the lake dug for sand".

307. **Fruittuin van Moerkerken**; Van Moerkerkenstraat 83, 52.378563, 4.811018 (Slotermeer-West, cell E8). Artists Roel Schoenmakers and Fiona de Bell (Cascoland) turned the public lawns at the end of the street into fruit gardens where residents also cook and make preserves, with the garden's harvest and fruit and vegetables rescued from the Plein '40-'45 market. Fits: a recall pick; a garden-city lawn put to use. Lead: https://www.iamsterdam.com/zien-en-doen/natuur-en-actief/routes/wandelroute-door-cultuur-in-nieuw-west . Caveats: one source, undated; no start year found (`check: Still there?`). Rank: **Okay**. New place.

308. **Jan Cupidohof and the lifeboat hofjes**; Jan Cupidohof, 52.374138, 4.823924 (Slotermeer-Zuidoost, cell F10). Three identical courts on the Noordoever of the Sloterplas (1954 to 1955) named after lifeboat skippers who saved hundreds: Jan Cupido of the *Brandaris*, Dorus Rijkers of Den Helder and Frans Naerebout; even the culvert nearby is the Dirk de Waterduikerbrug. Cornelis Keesman (of the listed Keesmanblokken) laid out each as a parallelogram of red-roofed low flats, and Jakoba Mulder went with him to photograph them on completion. First residents moved in on 31 August 1955; in 2009 residents fought demolition plans, and Stadgenoot renovated 258 homes in the Dorus Rijkershof in 2022 to 2023. Leads: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Cupidohof ; Geheugen van West 9216 (2009). Caveats: private homes, the courts are open; renovation status needs a check. Rank: **Okay**. New place, or drop.

### Slotervaart

309. **'t Landje, the hut-building island in the Rembrandtpark**; Staalmeesterslaan 197A, 52.365128, 4.846415 (Overtoomse Veld, cell H13; pin the island on site). A "bouwspeelplaats" on an island among the trees where children of 6 to 14 build their own huts, cook, keep ponies and canoe, run since the early 1970s, when the park was a wasteland; Piet de Bake was its "mayor" for 43 years until 2016, Susan Koenen's IDFA documentary *Het Wilde Westen* (2010) is about it, and Het Parool called it the best playground in the city. Still open year-round (Tuesday to Saturday) with its own pony club. Fits: Amsterdam's own wild west, a place a curious visitor with children would never find. Leads: Geheugen van West 12757, 11489, 6432; https://www.iamsterdam.com/en/whats-on/calendar/shopping/shops/het-landje ; kidsproof.nl listing. Caveats: founding year and De Bake's dates rest on Geheugen van West; `check: Location` for the pin. Rank: **Nice**. New place (the live "Rembrandtpark (western half)" pin is 300 m away and does not mention it).

310. **Westbeat and the dancers' Super Space**; Rijnlandlaan 3 / Delflandlaan 2, 52.357647, 4.840118 (Westlandgracht, cell J12). Studioninedots's block by station Lelylaan (opened Nov 2020): 150 flats around a raised courtyard garden, standing on a public ground floor of 86 brick arches that open a single hall of 65 by 50 m and 8.5 m high to the street. Since September 2021 the dance company ICK Amsterdam has its "Space for Dance Art" there (five years in Sept 2026). Fits: the recall's pick for recent architecture; a big room for culture in a housing block at a station. Leads: gebouwdin.amsterdam.nl record 3604; https://www.designboom.com/architecture/arches-studioninedots-westbeat-housing-complex-amsterdam-02-13-2021/ ; https://cultureelpersbureau.nl/en/2026/09/space-for-dance-art-viert-vijfjarig-bestaan-met-house-in-motion/ . Caveats: the recall's "Gouden AAP" prize was not confirmed, leave it out; round B's self-check dropped ICK as "a dance company with no site story", the building is the story. Rank: **Okay**. New place, or a Look for line in "Lelylaan viaduct (Brug 705)".

## Merges into existing places

**Geheugen van West (sweep 4), facts not yet in the drafts:**

311. **"Sloterplas: the lake dug for sand"**: the north-west beach was nicknamed "Varkensbaai" (probably after the 1961 Bay of Pigs), a name the district itself used until it was renamed Sloterstrand in May 2017; the Cramergracht lock (about 1953 to 1955, remote-controlled since 19 June 2010) is the only boat link between city water (NAP -0.40 m) and the garden-city polder (NAP -2.10 m), which is why the Van Tienhovengracht dead-ends at Plein '40-'45; Constance van Terheijden's seven-circuit labyrinth in the Sloterpark opened on 11-11-11; in 1963 people walked over the ice from Slotermeer to Osdorp. Leads: Geheugen van West 6384, 11462, 21605, 15391, 22128. Caveats: labyrinth's current state not checked.

312. **"Kampschooltje, Osdorperweg 665"**: before the 1954 school, the camp of De Rolleman had a four-room camp school at about Osdorperweg 705, where pupils of the Catholic Jozefschool were taught whenever the Germans requisitioned their own school; the camp stood empty from June 1943 and Sinti and Roma families were deported in the national raid of 16 May 1944. Lead: Geheugen van West 15889 ("Oorlog in Nieuw-West", 2013). Caveats: check the 705 address and that the camp's residents were among those deported (`check: Facts` if not settled).

313. **"Sloterweg village houses"** (with round B's item 208, the butcher's lettering at Sloterweg 1233): the Jugendstil glass reads "Vleeschhouwerij J. Meijer"; Julius Meijer died in 1927, his sons Maurits (murdered in Sobibor with his wife Dina, 30 April 1943) and Liepman (Auschwitz, 30 December 1942) did not survive the war; the Koning family then ran the shop until about 1980. Leads: Geheugen van West 15894, 15888; https://slotenoudosdorp.nl/het-dagelijks-leven-in-en-rond-de-slotense-slagerij-koning/ (Sep 2024); joodsmonument.nl for the dates. Caveats: SCOUT-3 dropped "Jewish butcher Meijer in the Dorpsstraat (until 1932): no trace"; this is likely the same family, and the glass is the trace. `check: Facts` until Joods Monument confirms names and dates.

314. **"Akermolen (De Oude Molen)"**: in 1944 Croatian anti-aircraft gunners manned guns around the mill stump and slept in its loft. Lead: Geheugen van West 15877. Caveat: one memoir.

315. **"Blauwe Boog (Lixenberg)"**: when the blue arch was losing tiles in 2010, Buurtweb De Aker ran a mosaic course in the Akermolen so residents could repair it, and pupils of De Horizon laid the first stones; children call it "the tunnel". Lead: Geheugen van West 11723.

316. **"De Meervaart and the theatre in the Sloterplas"**: the Amsterdam Blues Festival started here in 1983; jazz bills brought Art Blakey, Dizzy Gillespie and a Stan Getz quartet. Lead: Geheugen van West 12560. Caveat: a volunteer's memoir also has Mandela visiting in June 1990; not confirmed, leave out.

317. **"De 1800 Roeden"**: after the liberation the Kruithuis sheds at Haarlemmerweg 711 were part of "Sectorpark Sloten", the internment system for people held as collaborators (in November 1945 the Sectorpark camp at Halfweg held 231 women and 1,022 men). Lead: Geheugen van West 15892, 12030. Caveat: `check: Facts` (which buildings, which camp).

318. **"Groot Landschap"**: locals call Couzijn's hill "de puist" (the pimple); a korfball coach had his team run up it ten times. Lead: Geheugen van West 2218 (2006).

319. **"Lelylaan viaduct (Brug 705)"**: the tram on the Cornelis Lelylaan (opened 9 September 1962, line 17) ran on its own grade-separated track with stairs-only stops at Derkinderenstraat, Johan Huizingalaan and Piet Wiedijkstraat, said to be the first such fast tram in the country. Lead: Geheugen van West 6879. Caveat: "first in the country" from one article; keep only with a second source.

320. **"Former Pascal Lyceum (Broedplaats LELY)"**: Joop van den Broek's plastic-and-glass facade work (1968) was wrecked by nesting birds and removed between 1979 and 1988; its fixing frame is still on the wall. Lead: Geheugen van West 21144 (Ronald Aker). Caveat: site check of the frame.

321. **"De Hoeksteen and ULO school (Zanstra)"**: young people occupied the church's youth centre on New Year's Eve 1971 until police cleared it. Lead: Geheugen van West 30619 (cites De Volkskrant, 6 and 8 January 1971).

322. **"Raesberg and the lost village of Raesdorp"**: at the west end of the Osdorperweg (about no. 981) the 1920 pumping station of the Osdorper Binnenpolder (De Eendracht) still works, and beside it the walls of a 1920 lock that let peat barges down almost 3 m into the Ringvaart were uncovered in 2011 and given an information panel on the Ringvaartdijk cycle path. Leads: Geheugen van West 14865, 13332, 14874. Caveats: the lock remains lie just over the line in Haarlemmerliede, so this is a Look for line only; the gemaal's pin and the board's survival need a site check.

**Champion trees (from 302's source):**

323. **"Oud-Osdorp cemetery and the Deckers tomb"**: the weeping ash of 1860 by the old cemetery (Osdorperweg near 532) is one of the borough's champion trees. Lead: slotenoudosdorp.nl (21 Jul 2025).

324. **"Sint-Pancratiuskerk (Sloten)"**: the Oriental plane opposite (1910) is a champion tree. Lead: as 323. (If 302 becomes its own place, this goes there as a Look for line instead.)

**Smaller, take or leave:**

325. **"Westereindflat"**: its first buyers called it "de hunkerbunker" because so many single people lived there (bought 1956, moved in April 1958). Lead: Geheugen van West 17099. Caveat: one resident.

326. **"Vrijheidscarillon"**: the largest bell is named after Wilhelmina, others after Juliana, Bernhard and the eleven provinces; market traders say it can play for hours on a Saturday. Leads: Geheugen van West 578, 6164. Caveat: check against the carillon's bell list.
