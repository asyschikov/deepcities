## Status checks (Nov 2026)

- Verrijzeniskerk: merged into parish De Vier Evangelisten in 2020; no regular masses since
  2023 (nl.wikipedia, reliwiki); on the long-term list for closure. The parish website
  still shows activities there in late 2026, so the text says "regular weekly masses ended",
  not "closed".
- De Ark: west facade demolished 2015 for 12 homes, renovated 2015–2016 (nl.wikipedia,
  reliwiki). Still a Protestant church and neighbourhood centre (recent Rodi items on the
  neighbourhood chaplain).
- Telephone exchange: decommissioned, now part of Plesman Plaza (MVSA, construction from
  May 2022, completion listed 2024). MVSA says a café and offices are planned inside; I did
  not confirm they have opened, so the text says "planned".
- Knijtijzer blocks: Rochdale doing maintenance since 2025, renovation scenarios due end
  2025, preferred scenario spring 2026 (Rochdale newsletter Sept 2025, seen as search
  snippet only; the PDF was too large to fetch). No later outcome found.
- Calvijn College: school left in 2015; Broedplaats LELY (Urban Resort), De Appel there
  2017–2024; The Hive redevelopment due 2027.
- Former MTS: Lumion school since 2019 after Atelier PRO renovation (2015–2019).
- Staalmanplein kiosks: a 2020 renovation design by Vanschagen Architecten exists
  (restoring steel frames and glazing); I could not confirm it was carried out, so it is
  left out of the text.
- Stibbe factory: now let as business units (listings show a storage firm and holdings at
  12-x). The register gives use "wonen", which looks wrong; left out.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- Verrijzeniskerk date: register, reliwiki, Commons and amsterdamopdekaart say 1965;
  nl.wikipedia says built 1959–1961. Used 1965 (register). Address: register 74-76,
  reliwiki/wiki 76 (76 is the presbytery).
- Calvariekerk architect: register says M.E. Hartman; reliwiki infobox reads "Hartman, M.E.
  H. Geels (1964)" and a Commons caption names H. Geels. Used the register. Reliwiki dates
  the Maasbach takeover to 1982; one unrelated-looking search snippet said a church owned
  by the Maasbach foundation since 1979 was sold in 2018, but it did not name this church,
  so the current congregation is stated only as of reliwiki (Pinkstergemeente).
- Telephone exchange: register 1958, nl.wikipedia 1958–1960 (opened 28 Oct 1960), MVSA 1959.
  Used 1958–1960.
- Dominicus Savio school: register spells the name "Savia"/"Savius"; used the saint's usual
  spelling Savio. A funda listing says "Huygens College ... built 1962"; register says opened
  1968. Used the register.
- Stibbe factory: register record has year 1964; the register text's Duintjer list gives
  "Mantelfabriek Stibbe (1961-1964)" and mentions a 1961 permit drawing. Used 1961–1964.
  The factory's link to the Stibbe firm rests only on the register text ("STIBBE" on the
  roof; "Mantelfabriek Stibbe").
- Bluebanddorp: register gives the architect as "Gemeentelijke Woningdienst / Gool, F.J. van";
  kept both.
- Lau Peters towers: register names them Edith Steinhuis / August Povelhuis; nl.wikipedia
  calls the first the "Elisabeth Steinflat" locally. Used "Edith Stein".

## Walking route idea

"Slotervaart's 1950s and 60s, Lelylaan to Westlandgracht" (about 4 km):
1. Former Pascal Lyceum (Broedplaats LELY)
2. Former Stibbe coat factory (Koningin Wilhelminaplein)
3. Calandlaan housing (Kleinhout and Van der Steur)
4. Bluebanddorp (sawtooth houses)
5. De Ark (Slotervaart)
6. Verrijzeniskerk
7. Plesmanlaan telephone exchange
8. Abraham Staalmanplein and the Staalman bear
9. Former Christian MTS (Lumion school)
10. Lau Peters towers (Voorburgstraat and Poeldijkstraat)

The Overtoomse Veld pair (Knijtijzer blocks, Vliegbasis De Huygens) and the Calvariekerk
fit a short separate loop along the Jan Evertsenstraat and Postjesweg.
