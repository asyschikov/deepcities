**Murals and street art**

1. Street Art Museum Amsterdam (SAMA); Slotermeer/Geuzenveld, a route of roughly 4 km. The base address is given as Slauerhoffstraat 7H in one source and Immanuel Kanthof 1 in another; open-air museum since 2012 with 150 to 300 works (sources disagree), including Bastardilla's "Memories" (2019, about 180 m², its largest); the city's only "museum" that is just walls; https://www.iamsterdam.com/uit/agenda/musea-en-galeries/musea/street-art-museum-amsterdam ; individual murals are hard to pin and some may have been painted over, best done with a guided tour; Nice.
2. Graphic Surgery lines on the ACTA building; Louwesweg 1, Slotervaart; giant white diagonal lines with a deliberate graffiti-style drip, which muralist Jeroen Koolhaas at first took for part of the architecture; the building is a squatted former dentistry faculty that now houses techno club Radion (opened 2014, 24-hour licence, renewed July 2026); https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/graphic-surgery/ ; the building has been slated for demolition since 2016, so check the lines are still there; Nice.
3. Reimerswaal murals; Reimerswaalbuurt, Osdorp-West; Gino Bud Hoiting painted seven housing blocks (2024) from poems by local children; https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/muurschilderingen-reimerswaal/ ; spread over several blocks; Okay.

"If you know, you know..."; Schipluidenlaan/Willem Frogerstraat; Narges Mohammadi's hand-drawn tapestry turned into an aluminium facade, with a poem by Zeinab El Bouni about growing up in Nieuw-West; https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/if-you-know-you-know/ ; Rodi calls a Mohammadi work the first public artwork in Amsterdam by a Muslim woman, but it is unclear whether that means this one or her Kolenkit statue; Okay/Nice.
5. Binnen & Buiten; Adriaan van Oordthof, Slotermeer; abstract mural (2025) by Jantien Jongsma, designed in workshops with local children; https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/binnen-buiten/ ; Okay.

8. Surround Sound; Plein '40-'45; Bloomberg-funded asphalt art by Kenor with SAMA, covering benches, planters and the pavement; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/292873/kunstwerk-surround-sound-kleurt-plein-40-45 ; paint wears off; Okay.

**Public art and sculpture**

10. Animaris Rhinoceros Transport; in the Albadagracht canal, Bedrijvencentrum Osdorp (De Punt edge); a 4.5 m, roughly 3-ton Theo Jansen strandbeest, the size of a small house, bolted just above the water in a business park, dragged into place by local youths in 2004; https://www.artatsite.com/Amsterdam/details/Jansen_Theo_Animaris_Rhinoceros_Transport_beelden_statues_sculptures_kunst_Art_at_Site_Amsterdam.html ; current status unverified (no news since install); Amazing.
11. Groot Landschap; hill in Sloterpark near President Allendelaan; Wessel Couzijn's 22 m corten-steel "modern hunebed", which locals compare to a plane crash or worse; it nearly rolled off its hill before an €80,000 rescue (re-unveiled 2006); https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/groot-landschap-2/ ; Nice.
12. Poort van Constant; Troelstralaan at Herman Bonpad, entrance to Sportpark Ookmeer; Constant's 1963 tangle of concrete pylons stood anonymous for decades until a former district employee left money in her will for an info plate (2011); https://stadscuratorium.nl/... (Stichting Constant documentation: https://stichtingconstant.nl/documentation/de-poort-van-constant-marking-the-entrance-of-sportspark-ookmeer-1963) ; made a district monument in January 2024, but the story is not architectural; Nice.
13. Erotica; green strip between Jan Voermanstraat and Jan Tooropstraat, Slotervaart; a 5 m aluminium "ode to eroticism" (1973) by Erwin de Vries, made from 500 kg of aluminium shipped from Suriname; https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/erotica/ ; Nice.
14. The "Satéprikkers"; junction of Cornelis Lelylaan and Johan Huizingalaan; Joost van Santen's slanted lamp posts with mirrored cubes that make the posts look broken (1981), nicknamed "satay skewers"; https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/kubus-spiegel-palen/ ; the blue cube was replaced in a lighter tint after 2023 storm damage; Nice.
15. Wegwerphuisje ("throwaway house"); Cornelis Lelylaan side of the Sloterplas; Herman Makkink's oozing-mortar brick hut (1989) recalls the nailed-up wooden shacks that stood in Slotervaart before the garden city; https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/wegwerphuisje/ ; the exact spot needs a field check; Nice.

17. Staalman; Abraham Staalmanplein, Slotervaart; Florentijn Hofman's 10 m bear with a pillow under its arm (2011), made with residents as a symbol of pride through years of renewal; https://stadscuratorium.nl/collectie/staalman/ ; Nice.

**Hidden history (WWII, post-war garden cities, renewal fights)**

26. Van Eesteren Museum and Van Eyck playgrounds; museum at the Sloterplas (Noordzijde 31), plus Witte Klok 1-20 in Osdorp; the outdoor museum has a 1950s show flat and an original Aldo van Eyck climbing frame it crowdfunded €6,000 to save. Witte Klok (1965) is said to be the last Van Eyck playground fully intact; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/nieuws/500028/glijbaan-2-0-bij-van-eesteren-museum-voor-de-deur ; Witte Klok is a monument but the story is about play, not buildings; the SLIDE pavilion was temporary (ended 31 Oct 2026); Nice.
27. The hand-dug Sloterplas; the whole lake; digging by hand started in 1942, possibly as a work-creation scheme, and the design was nearly copied from Hannover's Maschsee. It is about 30 m deep, and its sand raised the ground for the garden cities; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/amsterdam-750/449780/zomers-plezier-en-vertier-rond-de-sloterplas-in-nieuw-west ; swimming is now discouraged because of water quality; Nice.
28. Allende monument and President Allendelaan; NW Sloterpark; the road was renamed from Westoever in 1974 in protest at the Chilean coup, and has a monument; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloterpark ; I did not find the monument's design or date; Nice.
29. Vrijheidscarillon; Plein '40-'45, Slotermeer; a 1952 freedom carillon that wandered (it stood on the Dam in 1955) before settling in the resistance-fighters' neighbourhood in 1961; the young carillonneur takes requests; https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/358635/jim-23-is-beiaardier-op-plein-40-45-en-hij-doet-ook-verzoeknummers ; the carillon is a municipal monument, so the story is the point; Nice.
30. Verzetsstrijdersbuurt portraits; streets around Plein '40-'45; 80+ portraits on street-name signs show the resistance fighters the streets are named after (the "Geef Straten een Gezicht" project, still being added to in 2026); https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/historie/508427/geef-straten-een-gezicht-geschiedenislesjes-op-straat ; spread out, so a walk rather than a pin; Nice.
31. Sierplein bird monument; Sierplein, Slotervaart; a bird escaping a steel cage (Siep van de Berg, 1980), near where RAF airmen Peter Adams and William Barret crashed in June 1940, now joined by a rotating six-sided WWII cube; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/nieuws/380989/draaibare-kubus-toont-tweede-wereldoorlog-van-alle-kanten ; Okay/Nice.
32. Kars and Bierman memorial; Osdorperweg at Lutkemeerweg; a roadside stone for resistance fighter Jan Kars and conscript soldier Piet Bierman. A 1979 tile wrongly linked a crashed aircraft (thought to be pilot Fleming's) to the spot; https://www.tracesofwar.com/sights/3261/Memorial-PF-Bierman-en-J-Kars.htm ; the crash detail is muddled in the sources; Okay.
33. Tenerife national monument; Westgaarde cemetery, Ookmeerweg 275; most Dutch victims of the 1977 disaster (583 dead, the worst in aviation history) are buried here, with a yearly ceremony; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_airport_disaster ; it is a working cemetery, so visit respectfully; Nice.
34. 1800 Roeden; Joris van den Berghweg 101-113, Osdorp; a former Stelling van Amsterdam munitions park (military until 1984), named for its distance in rods to the old Haarlemmerpoort, which is still painted on a shed roof. It now holds artists' studios and the Rijk van de Keizer garden; https://alaturka.info/en/netherlands/6219-de-1800-roeden-a-cultural-park-worth-seeing-in-amsterdam ; the c.1900 build date has a single source; Nice.
35. Oud Osdorp signs; polder edges along Osdorperweg; the real village of Osdorp lost its name to the new town in 1962, and new "Oud Osdorp" signs (2026) confused Geuzenveld residents who thought they now lived outside Amsterdam; https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/363492/verwarring-over-nieuwe-oud-osdorp-borden-ik-woon-al-70-jaar-in-geuzenveld ; Okay.
36. Sloten village square; Dorpsplein Sloten; a 1794 boundary post marking Amsterdam's jurisdiction. Oslo's Nasjonalmuseet holds a drawing of Sloten's ruined church (1650-52) possibly by Rembrandt; https://nasjonalmuseet.no/en/collection/object/NG.K_H.B.15804 ; the Rembrandt attribution is uncertain and the church is listed; Nice.
37. Lutkemeerpolder fight; Lutkemeerweg/Osdorperweg, the far west; the last polder farmland in the city, which activists and care farm De Boterbloem defended against distribution centres. Voedselpark Amsterdam won 9.5 ha, starting 2027; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/nieuws/447662/voedselpark-amsterdam-wint-tender-maar-blijft-strijden-voor-l ; still disputed, and I found no news after February 2026 (monthly walks were still running then); Nice.
38. Renewal flashpoints; Louis Couperusbuurt (Slotermeer) and Nieuwenhuysenbuurt; where the garden-city demolition story is still live. One review counts 7,000 homes demolished against 9,500 built up to 2016; Couperusbuurt's residents' chair has fought the plan since 2008, and Nieuwenhuysenbuurt demolition began September 2025; https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/358752/deze-geliefde-amsterdamse-buurt-verandert-voorgoed ; sensitive and changing, better as text than as a pin; Okay.

**Curiosities and odd museums**

39. Molen van Sloten and Kuiperijmuseum; Akersluis 10; Amsterdam's only windmill open to visitors daily, actually a 1990 rebuild, with a coopers' workshop and attic tableaux from Rembrandt paintings (Rembrandt was a miller's son); https://go-kids.nl/amsterdam/erop-uit/rembrandt-molen-van-sloten-en-kuiperijmuseum ; touristy for Nieuw-West, and an old maintenance-closure notice exists; Nice.
40. Blauwe Moskee; Slotervaart, around 52.3486, 4.8314; a 2008 mosque in blue glazed brick with no minarets and services in Dutch. In 2019 it became one of the first in Amsterdam to amplify the Friday call to prayer, a cable was cut, and it became a political row; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blauwe_Moskee_(Amsterdam) ; contested (Kuwaiti funding, controversial speakers), so it would need careful neutral wording or a skip; exact address not confirmed; Okay.

**Green escapes**

41. Natuurpark De Vrije Geer; edge of Sloten village; the last strip of peat meadow between Amsterdam and Sloten, saved when 200,000 Amsterdammers voted in the 17 May 1995 referendum. Volunteers still run it; https://www.hartvannederland.nl/nieuws/2020/precies-25-jaar-geleden-wonnen-strijdende-slotenaren-de-slag-om-weilandje-de-vrije-geer/ ; Nice.
42. Het Ruige Riet; NW Sloterpark near Geuzeneiland; a 14.3 ha island left alone since the 1950s ground-raising, once known as "Vogeleiland"; https://go-kids.nl/amsterdam/erop-uit/heemtuin-sloterpark-en-het-ruige-riet ; a 2026 demolition drawing mentions works on the Sloterplas bird islands, details unclear; Nice.
43. De Oeverlanden; Nieuwe Meer shore, Henk Gerritsenbrug (bridge 1859); a wild lakeside strip saved from a 1980s plan to dump canal sludge. The bridge was named in May 2026 for Henk Gerritsen, a Dutch Wave and Priona gardens pioneer; https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/nieuws/495139/brug-voor-oeverlandenman-henk-gerritsen-onthuld ; known gay cruising area, with more violent incidents reported in 2026; Nice.
44. Lange Bretten; strip from Sloterdijk west toward Halfweg; 130 ha of "the last wild nature in Amsterdam", named after the vanished country house Huis te Bretten; https://www.iamsterdam.com/uit/agenda/attracties-en-bezienswaardigheden/parken-en-natuurgebieden/natuurgebied-de-bretten ; long and linear, so pin an entrance; Nice.
