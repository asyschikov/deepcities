## Conflicts and uncertainties

- Sleutelsteeg 3: the national register says "voormalig Rabbihuis". dutchjewry.org says Weesp
  never had its own rabbi and the house (1878) was built for the cantor; Joods Monument confirms
  Samuel van der Hal was the voorzanger living in the official residence (though its header lists
  "Rabbijn"). Text uses "cantor's house" and quotes the register. Place of death of Samuel van der
  Hal differs (Stolperstein: Sobibor; Joods Monument: Auschwitz), so the text gives no camp for him.
  The 1878/5638 stone is mentioned from dutchjewry; I could not confirm it is still visible.
- Hoogstraat 24: register "aanvang 19e eeuw"; nl.wikipedia and tgooi.info "built 1866"; DBNL
  (Monumenten in Nederland) "18th-c. core, new front house 1866". Used the DBNL reading.
- Hoogstraat 44: city register text says rebuilt 1911 to a design by H. Bletz; the nl.wikipedia
  municipal list gives architect A.J. van Doorn. Used the register, no architect attribute.
- Hoogstraat 36: city register suggests the front may be by "architect Walenkamp"; the wiki list
  says "Walenkamp G.H.". Text says "possibly by the Walenkamp family of architects".
- Slijkstraat 21-23: register 1892 by C.H. Walenkamp; DBNL "circa 1903". Used register.
- Binnenveer 2-6: register 1904, DBNL 1905. Used register. Architect J. Blüm (shopfront) only
  from the wiki list.
- Middenstraat 56: register calls the shopfront art deco, DBNL jugendstil (c. 1910).
- Middenstraat 9-11: DBNL says the three virtue stones were kept when the 1665 gables were
  renewed in 1885; the register dates the stones 18th century. The meaning of "In de gemeste Os"
  is not explained in any source, so no interpretation given.
- Nieuwstad 100: DBNL says malt house and brewery; tgooi.info says malt house, later toy storage,
  blueprint printing, office. Current use not stated.
- Klaas Listinghsteeg 5 "former brewery" rests only on the object name in the nl.wikipedia
  municipal list; the register text just says early business building.
- Buitenveer 10: the register (1990s) says a barber still worked there; I could not confirm
  current use, so the text only says the front was made for a barber's shop.
- Nieuwstad 124a-126a: a Commons file calls it "Kostschool" (boarding school); no other source,
  left out.
- The Commons category for 38496 is mislabelled "Binnenveer 60"; it is Buitenveer 60.
- Names Binnenveer/Buitenveer: no source explains them; not glossed.
- DBNL (Monumenten in Nederland, 2006) is copyrighted: used for facts only, not spliced into Dutch.

## Dutch text

histories), nl.wikipedia (Raadhuis van Weesperkarspel, Stadsbrand 1968, Stolpersteine list,

(e.g. Middenstraat c. 1550, Achtergracht 1613, the virtue stones' 1885 rebuild) come from DBNL
facts and are written in my own Dutch, since that source can't be quoted at length.

## Walking route idea

"Weesp's old core, dike to ditch" (about 1.5 km): Hoogstraat, Vecht end -> Former meat hall ->
Former town hall of Weesperkarspel -> Hoogstraat, south -> (back north) Slijkstraat ->
Late-medieval house, Slijkstraat 27 -> Drosthagen -> Middenstraat and its side streets ->
Remains of the Jonge Convent -> Former cantor's house, Sleutelsteeg -> Nieuwstad, north ->
De Oranjeboom malt house -> Nieuwstad, south; then back via Jugendstil corner shop,
Binnenveer 11 and Binnenveer and Buitenveer, with an optional cycle out to the bridge
keepers' houses.
