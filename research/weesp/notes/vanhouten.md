## Corrections to the scout list and source conflicts

- **Scout 31 (rowing club in a former pool)**: settled by the RCE complex description 512877. The pool is the Utrechtseweg 17 natural bath (1937, K. Breijer, wooden because of the Kringenwet), in use until 1963; offices (canoe federation) after that; Roeivereniging Weesp only since **1978**, not 1963. Not on the Buitengracht.
- **Scout 9 (war memorial)**: it is not a memorial to staff who died. It is a thank-you from the staff to the management for wartime support (4 en 5 mei, nl.wikipedia, vanderkrogt charter text). Unveiled 16 July 1947 (register record says year_from 1946; all other sources say 1947, used 1947). Listed 30 January 2024.
- **Villa Casparus**: register says completed 1901, address Casparuslaan 1-3; DBNL (Monumenten in Nederland) says 1897-'99 and Korte Stammerdijk 13; nl.wikipedia says built 1897-1901 (Van Houten article) and lists it on the Amstellandlaan. Used register address and "1897–1901".
- **Van Houten monument material**: register says bronze relief, kunstwacht says "koperplaat". Used bronze (register + Wikipedia).
- **Van Houten monument imagery**: sources disagree on the kneeling figure. kunstwacht (city art guide, current) calls her Afro-Surinamese with her hand at about Suriname on the map and links the image to slavery; vanderkrogt and nl.wikipedia read the globe as America plus the African coast where Van Houten's beans were grown. The text gives both readings, attributed. The patent: nl.wikipedia (Peter van Dam's research) says the 1828 patent was in the name of Casparus sr., not Coenraad; the monument honours Coenraad.
- **Noodslachthuis architect**: DBNL names "W. Breijer"; the municipal works director at the time was K. (Kors) Breijer. Unresolved, so no architect attribute. The register says it became a mosque after 1990; I could not confirm whether it is still a mosque (Weesp's Assoenat mosque got a new building on the M. Nijhoffstraat around 2015-2017, but no source ties it to Papelaan 47). The text says only "after 1990 it was taken into use as a mosque" plus the 2025 permit request.
- **Factory hall**: built 1895 per the Commons RCE/register caption, galleries 1936. Current user Abbott Healthcare Products B.V. per 2025-2026 environmental permit notices (search snippets only). Scout's "small explosion in 2020" not verified, left out.
- **De Valk chimney**: Heemschut/Binnenlands Bestuur date the chimney 1926, while the company was founded in 1927 (Staatscourant notice cited by joodsamsterdam.nl). Kept "1926" for the chimney. **Pin is inferred**: no source gives the house number. Fire brigade log has a major fire at Nijverheidslaan 3 on 2 October 2009 (De Valk burned "October 2009"); BAG shows a new building (bouwjaar 2022) at Nijverheidslaan 3a with many business units, matching the 2020 plan for a "bedrijfsverzamelgebouw". Pinned on that 2022 building (52.311727, 5.049304). A different 2025-26 multi-unit block exists at Nijverheidslaan 2A; if a better source turns up, move the pin. The WeesperNieuws history article is paywalled.
- **Landscroon**: no visiting hours found; text does not state hours. Pinned at the keeper's house by the entrance bridge (Korte Muiderweg 10), not the register point (which lies in the middle of the grounds). "Van Houten graves in the general (eastern) part" is my inference from the register's description of the general part; the register only says some Van Houten graves are present. Consider dropping that Look-for line if unsure.
- **Van Houten monument setting**: Vivium Oversingel (Hugo de Grootlaan 30) is being "transformed" (Vastgoedjournaal snippet, details unknown). The listed monument should stay, but worth rechecking later.
- **WaterWeespers**: opened 18 December 2025; planned to stay eight years (TaartRovers page).

## Ranks

Nice: swimming baths, war memorial, Villa Casparus group, Landscroon, Het Duivenprieel.
Okay: factory hall (private site), noodslachthuis, Moppenbakker, WaterWeespers, De Valk (gone; strong story but nothing to see, scout had Nice).

## Walking route ideas

"Cocoa town" (about 4 km): Van Houten war memorial (Stationsplein) → WaterWeespers (tunnel) → Landscroon cemetery (Van Houten graves) → Weesper Moppenbakker → Het Duivenprieel → Villa Casparus and the Van Houten houses (Singel 67-73, Korte Stammerdijk) → former emergency slaughterhouse (Papelaan) → Van Houten centenary monument → last Van Houten factory hall. Add the Van Houtenkerk (Oudegracht, civic slice) between Duivenprieel and the Singel.

"Breijer's Weesp" (town works of the 1930s): Landscroon keeper's house and bridge → noodslachthuis → swimming baths on the Utrechtseweg.
