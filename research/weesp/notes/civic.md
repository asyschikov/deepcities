All hints followed. W6: the record that is the synagogue is **38600 (Nieuwstraat 5)**. Its national register text calls the right half, with three pointed windows, the "restant van de v.m. synagoge". The Stenvert guide (DBNL) says the plastered left half of the same building was probably the cantor's house. 38599 (Nieuwstraat 3) is an 18th-century plastered house that "een geheel met kerkgebouw vormt". HMdb and a 2026 event listing put the plaque and the synagogue at "Nieuwstraat 3", so the house numbering seems to have shifted. Both records sit in one place, pinned on the 38600 register point (the plaque is a few metres away).

## Scout items used

- 28 (town hall dungeons, merger): in the Stadhuis place. Museum Weesp confirms that the cells and hall are open to the public during museum hours. Referendum result: 57.4% for Amsterdam and 42.6% for Gooise Meren (NH Nieuws, 22 March 2018). The province document gives 56.5%, probably on a different base; I used 57.4%. I left out the scout's line that older residents mostly voted for Gooise Meren, because I found no source for it.
- 25 (Laurentiuskerk): the 8 Nov 2016 fire, the weathercock missing and believed stolen (NH Nieuws, 21 Nov 2016), and the spire rehoisted on 8 Nov 2018 are all in the place. Sources disagree on whether the weathercock on the new spire is new or the old one recovered; I found no report of it turning up, so the text only says it disappeared. I **left out** Christian Pfeiffer's genever still in the side wing: the only source is from 2021, and I could not confirm it is still running in 2026. That article also says a distillery stood "on the spot" until 1860, which conflicts with the hidden church of 1792 on the same plot. The bells were taken out in 2019 (NH Nieuws). The Wispe brewery is mentioned only as the building's current use, in line with the food rule.
- 20 (Van Houtenkerk): it is a national monument (512906 to 512909, listed 1999). Visiting hours are from Stadsherstel. Stadsherstel gives 2009 for the change of owner, nl.wikipedia gives 2010; I used 2009.
- 11 (synagogue and stolpersteine): the plaque text (1984) is from HMdb. The stolpersteine in front are confirmed by a 2022 Commons photo. Community figures are from dutchjewry.org (65 Jews, 7 returned) and jck.nl (49 in 1930). **Conflict:** jck.nl says "by the end of 1941 all Jews had been expelled from Weesp", while the plaque and HMdb give 29 April 1942. I used the plaque date. Current use: dutchjewry says the building has been a synagogue again since 1997, and Stenvert (2006) calls it a Jewish cultural centre. Masorti Almere-Weesp has used it since about 2005, but I found no 2026 service schedule.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **Gereformeerde Kerk fire:** Stenvert says the fire was "around 1965", the MIP says the roof burned in 1969 and was rebuilt flat with the church demolished later, and nl.wikipedia (Stadsbrand van Weesp) says 3 April 1968. An Anefo photo dated 1968-04-03 shows the burnt interior of the Gereformeerde church on the Hoogstraat, so I used 3 April 1968. The MIP puts the church at Hoogstraat 67 and the parsonage at no. 68, but the city register lists the parsonage at Hoogstraat 67; I used the register. I could not find what "BIOS" stands for (it is the register's name for the building), so the text does not explain it.
- **City of Wesopa:** the municipal list gives 1947 as the building year, Stenvert 1948, and nl.wikipedia says it opened on 14 October 1948. I used "1947–1948" for built. The city register text talks of plans for a cinema in 1936. A 2026 Gemeenteblad notice (renewed lease to Stichting Theater City of Wesopa) confirms it is still running.
- **Roskamstraat dates:** the MIP gives "ca. 1895", and the municipal list gives 1868–1876 for the corner blocks and about 1880–1900 for the rest. The register says Dr Kortenhorst lived at no. 1 from 1876. I used "about 1870–1900".
- **Amsterdamse Huizen:** the MIP says 1875, while the municipal list and the Commons description say 1876–1878. I used 1876–1878.
- **Grote Kerk choir screen:** the register says about 1530, Stenvert about 1525. I used 1530 in both languages.
- **Gate "Al voor een ander":** no source explains the motto or the relief, so the text says so plainly.
- **Waagplein:** I found nothing on a weigh house that gave the square its name, so I left the name unexplained. The architect (J.D. van Arkel) comes from the Weesp municipal monument list (nl.wikipedia and Commons), not from the register text.

## Dutch texts

All Dutch texts are spliced from Dutch sources: city and national register texts, nl.wikipedia, the MIP Weesp inventory (020apps.nl), museumweesp.nl and NH Nieuws quotes. Stenvert (DBNL) is copyrighted, so I used it for facts only. The short Dutch text for the gate is built around the register sentence; nothing longer exists.

## Walking route idea

"Church and town hall loop" (about 1.5 km): City of Wesopa → Laurentiuskerk → Former synagogue, Nieuwstraat → Grote Kerk Weesp → Waagplein houses → Stadhuis Weesp and St. Bartholomeus Gasthuis → Gate "Al voor een ander" → Van Houtenkerk → Gereformeerde parsonage and BIOS building. Add Lutherse Kerk and Roskamstraat for a western loop, and Amsterdamse Huizen on the way from the station.
