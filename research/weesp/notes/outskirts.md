The F1/F2/F3 hints were one group each, but the farms are spread over 6 km, so I split them into stretches of road (records within ~300 m and on the same road) and pulled out the ones with their own story: Vechtwijk (vanished country estate), Huis ten Bosch (castle site, haystack on the same spot since 1732), De Melkkoe (1747 gable stone, 700 m from the other Dammerweg farms, so a curiosity of its own). Kerklaan 8 and Stationsweg 5 are on different streets 170 m apart; merged as "town farms engulfed by the station quarter". If the coordinator prefers, they can be two places.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **Kerklaan**: the city slice gives "Kerklaan 2 (vh)" (= voorheen, former number); the RCE register and DBNL give Kerklaan 8. Used Kerklaan 8 and mentioned the old number in the English body.
- **Gaaspermolen dates**: RCE says screw pump fitted in 1893, nl.wikipedia 1892; English says 1892–1893, Dutch follows RCE (1893). Engine: nl.wikipedia 1926, MIP 1927 (diesel). Listing: slice says 1996, RCE register 21 January 1997; used 1997. Gaaspermolen.nl was down (Cloudflare 520), molendatabase lookup failed; no current-status news found, nl.wikipedia says it is inhabited and not open to visitors.
- **'s-Gravelandseweg 4**: RCE says built 1928 for G.G.E. Pronk; DBNL says 1925, contractor "J.C. van Houten"; a Commons photo caption from the Weesp municipal list says "J.C. van Kooten, 1925-1926". Used RCE's 1928 and named no builder. The Commons municipal photos of the wooden farms (Weesp-s-gravelandseweg-208809/208811) could not be matched to a house number with certainty, so they are not used.
- **Overaetwijs**: the Aetsveldroute brochure calls Overaetwijs a late-medieval settlement and dates the 1966 finds to 300–200 BC; the RCE records say Late Iron Age. Both used, kept apart. Scout called it a "geological monument"; the brochure says "aardkundig monument" (earth-science), used that.
- **Driemond name**: nl.wikipedia says De Geinbrug became a village named Driemond in 1961; en.wikipedia/scout say before 1966 (the year of the transfer to Amsterdam). Used 1961.
- **Vechtwijk**: a Utrecht archive drawing (HUA 135677, 1718–1719) has a note "Vechtwijk had later de naam Huis den Bosch". Not used: the castle Huis ten Bosch lay 1.5 km away at Gooilandseweg 7. Worth checking with a local historian.
- **1918 disaster pin is approximate**: no memorial exists (none found in Wikipedia, NH Nieuws 2018, NOS 2018). Pinned on the rail embankment about 120 m east of today's Gooilijn bridge over the Amsterdam-Rijnkanaal (52.3239, 5.0188), on the Weesp side, as the sources say the collapse was on the slope from Weesp station to the bridge. The canal was widened in the 1930s and the line rebuilt, so the 1918 bridgehead may have been a few dozen metres off.
- **Waterworks house**: very thin sources. The city register gives only name, 1901 and procedure status; no designation text yet. Context (Bethunepolder to Waterleidingplas to Weesperkarspel) is from nl.wikipedia. I could not find when the Weesperkarspel pumping station was founded or what it originally pumped (scout 29 mentions the Vecht as a drinking-water intake; not verified). Rank Okay; revisit once the designation text is published.
- **De Hogeweyk**: numbers vary by source (23 houses/152 residents in older sources, 27 houses in RIVM). Used ranges. Architects from DVA ("Buro Kade", "Architect Roozen landscaped"); I did not add a first name. Visitor access: RIVM and DVA say the restaurant, café and theatre are open to outsiders but inside a staffed entrance; study visits for professionals are offered via hogeweyk.dementiavillage.com. No public tours found. Ranked Nice, not the scout's Amazing, because it is a home and visitors should not treat it as a sight; coordinator may decide otherwise.
- **Nelly Dyserinck**: no information found on who she was (stone only). Not speculated.
- **Batavia's Hoeve**: no source for the name's origin; not speculated.

## Walking and cycling routes

1. **Aetsveldroute (walk, about 13.5 km, from Weesp station)**: Town farms by the station, Kerklaan 8 and Stationsweg 5 > Town farm on the skew, Groeneweg 25 > Nelly's Hoeve and the stolp farm > Landlust and Utrechtseweg 116 > (Fort Hinderdam, outside Weesp) > Aetsveldseweg and the lost village of Overaetwijs > Merwedehoeve. Theme: fortress zones and polders.
2. **East bank farms (cycle, about 12 km)**: Farms on the Lange Muiderweg > Wooden farms and Boerderij America > Vechtwijk coach house > De Overhorn, De Vogelkooi and 's-Gravelandseweg 39 > Huis ten Bosch farm and castle site > Farms on the Dammerweg > De Melkkoe gable stone, combined with Fort Uitermeer from the fortress slice.
3. **Along the Weespertrekvaart to Driemond (cycle, about 6 km)**: Van Houten tenant farm, Verlengd Buitenveer 11 > Geinbrug and the three mouths of Driemond > Former waterworks house > Farm Lange Stammerdijk 12-14 > Gaaspermolen. Detour north for De Hogeweyk (from outside) and the 1918 disaster site.
