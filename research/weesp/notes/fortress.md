## Departures from the hints

- **W9 ('t Haantje and the miller's house):** split. The city register says Korte Stammerdijk 5 was probably the miller's house of the neighbouring mill **Het Anker**, not of 't Haantje. It also stands about 280 m away. It is now its own Okay place. The DBNL guide (*Monumenten in Nederland: Noord-Holland*) calls it a "hovenierswoning, circa 1870" (gardener's house). The body flags that the first use is not settled. I found no source for when or where the mill Het Anker stood. Wikipedia's De Vriendschap article says De Vriendschap was called Het Anker between 1813 and 1817, so the register may confuse the two, or there may have been a second Het Anker on the Stammerdijk.
- **W13 (defence works):** monument 38662 covers all four bastions and the tower fort, so it is one place pinned on the tower. I added two separate places:
  - The Bakkerschans tea garden (SCOUT 18) is a green place with no heritage fields, so that 38662 doesn't appear twice.
  - The WWII casemate and type P shelters (SCOUT 13) are in the "Look for" list of the tower fort place.
- **38552** has no national register page any more and is not on Wikidata or the Wikipedia list. The city has only a point "bij Kreugerlaan 1". DBNL mentions "de Uitermeerse Sluis (bij Kreugerlaan 1)", so it is almost certainly the old listing of the lock and bridge. That listing was superseded in 2014 by 531347 (complex 531341). I kept the number in Fort Uitermeer. The coordinator may want to drop it.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **Fort Uitermeer gun shed:** the national register says 1885, Wikipedia says 1874. I used 1885.
- **Uitermeer lock:** the national register says 1878, with the bridge of 1888 placed in 1947. DBNL says the lock was built in 1888. I used 1878 for the lock and 1888 for the bridge.
- **Fort Uitermeer status:** the restaurant Paviljoen Uit & Meer next to the fort burned down in May 2018 (Hart van Nederland). I found no reliable news of it reopening, and it is left out anyway under the food rule. The ruin is fenced. Volunteers of Stichting Uiteraard Uitermeer run the island, and there is a walking route around it.
- **Porcelain dates:** the national register says the factory was founded in 1757. Other sources (Wikipedia on Van Gronsveld, DBNL, the Rijksmuseum, the V&A) show that 1757 is the earthenware venture by English and Amsterdam merchants, and that porcelain under Van Gronsveld began in 1759. I used 1759–about 1770. I could not find the wording of the gable stone, so it is mentioned without a description.
- **'t Haantje:** the city register says the mill was moved in 1820 and became a corn mill around 1830. Wikipedia says it became a corn mill in 1828, and SCOUT says it was moved in 1828. I used 1820. It was built around 1663 on the Boerenwetering, at today's Ruysdaelkade. That is an Amsterdam link worth noting.
- **De Eendragt:** the national register says "1743". The city register says the year can't be traced and that the windbrief (wind licence) dates from 1691. I used 1691. The hint title "(1743)" is dropped.
- **De Vriendschap:** the replacement mill of 1900 may have come from Amsterdam (Wikipedia and DBNL), but this is not certain.
- **Torenfort:** the IVN leaflet has errors (it says restoration in 1922 and that the first high-explosive shell was fired at the fort), so I ignored those claims. I used the national register, Wikipedia, DBNL and the HMdb panels.
- **Theetuin:** the theetuin.nl site says the tea house is a former munitions shed of 1875. The national register lists a wooden artillery shed on the Bakkerschans, and Wikipedia says a genieloods (engineers' shed) of 1877 is an exhibition space. The body says "late 19th century".
- **Opening hours:** theetuin.nl says the garden is open daily 9:00–18:00, but not on Sundays or public holidays, and the tea house only on Friday and Saturday. IVN gives April–October only. The body keeps this general.
- **Weduwe met Kind:** neither the year it was made nor the date of the original unveiling is confirmed (Commons files say 1955 and 1956), so no `built` value is given. The name plate was unveiled on 4 May 2018 (NH Nieuws). This confirms SCOUT 8.
- **Sluisbrug:** the city register gives year_from 1914 and describes the bridge as an early 20th-century riveted steel bridge. tgooi.info says it lay at the Zwaantjesbrug site until 1978. DBNL calls it a steel truss bridge renewed in 1978. I wrote "about 1914".
- **IJsfabriek:** the HMdb's English translation says "ice cream bars", but the Dutch says *ijsstaven*, which means blocks of ice. I used blocks of ice.
- **Wooden houses (SCOUT 16):** the DBNL guide names Molenpad 1-13, 15, 19 and the Utrechtseweg houses, and IVN says stone houses only came after 1947. The houses are not individually listed. The pin is on Molenpad 1. The Kringenwet date (1853) comes from the national register complex text 531341.

## Walking route ideas

1. **The fortress ring (about 1.5 hours):** Noordersluis and Sluisbrug → Mouterij De Leeuw → Lange Vechtbrug → Weduwe met Kind → Torenfort aan de Ossenmarkt and the bastions → Theetuin on the Bakkerschans → (back over the Vecht) Wooden houses on the Molenpad → Molen De Vriendschap → Molen De Eendragt.
2. **Mills and industry:** First Dutch porcelain factory → Mouterij De Leeuw → Former miller's house, Korte Stammerdijk 5 → Molen 't Haantje (with the Van Houten slice nearby) → back through town to De Vriendschap and De Eendragt.
3. **Waterline by bike (about 5 km out of town):** Torenfort aan de Ossenmarkt → along the Vecht to Fort Uitermeer → Koepelkazemat U 44 and the concrete works → back via the Aetsveldse polder (see the outskirts slice).
