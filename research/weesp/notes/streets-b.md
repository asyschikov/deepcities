## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- Nieuwstraat 50: RCE says *halsgevel* (neck gable), Monumenten in Nederland says *tuitgevel*; the text just says "gable".
- Nieuwstraat 12a-14a: the city register address; Monumenten in Nederland calls it Nieuwstraat 14 (used in the title). Register says architect "E.M. Raadsheer", Monumenten in Nederland has "E.W. Raadsheer" for his other Weesp work; used E.W. in the attribute, plain "Raadsheer" in the bodies. Current use of the shop unknown.
- Nieuwstraat 34: register dates the rebuild 1721-22 (with 1710 gate); Monumenten in Nederland gives 1710. Used the register.
- Hidden church: city register says it began as a coach house (1649-1662) and was found in use in 1662; Monumenten in Nederland says an existing house was fitted out in 1687. Used the register (more detailed, archival).
- Ora et Labora: city register year 1914; Monumenten in Nederland "circa 1925". Used 1914.
- Vechtzicht: RCE "straight cornice"; Monumenten in Nederland mentions a central bay with pediment. Kept to "broad, stately front".
- Ossenmarkt 1 "for some time also held a church" (Monumenten in Nederland; RCE text garbled "met kerk XVIII"): which congregation is unknown.
- Left out: the city register for Singelstraat 2 / Achtergracht 48 says the Achtergracht house was demolished in 2020 except the Singelstraat wall. Current state not verified, so the body mentions only the owners' history, not the house's present form. Check before publishing.
- Bridge Groeneweg: no build year found (municipal register gives none); park name Zeerust from a 2021 permit notice.
- Register points: Nieuwstraat 10-10a carries the same point as Nieuwstraat 87 (wrong; 10-10a is at the north end); Herengracht 17 shares a point with 18; Oudegracht 37 and 41 share one. Pins avoid these.
