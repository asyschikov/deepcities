## Merges

- **Garage churches (SCOUT 33)** sits inside *The Botanical Garden of the Bims*, as the brief asked: the
  Haardstee garage is one of the few original-type Bijlmer garages still in use (BijlmerMuseum, 2019).
  The story also links to De Kandelaar: the Pentecost Revival Church once met in garage Klieverink
  (ROODS) and bought De Kandelaar in 2017. Both places mention this.
- **Kwaku** sits inside *Nelson Mandelapark and Kwaku*. The Mandela memorial (Rona Batho) belongs to the
  `memory` slice and is only mentioned in passing.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- **Now Speak! location.** The Kunstwacht street label is "Kelbergenpad", but its coordinates
  (52.316887, 4.968794) fall on the **Krimpertplein** (PDOK), which nl.wikipedia and Ceescamel's 2021 Commons
  photos give as the new spot after the restoration. Public Art Amsterdam still describes it at the
  Gulden Kruispad in its abandoned state (older text). The year of the restoration is not given anywhere.
- **Bims mural pin.** No coordinates in any register. Pinned to the 1982 Haardstee garage building (BAG pand
  0363100012217860 / OSM way 282360064, 52.310664, 4.951948). The opening invitation (LinkedIn) gives the
  address as "garage Haardstee aan de Foppingadreef 100"; the BAG has no such address (Haardstee 100 is a
  dwelling). Dates: CBK Zuidoost announced an opening on 8 Dec 2022 while work was in progress; the official
  unveiling was 21 April 2023 (Stadscuratorium, LinkedIn invitation). Used built 2022–2023.
- **Perfumed with Mint.** Kunstwacht has house number 100, trans-urban says Hakfort 42; the Kunstwacht pin
  matches Hakfort 42 in PDOK. A Rochdale Facebook post ("Onze flat Hakfort krijgt een makeover... de kopgevel")
  could not be dated or read; not used. Rochdale's plan names insulation of "zijgevels" without saying
  anything about the mural; worth rechecking when the plan is presented (early 2027).
- **Taibah founders.** en.wikipedia names Muhammad Yunus Gaffar (1940–2020) as founder; nl.wikipedia says it
  was realised by Mohamed Idris Luqman (1950–1999) at the wish of Mohammed Radja Imamdi, with Abdul-Gaffar
  behind the 1998 enlargement. The mosque's own site mentions a board dispute involving a Gaffar. Left all
  founder names out. Dates: architectuurgids 1982–1984, ONH and both Wikipedias "completed 1985"; a YouTube
  newsreel gives the opening as 19 Jan 1985 (not used). Reliwiki names the architect as "Ruimte '68,
  Eindhoven" (presumably Haffmans's office); used Paul Haffmans.
- **Steven van Dorpel.** Wikipedia's title spells him "Steve"; Kunstwacht and Public Art use "Steven". Death
  toll: 176 of 187 (11 survivors), consistent across Kunstwacht ("met 175 anderen") and Public Art.
- **Floriade visitors.** Groengebied Amstelland 4.5 million, BIE 4.6 million; used "some 4.5 million".
- **Gaasperpark pin.** Pinned to the former planetarium (Wikidata Q61771717). *Big Bang* (Charles Hook) is in
  the city art register (Kunstwacht 2003, 52.308632, 4.987726); I could not confirm it survived the
  2025–2026 renovation untouched. nl.wikipedia also mentions "an artwork of six steel boxes by the former
  entrance" that may or may not be the same piece.
- **Nelson Mandelapark pin.** Central park point (nl.wikipedia coordinates, 52.3125, 4.9625). The exact
  festival field could not be pinned (kwakufestival.nl returned 503 throughout).
- **Kwaku name.** The Wikipedia link label says "Kwaku Summer Festival" but the URL is the old title
  `Kwakoe_Zomerfestival`, which redirects.
- **Wapen van Holendrecht.** The demolition date comes from the district's own request in the Stadscuratorium
  advice (published April 2026): "Begin 2027 beginnen de sloopwerkzaamheden". No newer news found on whether
  the tiles will be saved.

## Route ideas

   Steven van Dorpel memorial → Nelson Mandelapark and Kwaku → Now Speak! → Taibah Mosque → De Kandelaar.
2. **Kwaku and the park** (short loop): Nelson Mandelapark and Kwaku → Steven van Dorpel memorial →
   (memory slice: Rona Batho) → lookout tower.

   (via Gaasperplas metro).
