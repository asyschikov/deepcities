## Merges and choices

- Groenhoven (200955) and Gouden Leeuw (200956) are one place. The pin is on the Groenhoven register point. Both share one architect, one listing (Aug 2021) and one register text.
- Zandkasteel and Atlas are filed as `civic`, as the Nieuw-West IBM office was. Langerlust is `housing`, as the Weesp farms were.
- Zandkasteel ranked **Amazing**: it is the most remarkable building in Zuidoost, its inner street is reachable and guided tours run in 2026. Change it to Nice if the Amazing quota is tight.

## Status (checked Nov 2026)

- **Hoptille**: in April 2026 Ymere (newsletter 8) decided to demolish all of it, mid-rise and low-rise, and replace it with one new plan. Residents won't move for another 1.5 to 2 years. Not gone yet, and the text says so.
- **Langerlust**: empty since The Colour Kitchen left. The shelter plan was dropped as the preferred option in 2024. In April 2026 (AT5) the swap with the Abcouderstraatweg site failed, and plans for Langerlust are "on hold", not ruled out. The scout's date for a withdrawn permit (10 July 2025) did not turn up in what I read, so it is not in the text.
- **Groenhoven**: renovation and energy upgrade started 3 Sep 2026 (about 18 months). Scaffolding is possible.
- **Heesterveld**: demolition called off in 2022. Ymere (Apr 2026) is renovating. Events are still running (ADE Oct 2026).
- **Atlas**: listed 3 Mar 2026 (register `datumAanwijzing`). The Europe building gets a sustainability upgrade from 2028 (Vastint).
- **Zandkasteel**: converted. Homes since May 2023, the AICS school since 2023–2024. Tours on sale for 2026.
- **Bijlmer Museum**: open by appointment only. The website was updated in Jan 2026.

## Source conflicts and uncertainties

- Groenhoven/Gouden Leeuw dates: the register gives `year_from` 1972 for both. nl.wikipedia and NH Nieuws say they were built in 1974 (Gouden Leeuw) and 1975 (Groenhoven). I used 1974–1975. nl.wikipedia also gives Groenhoven the wrong monument number (200956).
- Zandkasteel dates: the register says 1983, and the building opened as the head office in 1987 (nl.wikipedia Hoogoorddreef; NRC quote of Feb 1987). I wrote "1983–1987".
- Atlas: the register spells the architect "Dekeukeleire", the COK advice "Dekeukelaire". The register's `oorspronkelijkeFunctie` is "wonen", which is clearly wrong: it was built as offices. Fokker's move-in and bankruptcy come only from nl.wikipedia (Hoogoorddreef).
- Kleiburg size: nl.wikipedia says 10 floors and 450 m galleries. Dezeen says 400 m and 11 storeys. Archello says "10 + 1". I wrote "about 400 m, ten storeys".
- Heesterveld façade: nl.wikipedia credits murals on flats 1-87 to the artist "Ovni". HCC's own history says graphic designer Floor Wesseling made "the iconic mural on the façade". I followed HCC. The two may be different works.
- Heesterveld architect: nl.wikipedia says the office of Frans van Gool (Pi de Bruin worked there). HCC says "designed by Pi de Bruin". The Bijlmer Museum site gives Heesterveld to Van Gool and Hoptille to Rijnboutt with De Bruijn.
- Hoptille date: nl.wikipedia says completed 1981. The scout says 1979–82. I wrote "c. 1979–1981".
- Bijlmer Museum address: the museum site, MuseumsAtlas and Stadsdeel Zuidoost all say Grubbehoeve 38. The pin is the PDOK point for no. 38.
- Langerlust: one source says The Colour Kitchen closed 30 Sep 2018 (gein3dorp), another says "since 2019" (NH). I wrote 2018. The shelter designation is given as 2020 (NH) and as 2021 (amsterdamopdekaart), so the text says 2020–2021.
- The bijlmermuseum.com page on Heesterveld and Hoptille describes former tenants in derogatory terms. I took only neutral facts from it (the inner street was removed, who designed what) and no wording.

## Walking routes
