  > In 2015 Ali Mohamed, an Amsterdammer from Egypt, took over a greengrocer's on the Javastraat. With a Christian business partner he turned it into a Middle Eastern delicatessen, named after the two rivers of Iraq, the Tigris and the Euphrates. That was the year many Syrians fled to the Netherlands, and the team behind the counter is made up of Syrian refugees. The shelves hold what a Syrian or Lebanese kitchen needs, and the falafel and shawarma made here became an Amsterdam favourite. ## Claims dropped or hedged

- **"Syrian/Lebanese" owners**: the owner is Egyptian (Oost-Online, iamsterdam); the Syrian part
  is the cuisine and the team. Written that way.
- **Founding date**: a directory (Drimble) gives an establishment date of 3 Dec 1999 for the
  Javastraat 20 business, and Ons Amsterdam calls it an "Iraqi supermarket", which with the name
  suggests the shop and name predate 2015 under an earlier (possibly Iraqi) owner. Not confirmed
  by any authoritative source, so the text says only that it began as a greengrocer and was taken
  over in 2015, and does not say who named it.
- **Did the founders come as refugees, and since when in Amsterdam?** No source. Left out.
- **Name of the Christian partner**: not published. Left out.
- **"Meeting point of the Syrian community"**: no source says this in so many words; the text
  sticks to the Syrian refugee team and the Syrian and Lebanese stock.
- **"Best falafel in Amsterdam"**: review language, left out (brief: no "best").
- **Token routine**: from Ons Amsterdam (article undated in the page, probably 2021-2023);
