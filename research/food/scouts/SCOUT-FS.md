# SCOUT-FS: food, drink and shops, Centrum south and east

Scouted 2026-11-25 for the food, drink and shops round (`../SCOUT-BRIEF.md`). Slice FS: wijken
Grachtengordel-Zuid (GGZ), De Weteringschans (WS), Weesperbuurt/Plantage (WP) and Oostelijke
Eilanden/Kadijken (OE): the southern canal belt, Leidseplein, Rembrandtplein and Thorbeckeplein,
Utrechtsestraat, Reguliersdwarsstraat, Weteringschans and Vijzelgracht, the Plantage, the
Weesperbuurt and the Eastern Islands. Nothing is written to the map: the user picks.

Method: pool read with `pnpm -s dc places sights --all` (2,437 places); SCOUT-FI sections 1 and 3
read so covered places are not proposed again; borderline lists of SCOUT-AE, BE, CE, DE, G and GR
read. Sources: Amsterdam op de kaart (theme "Cafés en restaurants", read per address), Het Parool,
Stichting De Bruine Kroeg (bruinekroeg.com, a 2026 heritage platform: a lead, never the only
source), Ons Amsterdam (search snippets only), Rijksmonumentenregister, shop and café sites,
Wikipedia. Pins are BAG address points from PDOK Locatieserver (building centroid of the address;
the door is within a few metres), wijk as PDOK returns it. "Checked" means current status checked
on 2026-11-25.

Summary: 10 new candidates (5 bars and cafés, 1 eatery, 3 shops, 1 market), plus pointers for
two eateries inside existing places. The benchmark Chagall shop is in this slice:
**Chagall Gallery Wuyt, Spiegelgracht 32** (FS-07). The Plantage, the Weesperbuurt and the
Eastern Islands gave nothing new that passes: the clearances of the 1960s and 70s took the old
cafés, and what is left is already in the pool (Eik en Linde, De Druif, Volkskoffiehuis,
Brouwerij 't IJ, Scheepskameel).

## Bars and cafés

### FS-01 Café Eijlders, the artists' café of the occupation (Korte Leidsedwarsstraat 47)

- **Hook:** opened on Christmas Eve 1940 under the occupation, it became the poets' café of the
  war and the room where the magazine Braak, cradle of the Vijftigers, was edited.
- **Why it passes:** history of national literary weight; poets still read here (a monthly
  poets' afternoon for over 20 years, an 85th-anniversary anthology "Thuis" presented in the
  café on 21 December 2025). The café says the interior is still largely as John Eylders set it
  up in the early 1940s.
- **Address, pin:** Korte Leidsedwarsstraat 47, 52.364905, 4.882462 (WS, Leidsebuurt-Noordwest).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** open; poets' events running in December 2025 and May 2026.
- **Sources:** nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eijlders (opening 24 Dec 1940, Braak editors); DBNL, Ons
  Erfdeel 2001 "Vijftig jaar na Vijftig" (Eijlders as the breeding ground of the little
  magazines, Hans Renders); cafeeijlders.com/geschiedenis-cafe-eijlders; Ons Amsterdam, "Café
  Eijlders is al 80 jaar een artistieke huiskamer"; eijldersdichters.com/historie.
- **Pool:** new (not named anywhere; CE listed it as borderline).
- **Caveats:** the "Voor Joden verboden" sign story (hung on a rubber sucker so it fell behind
  the stove) is told by the café, from Eylders's own 1971 autobiography, and by Ons Amsterdam,
  but they disagree on the year (1942 vs 1943); tell it as the owner's story, without a year.
  The claim that Eylders was jailed for months over hidden weapons is café-site only: leave out.
  Interior "unchanged" is the café's own claim. Sits in the Leidseplein tourist strip.
- **Rank:** Nice (the strongest of the slice; Amazing only if a literary-history source confirms
  more of the war story).

### FS-02 Café Slijterij Oosterling, bar and off-licence in one (Utrechtsestraat 140)

- **Hook:** a brown café that is also a liquor shop, with the old shop counter still serving as
  the bar: a double licence Amsterdam no longer grants.
- **Why it passes:** a rare survival of the café-slijterij type, in an 18th-century corner house
  (rijksmonument 5858) with a wooden shopfront; casks on the wall; a "silent" café without music.
  In the Oosterling family from the 19th century until 2023.
- **Address, pin:** Utrechtsestraat 140 (corner Frederiksplein), 52.361074, 4.899256 (WS,
  Utrechtsebuurt-Zuid).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** open, run since 2023 by Paul and Lucas Verbunt (the fourth
  Oosterling generation, Marcel and Oscar, handed it over); now opens at 9:00 for breakfast,
  "stamcafé" from 14:00. Interior kept, per the Parool piece on the new owners.
- **Sources:** cafeoosterling.nl/geschiedenis.html; Het Parool (PS), "Wel havermelk en
  natuurwijn, maar ook leverworst en kranten op tafel: zo houden deze nieuwe cafébazen de bruine
  kroeg in leven"; Het Parool, "Stokoude kroeg de beste in de stad"; monumentenregister
  cultureelerfgoed.nl monument 5858; bruinekroeg.com.
- **Pool:** new. The house is not in "Utrechtsestraat shop fronts" (yeplflqrfyo079vfc2cloqa0,
  nos. 104, 129, 132, 134).
- **Caveats:** dates conflict: own site 1877 (Gouda distiller family buys the house), Parool
  once 1897, bruinekroeg.com "café since 1820". The own-site claim that it was a VOC spice
  warehouse of 1735 has no second source: do not use. Write "family-run from the late 19th
  century" until the Stadsarchief settles it.
- **Rank:** Nice.

### FS-03 Café Reynders, the theatre's café across the square (Leidseplein 6)

- **Hook:** the café facing the Stadsschouwburg since the 1870s, first named after the theatre
  ("De Nieuwe Schouwburg"), then after the coachman who ran it for forty years.
- **Why it passes:** one of the oldest cafés on the Leidseplein, in a front of 1873 by J.H.
  Schmitz; Anton Reijnders ran it from about 1897 until his death in 1937, his two daughters
  after him; in the 1950s and 60s the haunt of artists, writers, actors and journalists. Old
  photos of the square hang inside.
- **Address, pin:** Leidseplein 6, 52.364619, 4.882891 (WS, Leidsebuurt-Noordwest).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** open (owner Kit de Romph, per Parool on the Heineken glasses).
- **Sources:** amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Leidseplein/6 (archival: population register,
  newspapers); Het Parool, "Café Reynders, daar kwamen alle kunstenaars en journalisten";
  amsterdamseschrijverscafes.wordpress.com (exhibition on writers' cafés).
- **Pool:** new. Leidseplein places (ydny5ublabvj4lt3qnlim3b5, x4ceovn5pexfe46lyho1413h) do not
  name it.
- **Caveats:** founding years vary by source (1880, 1896); use Amsterdam op de kaart's 1873 front
  and c. 1897 Reijnders. Today mostly tourists (Parool says so). Interior age not verified.
  Could be one place with Eijlders round the corner ("the Leidseplein's two artists' cafés").
- **Rank:** Okay.

### FS-04 Café Krom, the Monopole Bar of 1913 (Utrechtsestraat 76)

- **Hook:** a corner bar of 1913 with red octagonal tables, a wooden bar, old neon and a jukebox,
  named after the father and son who ran it from 1949 to 1994.
- **Why it passes:** intact inter-war interior (art deco elements), a municipal monument
  building of 1912, a neighbourhood institution with a Parool following (a barman wrote a book
  about it; the café counted its 101,000th Indisch meatball).
- **Address, pin:** Utrechtsestraat 76 / Kerkstraat 381, 52.362923, 4.898516 (GGZ,
  Amstelveldbuurt).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** open.
- **Sources:** amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Utrechtsestraat/76 (Monopole Bar 1913, Krom
  1949, gemeentelijk monument 201040); Het Parool, "De barman van café Krom schreef een boek over
  zijn kroeg"; Het Parool (PS), "Altijd bal: café Krom viert z'n 101.000ste Indische balletje";
  bruinekroeg.com.
- **Pool:** new.
- **Caveats:** Parool gives 1923 for the café; Amsterdam op de kaart's 1913 Monopole Bar is
  archive-based. Interior date ("1920s") is a shopping-street site claim: say "inter-war".
- **Rank:** Okay.

### FS-05 Café De Wetering, the fire upstairs (Weteringstraat 37)

- **Hook:** a corner brown café where a creaking stair leads to a mezzanine with a real wood
  fire, chess and Scrabble.
- **Why it passes:** a locals' café off the Spiegelgracht with an intact brown interior; Het
  Parool's obituary of owner Jan de Beer (1944-2021) called it "an iconic café in brown
  Amsterdam".
- **Address, pin:** Weteringstraat 37, 52.361229, 4.888831 (WS, Weteringbuurt).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** open under a new owner since De Beer's death.
- **Sources:** Het Parool, "Jan de Beer (1944-2021) maakte van De Wetering een iconisch café in
  bruin Amsterdam"; bruinekroeg.com (data 6 Nov 2026).
- **Pool:** new.
- **Caveats:** thin history (a 17th-century house is claimed by guides, unverified); passes on
  interior and fabric only.
- **Rank:** Okay.

## Eateries

### FS-06 Eetsalon Van Dobben, the croquette counter since 1945 (Korte Reguliersdwarsstraat 5-9)

- **Hook:** opened on 28 June 1945, weeks after liberation: three little houses knocked into one
  lunchroom where Amsterdam eats its croquette standing at the counter.
- **Why it passes:** an institution by any measure (the croquette brand became national); the
  room is the attraction: counter, tiles, white-coated staff. Artists and footballers' haunt from
  the start.
- **Address, pin:** Korte Reguliersdwarsstraat 5-9, pin on no. 7: 52.365973, 4.895076 (GGZ,
  Rembrandtplein e.o.).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** open; celebrated 80 years in June 2025 with free croquettes
  for 80-year-olds (Parool).
- **Sources:** eetsalonvandobben.nl/geschiedenis (founding, Aat van Dobben and Eugenie Laaper;
  no. 7 added 1950, no. 9 in 1958); Het Parool, "Eetsalon Van Dobben viert 80-jarig jubileum met
  gratis kroketten voor leeftijdsgenoten"; Culinaire Ambiance (interior "hardly changed").
- **Pool:** new.
- **Caveats:** the supermarket brand is a separate company (Parool: leaving supermarkets after a
  merger); the eatery had plans for a chain under newer owners. "Interior unchanged" is one food
  site's claim: check on site. Food-led, so it passes on institution status, not taste.
- **Rank:** Nice.

## Shops

### FS-07 Chagall Gallery Wuyt, the Chagall print shop (Spiegelgracht 32)

- **Hook:** the brief's benchmark: a canal-side gallery holding about five hundred original
  Chagall lithographs, etchings and woodcuts in passe-partouts, with a research centre that
  issues certificates of authenticity.
- **Why it passes:** a one-artist specialist found nowhere else in the city; part of the
  Spiegelkwartier (FS-09); the research centre has given some 500 lectures and 50 publications
  on Chagall's prints.
- **Address, pin:** Spiegelgracht 32, 52.362020, 4.886855 (WS, Leidsebuurt-Noordoost).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** site current (blog to October 2025, stock list live), but
  **open by appointment only** (gallery owner Erik de Wolf).
- **Sources:** chagall.nl (home and "Gallery Wuyt" page); kunstinzicht.nl gallery listing.
- **Pool:** new (FI: no text names a Chagall shop).
- **Caveats:** by appointment, so it fails "worth visiting without buying" unless the window
  counts; no founding date found; a company register aggregator (oozo.nl) lists the old
  registration in the past tense, so confirm it trades under that name. Best as a line inside
  FS-09, or a place only because the user named it.
- **Rank:** Okay (as its own place); Nice inside the Spiegelkwartier.

### FS-08 Concerto, the record shop that ate five houses (Utrechtsestraat 52-60)

- **Hook:** opened in 1955 in one house selling instruments and records; now five linked houses
  of new and second-hand vinyl, with its own stage for in-store gigs.
- **Why it passes:** a national institution (the largest record shop in the Netherlands, per its
  own claim) and a browsing destination; a 2020s book by historian Ewoud Kieft tells its story.
- **Address, pin:** Utrechtsestraat 52-60, pin on no. 54: 52.363451, 4.898321 (GGZ,
  Amstelveldbuurt).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** open seven days a week.
- **Sources:** concerto.nl/ons-verhaal; Het Parool (PS), "Graaien en grijsdraaien: het verhaal
  van Concerto, méér dan een platenzaak"; AT5, "Platenzaak Concerto bestaat 65 jaar" (founder
  Gijs Molenaar, 1955).
- **Pool:** new.
- **Caveats:** a modern business, not an old interior; passes on institution and local fabric.
  "Largest in NL" is a self-claim.
- **Rank:** Nice.

### FS-09 The Spiegelkwartier, antiques street (Nieuwe Spiegelstraat and Spiegelgracht)

- **Hook:** from the Herengracht to the Rijksmuseum, more than seventy antique dealers and
  galleries, from Delft tiles and roemers to Chagall prints; antique dealers have traded here
  since about 1900.
- **Why it passes:** a shop quarter that is a sight in itself (window-shopping is the point);
  Amsterdam op de kaart traces dealers on the street from 1910 (De Friesche Antiekwinkel of
  Samuel van Messel at no. 39, Aronson until 2014).
- **Address, pin:** Nieuwe Spiegelstraat, pin mid-street at no. 40: 52.363700, 4.888818 (GGZ).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** active quarter (Vereniging Spiegelkwartier site live).
- **Sources:** nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiegelkwartier (name coined by glass dealer Frides
  Laméris, a founder of the association); spiegelkwartier.nl/nl/over;
  amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Nieuwe_Spiegelstraat/39.
- **Pool:** new as a shop quarter; "Around the Spiegelgracht, a lesson for the rich and the worst
  hotel" (on5431qmdounxj2cvg1u0tkj) covers the street's buildings, not its trade: could be
  extended there instead.
- **Caveats:** a cluster, not one shop; the dealer count varies by year. Lines to fill it: Chagall
  Gallery Wuyt (FS-07), the oldest surviving dealer (not identified yet).
- **Rank:** Okay (Nice if a named old dealer with a period shop is found).

## Markets

### FS-10 The Monday plant market on the Amstelveld

- **Hook:** every Monday some thirty stalls of plants, herbs and bulbs fill the square beside
  the wooden Amstelkerk.
- **Why it passes:** a neighbourhood market on one of the canal belt's few squares, with the
  temporary church as backdrop; local fabric rather than history.
- **Address, pin:** Amstelveld, 52.362030, 4.897537 (GGZ, Amstelveldbuurt).
- **Status (checked 2026-11-25):** running (Mondays, about 9:00 to 18:00; market listings dated
  23 Nov 2026).
- **Sources:** hollandsemarkten.nl (weekmarkt Amstelveld); marktenmarkten.nl.
- **Pool:** not mentioned in "Amstelkerk, the temporary wooden church" (crl2f78zbjifgn1wqnyqnjkf)
  or "Reguliersgracht and the Amstelveld" (jypicc5pdsxydgqkxd2i4u14).
- **Caveats:** no history found (start year unknown); only market-listing sources. Better as one
  line in the Amstelkerk place than as a place of its own.
- **Rank:** Okay (line, not place).

## Pointers for "eateries inside existing places" (SCOUT-FI section 2, not numbered here)

- **Café Schiller**, Rembrandtplein 24A, inside Schiller place cunjo2jebngw6z8xk6lyuehc. Facts
  found: café own entrance on the square from 1933; art deco interior with Joop Sjollema's glass
  panel "Carnaval" (1948); building of 1921 (Lippits and Scholte), municipal monument
  (amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Rembrandtplein/Cafe_Schiller). Open; Het Parool's Mara Grimm
  reviewed it as a classic ("nog steeds een heerlijke huiskamer"); a forgotten Frits Schiller
  still life turned up in its cellar (Parool). Strong.
- **Café-Restaurant De Plantage**, Plantage Kerklaan 36, in the glass serre of Artis's
  Ledenlokalen (G.B. Salm, 1868-70, rijksmonument 518278), the building that held the population
  register bombed on 27 March 1943: place u6p2ol80uyz57kfkqse7uzdl. Open, terrace on the free
  Artisplein (hbiljbj4a04sg7rihtumeb3f). Worth one line there.
