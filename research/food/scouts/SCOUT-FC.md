## Bars and cafés

**FC-01. De Pilsener Club, "De Engelse Reet", Begijnensteeg 4.** Pin 52.369542, 4.890852.
Hook: a beer cellar opened as "Zum Pilsener Club" in 1893, here since 1899, in one family since
1928; in May 1940 the grandfather scratched "Zum" off the window, and everyone calls it the
Engelse Reet anyway. Why it passes: one of the most intact brown cafés in the city (no counter to
lean on in the old manner, sand on the floor, a round table given by the auctioneers of Mak van
Waay when they left the Rokin in 1974); the fourth Teun van Veen pours and still lives upstairs.
Status: open, family-run (Ons Amsterdam Oct 2018 "125 jaar"; listings 2026). Sources:
https://onsamsterdam.nl/artikelen/125-jaar-pilsener-club-annex-engelse-reet (NL, read via
BrowserOS); https://www.dutchfoodie.nl/pilsnerclub-de-engelse-reet (EN/NL, 2013); BC scout cites
Parool 2023 "most monumental brown café". Pool: new. Caveats: the name has three folk
etymologies (narrow alley to the English Church, English customers, musicians' "raid"); tell it
as a dispute. Rank: **Nice** (Amazing candidate if the interior photographs well).

**FC-02. Café Hoppe, Spui 18-20.** Pin 52.368775, 4.888615 (no. 18).
Hook: a brown café on the Spui since 1670 by its own count, the old half at no. 18 with jenever
barrels behind the bar, the newer half at no. 20 a former hotel joined on by Harry Mustert, who
gave it the Hoppe name in the 1930s. Why it passes: the Spui's living room for journalists,
politicians and students; Hans van Mierlo is said to have laid the ground for D66 here; Parool
keeps writing about its barmen (Ron and John, 35 years behind the bar, Ps Sept 2024).
Status: open (cafehoppe.com 2026, owners Jan-Wieger van der Linden and Pepijn Zonneveld).
Sources: https://www.parool.nl (Ps 14 Sept 2024; "De kracht van Hoppe op het Spui", 10 July 2016);
https://cafehoppe.com . Pool: new (SCOUT-FI "not named anywhere"). Caveats: "1670" is the
café's own claim (guides repeat it); several guides say no. 18 is a rijksmonument: confirm
in the register before using; the D66 and Provo stories need a second source. Rank: **Nice**.

**FC-03. Lokaal 't Loosje, Nieuwmarkt 32-34.** Pin 52.372391, 4.899498.
Hook: a waiting room for the horse tram turned brown café, its walls lined with tile tableaux
for jenever, liqueur and beer brands, among them a big Zuid-Hollandsche Bierbrouwerij wall of
about 1930-35. Why it passes: something to look at without drinking; one of the few tile-tableau
café interiors left in the old town; on the Nieuwmarkt, the Waag in front. Status: open daily
from 08:30 (loosje.nl, 2026). Sources: https://shie.nl/tegeltableau-zuid-hollandsche-bierbrouwerij
(SHIE: ZHB tableau ca. 1930-35, café owned
by the ZHB); https://www.loosje.nl/about ("wachtplaats voor de paardentram"); FC Centrum
"Ode aan de bruine kroeg", 16 April 2025. Pool: new. Caveats: tram waiting-room claim rests on
the café and guides; find a Stadsarchief photo or tram history to carry it; tile makers unknown.
Rank: **Nice**.

**FC-04. Café Scheltema, Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal 242.** Pin 52.372083, 4.890368.
Hook: the journalists' café of 1908, from the days when the Nieuwezijds was the city's Fleet
Street; the De Lange family has run it since the late 1960s. Why it passes: the last trace of
the newspaper street that the pool tells in "Behind the Palace" `wwk17qjojkuw89oq1riokm1b` and
't Kasteel van Aemstel `rgr895410m8qor8bmrldld5k`; the city gave it its jubilee medal in 2017.
Status: open Mon-Sat (caferestaurantscheltema.nl, 2026); survived a near-bankruptcy in the
corona years when well-known Amsterdammers rallied for it (Parool / AD). Sources:
https://caferestaurantscheltema.nl ; Parool "Bekende Amsterdammers in de bres voor bijna failliet
journalistencafé" (via indebuurt.nl). Pool: new as a café. Caveats: interior not checked in
person; could also be a member of the newspaper-street place instead of its own pin. Rank:
**Okay** (Nice if the interior is intact).

**FC-05. Café Karpershoek, Martelaarsgracht 2.** Pin 52.378123, 4.896763.
Hook: claims to be the oldest café in town, pouring since 1606 for sailors on the IJ, with sand
on the floor. Why it passes: the claim itself is part of the city's café lore; old tiles and
woodwork. Status: open; now run by the hospitality group RB Horeca ("Try our new menu"). Sources:
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martelaarsgracht (1606 inn for VOC sailors, silver sand on the
floor; 1629 is the date of the building opposite with "Kopje Koffie Karpershoek" on its cornice);
https://www.dutchnews.nl/features/2015/10/proost-heres-five-of-the-oldest-taverns-in-amsterdam/ .
Pool: new. Caveats: 1606 is tradition, not documented; owner by a group; may be more pub than
monument now. Rank: **Okay**, pitched as "the oldest-café claim" with the dispute told.

**FC-06. Café De Dokter, Rozenboomsteeg 4.** Pin 52.369254, 4.890826.
Hook: about 18 m², said to be the smallest café in town, opened in 1798 by a surgeon of the
Binnengasthuis, run by the same family (Beems) for six or seven generations. Why it passes: a
one-room relic by the Begijnhof. Status: open (Facebook, Christmas post 2026). Sources:
https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/cafe-de-dokter ; indebuurt "Mysterie opgelost: dit is het
kleinste café van Amsterdam" (6 Jan 2026, not read: may name another café as the smallest).
Pool: new. Caveats: only tourist sources for 1798 and the surgeon; fails our authority rule
until a Dutch local source carries it. Rank: **Okay**, conditional.

## Proeflokalen and brewers

**FC-07. Wynand Fockink tasting room, "De Gekroonde Wildeman", Pijlsteeg 31.**
Pin 52.372320, 4.895300.
Hook: a tapperij of 1689 whose landlord Bierman called it De Gekroonde Wildeman to tell it apart
from two other Wildemans nearby; Wynand Fockink took it over about 1730, and the 17th-century
signboard hangs inside. You bend to the brim-full glass: "buigen voor de borrel". Why it passes:
brief benchmark; 18th-century wooden shopfront and shop interior; Stadsherstel bought the
complex from Krasnapolsky in 1994 and a distiller revived the tasting room after persuading the
squatters to leave. Status: open; tasting room plus shop at no. 35; brand owned by Lucas Bols,
distillery renamed Lucas Bols Distilleerderij in 2013 (wynand-fockink.nl). Sources:
https://stadsherstel.nl/monumenten/pijlsteeg-35/ (NL: 1689, Bierman, 1730, signboard, 1994);
https://wynand-fockink.nl/nl/pages/our-location (firm: building 1679, Fockink from 1724);
Amsterdam Monumentenstad grachtenboek: Fockink buys in 1731. Pool: named only inside Pijlsteeg
`j4j5jv0g6p0kad8pojtqd7bp` and Behind the Dam `na99ycibssybp0qaq3hzafl9`; split out as its own
place. Caveats: **date conflict** 1679 (firm, and our Pijlsteeg text) vs 1689 tapperij / 1730-31
takeover (Stadsherstel, Monumentenstad): use Stadsherstel's and fix the Pijlsteeg text. "De
Gekroonde Wildeman" is the old tavern's name and signboard, not the firm's trading name.
Rank: **Amazing** (rare), matching De Drie Fleschjes.

**FC-08. Brouwerij De Prael, Oudezijds Armsteeg 26.** Pin 52.375572, 4.899390.
Hook: a brewery started in 2002 by Arno Kooy and Fer Kok to give work to people with a
psychiatric history, brewing in the old town behind the Oudezijds Voorburgwal, in a
Stadsherstel building. Why it passes: a working brewery you can see in a medieval lane, and a
social firm in the middle of the Wallen. Status: open, events listed late Nov 2026 (deprael.nl).
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brouwerij_de_Prael ; Stadsherstel "Oudezijds Armsteeg
26A, Bierbrouwerij de Prael in één van onze KLM-huisjes". Pool: mentioned only in Het
Veilinghuisje `ykinnyg8tbrbdhh8o3hhjkdc` (as a viewpoint). Caveats: young (2002); passes on
fabric and mission, not history; check whether ownership changed after its money trouble (not
found). Rank: **Okay**.

## Eateries

**FC-09. Nam Kee, Zeedijk 111-113.** Pin 52.373715, 4.900321.
Hook: the Chinatown restaurant of 1981 that Kees van Beijnum's novel "De oesters van Nam Kee"
(2000, film 2002) made a national name. Why it passes: the Zeedijk's literary landmark, part of
Chinatown's comeback after the heroin years. Status: open. Sources:
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_Kee ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_Kee . Pool: new (Chinatown
told in `q1ogooimbt808nocosyfc1nu` and `j5ynez7kwp0ww2j5z9gw3wo7`). Caveats: an ordinary
restaurant room; fails "worth it without eating" unless pitched as the novel's place. Rank:
**Okay**, or a line in the Chinatown cluster.

**FC-10. D'Vijff Vlieghen, Spuistraat 294-302.** Pin 52.369650, 4.888736.
Hook: a restaurant across five 17th-century houses, with a Rembrandt room and original Rembrandt
etchings on the walls. Status: open. Sources: Time Out Amsterdam; In Your Pocket (both EN, tourist).
Pool: new. Caveats: founding 1939 rests on one foreign guide; expensive tourist dining; houses'
monument numbers not checked. Rank: **Okay**, probably a near miss unless a Dutch source tells
its history.

## Bakeries, fish and food shops

**FC-11. Banketbakkerij Van der Linde, Nieuwendijk 183.** Pin 52.374895, 4.894272.
Hook: one flavour only, vanilla whipped-cream ice, sold from the same shop since 1937 with a
queue on the Nieuwendijk. Why it passes: a city institution in a chain-store street; on the
city's own short list of typically Amsterdam firms (with Van Wees, Jacob Hooy, Hartog). Status:
open. Sources: iamsterdam "Typisch Amsterdamse ondernemingen" (updated 19 March 2025, city
list only, not to be cited); Lonely Planet. Pool: new. Caveats: food-quality fame; needs a Dutch
local source (Parool) for 1937; the shop interior unknown. Rank: **Okay**.

**FC-12. Toko Dun Yong, Stormsteeg 9 / Zeedijk.** Pin 52.374338, 4.900931.
Hook: in 1959 Yong Sau Dun opened a toko in a former grocer's on the Zeedijk with his
Rotterdam wife: the first Chinese business on the street, now a large Asian store run by
the third generation. Why it passes: the seed of Chinatown on the Zeedijk. Status: open. Sources:
https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/356249/familie-dun-yong-haalt-al-decennia-de-smaak-van-azie-naar-amsterdam
(NH Nieuws; dated Jan 2026 per SCOUT-DC); https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown_(Amsterdam) (first Chinese business
on the Zeedijk 1959); OpNieuw 2024-2 (DC scout). Pool: named in the sources of Binnen
Bantammerstraat `j5ynez7kwp0ww2j5z9gw3wo7`. Caveats: "first Chinese supermarket in the country"
is a tour blog's claim: leave out. Rank: **Okay** (own pin) or a member line in the Chinatown cluster.

**FC-13. Stubbe's Haring, Haarlemmersluis (Singel).** Pin about 52.379096, 4.894428 (the lock
bridge; set on the stall). Hook: a family herring stall on the lock bridge where the Singel
begins; grandfather Stubbe started selling fish in 1967, first in the Jordaan. Why it passes:
the herring cart as a city ritual, at the old harbour edge. Status: open 2024; the family feared
for it when the city moved stall permits to fixed terms and lotteries (Hart van Nederland, July
2024). Sources: https://www.hartvannederland.nl/politiek/beleid/artikelen/blijft-de-haringkar-in-amsterdam-nieuwe-eis-jaagt-familiebedrijven-stad-uit ;
Frommer's. Pool: new. Caveats: tourist sites say "since 1903": wrong per the family; edge of my
slice (may belong to the west scout). Rank: **Okay**.

## Shops

**FC-14. De Posthumuswinkel, Sint Luciënsteeg 23-25.** Pin 52.370602, 4.890869 (no. 25; no. 23
has no BAG point). Hook: the engraver Jan Daniël Posthumus founded the firm in 1865; its shop by
the Amsterdam Museum still sells rubber stamps, sealing wax and seals, embossing and letter paper.
Why it passes: an old trade shop with a single craft, beside the gable-stone wall. Status: open
(posthumuswinkel.nl 2026; Amsterdamian, July 2026). Sources: https://www.posthumus.nl/informatie
(Koninklijke Fabrieken Posthumus, founded 1865); https://www.posthumuswinkel.nl/over-ons ;
https://www.postfabriek.nl/de-posthumuswinkel (2020). Pool: named in Sint Luciënsteeg
`zgmpb3yrn31hzncv1v3elcha`. Caveats: shop interior age unknown; check whether the firm and the
shop are still one company. Rank: **Nice**.

**FC-15. Antiquariaat A. Kok & Zn., Oude Hoogstraat 14-18.** Pin 52.371241, 4.897455.
Hook: a second-hand and antiquarian bookshop begun in 1946 on the Oude Hoogstraat, with some
60,000 books, prints and maps in a deep old-town building. Why it passes: one of the last big
walk-in antiquarian shops; browsing is free. Status: open Tue-Sat (ILAB member; Ton and Sascha
Kok). Sources: https://ilab.org/affiliate/antiquariaat-a-kok-zn-b-v ; AbeBooks shop page
(1946, moved into the present building about 40 years ago). Pool: new. Caveats: no local-source
story found; next to the Oudemanhuispoort, so could be a line there. Rank: **Okay**.

**FC-16. Condomerie, Warmoesstraat 141.** Pin 52.373679, 4.895872.
Hook: opened 1 May 1987 in the squatted Blaauwlakenblok, probably the first shop in the world
selling only condoms. Status: trading 2025. Sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condomerie ;
our Blaauwlakenblok text. Pool: named in Blaauwlakenblok `avb1dqu2ubtrhgalwlnhzhi1`. Caveats:
already told there; own pin only if the list view wants shops. Rank: **Okay**.

### Shops benchmark: the Chagall print shop (outside this slice)

**Chagall Galerie Wuyt, Spiegelgracht 32** (Spiegelkwartier). Pin 52.362020, 4.886855.
The "Marc Chagall-Huis" a stone's throw from the Rijksmuseum, "with the little Marc Chagall
houseboat opposite"; the ground floor was turned into a print gallery at the end of the 20th
century: some 500 Chagall lithographs, etchings and woodcuts in thirty drawers, each sold with a
registered certificate; the Chagall Research Centre in the same house does authenticity
reports (150 euro). Open **by appointment** with gallery owner Erik de Wolf. Source:
https://www.chagall.nl/nederlands/galerie/ . Coordinator confirms (south scout). The other
Chagall specialist, Kunsthuis Marc Chagall (Leo Verdegaal, 1999), left the Bloemgracht in
October 2008 and is now in Bergen. As a yardstick: specialist, one-of-a-kind, browsable, but
appointment-only, so it tests whether "walk in and look" is required.

## Markets

**FC-17. Postzegelmarkt, Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal (in front of no. 280).** Pin 52.371237,
4.890099. Hook: stamp, coin and postcard dealers on the filled-in Nieuwezijds every Wednesday and
Saturday, on the spot once called the Deventer Houtmarkt and later a flower market. Status:
listings say still running; no dated 2025-26 source found. Sources: amsterdam.info, Frommer's
(both tourist). Pool: new. Caveats: needs a walk past on a Wednesday or Saturday and a local
source. Rank: **Okay**, conditional.

## Near misses (and why)

- **Dorrius** (Spuistraat 289 / NZ Voorburgwal 338-342): billiard café 1890, Amsterdam School
  interior and Bogtman glass of 1921-24; closed 1987, then Kantjil & de Tijger (bankrupt May
  2025); the interior panelling was **illegally stripped in December 2025**, the city stopped
  the works, the glass is still there but partly damaged (Parool 24 Dec 2025; tikjeanders blog,
  July 2026). **Our place `nx21aa8bvlptujkrpk046pq7` needs a status update.**
- **Grand Café 1e Klas** (Centraal Station, platform 2b): the old first-class waiting room;
  bankrupt August 2025 (Parool 13 Aug 2025), a pop-up ran Sept-Oct 2026 (Parool 20 July 2026).
  No lasting business; the room could be a line in Arriving at Centraal Station
  `tlp9ba6wgk9zvadb07yuunha`.
- **Geels & Co** (Warmoesstraat 67): roaster and tea house since 1864, but the shop closed in
  2013 after almost 150 years (geels.nl); the firm trades wholesale elsewhere.
- **Casablanca** (Zeedijk 26): jazz café of the Zeedijk, now a karaoke bar; history unsourced.
- **Proeflokaal In de Olofspoort** (Nieuwebrugsteeg 13): listed permanently closed.
- **In 't Aepjen** (Zeedijk 1): café since 1987, already told in 't Aepgen `o8ulepnxt9mh76pv3ihaqnqh`.
- **De Bekeerde Suster** (Kloveniersburgwal 6-8): modern brewpub on the convent site; the
  convent-brewery story is unsourced.
- **Vleminckx** (Voetboogstraat 33): chip hatch since 1957 and a queue, but fame is food only.
- **Café Bern**, **Café Belgique**, **Gollem**, **Kapitein Zeppos**: ordinary bars, no story found.
- **The Bulldog** (Oudezijds Voorburgwal 90, 1975) and **Rusland** (one of the oldest

- **Maison de Bonneterie** (Kalverstraat/Rokin): store closed; told in Olieslagerssteeg
