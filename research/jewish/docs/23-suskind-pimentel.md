From the autumn of 1942 two people faced each other across the Plantage Middenlaan. In the [Hollandsche Schouwburg](place:Hollandsche Schouwburg, from theatre to deportation centre), the theatre where the occupier held Jews before deportation, Walter Süskind ran the administration for the Jewish Council. In the Jewish crèche opposite, where the children were kept apart from their parents, Henriëtte Pimentel was the director. What they did together is told in [The Hollandsche Schouwburg, the crèche and the children](doc:The Hollandsche Schouwburg, the crèche and the children); this is a short portrait of the two of them.

## Henriëtte Pimentel

Henriëtte Henriquez Pimentel was born in Amsterdam on 17 April 1876, the daughter of Nathan Pimentel and Rachel Oppenheimer, one of at least six children. In 1926 she became director of the crèche at Plantage Middenlaan 31, a day nursery opened two years earlier, and she lived in the building.

In October 1942 the occupier made the crèche an annex of the theatre: children up to about twelve were taken from their parents in the Schouwburg and kept across the road. Almost at once Pimentel began to look for ways to get them out. With Süskind and her staff she altered the records so that children could disappear from them, and she made contact with resistance groups that had hiding addresses. From the spring of 1943 she worked with Johan van Hulst, head of the Protestant teacher-training college next door, now the [National Holocaust Museum](place:National Holocaust Museum (Nationaal Holocaustmuseum), in the old teacher-training college): children were let into the college garden and, from May 1943, napped in one of its classrooms, from where escorts could take them out of the front door.

![The dormitory of the crèche on the Plantage Middenlaan, around 1942](commons:File:Slaapzaal van De Creche aan de Plantage Middenlaan, circa 1942.jpg)

On 26 July 1943 the crèche's children and staff were taken away, and Pimentel with them. She was held in Westerbork, deported to Auschwitz on 14 September 1943 and murdered there on 17 September, aged 67. The crèche was closed for good on 29 September 1943. Its building is gone; [Aldo van Eyck's Mothers' House](place:Aldo van Eyck's Mothers' House (Hubertushuis)) of 1980 stands on the site.

## Walter Süskind

Walter Süskind was born in Lüdenscheid, in Germany, on 29 October 1906, the son of Heymann Süskind and Frieda Kessler. Before the war he left Germany for the Netherlands with his wife, Johanna Natt. The family, with a daughter, Yvonne, born in 1939, lived in Bergen op Zoom and later in Amsterdam. He was a businessman with a lively personality, and as a German he knew the language and culture of the occupier well.

When the deportations began in July 1942, the Jewish Council made him responsible for the reception of the Jews brought into the Schouwburg. The post brought him into daily contact with Ferdinand aus der Fünten, head of the German office that organised the deportations, whose trust he cultivated. That position, and the use he made of it, is why he is remembered. He kept the theatre's card index, and he and his clerks made people vanish from it, above all children: names were left off the lists, and the children were taken across the road and on to the groups that hid them. From early 1943 he and Pimentel were in direct contact with those groups.

The same position put him in an impossible place. He had to deal with the Germans every day, keep the theatre running and watch the transports leave; what he could save was always a fraction of those who passed through.

How many children were saved is not known exactly. The National Holocaust Museum and the Jewish Cultural Quarter give about six hundred; other estimates run from five hundred to more than a thousand.

Süskind was caught in Amsterdam in September 1943 and sent to Westerbork. On 4 September 1944 he was deported with his wife and daughter to Theresienstadt, and on 23 October 1944 to Auschwitz. Johanna and Yvonne were murdered there. Süskind died on a death march in Central Europe in February 1945.

## Remembering them

On 30 August 1972 the city named a new bridge over the Nieuwe Herengracht, where it meets the Amstel, the Walter Süskindbrug; the chief rabbi, Aron Schuster, was at the unveiling of the plaque. Its text says that he withdrew people from the occupier at great risk to himself.

![Chief rabbi Aron Schuster at the unveiling of the Walter Süskind plaque on the Nieuwe Herengracht, 30 August 1972](commons:File:Plaquette onthuld ter nagedachtenis van Walter Suskind nieuwe houten brug over N, Bestanddeelnr 925-8433.jpg)

Pimentel waited longer. In July 2021 the Jewish organisation B'nai B'rith gave her, posthumously, its Jewish Rescuers Citation, for Jews who saved other Jews, in the Resistance Museum. The same year her name was given to the bridge over the Singelgracht at the Muiderpoort, moved there from a bridge in Nieuw-West to be closer to the theatre; it is one of the [bridges with wartime names](place:Bridges with wartime names (Roetersstraat and Plantage Middenlaan)) around the Plantage.
