### 01. Jewish Amsterdam: four centuries in one city (overview, front panel)
**Synopsis.** From the first Portuguese conversos around 1600 to the community of today: why
Amsterdam became "Mokum" (Yiddish for "the place"), how two communities (Sephardim and
Ashkenazim) lived side by side, the Jodenbuurt and its later quarters (Plantage, Weesperbuurt,
Transvaalbuurt, Rivierenbuurt, Zuid, Buitenveldert, Amstelveen), the 1796 emancipation, the
diamond trade and the labour movement, the murder of about three in four of the city's
roughly 80,000 Jews in 1940-1945, and the rebuilding after. Ends with a "how to use this map"
paragraph pointing to the other documents and routes.
**Places.** One or two per period: Portuguese Synagogue (cand.), Jewish Museum / Great
Synagogue (cand.), `fh0mc9q8q9xkj6yovtkgia65` J.D. Meijerplein and Vlooienburg,
`uet78dwntd2eiait1dr4qhew` De Burcht, `abc4cxzkbb2up9bt5ofdd59b` the Plantage at war,
`baktmd71by5dx5i3uikjobb9` Lekstraat synagogue, `nrgi6zgog3xjlyaryea20c1r` Liberal synagogue,
`pidq899ir2djko9s5glpmh6u` Weesp synagogue, Beth Haim Ouderkerk (cand., outside).
**Sources.** Blom et al. (above); USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia, "Amsterdam" (EN);
joodsamsterdam.nl "achtergrond" / "background" pages (NL/EN/DE/HE); Jaap Meijer, *Het
verdwenen ghetto* (1948, NL, a classic first-person portrait); Amsterdam 750, "De Jodenbuurt
na 1945".

### 02. The Portuguese Nation: the Sephardic arrival and the Golden Age
**Synopsis.** Conversos from Portugal and Spain, Catholic on paper, settle from about 1595 and
return to Judaism; three congregations merge in 1639 into Talmud Torah; Beth Haim at
Ouderkerk (1614) because the city refused a burial ground inside; the Esnoga of 1675 and the
Ets Haim library; trade with Brazil, the Caribbean and the Levant; the Jodenbreestraat
mansions, the De Pinto house; poor relief within the nation; and the Sephardim's place in
colonial trade, including the slave trade and Suriname plantations (state it plainly).

### 03. The Hoogduitsers: Ashkenazi Amsterdam

### 04. Citizens at last: emancipation and assimilation (1796-1940)
**Synopsis.** The Batavian decree of 2 September 1796 makes Jews equal citizens, the first in
Europe after France; the split of 1797 (Adath Jesurun); King Louis Napoleon's and King
William I's reorganisation into a state-recognised church (the Hoofdcommissie, the
Opperrabbinaat, Dutch replacing Yiddish in schools and sermons); the rise of a Jewish
bourgeoisie: Jonas Daniël Meijer, the first Jewish lawyer; Samuel Sarphati; A.C. Wertheim;
department stores (Gerzon, De Bijenkorf, Metz & Co); the move out of the old quarter to the
Plantage, the canal belt and Zuid.

### 05. The Jodenbuurt: life in the old quarter, and its clearance
**Synopsis.** What the Jodenbuurt was around 1900 (density, the markets on the Waterlooplein
and Jodenbreestraat, the Wazepergas, schools, bathhouses, charities, theatres); the first slum
clearances of Uilenburg and the Valkenburgerstraat from 1900 to 1930, which moved families to
the Transvaalbuurt and Nieuw Uilenburg; the emptying by deportation and the Hunger Winter
stripping of empty houses; and the post-war decision to clear rather than rebuild: the
Weesperstraat motorway, the metro, the Stopera, the riots of 1975 and what was saved (De
Pinto). Also the post-war recognition (in German compensation law) of three Amsterdam
neighbourhoods as "ghettos".

### 06. Diamonds and the union: the ANDB and the Jewish working class

IJsbreker as meeting place); the industry's collapse after 1940 (murder of most Jewish
workers, the trade moved to Antwerp).

### 08. Synagogues of Amsterdam: a guide (guide, not an essay)

### Biographies

### 18. Baruch Spinoza (1632-1677)
**Synopsis.** Born on Vlooienburg into the Portuguese nation, educated in its schools, ran
the family's import business, excommunicated by the Talmud Torah congregation in 1656, lived
and wrote outside the city and in The Hague; why Amsterdam both produced and expelled him;
the 2008 statue by the Stopera.
**Places.** `qs04c1uh8vx5juadbuq9a6kf` Vlooienburg under the Stopera, `fh0mc9q8q9xkj6yovtkgia65`,
Portuguese Synagogue (cand.), Beth Haim (his family's graves, cand.), `nin5wqx8nkfg5eubvbfa3cqs`
Spinoza Lyceum (Krop statue).
**Sources.** Steven Nadler, *Spinoza: A Life* (CUP, 1999; 2nd ed. 2018, EN); The Spinoza Web
(spinozaweb.org, locations pages, e.g. "Amsterdam, Houtgracht", EN); Ons Amsterdam, "De vaste
route van André Klukhuhn" (2017, NL, a Spinoza walk); Vereniging Het Spinozahuis.

### 19. Menasseh ben Israel (1604-1657)
**Synopsis.** Rabbi, teacher, the city's first Hebrew printer (1626), author in Spanish,
Latin and Hebrew, friend and sitter of Christian scholars, and the man who went to Cromwell
in 1655 to ask for the readmission of Jews to England; buried at Ouderkerk. The Rembrandt
connection (the etched portrait attribution is disputed: say so).
**Places.** `v2g7szo75t2que3ogpnbhbul` Jodenbreestraat (he lived in the street), Rembrandthuis
(cand.), Portuguese Synagogue (cand.), Beth Haim (cand.).
**Sources.** Steven Nadler, *Menasseh ben Israel: Rabbi of Amsterdam* (Yale UP, 2018, EN);
joodsamsterdam.nl "personen"; Rembrandthuis research pages (NL/EN).

### 20. Henri Polak (1868-1943)
**Synopsis.** A diamond cutter from the Jodenbuurt who built the ANDB, co-founded the SDAP and
the NVV, sat in the council and the Senate, fought for the eight-hour day and for worker
education and beauty (Berlage's Burcht), and died in Laren in February 1943 after arrest.
**Places.** `uet78dwntd2eiait1dr4qhew` De Burcht, `hiraeu6s6m3se6wmjhr9neyt` Henri Polaklaan,
`wx78pd7ntjks2jk1j84b02p8`, `gyh3uhrd2xaddjiig05gxlrf`, Polak's homes (cand.).
**Sources.** Bloemgarten (doc 06); parlement.com "Dr. H. (Henri) Polak" (NL); IISG ANDB and
Polak archives; Leydesdorff.

### 21. Samuel Sarphati (1813-1866) (optional, or fold into doc 04)
**Synopsis.** Physician and city improver: rubbish collection, a bread factory, the Paleis
voor Volksvlijt, the Amstel Hotel, the plan for the Pijp; why a Portuguese Jew became the
model citizen of liberal Amsterdam.
**Places.** `pjz6wbejfbc7mb01wbtrucmo`, `xt1qvhjm0c30868v1pk5llpq`, `sbhpzekdnypghuwnmnpv9ozf`,
`ah5lmw1xl9roe8ruvgjmmdrs`, `xkwompqwrry6n3m37ylhudgq` (Dr. Sarphatihuis).
**Sources.** joodsamsterdam.nl "personen" Sarphati and "Paleis voor Volksvlijt"; Amsterdam
750 (NL); a full biography exists (find exact author and title before citing).

### 22. Etty Hillesum (1914-1943)
**Synopsis.** A law and Slavic graduate from Deventer who lodged at Gabriël Metsustraat 6 by
the Museumplein, worked briefly for the Joodsche Raad, went voluntarily to Westerbork to
help, and was murdered in Auschwitz in November 1943. Her diaries (1981) became world
literature; her Amsterdam was a few streets in Zuid.
**Places.** `gjmvybkmm6bvqa9jf1zs7izc` her house, `klvfehuqt5mj0h0ni3abjhak` Museumplein 19,
`x8u6od6w0senquj43mdprgb2` Zentralstelle site, `f0e9sn9xj7yak1x2xyez1p30` Vondelpark mirror.
**Sources.** Judith Koelemeijer, *Etty Hillesum. Het verhaal van haar leven* (Balans, 2022,
NL); *Etty: The Letters and Diaries of Etty Hillesum 1941-1943* (Eerdmans, 2002, EN, complete
edition); Het Parool, "Wandelen in Amsterdam langs de fietstochten van de joodse schrijfster
Etty Hillesum" (31 Jan 2023, NL); Etty Hillesum Centrum Deventer.

### 23. Walter Süskind (1906-1945) and the children
**Synopsis.** A German-Jewish businessman made manager of the Hollandsche Schouwburg by the
Joodsche Raad, who with Henriëtte Pimentel and Johan van Hulst got children out of the
crèche; his ambiguous position (hero or collaborator, the question his biographer poses) and
his death on a march in 1945. A double portrait with Pimentel (murdered in Auschwitz) is
worth considering.
**Places.** Hollandsche Schouwburg (cand.), `ujned0pvuv0x2c275827m2b6`, `abc4cxzkbb2up9bt5ofdd59b`,
`lisy94enpr138l3ja80n3xjl` Van Hulst bridge, Walter Süskindbrug plaque (cand.), Henriëtte
Pimentelbrug monument (4en5mei 4565, cand.), Süskind's home on the Nieuwe Prinsengracht
(cand.; Stadsherstel owns it, see routes).
**Sources.** Schellekens (doc 11); Flim (doc 11); 4en5mei.nl records 2947 and 4565;
Stadsherstel, "Wandeling Joods Amsterdam" (20 Apr 2015); Joods Monument (Pimentel's page).

## 3. Stolpersteine

**How many.** Stichting Stolpersteine Amsterdam says "more than 4,500" on its "Waar liggen
Stolpersteine" page (read 24 Nov 2026). nl.wikipedia gave "almost 3,400" for April 2025
(third city after Berlin and Hamburg). Milestones: first stones 2010 (the Jewish gymnasts who
won gold in 1928, and their coach Gerrit Kleerekoper, coordinated by the Olympic Stadium);
nos. 999 and 1,000 on 17 Dec 2021 (Louis Lamm and his daughter Ruth Fanny); no. 2,500 on 27 June
2024 for Klara Borstel-Engelsman, the oldest Dutch Holocaust victim; in 2021 nine stones for
lesbian and gay victims; in 2025 stumbling thresholds at the tram stops Artis/National
Holocaust Museum, Victorieplein and Beethovenstraat. The foundation stopped taking requests
in November 2025 (waiting list about 1,700 at the end of 2024); the stop was lifted on 8
September 2026 (FAQ). It may order 56 stones a month from Stiftung Spuren (NU.nl, 2 May 2026).

**Where the data lives, and licences.**
- *stolpersteine.app* (Stichting 18 September): map and search by name and street, Europe-wide,
  updated daily; the foundation points to it. No open licence or API found; treat as look-up
  only.
- *nl.wikipedia* "Lijst van Stolpersteine in Amsterdam-Centrum / -Noord / -Oost / -West /
  -Zuid": inscription, address, person, photo; Centrum alone has 177 table rows; no
  coordinates; incomplete by its own admission; CC BY-SA 4.0. Useful for picking emblematic
  stones and for inscriptions.
- *OpenStreetMap*: 33 nodes tagged `memorial=stolperstein` in Amsterdam (Overpass, 24 Nov
  2026), ODbL. Not useful.
- *Wikidata*: 12 items (instance of Stolperstein, located in Amsterdam or below). Not useful.
- *Gemeente Amsterdam open data* (api.data.amsterdam.nl): no Stolpersteine dataset.
- *Joods Monument* person pages give the address the stone is laid at, and can be cited per
  person (see section 4).

**Recommendation.** Do not pin individual stones. Thousands of identical brass squares would
drown the map, the data has no open coordinates, and a stone's meaning is the person, which a
place card cannot carry well. Instead:
1. **A document** (doc 16, "Stolpersteine in Amsterdam"): what, how many, how to find one,
   the waiting list, the criticism, the thresholds, polishing on 4 May; link to
   stolpersteine.app for searching.
2. **A handful of emblematic places** where the stones are the story, attached to doc 16:
   - `v4dfz50jf57ixhkh46pcjnp6` Twelve stumbling stones at one door (Nieuwe Prinsengracht 18): keep.
   - `u14h17h0d9jqlybgo8px2jk9` the Brilleslijper house: keep.
   - `ktqm7g4r7suxs6vqvmoho41p` the stumbling threshold at the Artis stop: keep; add the
     Victorieplein and Beethovenstraat thresholds (2025) as candidates (slice R) or as one
     place "Three stumbling thresholds" with the Artis one as the pin.
   - `c4xruaeu78hpbgc0v72qjeo9` the Weesp cantor's house: keep (stones plus building).
   - Candidates for slice C/R to pin: the 1928 Olympic gymnasts' stones (first in Amsterdam,
     2010); the 1,000th stones (Louis and Ruth Fanny Lamm, 2021); the 2,500th (Klara
     Borstel-Engelsman, 2024); one of the 2021 stones for gay and lesbian victims. Each only
     if the address and story can be confirmed from two sources.
   - `xa5qogswc15o84txcx33ly1h` Stolpersteine for Meijer and Regina Turfkruier: on this map,
     fold into doc 16 as an example rather than a pin (it has no story beyond the stones),
     unless slice C finds more.
3. **A line in place texts**: where a pool place has stones at its door (many in INVENTORY
   do: `b06lfs8lzyqrbxo3w6frpfc0`, `pidq899ir2djko9s5glpmh6u` and others), mention them in
   the text rather than adding places. If the map gets custom fields, a simple
   `stolpersteine: <count>` attribute would let a list view show them.
Sensitivity: the stones name the dead, which is fine; avoid naming or implying anything about
the people who live at those addresses now (the [VZM] audio walk makes the same point).

**C. Joods Monument (joodsmonument.nl, run by the Joods Cultureel Kwartier).**
- What: an online monument for more than 109,000 Dutch Jews, Roma and Sinti who did not
  survive (Jews killed or died 10 May 1940 to 8 May 1945, and Dutch Jews deported from
  elsewhere); each person page has birth and death data, family links and the last address;
  stories, photos and archive cards (Stadsarchief archiefkaarten) are attached. Survivors
  appear only anonymised. Visitors' contributions are CC BY-NC by default (FAQ).
- How to query: the search page (/nl/search) is a JavaScript app with "Uitgebreid zoeken";
  search by street name to see who lived in a building. No public API found. Use it per
  address to check a house story, not for bulk download.
- For bulk work use **Oorlogsbronnen** (data.oorlogsbronnen.nl, Stichting WO2Net): the
  "WO2 Personen" linked-data set (person reconstructions built from sources including the
  Joods Monument) is CC BY-SA 4.0, served through an Open API
  (data.oorlogsbronnen.nl/services/api/docs) and a SPARQL endpoint at LDMax
  (ldmax.nl/organisaties/wo2net), with an API and a SPARQL cookbook. Whether residence
  addresses are exposed per person needs checking in the application profile
  (data.oorlogsbronnen.nl/schema) before relying on it.

**D. The 1941 "stippenkaart".** "Verspreiding van de Joden over de gemeente Amsterdam per mei

Plantage, Oud-Zuid, Centrum-Oost) and as an illustration for docs 01 and 09.

**E. Gemeente Amsterdam monuments API.** `https://api.data.amsterdam.nl/v1/monumenten/v1/`

building dates for synagogues, schools and institutions. No Stolpersteine or war-memorial
set in the DSO catalogue (checked the dataset list).
