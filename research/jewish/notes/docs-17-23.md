## 17 Jewish Amsterdam today

Sources per claim:
- Population 2010: about 53,000 (37,000 halachic, 16,000 with only a Jewish father); nearly half
  in the Amsterdam agglomeration: NIDI *Demos* 26/7 (2010), Van Solinge & Van Praag, table and text.
  Prewar 77,000 registered in the city: as in doc 01.
- NIHS self-description ("grootste orthodox-Joodse gemeente", the three T's, NIK affiliation, ten
  sjoelgemeenschappen, members from very orthodox to strongly liberal, mikwe, Beth Din, kasjroet,
  cemeteries Diemen and Muiderberg "onder meer", office Max Havelaarlaan Amstelveen): nihs.nl
  "Wat is de NIHS?", home page, "Kasjroet" (rabbinate supervises bread, meat, restaurants,
  catering in Amsterdam and Amstelveen; kasjroetlijst).
- Obrechtsjoel (largest Ashkenazi in use; most weddings), Gerard Dou (19 May 1945), Lekstraat
  (hall let; small shul in daily use): pool place texts, doc 13. All three are named on nihs.nl as
  NIHS sjoelgemeenschappen.
- PIG: Talmud Torah 1639, Esnoga 1675, winter in Ets Haim, UNESCO register, Beth Haim: pool
  place texts and doc 01. portugesesynagoge.nl refused connection; not used.
- LJG: founded 1932 (pool place text), nearly 2,000 members, NVPJ/EUPJ/WUPJ, equal roles of men and
  women, building of 2010 with two prayer halls, library, classrooms, mikwe, "grootste synagoge
  van de Benelux" (given as the congregation's own claim): ljgamsterdam.nl home and "Over ons".
- Beit Ha'Chidush in the Uilenburgersjoel since 1997: pool place text (Stichting Uilenburgersjoel
  link); beithachidush.nl confirms a progressive congregation (no location on the page read).
- JCK composition (Jewish Museum with children's museum, Portuguese Synagogue, Hollandsche
  Schouwburg, National Holocaust Museum): JCK-linked listings (UvA alumni page, IEMJ); NHM
  reopening March 2024: doc 11. Shared ticket Jewish Museum/Esnoga: Esnoga place text; memorial
  free: doc 11. Waag 1932 to 1987: Waag place text. Menasseh ben Israel Instituut (JCK, UvA, VU):
  SOURCES.md.
- NIW since 1865: Delpher holdings (SOURCES.md); Stadsarchief archive 1379.
- Kosher food around the Kastelenstraat, museum café without ticket, Sal Meijer back in an
  Amstelveen food court: JCK "Koosjer eten in Amsterdam". Sal Meijer closed 2019 after 62 years,
  lack of kosher supervisors and costs, corned beef: JTA (2019); return: NH Nieuws.
- Nationale Holocaust Herdenking, last Sunday of January, Nederlands Auschwitz Comité, Spiegelmonument
  Wertheimpark, silent walk, speeches, wreaths: NOS (several years), Tweede Kamer news.
- Open Joodse Huizen / Huizen van Verzet, 4 and 5 May, JCK: Joods Monument flyer (2016), dagjeweg

- Februaristaking commemoration 25 Feb at the Dokwerker: doc 10 / pool place.

## 18 Spinoza

Sources per claim:
- Herem: Stadsarchief archive 334, section 5.1.1, inv. 19 "Escamoth A" (1638-1680), found in the
  archive's PDF inventory; the Commons scan is described as from the Escamoth. Content and phrases
  ("evil opinions and acts", "abominable heresies", "monstrous deeds", four cubits, no reading of
  his writings): Nadler's translation in *Spinoza: A Life*; JTA 2015 confirms "evil opinions and
  acts" and that no specific act is named. NL wording paraphrased from the Portuguese ("más
  opiniões e obras", "horrendas heregias", "ynormes obras").
- Harshest herem, mainstream reading (God not a lawgiver, no immortal soul, Law not eternal),
  Da Costa and Prado, the 1616/1619 condition of orthodoxy, Van den Enden, family business with
  Gabriel, dried fruit and nuts, mother's death 1638, birth 24 Nov 1632, death 21 Feb 1677:
  Nadler. Spinoza Web: house on the Houtgracht/Leprozengracht corner (now Mozes en Aäronkerk),
  birthplace unknown, synagogue on the Houtgracht 1639, parents at Beth Haim, family via Nantes,
  Michael a parnas, Gabriel continued the firm after 1654. Canon van Nederland: languages, Torah as
  "product van de menselijke fantasie", Rijnsburg, The Hague, lens grinding, anonymous TTP 1670,
  Ethica posthumous.
- Ban today: JC (2012 request by the board, Toledano's decision); JTA, 10 Dec 2015 (symposium,
  "neither the authority nor the will", "would imply we share the views").
- Statue: pool place text (Dings, 24 Nov 2008, citizens' foundation, inscription).

Left out: the 2021 dispute in which the community declared a Spinoza scholar persona non grata (JTA,
Jüdische Allgemeine: a living person, a quarrel rather than history); the Toledano letter on
chazzanut-esnoga.org (behind a bot shield, not read); Menasseh as Spinoza's teacher (no evidence,
said so in doc 19); the Spinoza Lyceum (not on the map). Discrepancy for the place team: the
Vlooienburg place text says Spinoza was "born nearby on the Zwanenburgwal"; Spinoza Web says the
birthplace is unknown (records missing). The doc says unknown.

Pictures: herem page (Commons, public domain, 1656); Dings statue (Brbbl, 2008, CC BY-SA 3.0).

## 19 Menasseh ben Israel

Sources per claim: born 1604 probably Lisbon as Manoel Dias Soeiro (Nadler & Tiribás, Kroniek
Rembrandthuis 2021; NNBW; Britannica "Lisbon?"); Madeira and La Rochelle, Uziel 1620, marriage 1623
to Rachel Abravanel, children Joseph, Samuel, Gracia, press 1626 and first book a Spanish-rite prayer
book, Conciliador 1632, Esperança 1650, Nishmat Hayyim 1651, Piedra gloriosa 1655, autumn 1655 to
England, death Middelburg 20 Nov 1657 at his brother-in-law's, burial Ouderkerk: NNBW (J.M. Hillesum,
1937). Third of four rabbis in 1639, salaries 150/450/600, Nieuwe Houtmarkt address, not
Rembrandt's neighbour, 1636 etching doubted (Gersaint 1751), Belshazzar probable, Piedra etchings
certain, De resurrectione 1636, De fragilitate 1642: Kroniek 2021. Press first in Amsterdam: Spinoza
Web; NNBW hedges (an earlier press may have existed), hence "by most accounts". Whitehall
conference, no formal decision, informal toleration, Vindiciae 1656, son Samuel's death: Britannica
and Nadler (mainstream).

Left out: the £100 pension from Cromwell and "never received" (Wikipedia-level only); the 1654
stipend of 600 guilders "to dissuade him from going to England" (Kroniek, but the payer is not clear);
Rachel's descent from Isaac Abravanel; the "open letter of 1651" (Leeds library page only);
the 1636 etching as a portrait (presented as disputed, not used as a picture).

Pictures: Salom Italia's engraved portrait, 1642 (public domain); Rembrandt's four Piedra gloriosa
etchings on the uncut plate, Rijksmuseum RP-P-OB-66 (CC0).
