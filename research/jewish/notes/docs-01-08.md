# Notes: documents 01 to 08

Writer notes for `research/jewish/docs/01-*` to `08-*` (EN and NL). Sources per claim, what was left out and why, pictures, and title choices.

## Conventions

- Dutch files link with the **English** titles as `doc:` and `place:` targets (the import keys on the English title); only the visible link text is Dutch. Same as the 09 and 10 NL files.
- Dutch titles: 01 Joods Amsterdam, vier eeuwen; 02 De Portugese Natie; 03 De Hoogduitsers; 04 Emancipatie en assimilatie, 1796 tot 1940; 05 De Jodenbuurt en haar afbraak; 06 Diamant en de ANDB; 07 Liefdadigheid, scholen en zorg; 08 Synagogen van Amsterdam.
- Link texts in NL for docs by other writers (e.g. "Vervolging, 1940 tot 1943, en de Joodsche Raad", "Joods Amsterdam nu", "Terugkeer en de naoorlogse jaren") are my renderings; the other writers may choose other NL titles, which does not break the links.
- Quotations in NL turned into indirect speech where the Dutch original wording was not in hand (Wertheim; "Nederlanders onder de Nederlanders").
- Main source key: **W** = Levie Bernfeld and Wallet, *Jews in the Netherlands: A Short History* (AUP 2023, open access), read in full from the OAPEN PDF. **JM** = Joods Monument theme pages (482 nineteenth/twentieth century; 671 markets; 749 diamond workers). **Place** = the live place texts on the Jewish Heritage map, which were audited against authoritative sources earlier (audit.md).

## 01 Jewish Amsterdam, four centuries

- Arrival, Beth Jacob c.1602, 1639 merger, Esnoga 1675, Ashkenazi congregation 1635, Great Synagogue 1671, population 1650/1700/1725/1795/1808: W.
- 1796 decree, 1814 denomination, 1817 language law, 1848, Godefroi 1849/1860: W.
- Before 1941 about 75,000 to 80,000 Jews in Amsterdam (USHMM: ~75,000; over 79,000 registered 1941); Tammes 2017 (PMC5493707): over 77,000 registered, victimisation 74 to 75 per cent; NIOD/NOS: 140,000 in NL, about 102,000 murdered. Given as a range on purpose.
- About 25,000 in hiding NL-wide, about a third caught: USHMM.
- About 60,000 deported from Amsterdam; Hunger Winter stripping; 9 May 1945 service in the Esnoga with about 1,000: Anne Frank House timeline.
- Names Monument: place text.
- Left out: "a quarter of VOC shares" (contested, only in older literature); exact postwar community size (no single authoritative figure seen).

## 02 The Portuguese Nation

- Conversos, Zeeland 1587, States General 1588, Beth Jacob, Neve Shalom, Beth Israel, 1639 merger, Beth Haim 1614 with about 27,000 graves and Black Jewish servants buried there, Dotar 1615, Talmud Torah 1616, Ets Haim 1637, Pereyra silk, printing, Golden Age 1648 to 1672, Esnoga 1675 with 1,200/440 seats and its role as model for London, Willemstad, Paramaribo, New York: W.
- De Pinto family and the Aansprekersoproer of 1696; Isaac de Pinto (1717 to 1787): W and place text. "Owned", not "bought 1651" (the 1651 purchase is Wikipedia-only).
- Atlantic: Dutch Brazil 1630 to 1654, Aboab 1641, Zur Israel, sugar mills owned by Jews: W. Caribbean "considerable stake in the slave trade", Curaçao mother community from the 1690s, sending the poor to the colonies since 1622, Surinam project 1747: W.
- Jews one third to one half of the white population of Curaçao and Suriname by the 18th century; WIC capital 3,000,000 guilders of which Jews 36,000 (1.2 per cent); 7 of 167 (1656) and 10 of 192 (1671) major shareholders; misuse of the subject to blame Jews: Roitman, *Studia Rosenthaliana* 49:1 (2023).
- Jodensavanne: Cassipora 1650s, Jodensavanne 1680s, "inhabited, owned, and governed by Jews who lived there together with free and enslaved persons of African and Indigenous descent": UNESCO World Heritage listing (2023).
- Left out: number of plantations and enslaved people at Jodensavanne (only from secondary/Wikipedia-type sources); individual slave-trader names and numbers (Wikipedia only); "a quarter of VOC shares"; De Paepe as a source (removed from NL).

## 03 The Hoogduitsers

- Ashkenazi congregation 1635; Muiderberg 1642 (about 45,000 graves); Polish congregation 1660, forced merger 1673; NIHS still the largest congregation: W.
- Abodat Hahesed 1642 to 1670, Rasphuis, Portuguese condescension into the 20th century: W.
- Great Synagogue 1671 (Bouman and Stalpaert, 399 men, 368 women), Obbene 1685, Dritt 1700/1778, Neie 1730/1752, museum 1987: W.
- Uilenburg house synagogue 1724, Uilenburgersjoel 1766: place text and W. Kokshofje owned by the High German community in 1772: place text.
- 1,000 (1650), 9,000 (1725), 14,000 vs 4,583 (1750), about 24,000 including 20,000 Ashkenazim (1795), census complaint of 1795, 87 per cent on relief, trades, 1748 guild petition, rich families Gomperts/Goldsmid/Van Oven, *smous*, 1720 crash: W.
- Talmud Torah 1660, Loewenstamm and Beth Hamidrash Ets Haim 1740, Rapenburgerstraat 1883, rabbis from abroad: W.
- Hebrew/Yiddish printing, Yiddish loanwords: W; word list also in Beem.
- Zeeburg 1714, reasons for burial there, about 95,000 burials 1714 to 1942, about 100 stones left, Diemen from 1914: W.
- Left out: Polish arrival numbers (no firm figure); exact 1795 census text (paraphrased only).

## 04 Emancipation and assimilation, 1796 to 1940

- Felix Libertate 1795 (membership mix), Rights of Man in Yiddish, 2 September 1796, 1797 split with 21 members (Adath Jesurun, Naye Kille), Diskoersn, Bromet and De Lémon 1798 first Jews in a European national parliament: W.
- 1808 reunion and Upper Consistory under J.D. Meijer, 1810, 1814 Israelite Denomination, 1848, NIK/PIK 1870: W. Portrait caption: Rijksmuseum/Commons.
- 10 May 1817 law, enforcement around 1835, Seminary 1836 and 1839, Yiddish gone in a century: W.
- 1857 act effective 1861, three Jewish day schools by 1915, NIW 1865: W.
- Godefroi 1849/1860; Wertheim (1832 to 1897), chair 1886, did not keep kosher or Sabbath, quote: W (English translation in W). Wertheimpark 1898: place text.
- Bijenkorf 1870 Nieuwendijk, Gerzon, Metz & Co lost 1941: place texts.
- Rivierenbuurt 4,000 (1930) to 17,000 (1940): place text (Lekstraat).
- Eastern Europeans from 1881, Russensjoel 1889: W and place text.
- De Talmudjood 1890, libel conviction; Zionist League 1899, never over 1 per cent: W.
- "Dutch among the Dutch and Jewish among the Jews": JM 482.
- Left out: the claim that Jews formed their own pillar (pillarisation is debated; replaced by "no political party of their own"); the year 1908 for the Blasiussjoel (place text gives 1890 for the society and no firm year for the building, so no year given); "first in Europe" claims beyond the 1798 parliament one.

## 05 The Jodenbuurt and its clearance

- Waterlooplein from 1882, daily from 1893, closed Saturdays: place text. 1934 market figures, about 300 stalls each, May 1938 nearly a third of pedlars Jewish (excluding rag-and-bone men), September 1941 ban and four Jewish markets, Uilenburgerstraat market never reopened: JM 671.
- Valkenburgerstraat no. 195 (over 300 people, 39 one-room homes), 1926 "disgrace" and 532 homes, sixteen alleys: place texts (Valkenburgerstraat 202-210, Zonnehofje).
- 1910: 368 of 861 Uilenburg homes declared unfit, first slum clearance: place text. Nieuw Uilenburg 1917 to 1922, 1927 block, Handwerkers Vriendenkring 1869: place texts. Zonnehofje expropriation from 1901: place text.
- About 60,000 deported from Amsterdam; Hunger Winter stripping: Anne Frank House. Great Synagogue galleries: place text (Jewish Museum). Tip Top: place text.
- Weesperstraat four-lane road and demolitions: place text. Valkenburgerstraat IJ-tunnel road, Joden Houttuinen, Goslerhuisje, studio houses 1958: place texts.
- Huis De Pinto bought 1968, council vote of 5 January 1972: place text (audited). Metro riots 1975: kept general.
- Vlooienburg plaque 2022: place text.
- Left out: "three ghettos recognised in German compensation law" (single source, Ons Amsterdam); exact numbers of houses demolished after the war (no authoritative figure read); "1943 old quarter fenced off" phrasing kept to "sealed off and emptied in raids" (signs "Judenviertel" are well documented, but I did not read a primary institutional source for fencing).

## 06 Diamonds and the ANDB

- 1906: nearly 30 per cent of working Jewish men and almost 10 per cent of women in diamonds; Cape time 1870 to 1873; home workshops, horses, steam after 1850; ANDB 1894 after a strike, founders including Polak and Van Zutphen; minimum wage, insurance, paid holiday, library, courses; move to Belgium from about 1910; standstill late 1920s; 1935 5,000 of 9,000 left, more than half out of work; ANDB dissolved 1941; Reichskommissariat supervision; about 500 finishers and 300 entrepreneurs, more than 1,200 people with families; September 1943 Westerbork/Vught; 1944 Bergen-Belsen; nearly all men died, some women survived: JM 749.
- Eight-hour day 1911: IISG and DBNL chronology.
- 1748 guild petition: W. "Voor Oranje" gang: W.
- Boas 1870/1879, 357 mills, wages not rent, Hartog Boas TB 1894, stop in 1930s, Gassan: place text. Asscher 1907, Cullinan 1908, Abraham Asscher survived Bergen-Belsen: place texts. Diamond Exchange 1910-11, 1950 memorial stone: place text. Burcht 1899-1900, Roland Holst murals, Henri Polaklaan 1945: place text.
- Left out: "eight-hour day first in Europe/world" (claim found only in popular sources); "oldest trade union building in the Netherlands" (not verified); July 1942 start of exemptions and the January 1943 cut of the lists (not in the source read); date of Polak's arrest (sources differ); earnings of top-grade workers.

- Aby Jetomim home 1817 and 1930, Drukkers murdered 4 June 1943: place text. Megadlé building from 1865, March 1943, three carers, Sobibor: place text (monument text). Girls' orphanage 1861 to 1943: place text. Nieuwe Herengracht 33 from 1794, deportation February 1943: place text. Fernandes Nunes (d. 1788), six rooms, nursery school, January 1943: place text.
- Nursery school 1887, 600 children: place text. Central kitchen 1917 with kosher kitchen: place text. Witte Olifant 5 cents, Jewish school no. 2: place text. Joods Lyceum, Kweekschool: place texts.
- Hospitals 1804, Nieuwe Keizersgracht (110 beds), Portuguese hospital 1834 and 1916: Vanderhoek 2018 (PMC5916234) and place texts. Nurses' home 1914, metaheirhuis 1927: place text. CIZ 1916, cleared August 1943, open until 1978: place text.

## 08 Synagogues of Amsterdam

- Houtgracht until 1675, Esnoga 1675 and seats, candles and sand: W and JCK. Ashkenazi four synagogues with dates, last service 28 September 1943, galleries torn out, museum 1932 in the Waag and 1987 on the square: W and place text.
- Uilenburgersjoel 1724/1766, until 1943, city 1954, Beit Ha'Chidush since 1997: place text.
- Rapenburgersjoel from 1799, Ets Haim 1883: W and place text. Nieuwe Kerkstraat 10-14 (Elte, 28 October 1913, 192/26): place text. Russensjoel 1889: place text. Blasiussjoel society 1890, Eastern European rite after the war: place text. Hidden synagogue Nieuwe Keizersgracht 33: place text.
- Gerard Dou (Rood 1891-92, Tesjoengas Israel, diamond shul): place text and RCE. Commelinstraat (Baars 1921-22): place text. Synagoge Oost (Baars, September 1928, 45 degrees, demolished 1962, glass to Israel): place text.
- Obrechtsjoel (Elte, 18 May 1928, Elte murdered in Theresienstadt 1944, restored 1997, Schuster chief rabbi 1955 to 1973, largest Ashkenazi synagogue in use): place text. Lekstraat (Elzas, 30 November 1937, furniture store from 1943, Verzetsmuseum 1985 to 1999, restored 2006, small shul in daily use): place text.
- Liberal: 1932, Cinétol from 1937, De Lairessestraat 1957, Soetendorp, Graafschapstraat 1966, SeARCH 2010, 800 seats: place texts.
- Weesp synagogue 1840, garage, synagogue again 1997: place text.
- Left out: opening hours and visiting days (change too often); which smaller buildings still hold services today beyond those named in the place texts; claim that the Esnoga was "the largest synagogue in the world" (softened to "among the largest").
