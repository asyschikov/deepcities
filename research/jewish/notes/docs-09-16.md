## 09 Persecution, 1940 to 1943, and the Jewish Council (map: true)

Sources per claim
- 75,000 Jews in Amsterdam in 1940, more than 79,000 in 1941, more than 10,000 refugees, nearly 10,000 homes emptied in 1942, labour camps in October 1942: USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia, "Amsterdam".
- 140,552 registered in 1941. The May 1941 dot map shows one dot per 10 people, about 80,000 of 140,000 in Amsterdam. Yellow star from 3 May 1942: Anne Frank House timelines.
- The Jewish Council was founded on 13 February 1941 with about twenty men; 8,000 staff by April 1943; the Lages quote: NIOD/EHRI archival description of the Joodsche Raad.
- 17,500 Council staff exempted, plus about 15,000 Sperre: JCK.
- Municipal role (population register, GVB, social services): Kemperman / UvA report (21 May 2025) and NIOD (2025), as reported by NOS.
- Places: place texts (Nieuwe Keizersgracht 58, Zentralstelle, Lippmann Rosenthal, Joods Lyceum, Kindermonument, Vondelpark mirror, Muiderpoort, Schaduwen).

Left out
- "Every Jew aged six and over" for the star: not confirmed in the source read.
- Vastenhout's specific verdict on the Council: softened to a general statement of the debate.
- The reasons people did not go into hiding (a list): too interpretive without a single authoritative source.

## 10 February 1941 (map: false)

Sources per claim
- Fights, WA, Koot (11 February), Judenviertel signs, Böhmcker's order for a Jewish Council: NIOD/EHRI history of the Council; Anne Frank House timeline.
- Koco raid (19 February), Cahn executed 3 March 1941, Kohn died in Auschwitz: place text (Koco) and Joods Monument.
- Raid of 22 and 23 February: 427 men (Nationaal Comité 4 en 5 mei, Dokwerker record); 8 released at Schoorl, about 390 deported to Buchenwald and Mauthausen, some killed at Hartheim, 2 survivors (Joods Monument; Anne Frank House).
- Noordermarkt meeting of 24 February with about 250 people, the CPN pamphlet, the strike on 25 and 26 February and its spread, Christiansen, 9 dead, 18 executed: 4en5mei (Dokwerker record), place texts (Noorderkerk, Van Nimwegen, De Antifascist).
- Monument and commemoration: place text (Dokwerker), 4en5mei. The procession of people laying flowers is the standard form of the annual commemoration.

Uncertainty
- 427 (4en5mei) against "about 425" elsewhere; about 390 sent on from Schoorl. The text gives the 4en5mei figure and "about 390".

Left out
- "Cahn was the first person executed in the Netherlands": not confirmed.
- The "heavy fine" imposed on Amsterdam and other towns: not confirmed in sources read.

Photos: "Tegen de muur van het Tip-Top Theater in de Jodenbreestraat te Amsterdam.jpg" (PD); "Pamflet Februaristaking 1941.jpg" (PD); "Februaristaking Raamplein Amsterdam 25 februari 1941.jpg" (PD).

Correction made while checking: the Monument Joods Verzet stands on the Amstel at the corner of the Zwanenburgwal, not on the Jonas Daniël Meijerplein (place text).

- Süskind died on a death march in February 1945: Joods Monument.

Uncertainty

Left out

- The crèche "for working mothers": not confirmed.
- The exact wording of the 1982 plaque: paraphrased only.

## 13 Return and the postwar years (map: false)

Sources per claim
- Survivor figures (more than 5,200 deported survivors; about 1,150 Auschwitz, 19 Sobibor; about 16,000 in hiding; about 10,000 in mixed marriages; about 36,000 in all): NIOD, "Jewish survivors (in numbers)", after De Jong.
- 4,000 to 6,000 surviving children: Verhey; USHMM ("Hidden children: quest for family").
- OPK founded August 1945 with a non-Jewish majority, Jewish opposition: NIOD archival record; USHMM; Verhey.
- Centraal Station plaque (2019, Zwarts, "extremely cold", the occupier no excuse): place text.
- Lotty Veffer (26 August 1945, strangers in her house, horse blanket, bench plate 2017): place text.
- Hondius on antisemitism and impatience after the return: Hondius, *Terugkeer*.
- Ground rent fines: found in 2013 by a student (not named in the text, as she is a private person); NIOD inquiry; repayment from 2014: Binnenlands Bestuur; DutchNews (May 2014).
- Monument van Joodse Erkentelijkheid (23 February 1950, "with pride, but also with shame", moves of 1968, 2020 and 2021): place text.
- Schouwburg memorial 1962: JCK. Presser's *Ondergang* 1965: standard.
- Gerard Dou Synagogue first service 19 May 1945; Joodsche Invalide building handed to the city; Liberal congregation under Soetendorp from 1954, synagogue 1966; Le-Ezrath Ha-Jeled and the home of 16 November 1965; Kriterion opened 6 November 1945 in the former clubhouse of a Jewish workers' association: place texts.
- GVB and city fund (March 2024, €100,000 for the CJO): NOS, 2514671.
- Halsema's apology on 24 April 2025 and the €25 million fund, after the NIOD study: NOS (11 April 2025); press reports of 24 April 2025.

Left out
- The Jewish Council of Honour (1947) and the dropped criminal case against Asscher and Cohen: belongs to doc 09's subject and needs more careful sourcing than read here.
- The late-1990s settlements with banks, insurers, the stock exchange and the state: true in outline but not read in a source this round; cut to "restitution dragged on for decades".
- "Some survivors left for Israel or America": no figure read; cut.
- That *Ondergang* "found a very large readership": cut as unsourced.
- The amount refunded for ground rent (about €820,000 to €870,000, plus €10 million for collective purposes): sources differ; left out.

## 16 Stolpersteine (map: true)

Sources per claim
- Demnig, 1993; stone of 10 by 10 cm with a brass plate; last freely chosen home; "not gravestones": Stichting Stolpersteine Amsterdam, "Wat zijn Stolpersteine".
- More than 100,000 in over 1,200 places in Europe; more than 16,000 in over 230 Dutch municipalities: same foundation.
- Amsterdam: "more than 4,500" on one page and "almost 5,000" in the FAQ, about 50 new a month. The text gives both as the foundation's figures.
- €150 per stone; relatives' priority (80 and over); refusal if relatives object; general permission from the city; letter to residents about two weeks ahead; city paving workers lay them: FAQ.
- Waiting list about 1,700 at the end of 2024; stop on requests lifted 8 September 2026; wait now one to three years: foundation news and FAQ.
- Stiftung Spuren, six workshops, one in Amsterdam at the Goethe-Institut on the Herengracht making stones for the Benelux: foundation.
- stolpersteine.app by Stichting 18 September, search by name, street and place, updated daily; "not found means not requested"; info@stolpersteine-amsterdam.com: foundation, "Waar liggen Stolpersteine".
- Polishing with a Flitz-type cleaner (Flintoline, Sidol), no acid: foundation, "Poetsen".
- Stolperschwelle 30 cm to 1 m, up to five lines: foundation.
- Thresholds of January 2025 at Artis, Victorieplein and Beethovenstraat; the GVB invoices; the fund: place texts; NOS (March 2024).
- Places: place texts (Nieuwe Prinsengracht 18, 2 April 2017, twelve stones; Brilleslijper house, six stones, none for the sisters; Rosenbaum stones at J.D. Meijerplein 13 in the Dokwerker place text; Turfkruier 28 January 2026 with three great-grandchildren; Pollack family at Beethovenstraat 55; Weesp stones; Leendert Pop; Olympic Stadium gymnasts 2010).
- Munich and Charlotte Knobloch: The Irish Times; other press. Phrased as "for years kept them off public ground", as the present state was not confirmed.

Left out
- The 2,500th Amsterdam stone (said to be for Klara Borstel-Engelsman): not confirmed.
- "The first stones in Amsterdam" for the 2010 gymnasts: only the year is confirmed; the text says "among the earliest".
- Stones for gay and lesbian victims in Amsterdam (2021): not confirmed in detail; cut.
- That the foundation is run entirely by volunteers, the size of the monthly intake of requests, that inscriptions are read aloud, that stones taken up for street works are stored and relaid, that Amsterdam is "one of the cities with the most stones": not confirmed; cut.
- Names of the people polishing stones, or living relatives at layings: not named.
