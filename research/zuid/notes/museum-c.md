## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Museumplein 19, the client's name.** The national register spells him "W.F. van Heulekom";
  Amsterdam op de kaart has Willem Frederik van Heukelom (1858–1937), with dates and career. Used
  Van Heukelom. The consulate is stated as current on the strength of Amsterdam op de kaart (page
  last changed November 2026); no newer source was found on a move.
- **Gabriël Metsustraat 2-6, build year.** The slice has no year; nl.wikipedia says 1898 (the year
  the plot was leased); Amsterdam op de kaart dates the building 1901 and the drawings 1900. Used 1901.
- **Christian HBS (222138), year.** The city register says the complex was "realised in 1910"; the
  school was founded in 1910 but the building came into use in September 1912 (De Standaard, via
  Amsterdam op de kaart). Used 1912. The glass artist is "N. Schrik" and "N. Strik" in one and the
  same register text; left out.
- **Hillehuis.** Titles avoid "first": sources say it "is regarded as" De Klerk's first independent
  work and that it "announces" the Amsterdam School, and the body says just that. nl.wikipedia calls
  it a block for workers, which does not fit the neighbourhood or the other sources; left out.
  Wikipedia says no. 30 is outside the listing, while the register record 1111 reads "28-30"; all
  six numbers are kept. Listed year 1974 is from nl.wikipedia (the slice has none).
- **Honthorststraat 2A-16, number of houses.** City register: eleven houses; Amsterdam op de kaart:
  nine (four of them split into two flats). The body says "the row".
- **Lucky Luijk.** Wikipedia has Hitma in no. 3 as an office from 1926; the national register dates
  the conversion to 1939. Left out. Riot date 11 October 1982 from Wikipedia. The current tenure of
  the four flats (Ymere, partly free sector) is left out as too detailed and changeable.
- **Villa block.** Honthorststraat 5–7: slice says 1901/1902, the register 1902, Amsterdam op de
  kaart 1904 (sale adverts from March 1904). Written as 1902–1904.
- **Current use not confirmed** (web search was throttled): Gabriël Metsustraat 8 (Nieuwe
  Huishoudschool) and Museumplein 4 (Villa Troostwijk) have `check: Facts` with a note. For
  Gabriël Metsustraat 7 nl.wikipedia (April 2020) names a dance academy annexe; not repeated, the
  body only says it is not open. For Museumplein 5–7 the register only says the ANWB has left.
- **Berlage school.** The Concertgebouworkest gives Gabriël Metsustraat 16 as its office address
  (contact page, November 2026). Whether the house has public concerts or open days was not
  confirmed, so the body says it is a place of work.
- **Fire station Dirk.** "Oldest fire station in the country still in use" is a literal claim of
  Amsterdam op de kaart (page changed August 2024); Dirk is on the brigade's current station list.
  Commons has a 2022 photo of a temporary building for brigade Dirk, so there was a renovation
  around then. Listed year unknown (early listing), field left out, as for Museumplein 4.
- **Private people not named:** the accountant who let Etty Hillesum her room, the shoe retailer of
  Honthorststraat 36, the woman dentist of Jan Luijkenstraat 31, the first residents of the
  Museumplein villas and of Van Miereveldstraat 5 (a coffee merchant who died in Bergen-Belsen in 1944).
- **Mixed listings.** Three ensembles combine national and municipal monuments and are marked
  "National + municipal monument"; `listed` gives the year of the pinned building (1996 or 2012).
- **Jan Luijkenstraat 16.** Its hook is indoors (a dining room built around a copy of Asselijn's
  Threatened Swan) and cannot be seen, so it went into the ensemble without that story.
