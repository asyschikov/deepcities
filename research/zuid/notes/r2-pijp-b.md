## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **213, what was printed here.** The scout wrote that the Deutsche Zeitung in den Niederlanden was printed "here". The sources say Holdert's firm printed it, not that it came off the press in the Van Ostadestraat. The text says "his firm printed".
- **213, the theatre.** The Ostadetheater (later Ostade A'dam) no longer appears among the users on ostade233.nl, and its old domain forwards to CC Amstel. The text only says it has moved out. The council "vote for demolition" is the district council's investment decision "De Nieuwe Fabriek" of 25 September 2013.
- **213, current status.** Evidence that it is in use: the site's list of users (undated), the OpenStreetMap entry for Smerig. No dated 2025 or 2026 news item found.
- **214, the renumbering date.** nl.wikipedia says the old numbers 104 to 132 were renumbered 362 to 468 when the name came back in May 1945. But the same encyclopedia's notes on De Avonden, set at the end of 1946, still use the old numbers (115 to 118) for the neighbours. The text gives the renaming dates and the renumbering without tying the renumbering to May 1945.
- **214, the signs.** No dated evidence after 2015 for the "103" plate and after 2006 for the "66 Van Egters" sign (the 2006 Commons panorama shows a small plate by the door, not legible). Both are flagged for a site check. The cigar shop's name ('t Hoekje) is a guess by the 2015 author from a postcard and is left out.
- **215, still there.** Latest dated photos are of April 2019 (Commons); the nl.wikipedia article was updated in late 2025 for the new mural at number 35 and still describes the wall in the present tense. Not flagged. The wording of the first text is given on Wikipedia as "Wir hebben es Gümüst"; the text only says it punned on the name.
- **215, Jacob Olie.** nl.wikipedia calls him the best-known pupil of the 1888 school, which cannot be right (born 1834); the Commons photos show his class there, so he taught. Left out.
- **218, closure.** Het Parool of 6 September 2025 is behind a paywall; only the headline ("zal nooit meer een tafel het pand verlaten") and one caption were read. nl.wikipedia gives the end as 2025. What happens to the building is not known. The scout's "Night Watch" by Kaagman could not be confirmed; the 2016 photos show Delft-blue patterns and a billiard player, which is what the text says.
- **218, names and dates.** De Pijp Krant writes Isaac Salomons, nl.wikipedia Izak Barend Salomon (followed). Founding: 1898 by the firm, first newspaper mention 1902, a 25-year jubilee in 1929. De Pijp Krant says the factory was expropriated; nl.wikipedia only that it was renamed J. Slot in 1941. The text gives the renaming.
- **220, the date.** The court of appeal's ruling and the nl.wikipedia article give 6 October 2003; the Gerard Douplein article and De Pijp Krant give 5 October. The ruling is followed. The scout called her homeless; NRC (20 October 2003) reports she had a home again by then, so the word is not used. The silent march: "a thousand" on Wikipedia, "a few hundred" walking plus a few hundred waiting in NRC; the text says hundreds. The suspects are not named.
- **220, the pillars.** Duijn's pillars are dated 1993 (OpenStreetMap, a Commons caption) and 1996 (article text); the text says "from the 1990s".
- **221, the clubhouse.** De Pijp Krant of September 2025 says the clubhouse was burned in the Hunger Winter and rebuilt in the old style; the same paper in 2017 and nl.wikipedia treat numbers 45 and 47 as the 1920 buildings. The text follows the 2025 article and the place is flagged check: Facts. The scout's shelter barrack on the square in the war was not found in any source read and is left out. Whether the bandstand rim is still the sandpit today is not confirmed, so it is told as history only.
- **253, sizes and dates.** The plot is 4,000 m² in De Pijp Krant and 40,000 m² on nl.wikipedia (impossible for this block). The fire is "the night of 2 February" in De Pijp Krant; the Anefo photos and nl.wikipedia date it 1 February 1977, alarm at 6.24. The text follows the latter.
- **253, access.** No source says how a visitor gets in. The association's address is Frans Halsstraat 16K, the same number as the surviving school gate (14–16) in the Frans Halsstraat place, which suggests the gate is the way in; not stated in the text. Participation in the open garden day is confirmed for 2016 only.

## Leads

- **Poggenbeekstraat, two crowned W's** (item 217): painted for Queen Wilhelmina right after the liberation by a house painter with paint left over, restored about 2008 with money from housing association De Key, "on a long front". Source: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/improviseren-en-mensen-op-sleeptouw-nemen-2/ (June 2020). Needs someone to walk the street; if found, a Look for line in "De Dageraad" or its own Okay place.
- **Jan Lievensstraat 52**: birthplace of the artist Armando (1929), third floor. Same article as the "103" plate (De Pijp Krant, June 2015). No plaque known.
- **Schoenmakerij Cowboy**, corner Jozef Israëlskade and Diamantstraat: the cobbler in De Avonden from whom the mother borrows coal; photographed in 1976 (Stadsarchief beeldbank). Gone as a shop; a line for the Jozef Israëlskade place if a trace turns up.
- **Splitskast by Marnette** on the Quellijnstraat side of Frans Halsstraat 35 (Amsterdam School cable box, next to the 2025 Schwartze mural): https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muurschildering_Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Schwartze
- **Sint Barbara cemetery**: the communal grave kept by the drugs chaplaincy, where Anja Joos lies (outside Zuid).
- **Het Hol, Bluf!**: the editorial room in De Fabriek; Bluf! is the paper that published a secret service report in 1987. Not checked beyond the building's own history page.
- **Café Hermes**, Ceintuurbaan 55, and the **Gerard Revebrug**: see the scout's notes; nothing added.

## Route ideas

- **Oude Pijp, north-west corner** (about 1 km): Frans Halstuinen and Stadhouderskade 55, the Frans Halsstraat (4711 lettering, school gate, Schwartze mural, the wall at 64), then east along the Gerard Doustraat to the Anja Joos stone and on to the round-1 places (De Punt, the synagogue, the pawn bank).
- **De Avonden walk**: Jozef Israëlskade 415, the "103" plate, the Gerard Revebrug, and back through Van Epen's Diamantbuurt past De Fietserij and Heineken's cottages.
- **Fought for and kept**: De Fabriek, the Oranjekerk, the Henrick de Keijserplein playground and the Coöperatiehof: four places within 600 metres that exist because residents organised.
- The Billardfabriek sits one block from "The clock of Hemony and its neighbours" and the Amstel bathhouse (round 1): a short Stadhouderskade and Hemonylaan loop.
