## Belongs to an existing place

None of the records belongs to the Vondelpark place.

**Hillehuis (3738, 3739: Nicolaas Maesstraat 32 and 34), needs a decision.** These two are the
Nicolaas Maesstraat wing of Michel de Klerk's Hillehuis (1911–1912). The other six records of the
same building (1108 to 1113, Gabriël Metsustraat 22–34) are in slice museum-c. I drafted nothing, to
avoid two places for one building: the museum-c place should take both numbers in `monument_number`.
Worth adding there:

- The block has three parts: a wing on the Gabriël Metsustraat, a front on the Johannes
  Vermeerplein and this wing on the Nicolaas Maesstraat, tied together by one continuous balcony,
  the ornament and the roof (nl.wikipedia, Hillehuis).
- The national register text for both records is one line: built in 1911 by De Klerk "in a manner
  whose angular and spiky detailing announces the beginning of the Amsterdam School".
- Builder-developer Klaas Hille took the ground in leasehold; number 30 is left out of the listing
  (nl.wikipedia).
- Photos: `File:Hillehuis Nicolaas Maesstraat 32-34.JPG`, `File:Nicolaes Maesstraat 34.jpg` (both
  checked on Commons, category Hillehuis).

If museum-c did not draft the Hillehuis, these two records need a place of their own.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Slice years and names I corrected from the register text.** Jacob Obrechtstraat 2: 1899–1900
  (slice 1910). Paulus Potterstraat 8: architect L. Beirer, signed in stone on the front (slice: Van
  Gendt). Paulus Potterstraat 40-42: 1901–1902 (slice 1910; H.H. Baanders died in 1906). Paulus
  Potterstraat "2-8" is number 2 alone. Staal's block 1922–1924 (slice 1924). Huize Lydia 1924–1927.
  Vossiusstraat 56-74: 1883–1885. Heinzestraat 13: 1925–1926 (slice 1921). Concertgebouwplein 20:
  E. Verschuyl (register: long credited to J. London, corrected in 2012; Amsterdam op de kaart still
  says London). Concertgebouwplein 3–13: the slice gives H.C. Janus jr. as architect; Amsterdam op de
  kaart says he was the developer and his brother J.B. Janus the architect. I followed the latter.
- **222016 substation**: the city record has no description, and its client field (Christelijke
  School Vereeniging Aeneas Mackay) is plainly a data error. Everything in the draft comes from
  Amsterdam op de kaart (building files and newspapers). Present use not confirmed: `check: Facts`.
- **222159 Hondecoeterstraat 4-8**: no register text. Wikipedia (Hondecoeterstraat) says Van der Mey,
  about 1920, "with buildings in the yard"; the register point lies beside the studio houses, so I
  treated it as part of Van der Mey's block. I describe nothing of its front beyond that.
- **Studio houses**: whether the tennis court and the studio row can be seen from the gate is
  unknown (`check: Facts`). The Commons photo shows the court with the studios behind, so someone
  got in.
- **Obrechtkerk dates**: Wikipedia says built 1908–1911, Amsterdam op de kaart 1910–1911; a
  newspaper page on Commons dates the first stone to July 1909. I wrote first stone 1909,
  consecrated 1911. **Clock**: Wikipedia says the IX and XI on the tower clock are swapped. A 2002
  photo confirms a blue six-sided dial on the left tower but is too coarse to read the numerals, so
  the claim is only in `check_note`. **Mengelberg**: the register says six apse windows were given
  by "Willem Mengelberg" in 1922; I identify him as the conductor (the designer Otto Mengelberg was
  his relative), which the register does not spell out.
- **Girls' lyceum**: end of the school is 1968 in Amsterdam op de kaart and "early 1970s" in the
  register; I wrote "around 1970". Current user (Joke Smit, ROC van Amsterdam) from OpenStreetMap; the
  ROC page could not be fetched (certificate error). The Euwe and Margot Frank facts rest on
  Amsterdam op de kaart (page updated November 2026). "First municipal lyceum for girls" is the
  register's wording.
- **De Roos**: the register dates the house to 1880, Amsterdam op de kaart to 1873 (a drawing in the
  city archive); I wrote "1870s". The register says "around 1987" for the sale to Kahn and Berg, a
  typo for 1897. nl.wikipedia (Sally Berg) says Jacot did the 1897 extension and that the Kahns had
  the Van Eeghenlaan end; the register and Amsterdam op de kaart say Van Arkel in 1897, Jacot in
  1912, Berg at the Van Eeghenlaan end. I followed the register. The glass conservatory was boxed in
  in 2012; state after the 2024–2026 refit unknown (`check: Facts`). Opening hours left out: the
  site gives none without scripts.
- **Institute for the Blind**: the register says converted to apartments "around 1997"; Amsterdam op
  de kaart says the Paedologisch Instituut stayed until 2006. I wrote "for decades" and "since
  converted" without a year.
- **Vossiusstraat 21-32**: architect unsettled (register: Schermerhaven or Schermhaven; Amsterdam op
  de kaart: Joh. Draijer Hzn). Both named in the text, architect attribute left to Gosschalk for
  2–15 only.
- **Paulus Potterstraat 6**: initials of Vita Israël differ (register J.E., Amsterdam op de kaart
  I.E.); I left them out. **Number 8**: Amsterdam op de kaart still has the Diamond Museum there;
  the museum's site refuses requests. The Miniatuur Museum Amsterdam gives Paulus Potterstraat 8 as
  its address (site read November 2026) and a Commons photo of March 2026 shows it there, so I wrote
  that it has replaced the diamond museum. The year of the change is not known to me.
- **Coster's move**: bought number 2 in 1966 (Amsterdam op de kaart), moved in 1970 (Wikipedia). Both
  in the text.
- **Huize Lydia in the war**: nl.wikipedia says 120 SD men took their daily meals here. No source
  given; left out.
- **Synagogue**: listed 1996 in the slice, 1995 on Wikipedia; I kept 1996. "Largest Ashkenazi
  synagogue still in use in Amsterdam" and "most Jewish weddings" are Wikipedia's wording.
- **Rijkspostspaarbank**: `listed: 1983` comes from Wikipedia (28 September 1983); the slice has no
  year. The bank left in 1977 (merger) or 1978 (Wikipedia, elsewhere in the same article).
- **Number of places**: 18 for 38 records is more than one per three. The slice is heavy on public
  buildings (synagogue, church, school, bank, fire station, home, institute, substation), which the
  brief allows for.

## Leads

- **Vossiusstraat 16** (not listed): squatted in November 2022 in protest against its owner, Yandex
  founder Arkady Volozh, then on the EU sanctions list; squatters left in 2024, offered for sale in
  2025 split into three homes. Source: https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Vossiusstraat/16-18
  (cites Het Parool 16-11-2022 and 25-3-2024). Sits between my two Vossiusstraat rows.
- **Frankey street art, Vossiusstraat 8**: Commons has two photos of December 2019
  (`File:Vossiusstraat 8, Street Art Frankey (1).jpg`). Still there?
- **Miniatuur Museum Amsterdam**, Paulus Potterstraat 8 (in the listed villa 505735): 750 years of
  Amsterdam in scale models, open daily. https://miniatuurmuseumamsterdam.nl/ . Candidate for odd
  museums, or too commercial.
- **Dinosaur canvases in the girls' lyceum**, Reijnier Vinkeleskade 62: seven canvases by Maria
  Hubrecht (with Tine Lepoutre) of the geological eras, an early municipal art commission, in the
  hall of a working school. Behind the gate: is there an open day? Also there: a frieze of horses
  and people by Samuel Jessurun de Mesquita (1930) painted over in the old staff room, and a tile
  picture by Gerard Westerman (1936) on the outside (nl.wikipedia, Reijnier Vinkeleskade).
- **Bernard Haitink's birthplace**, Reijnier Vinkeleskade 76 (born 4 March 1929). nl.wikipedia,
  Reijnier Vinkeleskade. Plaque?
- **Zuidermarkt**: the Saturday market on the unnamed square at Jacob Obrechtstraat / Johannes
  Verhulststraat, informally "Zuidermarktplein".
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Jacob_Obrechtstraat/42-46
- **Hondecoeterstraat**: a cable cabinet of a type used until 1913 (one in the Langestraat was
  listed in 2013), a small mural by Ivar Vičs, and a large relief at number 12. nl.wikipedia,
  Hondecoeterstraat.
- **Tile panels inside the Rijkspostspaarbank** (bees, spiders in webs, arms of the provinces): how
  far a non-guest gets in the hotel is worth checking on site.
- **Jacob Olie's 1892 photos of the fire station** with the steam engine "Triton", taken over by
  Amsterdam on 1 May 1896 (Commons, `File:Van Baerlestraat 33-37 Jacob Olie (max res).jpg`).
- **Het Nieuwe Huis**, Roelof Hartplein (other slice): the companion of Huize Lydia, for single women
  and men; the two should cross-refer.
- **Berg-Stichting, Laren** (outside the map): the children's home founded by Sally Berg; relevant
  only as background to De Roos.
- **CIZ, Jacob Obrechtstraat 92**: the former Jewish hospital by Harry Elte with an auxiliary
  synagogue (Stadsarchief photo on Commons). Probably another slice; pairs with the Obrechtsjoel.

## Route ideas

- **Jacob Obrechtplein loop (20 minutes)**: Obrechtsjoel, the substation opposite, Kramer's houses
  and the caretaker's house in the Heinzestraat, round to the girls' lyceum on the canal, then east
  along the Bartholomeus Ruloffsstraat to Staal's block and Huize Lydia on the Roelof Hartplein.
  Six places within 400 metres, all Amsterdam School of 1916–1928, from playful to strict.
- **The lost border of Nieuwer-Amstel**: fire station (Van Baerlestraat 35–37), the bend of the
  Alexander Boersstraat, De Roos (built in Nieuwer-Amstel) and on into the Willemspark of slice
  museum-a.
- **Samenwerking walk**: the flower-tile houses (Moreelsestraat, then Nicolaas Maesstraat 125 and
  Jacob Obrechtstraat 76–90) to Staal's block, with the Harmoniehof (other slice) as the finish.
- **Park edge**: Vossiusstraat palace row, Institute for the Blind, De Roos for tea, the tile villa
  at Jacob Obrechtstraat 2, and into the Vondelpark for the Blauwe Theehuis.
