## Belongs to an existing place

All eight to the existing place **Vondelpark** (complex 504733). One fact each worth adding there:

- **504839 Vondelpark (park layout)**: the narrow entrance strip from the Stadhouderskade is nicknamed
  *de slurf* (the trunk); the 1877 extension was laid out like a country estate, with a cow meadow and
  a sheep meadow, and its winding pond in the south-west corner runs on into the Willemspark.
- **504766 Gate, Stadhouderskade**: designed in 1883 by the German architect Alexander Linnemann for
  the International Colonial Exhibition; the Amsterdam city maiden between the carriage gates is since
  2010 a copy of Friedrich Schierholz's original; the gilded lettering reads "VONDEL PARK", with a
  space too many (nl.wikipedia).
- **504736 Vondelpark 1 (park keeper's house)**: of about 1867, probably by J.D. Zocher; one of only
  eight buildings with an address in the park; plastered, with saw-tooth boarding under the eaves.
- **504742 Vondel monument**: unveiled 18 October 1867; Louis Royer designed the seated Vondel, Pierre
  Cuypers the pedestal, and the four angels on the corners stand for tragedy, satire, didactic verse
  and sacred poetry. The back reads SCRIPSIT AETERNITATI (he wrote for eternity).
- **504754 Bandstand**: cast iron, ten-sided, of 1873 by the Zochers, cast by L.J. Enthoven in The
  Hague, on its own round island; the iron footbridge to it replaced a wooden one in the early 1900s.
- **504808 Bridge 450**: steep cast-iron footbridge of 1889 by C.B. Posthumus Meyjes sr., made by
  G.J. Wispelwey & Co. in Zwolle (maker's plate on the bridge); long called the Iepenbrug, since 2017
  officially the Pater van Kilsdonkbrug. It is the bridge on the way to the Blauwe Theehuis.
- **504790 Gate, Van Eeghenstraat**: four passages, the outer two between carved hardstone posts.
  See Leads for the memorial hung in front of it.
- **504772 Five gates** (Koninginneweg, P.C. Hooftstraat, Roemer Visscherstraat, Vondelstraat twice):
  iron posts with lion heads and acanthus; on the P.C. Hooftstraat gate one post has lost its lower
  ornament.

The Blauwe Theehuis (504760) has its own place, as instructed.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Slice years are often off; I used the register text.** Loma is 1913–1914 (slice: 1922; the extra
  floor is 1924). Zonnehoek 1915–1916. Cornelis Schuytstraat 65: 1918–1919
  (slice: 1925). Van Eeghenstraat 76-78: 1899–1900 (slice: 1905). 80-82: 1899–1900 (slice: 1890 and
  1900). 92: 1901–1902 (slice: 1910). Blauwe Theehuis: register 1936, Wikipedia 1936–1937,
  Amsterdam op de kaart 1937; I wrote 1936–1937.
- **504725 "Cornelis Schuytstraat 74, architect J. Gratama, 1921"**: the record mixes two things. The
  monument is the boathouse and garden pavilion of the complex Cornelis Schuytstraat 72 / J.J.
  Viottastraat 31 (complex 504701, Warners). Gratama and 1921 belong to the unlisted villa at no. 74,
  in whose present garden the boathouse stands. I filed it with the Warners villas.
- **Garage fountain (504717)**: the fish or dragon spout is on the east wall according to the 1996
  register text. I could not confirm it is still there or visible from the pavement: `check: Still
  there?`. The Commons photo only shows the garage doors from the driveway.
- **Boathouse viewpoint**: I say "from the bridge at the end of the Cornelis Schuytstraat, looking
  west". That follows from the register (west end of the garden of no. 74) and the Commons photo
  (taken from the water side with the bridge behind). Worth a look on site.
- **Christie's dates**: nl.wikipedia (two articles) and PropertyNL say Christie's came in 1973. The
  register (office conversion 1974, Christie's 1981), Amsterdam op de kaart (empty until 1981, Parool
  15-2-1980) and the Commons category say 1981. I used 1981. End: sold January 2018 (PropertyNL);
  Amsterdam op de kaart says Christie's left in 2019. I wrote "until 2018".
- **Current use of Cornelis Schuytstraat 57**: "a gym" rests on Amsterdam op de kaart only (photos
  November 2020). I could not find the operator. Worth confirming.
- **Huize Loma, client**: register says the manufacturer C.W.D. König paid for it; Amsterdam op de
  kaart says the Nationale Bouw-Maatschappij was the client and lists a C.D.W. König (starch factory
  director) as a first resident. I left the client out. Lift: register says added in 1935, Amsterdam
  op de kaart lists it among the original comforts; left out. Extra floor: register 1924, Amsterdam
  op de kaart 1923; I used 1924.
- **"First block of flats in the Netherlands"**: the register says earliest in Amsterdam and calls
  Warners the Dutch pioneer; nl.wikipedia says "regarded by some as the first in the Netherlands". The
  body says exactly that much; the title and summary say "first Dutch flats" and "the country's
  first". Tone down if that is too strong.
- **Willemsparkweg 29-35, the eight heads**: the register says heads "in the style of about 1600".
  Amsterdam op de kaart, citing Ons Amsterdam February 2008, says they portray Surinamese people,
  "presumably" from the 28 shown at the 1883 exhibition. I could not read the Ons Amsterdam article
  itself (old URL dead, not in the Wayback Machine). The body attributes the reading to the magazine
  and I set `check: Facts`. Commons photos of 2021 show the heads are there. This is the hook of the
  place, so the article is worth pulling from a paper copy.
- **De Lairessestraat 39**: register says 1918; Amsterdam op de kaart says the row started in 1916
  and the first family moved into 39 in 1917. I wrote that the row started in 1916 and gave
  1916–1920 for the two rows. The first resident (named in Amsterdam op de kaart and by the Grimmon
  archive) is not named, nor is the couple at no. 93 that the register names.
- **Van Eeghenstraat 57**: the national register gives C.A.A. Wübbe as the designer; Amsterdam op de
  kaart says he was a merchant and the first resident, and leaves the designer open. I wrote "built
  for" and left the architect out of the attributes. The same source says a photo suggests an NSB
  office was in the house during the war; too thin, left out. The refugee couple who ran the
  boarding house are on Joods Monument; not named in the text.
- **Van Eeghenstraat 80**: the convicted bank director (1907) is named in Amsterdam op de kaart; I
  left the name out. "Most expensive house" is Quote, December 2016.
- **Van Eeghenstraat 66-68 tiles**: register says Lotus, design Lambertus (Bert) Nienhuis, a pelican in
  the gable; Amsterdam op de kaart says Nienhuis and Albert Smit, marabous among water lilies; the
  Commons captions say De Distel. I wrote Lotus after Nienhuis and "a large bird".
- **Van Eeghenstraat 64, four women's portraits**: on the side wall per the register. Whether they
  can be seen well from the pavement between the villas is not confirmed.
- **Van Eeghenstraat 88 (200949)**: the 2021 listing is for the first-floor interior of no. 88; the
  register text covers 84-88 as a group, which is how I treated it.
- **Berlage and Sanders**: register says Berlage left in 1889, Amsterdam op de kaart 1888. I wrote
  "the end of the 1880s".
- **Klinkhamer**: the register has him become professor in Delft in 1899; nl.wikipedia gives 1905–1924
  for the chair. I only say he left for Delft in 1899 and became a professor there.
- **Willemsparkweg 217**: register 1896, Amsterdam op de kaart 1898. I used 1896.
- **J.J. Viottastraat 36 (222152)**: the slice has no text. It is a municipal monument since 2009;
  the RCE photo series on Commons suggests it was listed for its interior. It gets one sentence in the
  Cornelis Schuytstraat ensemble.
- **Zuiderkwartier pin**: placed on Reijnier Vinkeleskade 82 (PDOK address point), the corner with the
  vultures and monkeys, not on the register point of the complex.
- **Sources I could not use**: web search through curl was throttled after two queries, so current
  status rests on the sites named in each place's `sources`. Wikimedia was rate-limited throughout.

## Leads

- **War memorial "Sobibór-Wat doe jij?"**: hung in April 2021 on the lamp post in front of the
  Vondelpark gate on the Van Eeghenstraat (opposite no. 89-103).
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toegangshek_Vondelpark_(van_Eeghenstraat)
- **"In den bescherfden schuyt" (2012) by Fabrice Hünd**: artwork on the electricity substation at the
  crossing of the Willemsparkweg and the Cornelis Schuytstraat. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willemsparkweg
- **Willem Spark**: the wartime story that officials saved the street name in 1942 by claiming it
  honoured a composer called Willem Spark; a mock lecture on his "100th birthday" was given in the
  hostage camp at Sint-Michielsgestel on 24 June 1943. Could be a line in a Willemsparkweg place.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willemsparkweg
- **Villa Parkwijck (gone), Van Eeghenstraat 90**: nicknamed "De Theepot" or "Het Handvat"; Jacob Olie
  photos on Commons. Gone, so only if a trace remains.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Van_Eeghenstraat_90,_Villa_Parkwijck,_bijgenaamd_%27De_Theepot%27_of_%27Het_Handvat%27_Jacob_Olie_(max_res).jpg
- **Max Möller, violin maker**: at Willemsparkweg 15 from 1925 to 2006. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willemsparkweg
- **Huize Zuidwyck (1919)**, De Lairessestraat 94-98 at the Cornelis Schuytstraat: Warners's third
  block of flats, opposite Zonnehoek. Not in this slice; if it is in another, link it to the Loma
  place. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Anne_Warners
- **Garages in the Banstraat** built by Venemans for residents of De Lairessestraat 33-51 (early
  private garages). https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/De_Lairessestraat/33-51
- **Faun gable stone by Theo Vos (1919)** on Villa Klatte, Cornelis Schuytstraat 65: inside one of my
  monuments, mentioned in the Look for list. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_Vos
- **Erfpacht row over Cornelis Schuytstraat 74 (1921)**: communist councillors objected that the
  ground rent for this villa plot was lower than for workers' housing (De Tribune, 26-3-1921). The
  family who built it were textile traders; the parents and a son were murdered in Sobibor.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Cornelis_Schuytstraat/74
- **The "Jofferrol"**: expensive cars pausing on the speed bump in front of café Joffers in the
  Cornelis Schuytstraat (a television joke that stuck). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_Schuytstraat

## Route ideas

- **Warners in half a mile**: Huize Loma (De Lairessestraat 4-8), along the street to Zonnehoek and
  the first Scheepvaartmuseum at the Cornelis Schuytstraat, down to the La Croix houses and Villa
  Klatte, the Wright villas and boathouse on the J.J. Viottastraat, then back along the Hacquartstraat
  past the vultures and monkeys and the dice houses. About 1.5 km, three Nice places.
- **South edge of the Vondelpark**: from the Blauwe Theehuis out through the Van Eeghenstraat gate,
  east past the villas at 92, 80-82 and 76-78 to the tile villas (62-68) and the wild row at 59-63,
  then one block south to Huis Galesloot on the Willemsparkweg. About 1.2 km.
- **Carved heads and tiles**: Huis Galesloot (eight heads), Van Eeghenstraat 59-63 (heads on
  brackets), tile villas, Klinkhamer's house, the cement house at 217; a looking-up walk along tram 2.
