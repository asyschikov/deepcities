## Conflicts and uncertainties

Corrected against the slice file:

- **222136 Ruysdaelkade 215**: slice says 1912 and client "Christelijke School Vereeniging Aeneas Mackay".
  The register text says a municipal corner school of 1915; Amsterdam op de kaart has the design at 1915
  and dates it 1916. Used 1915–1916, a public school. The same wrong client is on 222134.
- **222134 Smaragdplein 3-5**: slice says 1920; nl.wikipedia has the tender on 20 June 1921. Body says
  "about 1921". Attribution to Lansdorp is the register's ("mogelijk") and Gratama's; given as attributed.
- **527853 Ceintuur Theater**: nl.wikipedia opens it on 2 April 1921 and adds Van Arkel as co-architect;
  the national register says 1921–1922 and Noorlander only; Amsterdam op de kaart dates it 1922. Followed
  the register. Closing: end of 1976 (nl.wikipedia) or 1977 (Amsterdam op de kaart); used 1976. Kitchen
  shop until 2012 (nl.wikipedia) or 2014 (Amsterdam op de kaart); left the year out.
- **527800 Asscher**: nl.wikipedia puts the cleaving of the Cullinan in 1907; Amsterdam op de kaart says
  1908, which is right (10 February 1908). Used 1908. Number of Jewish staff and of survivors differs per
  source (300 or 500; "fifteen returned" in Scout B): no numbers in the text.
- **1508, 37649 De Dageraad**: the register says 1918–1923, nl.wikipedia has the commission in 1920 and
  elsewhere 1919–1922. Used the register's span. The slice has no listing year for either, so `listed` is
  omitted. The museum's address (Burgemeester Tellegenstraat 128) does not fit the register's numbering
  (which stops at 60): the street was renumbered. Pinned on P.L. Takstraat 1.
- **5736, 5737 theosophical buildings**: no listing year in the slice; nl.wikipedia gives 7 November 1978
  for no. 160, used for the place. Seats in the temple: 600 (De Pijp Krant) or 750 (Amsterdam op de
  kaart): left out. "First film house of Amsterdam" is De Pijp Krant's claim and is attributed in the text.
  "Their only building in Amsterdam" (Amsterdam op de kaart) left out.
- **222145 Afrikahuis**: the slice says 1968. The register says it was still being built in February 1968;
  nl.wikipedia says "opening in 1968" but cites a Telegraaf report of 23 May 1969. Used "late 1960s".
  Number of linked units: four squares (register) or five octagons (nl.wikipedia); text says "linked
  blocks". Outlook "Under threat" rests on nl.wikipedia (undated summary of the dispute, rulings of 2011
  and 2014) and Scout B (Heemschut campaigning in March 2025). I could not reach a 2026 news item: worth a
  check before import.
- **Heineken's cottages**: Amsterdam op de kaart counts 68 houses; the five national register texts add up
  to 70 original houses (two since demolished). Text says "nearly seventy". Tolstraat 136–142: 32 flats
  (four entrances, two flats a floor, four floors). nl.wikipedia says the cottages were built to thwart
  annexation; no other source says so, left out. The Woning-Maatschappij's founding year (1873, De Pijp
  Krant) left out.
- **Amsteldijk 75–78**: the register calls the Father Time relief "left" and the spinning woman "right";
  Amsterdam op de kaart puts them on nos. 78 and 75. Both agree if you face the houses from the river.
  Leliman's houses: designed 1874 (Amsterdam op de kaart), finished 1876 (register).
- **Amsteldijk 86**: Amsterdam op de kaart notes that a drawing is dated "hooimaand 1941", a month name
  then used in NSB circles, and adds that nothing is known of the family's stance. Left out: an insinuation
  about private people. The client (the architect's father) is not named.
- **Polderhuis**: listed 2001 (slice, nl.wikipedia) or 2000 (owner's site); used 2001. The owner is named
  on his own site and on nl.wikipedia; not named here. The painted advertisement on the side wall is from
  Amsterdam op de kaart (page of August 2017): worth a look on site, not flagged.
- **Vredeskerk**: listed 2001 (slice) or 2004 (nl.wikipedia Vredeskerkplein); used the slice. "Open around
  services" is an assumption for a working parish church; opening hours not checked. The brewery in the
  sisters' convent is from Scout B (item 212), not checked again. Scout B's information boards in the church
  (since 29 April 2025) and the figure "nine of 24 came back" are not used: I did not see the source.
- **Coöperatiehof**: the national register reads Trapman's stone as being about the socialist attitude to
  the church; nl.wikipedia describes books, key and snake. Described, not interpreted. Scout B's "Joodsche
  Boekerij" of 1932 and the bell that "rings twice an hour to urge residents to read" are not used: not
  verified.
- **Van der Helstplein**: "first" is the national register's own wording ("het eerste door Kramer ontworpen
  woongebouw") and is attributed to it. Which side of the square the block closes I could not settle from
  the texts, so the body does not say.
- **Oranjekerk**: rebuilt 1997–1999 (nl.wikipedia) or 1998–2000 (Amsterdam op de kaart); used the first.
  Scout B's "saved by three neighbour women in 1993" not used: unverified. Current office tenants not named.
- **Bathhouse**: portraits "2022" (nl.wikipedia) or 2021 (Scout B); text says "about 2022". Scout B's
  "Jews banned from March 1941" not used: unverified here.
- **Records with no register text**: 200443 (Polderhuis), 200431 (Oranjekerk) and 222067 (Sarphatipark 42)
  come with an empty `text`; the places rest on Amsterdam op de kaart, nl.wikipedia and the sites named.

## Route ideas

- **Plan Zuid in half an hour**: Van der Helstplein (Kramer's first) → Pastelstraat prows → Coöperatiehof
  through the west gate → Tellegen monument → De Dageraad and its museum → the twin schools on the canal.
  Thursday to Sunday afternoon for the museum walk.
- **The village that lost to Amsterdam**: Sint Willibrordusstraat → Polderhuis → town hall of
  Nieuwer-Amstel → Cullinanplein and Asscher → theosophical temple → Heineken's cottages → Amsteldijk 75–86.
  All within 400 metres; it tells the annexation of 1896 from both sides.
- **Diamantbuurt loop**: Asscher → Heineken's cottages → round bathhouse and school → Van Epen's Topaasstraat
  → out under the gateway to the Jozef Israëlskade (Reve's corner, if Scout B's place is made).
- **Berlage to Dageraad**: the bakery of the cooperative De Dageraad on the Toldwarsstraat, then ten minutes
  south to the housing the same movement built fifteen years later.
- **Churches that changed**: Oranjekerk (rebuilt inside) → Afrikahuis (empty) → Vredeskerk (in use), with
  the Ceintuur Theater on the way for lunch.
