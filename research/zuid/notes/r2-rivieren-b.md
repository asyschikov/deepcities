## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **229, authenticity.** That a German soldier painted the V rests on one eyewitness (born 1931, lived at
  Diezestraat 12), published with photos by the local history foundation in May 2011. The German
  V-campaign of summer 1941 itself is well documented. The body attributes the story to him and does not
  name him. His link between the soldiers in the flats and the Sieberg garage was his own assumption
  ("ik neem aan"); the text says "as far as he knew". That the Wehrmacht held the garage is stated
  separately by Geheugen van Plan Zuid.
- **229, readers.** The scout wrote that readers said no to removal. Of six printed reactions five say keep
  them and one is non-committal, so the text says "most".
- **229, what you see.** The 2011 photos and the 2024 imagery show a dark V, which fits the black overpaint
  showing through rather than the original paint. The body calls it "the dark shadow of a letter V".
- **230, purpose.** The project's own conclusion is cautious: no order found, "presumably private
  initiative", blackout control by the block warden as the best explanation for numbers front and back,
  and doubt about the link with raids. The body follows that and no more. Its contributors also note that
  some numbers were repainted after the war (one says into the 1970s), so not every white number is of
  1940 to 1945.
- **230, the scout's pin** (Vechtstraat 10) is the PDOK address point; kept.
- **231, opening year.** Wikipedia: in use 1931, fully opened 30 January 1932. Architectenweb and Geheugen van
  Plan Zuid say 1931. Both are in the text. The strike: "more than ten weeks" is Wikipedia, "all strikers
  dismissed" is Geheugen van Plan Zuid after De Tribune of 11 May 1932.
- **231, left out:** Canadians billeted in the garage in 1945 (scout): I found no source. The bank the
  2013 page mentions among today's users: not confirmed for 2026. "First Bendix brake tester in the
  country" is the claim of Sport in Beeld (1932), stated as fact.
- **231, bowling year.** Wikipedia "about 1970", Architectenweb "ten years after 1960", Knijn's own site
  "1971". Text: "about 1970".
- **232, the purchase.** The shop says Anne chose the diary here with her father on 11 June 1942. The Anne
  Frank Stichting's Kennisbank says the Variété card game certainly came from Blankevoort and the diary
  "very likely", "although there is no clear source for this"; its event page states it more firmly
  ("had been bought at Boekhandel Blankevoort"). Anne wrote she was there when it was bought; Otto Frank
  wrote in 1952 that his wife bought it. The scout's "saw it in the window" is from the local history
  page and is not in the Kennisbank; left out.
- **232, left out:** the 25-year campaign and 1,200 signatures for the statue (no source found; the shop
  says only that the owner arranged the statue in 2005), and the bakelite fountain pens in the cellar
  (true per the 1991 booklet, but postwar and nothing to see).
- **232, opening hours** are from the shop's own site (no date on the page). The shop's site is live, but I found no dated 2026 report that it is open.
- **232, the hour for Jews.** The booklet's witness says "between four and five"; the regulation is usually
  given as three to five. The text says "a late-afternoon hour".
- **236, the sabbath chain.** Two sources agree that the bridge was given one when built: Geheugen van Plan
  Zuid (a box on one of the piers, gift of the Jewish community) and M.M. Bakker in "Bruggen" 2010 (tubes
  and chains, never used). Whether it is still there is not confirmed. A Commons photo of 10 December 2022
  shows a grey metal cabinet beside Kramer's seat, captioned by the uploader as the eruv's storage box;
  that is one person's identification and I could not tell which bridgehead it is (the east one would be
  in Oost). Not used as a photo. Hence the flag.
- **236, "first prestressed-concrete bridge over the Amstel"** is from Geheugen van Plan Zuid only.
  Wikipedia says the Parool contest drew "Dr. A. Plesmanbrug" and "Utrechtsebrug" as the most entered
  names, not that one was runner-up; the text says "the other favourite". Wikipedia's "oldest freerunning
  park of Amsterdam" carries a citation-needed tag; the text says only that there is one.
- **236, pin.** West end of the bridge deck from OpenStreetMap (52.339410, 4.908540), in Zuid.
- **237, rivers.** The scout's "all streets here are rivers" is too strong (Rooseveltlaan, Churchilllaan,
  Victorieplein); the text says "nearly every street". "Miep Gies is geen rivier" is the neighbour's own
  account of the first reply; attributed to him, not named.
- **237, the diary.** The neighbour's guess that Otto Frank first read the diary in this house is not
  used. The Anne Frank Stichting says he came to the door in early June 1945, stayed more than seven
  years, and was handed the papers in mid-July 1945; the text says he was living here then. (Whether all seven years were
  at no. 25 was not checked; the text does not say so.)
- **237, pin.** Centre of the green from OpenStreetMap (52.344563, 4.904095); the scout's pin was on its
  east tip.
- **256, pin.** The scout's pin (52.341050, 4.900600) is west of the Moerdijkstraat. The garden lies
  between Moerdijkstraat and Waalstraat; the fence on the Almstraat is at about 52.341314, 4.901451
  (OpenStreetMap), used here.
- **256, who built the block.** The residents' letter (after V.T. van Rossem of the monuments office) says
  1938, Gulden and Geldmaker, for Woningbouwvereniging Amsterdam Zuid. Ons Amsterdam (1996) calls the same
  addresses (Moerdijkstraat 1-51, Waalstraat 132-182) the socialists' homes "of Onze Woning". The two
  disagree on the association, so the body names neither and says only "the socialists' block".
- **256, names.** The local page spells "IJzerdraad" and gives the architect as "W. Geldmaker" and the
  mermaid's maker as "Lou(i)s Beyerman". I used Willem IJzerdraat (nl.wikipedia), "Gulden and Geldmaker"
  without initials, and Louise Beijerman in the `artist` field only (the same page lists "Loes Beijerman"
  among the Muzenplein sculptors, so the identification is likely but not proven; the body does not name
  her).
- **256, what is visible.** A Commons photo of 5 July 2025 shows the garden side with a ceramic figure on
  a planter pier, so the garden and figures are there. The mermaid and the two girls have no sighting
  after 2010; they are in the Look for list on the strength of the 2010 restoration. How much can be seen
  through the fence was not checked.
- **256, "Protestant".** Ons Amsterdam calls Patrimonium "protestants" in the text and its tenants
  "gereformeerden" in a caption; the text says Protestant. The Remonstrant block was cut for length.
- **Dutch.** All Dutch is written fresh from the Dutch sources. No Dutch source exists for the 2024
  sightings (TracesOfWar in English, street imagery).
