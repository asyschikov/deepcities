## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Eight graves, not nine.** The slice holds eight listed grave monuments at Zorgvlied
  (530061 to 530068). The complex number 530056 is not a record of its own.
- **Zorgvlied, listing year.** The slice says 2007; nl.wikipedia gives 10 March 2008 for the
  designation. I kept 2007 from the register data.
- **Zorgvlied, when the land became Amsterdam.** nl.wikipedia says 1896; the city register text
  on the pumping station implies the border with Nieuwer-Amstel still ran north of here around
  1915 to 1921. I left the year out ("the city has long since grown around it").
- **Janssen's sculptor.** The national register reads the signature as "H. Rautsche" of Paris;
  nl.wikipedia's list has "H. Kautsch" (probably the medallist Heinrich Kautsch). I did not name
  him.
- **Carré's dates.** Register: 1846–1911; nl.wikipedia: 1845–1911. Not used in the text.
- **Zorgvlied gates.** The register on the pumping station says the 1919 contract included a
  design for "a new monumental entrance gate" to the cemetery, because a ditch was filled in;
  the national register dates the present gates to 1926 (Van der Bijl). I used 1926 only.
- **Zorgvlied rank.** I set Amazing: it is the one place in this slice worth a trip on its own.
  Nice is defensible if the 1 in 20 quota is tight across Zuid.
- **Berlagebrug length.** nl.wikipedia says 80 m, the national register "over 100 m long, 24 m
  wide". I followed the register and did not repeat Wikipedia's "largest bridge of Amsterdam".
- **Berlagebrug plaques.** Scout A item 162 speaks of "two plates of 1945". 4en5mei describes
  one white stone plaque with an English text given by Canadian soldiers (unveiled 1945);
  geheugenvanplanzuid shows a plate with a Dutch text that got its present wording in 1995
  thanks to Tonny van Renterghem (Scout B's note). I describe both and flagged
  `check: Location`, because no source says where on the bridge they sit (Scout A: west side).
  The pin is the bridge tower, Amsteldijk 134, in Zuid.
- **Berlagebrug as a present.** geheugenvanplanzuid calls the commission a birthday present
  from the council to Berlage; the register says "ere-opdracht". I wrote "commission of
  honour". nl.wikipedia's story that lobbying by Zeeger Gulden got Berlage the job instead of
  Kramer carries a "source?" tag and is left out.
- **Scout A item 155** (the Canadians' pennant on the Apollolaan) is a different spot and
  stays with the scouts; the Berlagebrug place does not mention it.
- **Remise dates.** Sources differ on the first half (18 or 27 August 1927) and on the second
  (13 June 1929 per Erik Swierstra, 1930 per nl.wikipedia, "around 1930" per the register). I
  wrote "August 1927" and "by 1930". "Largest covered tram depot in Europe" is Swierstra's
  claim (also on nl.wikipedia); the text attributes it to tram historians.
- **Remise pin.** Estimated on the forecourt at the Kromme Mijdrechtstraat (`check: Location`).
  The register point lies on the Amsteldijk side.
- **Trompenburgstraat 45-95, who built it.** The city register says it is "not known" whether
  Gulden helped found the association Amsterdam Zuid and guesses the block was tied to the
  Spijker works. Dick Neijssel's history of the association (geheugenvanplanzuid, 2009) says
  workers of the Zuidergasfabriek founded it on 12 January 1911 and built these 88 homes. I
  followed Neijssel and wrote "by the association's own account" for the garden village story.
  Scout B item 235 (`check: Facts`: does the 1915 block still stand?) is answered: yes, it is
  monument 222201, renovated in 1983. Merge item 235 into this place.
- **Golden Coach.** The register says the Spijkers presented it in 1898; it was built before
  the Trompenburg works opened (1899), so the text does not say it was made here.
- **Architect of nos. 21-43.** Register: the draughtsman J. Vis; nl.wikipedia adds "or Dolf van
  Gendt". I kept Vis.
- **De Kiekenren, current use.** The register text names two users (Stichting Philadelphia
  Zorg at the top, the Ecole Française d'Amsterdam further down); nl.wikipedia's list of
  Finnish schools says "office of a care organisation". The text says "a care organisation"
  and the place is flagged `check: Facts` (who uses it, and whether it can be seen from the
  gateway at no. 60A). No usable photo: see below.
- **De Kiekenren, "140 schools".** The figure is the register's; nl.wikipedia lists other
  Finnish schools in Amsterdam beside the two the register mentions, so I did not write that
  only two are left.
- **Maranathakerk, shape.** nl.wikipedia calls it six-sided; the city register describes an
  octagon in detail. I followed the register. Demolition year of the Thomas van Aquinokerk on
  the Rijnstraat: 2005 (nl.wikipedia) or 2004 (Scout B); the text says "since demolished".
  Current use checked on kleintjezuid.nl (head office at Hunzestraat 89, November 2026).
- **Zomerdijkstraat, "first".** "The first studio flats built in the Netherlands" is the claim
  of Mariëtte van Stralen's monograph as quoted by geheugenvanplanzuid and nl.wikipedia; the
  text says "by its historians' account". Build year: the slice says 1933–1934, the register
  1934 (permit February, opening November 1934). Listing year 1988 is from nl.wikipedia (the
  slice has none).
- **Zomerdijkstraat, scout merges.** Scout A (table, line for 335836) and Scout B item 240
  merge the Van der Veen plaque into this record: verified on geheugenvanplanzuid (plaque by
  Fred Carasso at no. 22) and used. The liberation linden of 5 May 1945 at Kinderdijkstraat 48
  and the "Oorlogsmonument Kinderdijkstraat" plaque on the side wall were not verified by me
  and are under Leads.
- **Stadwijck, scout merge.** Scout B item 242 (the lock and the last stretch of the Wandelweg)
  is merged into this place. Its nickname "Lovers' Lane" for the Wandelweg is not in my
  sources and is left out. Access to the lock on foot is unconfirmed (`check: Facts`).
- **Villa at Zuidelijke Wandelweg 37.** The register name is "Woonhuis Teulings". The clients
  are private people, so the title and text do not use the name. The register (2012) says they
  still lived there; I did not repeat that as a present fact.
- **De Mirandabad** is not in this slice's records (it is not a listed monument here).
- Web search through curl returned nothing usable; all facts come from the registers,
  nl.wikipedia, geheugenvanplanzuid.nl, agv.nl, 4en5mei.nl, zorgvlied.amstelveen.nl and
  kleintjezuid.nl.

## Leads

- **Zorgvlied, unlisted graves and monuments** (Scout A already has Krop's own grave with "De
  eeuwige vrouw", Manfred Langer and the Vadermonument): also the "Gebroken Kolom" for the
  writers and poets buried here, the Indonesia field "Gordel van Smaragd" (2007, with earth
  flown in from Java), the corner "Paradiso" for unusual monuments that came out of the dispute
  over Peter Giele's grave, the mausoleum 't Lalibellum (2012), a landing stage for funerals by
  boat, and Paul Biegel's bench. Source: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorgvlied_(begraafplaats)
- **Zorgvlied tours**: the cemetery runs guided walks (Open Monumentendag 13 September 2026:
  graves of musicians plus a concert in the aula). https://zorgvlied.amstelveen.nl/
- **Two more Finnish schools** at Uiterwaardenstraat 234 and 238 (Alexander Rozendaalschool,
  1950, and Hendrik Dienskeschool, 1949), with photos on Commons; and one at Radioweg 64, now
  the café of the Jaap Edenbaan. Whether the Uiterwaardenstraat pair still stands needs a
  look. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finse_school
- **Volten's "Vier kolommen" (13 m) at Amsteldok / Rivierstaete** and a sculpture by W.C.
  Brouwer on the corner of the Trompenburgstraat and Gaaspstraat; an electricity hut in the
  Trompenburgstraat (photo on Commons). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trompenburgstraat
- **Gaaspstraat playground**: founded in 1921 by men of the same gas workers' association
  (Scout B item 235 has the clubhouse-by-barge story).
  https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/architectuur/woningbouw/gasarbeiders.htm
- **Liberation linden** (planted 5 May 1945) at Kinderdijkstraat 48 and the war memorial
  plaque on the side wall of the studio flats: Scout A table; nl.wikipedia
  "Oorlogsmonument Kinderdijkstraat".
- **Zuidelijke Wandelweg**: no. 23 by Auke Komter, house stones on nos. 9, 11 and 13, and the
  Liberal Jewish synagogue at no. 41 (SeARCH, with stones from the old synagogue; Scout B has
  it). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuidelijke_Wandelweg
- **The old water tower of Nieuwer-Amstel** stood on the depot site until 1928 (gone; one line
  in the depot text).

## Route ideas

- **Down the Amstel to the dead** (about 2 km one way): Berlagebrug, the depot's Amsteldijk
  entrance, the Trompenburgstraat blocks, then along the Amsteldijk past the Martin Luther
  Kingpark to bridge 424, Gemaal Stadwijck and Zorgvlied. Back along the Zuidelijke Wandelweg
  past Salomonson's villa.
- **Rijnbuurt loop** (about 1.5 km): depot forecourt on the Kromme Mijdrechtstraat, Lekstraat
  synagogue (`rivieren-noord`), Trompenburgstraat, De Kiekenren, Maranathakerk, Zomerdijkstraat
  studio flats.
- **Kramer against De Klerk**: the two sides of the Vrijheidslaan with the Berlagebrug as the
  finish; combines with the `rivieren-noord` route "Plan Zuid in an hour".
- **Public Works in one walk**: bridge 424 (Kramer), the pump house (Westerman), the tram depot
  and the Berlagebrug (Biemond with Berlage): four kinds of municipal design from 1921 to 1932.
