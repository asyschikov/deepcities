## Conflicts and uncertainties

Corrected against the slice file:

- **NLR (527825, 527826)**: `year_from` 1998 is wrong. The register text gives design 1938–1939 and
  building 1939–1941.
- **Aalsmeerder Veerhuis (5495)**: `year_from` 1971 is not a build year. The register text says early
  17th century with a front of 1634.
- **Horse tram depot (504667, 504673)**: slice 1885. Amsterdam op de kaart dates the tender to
  February 1883, De Binnenpret gives 1884 "(and 1885?)". Used 1883–1884. The register names G.B. and
  A. Salm; De Binnenpret credits Abraham Salm alone. Both are named.
- **222115, Amstelveenseweg 106-120**: no register text and no data in the slice. Identified through
  Wikipedia's monument list and Amsterdam op de kaart as the flats of 1955 by Göbel and Den Hertog
  (Wikipedia's list spells "den Hartog") on the site of the tea garden Schinkelhaven. Sources give
  32 flats (Amsterdam op de kaart) and 36 planned (Wikipedia): no number used. It has no register
  text, so it was folded into the depot place next door, which carried the same name.
- **Huis te Vraag (222151)**: the slice calls P. Oosterhuis the architect and the use "country
  estates". He was the carpenter and contractor who founded it; no architect attribute set (one
  sentence in the register hints at Leonard Springer but does not say it). Maximilian's visit is 1489
  in the register and 1486 on the cemetery's site: written as "the 1480s". The old house was pulled
  down in 1855 (register 200433), "the 1880s" (register 222151) or 1890 (Wikipedia, cemetery site):
  left out. Burials: about 12,000, some sources 15,000 (Wikipedia): 12,000 used. The boatbuilder of
  Rijnsburgstraat 75 is L.D. Spijdel in the register and L.J. Spijdel on Wikipedia; a private person,
  not named.
- **Calvijnschool (222126)**: the register text still describes a school annex with artists'
  studios. Wikipedia and Amsterdam op de kaart say it was converted to flats in 2015: followed.
- **Klokkenhof (222116)**: register "designed 1954, finished seven years later"; Wikipedia "plans
  from 1959, delivered 1962"; the hotel opened in May 1962. Built set to 1954–1961, summary says
  "finished in 1961". Client is Van Saane in the register and Van Saanen on Wikipedia: register
  followed. The register puts the mosaic on the north side and, further down, on the "south front":
  north is right (Wikipedia, Commons photo). The carillon being silent "for many years" comes from
  Wikipedia only: `check: Facts`.
- **Hillegomschool (222140)**: register 1953, Amsterdam op de kaart 1955 (opening report in De
  Waarheid of 20 June 1955), Wikipedia: building from 1952, first delivery 5 January 1954. Built set
  to 1952–1954 and the newspaper quoted with its date. Present users not confirmed by a current
  source (the school's site could not be read with curl): `check: Facts`.
- **Patrimonium block (222106)**: slice 1914; the register text says 1912–1913. Built set to
  1912–1914. The claims "Patrimonium's first block" (Amsterdam op de kaart) and "the architects'
  first Housing Act project" (city register) are attributed in the text.
- **Roodenburgh**: the register says his first big job was "the Ajax stadium (1920)"; the year looks
  doubtful, so the text says only that he designed Ajax's stadium.
- **Prison (200752)**: Scout B item 243 (after the last transport from the Hollandsche Schouwburg,
  deportations from Amsterdam left from this prison) could not be confirmed. The cited page of
  Geheugen van Plan Zuid does not say it, and Wikipedia and Amsterdam op de kaart do not mention it.
  Left out. Used instead, from Wikipedia: Hannie Schaft (17 April 1945) and Kleiman and Kugler. The
  "only 19th-century prison left in Amsterdam" is the city register's claim and is attributed.
- **Haarlemmermeerstation (267)**: the museum line's site says "closed until season 2027", which is
  the winter break after the 2026 season. The line has started at the Jollenpad since July 2024 with
  a shuttle bus from the station; no date is known for the return of the trams, so `outlook_when` is
  empty. Whether the platform side can be seen past the building site is flagged with `check`.

Other points:

- **Heritage status of mixed ensembles**: the depot place (two national records plus the municipal
  flats) and Jacob Marisplein (national house plus municipal school) are set to "National +
  municipal monument", as earlier slices did for mixed groups.
- **Surinameplein**: the square itself lies in West; only the southern quarter circle (222009) and
  the Klokkenhof are in Zuid. The northern half of Roodenburgh's scheme is outside this slice.
- **Pins**: Huis te Vraag is pinned on the address point Rijnsburgstraat 51 (PDOK). The Patrimonium
  block is pinned on Baarsstraat 50, taken to be the turret corner with the Vaartstraat
  (`check: Location`). The gateway blocks are pinned on the Baanders corner towers (register point
  of 222007).
- **Aalsmeerder Veerhuis**: the Hendrick de Keyser page could not be found with curl; present
  tenants (Wikipedia: Stichting Diogenes and the Vrienden van de Amsterdamse Binnenstad) are not
  confirmed, so the text says "offices". The rhyme on the gable stone was read from the Commons
  photograph; whether the stone sits in the inn's own wall or beside it should be checked on site.
- **Sites that curl could not read** (script-only or blocked): britams.nl, nlr.org, tobiasschool.nl,
  heemschut.nl, hendrickdekeyser.nl. Web search was not used.

## Route ideas

- **West bank of the Schinkel, north to south** (about 2.5 km): Aalsmeerder Veerhuis, Surinameplein
  half circle and Klokkenhof, Jacob Marisplein and the Calvijnschool, the gateway blocks down the
  Haarlemmermeerstraat, Sloterkade 142–144, Hillegomschool, Huis te Vraag (weekdays only), the Stam
  office, NLR.
- **East bank loop**: Vondelpark exit, horse tram depot (with the gable stone across the water),
  Patrimonium block, the prison and Haarlemmermeerstation; on a summer Sunday continue with the
  vintage bus to the museum tram.
- **The De Waal trail**: the developer's square (Jacob Marisplein 20, which he designed), then the
  pile works' office by Mart Stam on the Generaal Vetterstraat, 1.3 km south. One family, from a
  village water tower to the foundations of the Rijksmuseum.
- **Edge of the old municipalities**: the Schinkel was the border between Sloten (west, annexed
  1921) and Nieuwer-Amstel (east, annexed 1896). Veerhuis, Jacob Marisplein, the dike cottages and
  Huis te Vraag on one side; depot, prison and station on the other.
