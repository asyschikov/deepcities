## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **The 1933 building-site strike at the Van Heutsz monument (scout item 106) is not in the text.** I
  could not find it in any source I could open: nl and en Wikipedia, the national register,
  Stadscuratorium, Andere Tijden (VPRO, 2000), the 1998 note "Held of houwdegen?" and the archived
  Buitenbeeldinbeeld page all cover the protests but none mentions a strike. The Kunstwacht page
  (KW 1988) does not load without a browser. If the scout has the source, it is one sentence to add.
  What is in: the two years of council opposition, the banners at the unveiling, 1965, 1967, 1984 (twice),
  1991 and 2004. Left out for length: Roel van Duijn's blank plaque of 1998 and the protest of Van
  Heutsz's son in 1943 (an SS member who found the monument "too soft", per Andere Tijden).
- Same monument: the national register (2004) says the bolts of the letters were still on the plinth. The
  monument was restored in 2004–2007, so the body does not claim they are there now; the Look for line
  attributes them to the register. Sources differ on the 1984 victim (nl.wikipedia: a twelve-year-old;
  Stadscuratorium: a teenager, lightly injured): the text says "a boy". Sources differ on the year of the
  competition (register 1932, nl.wikipedia and Andere Tijden 1930): left out.
- **Van Tuyll monument: where it is now.** Confirmed: moved in March 2022 to the stairwell at the entrance
  of section I (Cuypersgenootschap, 11 April 2022), court ruling reported 15 December 2022, the stadium
  "still looking for a permanent place". Nothing later found. `check: Location`. Not confirmed either:
  whether Wils's brick plinth with the planters is still outside. The pin is the register point (old spot).
  Both photos are from 2018 and show the statue outside.
- Marathon tower height: the national register says 42.19 m ("after the marathon"), nl.wikipedia says
  46 m. No height in the text.
- "First Olympic flame": stated with its source (National Committee for 4 and 5 May; nl.wikipedia says the
  same and credits the idea to Wils, after the sports historian Jurryt van de Vooren).
- Prometheus (Carasso): made in three months according to the 4 and 5 May Committee; the scout note said
  two. Where it stands now differs too (4 and 5 May Committee: square on the west side; nl.wikipedia:
  beside the Marathon tower), so the text only says it has been outside since 2000.
- **Citroën building North: present use not confirmed** (`check: Facts`). The register text (2015) was
  written for redevelopment plans; nl.wikipedia only covers the south building's new tenant (2019). Both
  photos are from 2007 and show it white with the Citroën letters. Build years: the slice has 1959–1962,
  the register text the same.
- **Tripolis Park.** The register and nl.wikipedia stop in 2019. Commons photos of July and September
  2021 show MVRDV's block under construction; the site tripolis-park.com is live. The text says "under
  construction in 2021" and "now called Tripolis Park". Completion year and tenants are not in the text
  (the MVRDV page does not load without a browser).
- Burgerweeshuis: nl.wikipedia calls it "the first example of structuralism". Not used as a claim; the
  hook is the register's own closing line. "Closed in 1991" follows nl.wikipedia.
- Willem de Zwijgerkerk: the slice has 1932–1934; the register's literature list and nl.wikipedia give
  1931 (opened), the complex 1930–1931. Used 1930–1931. The register spells the competition motto
  "Pax in trantibus"; written as "Pax intrantibus". The wiki list of municipal monuments has "1913",
  a typo.
- Olympia: the register counts 572 dwellings and 28 shop dwellings, nl.wikipedia 535 dwellings and 8
  shop dwellings. The text says "more than 500".
- **Zuidwijkkapel (222146), folded into the Olympiakade ensemble as a Look for line.** No register text.
  The register has C. de Geus, 1933–1935; Reliwiki quotes a 2007 article (NIW) that says Cornelis
  Kruyswijk, 1928, built for the Reformed congregation with a film projection room, and passes on a story
  of a hiding place under the stairs. Reliwiki's own list adds a temporary synagogue in 1938–1942. Only
  what two sources agree on is in the text (Pentecostal congregation 1961–2007, Liberal Jewish
  Congregation end 2007 to 2010). The addresses 267A to 267C in the address register suggest it has been
  split into homes since; not confirmed. If the film room or the wartime story can be sourced, it deserves
  a place of its own.
- Olympiakade 52–60 (222005): a Commons caption says it was built for Patrimonium; the register does not
  name the client. Left out.
- Hygiëaplein: the slice has 1932–1934 for no. 40, the national register says 1921; for nos. 8–10 the
  slice has 1921–1923, the wiki list and an archive photo say 1924, the Marathonbuurt article says 1928.
  Used 1921 and 1924. Who uses which building today is not settled (`check: Facts`). The register calls
  8–10 "Montessorischool"; I found no source for that name and did not use it.
- Open-air school: the slice has 1932–1936; register and nl.wikipedia give 1931–1932.
- Van Tuyll van Serooskerkenplein: no register text for either record. That the two sculptures stand on
  pillars on the shop pavilions comes from the photos and the register outlines; which is on which
  pavilion and whether the pavilions are still shops is flagged (`check: Facts`).
- Bertelmanplein: the complex has seven blocks; six are in this slice. The seventh (the ACOB block) is not
  in the slice file. The pin is on the square (the playground), not on a block.
- Dutch texts are written from the Dutch sources, not translated; quotes are the sources' own words.

## Leads

- **Zuidelijk Citroëngebouw** (Stadionplein 24–28, Jan Wils, 1929–1931): not in the slice file. The city
  made Wils the architect as a condition; C6 lorries were assembled there in the 1930s; head office of
  Citroën Nederland until 2014; since October 2019 Pon's "Move". https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuidelijk_Citro%C3%ABngebouw .
  Check whether it is listed and which slice has it; it would pair with Citroën building North.
- **Prometheus** (Fred Carasso, 1947), Olympic Stadium: now a Look for line in the stadium place. Enough
  for a place of its own if wanted: 4 May commemoration each year.
  https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/zoeken/1435/amsterdam-monument-bij-het-olympisch-stadion
- **Voetballers** (Ek van Zanten, 1979), behind the stadium by the Cruyff Court: the plate of 2007 says it
  shows Cruyff being fouled by Vogts in the 1974 final; the sculptor said in 2014 it is a general football
  moment with Cruyff's head. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voetballers_(Ek_van_Zanten)
- **Wachters** (Peer Veneman, 1994), gate of Stadionkade 113: in the open-air school place.
- **Spiegel van de hemel** (André Volten), beside the Burgerweeshuis: in the Burgerweeshuis place as a
  Look for line (scout item 110).
- **De onbekende verliezer** (Ram Katzir, 2006), Marathonweg at the Achillesstraat: two reliefs of worn-out
  marathon runners on columns left empty in the 1920s for lack of money, a pendant to Kees Smout's fresh
  runners at the Herculesstraat. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_onbekende_verliezer
- **Slinky** (Simon Wald Lasowski and Sajoscha Talirz), over the entrance of Tripolis 300 (2019). Status
  after the rebuilding unknown.
- **Water, aarde, lucht** (Jan Meefout, 1958), IJsbaanpad at the Schinkel.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_beelden_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
- **Parkterrein Van Tuyll van Serooskerkenplein** (municipal monument 200359): not in the slice file;
  mentioned in the square's place.
- **Geert Groote School**, Hygiëaplein (the school across the Marathonweg, Waldorf primary): not a record
  here; a Commons photo of 2016 shows its entrance.
- **The Febo ticket booths** on the Stadionplein (two former stadium ticket offices, snack bars from 1971
  to 2013): gone, a sentence at most. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadionplein
- Under the Lyceumbrug: the passage, canoe stores and changing rooms. Whether any of it is in use or can
  be seen is unknown; worth a look on site.
