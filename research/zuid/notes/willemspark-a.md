## Belongs to an existing place

All four belong to the existing place **Vondelpark** (complex 504733). One fact each worth adding there:

- **504814, bridge 453 (Norbert Eliasbrug)**, by the rose garden. Wrought iron of about 1876 by Willem Hamer jr., with
  cast-iron women's heads in the middle of both edge beams and piers shaped like Corinthian columns in the water.
  Nameless until 2017, when it was named after the sociologist Norbert Elias. The iron came from Staal & Haalmeijer,
  the firm of the father of the architect J.F. Staal, whose villas stand at the Emmaplein. (nl.wikipedia Norbert
  Eliasbrug; national register.)
- **504820, bridge 454 (Diaconessenbrug, formerly Willemsbrug)**, the park exit to the Emmalaan. Not built by the
  park but by the developers of the villa district: the Bouwonderneming Willemspark put it out to tender in July 1882
  (Algemeen Handelsblad, quoted by Amsterdam op de kaart), which is why the shields in the railing carry a W. It was
  called Willemsbrug unofficially until 2016; since 2017 it is named after the deaconesses of the Lutheran hospital
  that stood on the Koninginneweg from 1898 to 1987. The iron gates between the stone pylons on the Emmalaan side
  are part of the listing.
- **504827, bridge 455 (Brandbrug, unofficially Weidebruggetje)**, in the west of the park. Of 1876, by Hamer, taken
  out of the park and put back during the bridge restoration of 1999–2010. (nl.wikipedia Brandbrug.)
- **504846, gate on the Amstelveenseweg**. Iron railings on a base and piers of coarse concrete. Set in the fence, left
  of the entrance by the playground, is a memorial stone by Ferdinand Jantzen, unveiled 25 May 1946, for four members
  of the Binnenlandse Strijdkrachten shot here on 7 May 1945, two days after the German surrender, in a firefight
  with passing German army vehicles. A fifth name was removed just before the unveiling on a suspicion of NSB
  membership that was never proven; a later plaque adds it. (nl.wikipedia Oorlogsmonument Amstelveenseweg; 4en5mei.nl
  monument 1443.) This memorial may deserve its own place in the scouts' round: see Leads.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Pumping station, date and designer.** The national register (505638) says about 1875, client possibly the park
  association, architect unknown; the slice file gives 1875 and "Publieke Werken". Amsterdam op de kaart found
  drawings marked "handed to the contractor 11 September 1883, the engineer J.G. van Niftrik" and a newspaper notice
  of the plan's approval (Algemeen Handelsblad 28-7-1883); nl.wikipedia also gives 1883. Used 1883 and Van Niftrik.
  Amsterdam op de kaart thinks the client was the Bouwonderneming Willemspark; the text says only that the developers
  paid the city in 1914.
- **Pumping station, successor.** nl.wikipedia: the city took over in April 1914 "with a new small pumping station on
  the Sophialaan". Amsterdam op de kaart: electric pump in the old building from 1914, made redundant by a new station
  on the Sophialaan in 1970. The text gives no year for the Sophialaan station. Current use of Amstelveenseweg 115
  not found: the text says only that it stands and is not open.
- **Sint-Agneskerk, "first church in Amsterdam with a free-standing bell tower".** Only nl.wikipedia says this, and
  it also calls the tower "almost free-standing". Left out.
- **Sint-Agneskerk, first phase.** Register: 1920–1921; nl.wikipedia: 1919–1920, in use 24 October 1921. Used the
  register years and the opening date.
- **Emma monument, stone and reliefs.** Register: "natuursteen"; nl.wikipedia: limestone; Amsterdam op de kaart:
  sandstone. No stone named. The back of the base shows, per nl.wikipedia, the sanatorium Oranje Nassau's Oord, and per
  the register two women holding an open book. Only the front and one side are described. nl.wikipedia cannot say
  whether Wilhelmina or Juliana unveiled it: left out. The 1987 vandalism rests on nl.wikipedia alone.
- **Vondelpark gate, date.** Register: "probably 1925–1930", Late Amsterdam School, client the park association.
  Amsterdam op de kaart: tendered April 1916, nearly finished April 1917 (Het Nieuws van den Dag 28-4-1917), by Public
  Works, moved back when the road was widened. nl.wikipedia adds that the fence and memorial were moved again in
  1963–1964. For whoever updates the Vondelpark place.
- **Bridge 454, designer.** nl.wikipedia: Willem Springer; the 1882 tender notice names the architect J.L. Springer.
  Register year 1885.
- **Huize de Windroos, number of flats.** Register: built for four families; Amsterdam op de kaart: originally five
  dwellings. No number in the text. That Kramer drew only the fronts is an inference by Amsterdam op de kaart from the
  sale advertisement; the text says so ("going by the sale advertisement").
- **Fontainehofje.** Founded by will in 1753; the stone says 1754. The register gives Joan Fontaine's dates as
  "1862-1731" (a typo for 1682). Age limit: 55 on the foundation's own site (used), 50 on hofjesinamsterdam.nl.
  Number of dwellings reduced from twelve to six: "in the 1960s" per hofjesinamsterdam.nl; register says showers were
  added in 1979. The page on hofjesvannederland.nl about this hofje is wrong throughout (Lauriergracht, 1927, Van der
  Mey, 59 dwellings): not used.
- **ACOB block.** Register: 88 flats, slice year 1922; Amsterdam op de kaart: 85 flats, 1920–1921. No number in the
  text; years from Amsterdam op de kaart. The block is one of seven by Van Epen (complex 504905); the other six are
  not in this slice and should be picked up by the slice that has them, possibly as one place with this block.
- **Build years corrected from the slice file.** Emmalaan 7, 8, 9, 10: 1911 (slice: 1880 and 1913). Emmaplein 3–5:
  designed 1919 (slice: 1917 and 1923). Oranje Nassaulaan 63: 1917–1918 (slice: 1925). Waldeck Pyrmontlaan 26: 1909
  (slice: 1915). Sophialaan 1–3: designed 1919 (slice: 1920).
- **Oranje Nassaulaan 20, explosion of 5 December 1962.** The only source is the Anefo press photo on Commons
  (caption "Ontploffing op Spaans consulaat", keywords "aanslagen, consulaten"). A 1970 Anefo photo of the consulate
  shows the left half of nos. 18–20, which fits Amsterdam op de kaart (no. 20, consulate of Spain from 1948). The text
  says "explosion" and that the photo is filed under attacks, nothing about who or why. A newspaper source (Delpher)
  would settle it.
- **Parkwijk.** The city register entry (listed 9 September 2025) has no text yet. Everything comes from Amsterdam op
  de kaart; the description of the front is from its photos. "One of the first free-standing blocks of flats in the
  Netherlands" is that site's wording. Cruijff's flat rests on that one source.
- **Handelsschool Hofland.** nl.wikipedia (Hemonystraat 9) says the founder H.J.A. Hofland was the grandfather of the
  journalist Henk Hofland. One unsourced line: left out, kept here as a lead.
- **Emmalaan 7.** Amsterdam op de kaart: orphanage for Jewish boys 1947–1965, about twenty boys; joodsamsterdam.nl:
  Joods Jongenshuis from 1947, closed 1965. The couples who ran it are not named in the text.
- **People not named.** Clients and residents who were not public figures (estate agent, builders, merchants, the
  banker and consul at Emmaplein 5, the banker at Oranje Nassaulaan 63, the director at Sophialaan 18) are described
  by trade only.
- **Cornelis Krusemanstraat 10.** Current use of the former ULO school not found; the text does not state one.
- **Length.** Bodies run 240 to 320 words including the "Look for" lists; the prose itself is about 180 to 250.

## Leads

- **War memorial in the Vondelpark fence**, Amstelveenseweg, left of the entrance by the playground (inside monument
  504846). Stone by Ferdinand Jantzen, 1946, for BS men killed on 7 May 1945; the fifth name removed and later
  restored on a plaque. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oorlogsmonument_Amstelveenseweg and
  https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/monumenten_zoeken/oorlogsmonument/1443
- **Park keeper's hut (Emmaplein 1, 1920) and transformer house** in the garden of the Emmaplein, both small Amsterdam
  School buildings, not listed. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmaplein_(Amsterdam)
- **Sculpture garden on the Emmaplein** ("Groep Adam", a residents' initiative) and Pearl Perlmuter's "Verschijning"
  (1990s). Same Wikipedia page; current state not checked.
- **Sotheby's Amsterdam**, Emmalaan 23, since 2012. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotheby's
- **Oranje Nassaulaan 14**: home of Feike de Boer, first post-war mayor, until 1962 (not a monument; mentioned in the
  ensemble). https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Oranje_Nassaulaan/10-14
- **Foundation stone of 1961 with a Franciscan scene**, Waldeck Pyrmontlaan (exact house unknown).
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gevelsteen_Waldeck_Pyrmontlaan.jpg
- **Vocational school for industrial seamstresses**, Waldeck Pyrmontlaan, from 1920 (house number unknown; archive
  photo on Commons). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kweekschool_voor_de_Detailhandel
- **Sonnevanck, Waldeck Pyrmontlaan 25**, and **Oranje Nassaulaan 1, 3–15, 16, 30–32, 40–56**: unlisted villas with
  pages on Amsterdam op de kaart. https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Waldeck_Pyrmontlaan/Sonnevanck
- **"De kleine Nicolaas"**, corner of Okeghemstraat and Cornelis Krusemanstraat (a shop, eleven photos on Commons from
  2018; not looked into). https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Okeghemstraat,_Amsterdam
- **The modern pumping station on the Sophialaan** at the edge of the pond, successor to Amstelveenseweg 115.
- **Joods Jongenshuis, Emmalaan 7**: a memoir by a son of the house parents appeared in Ons Amsterdam (linked from
  https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/emmalaan/); could carry a place of its own in a Jewish Zuid theme.

## Route ideas

- **From the park into the villa park** (about 1.5 km): leave the Vondelpark over the Diaconessenbrug (bridge 454),
  Staal's villas, Emma monument, Emmaplein 3–5, west along the Oranje Nassaulaan over the pond (nos. 18–20, 26, 63),
  Sophialaan 1–3, Parkwijk and the Handelsschool on the Waldeck Pyrmontlaan, back along the pond past Sophialaan 18.
- **The edge of two plans**: Fontainehofje, carriage depot on the Hendrik Jacobszstraat, Huize de Windroos, the
  Cornelis Krusemanstraat corner, Sint-Agneskerk (time it for the 11.00 Mass), then north up the Amstelveenseweg to
  the pumping station and the park gate with the memorial.
- **Water**: pumping station (Amstelveenseweg 115), the pond at the Sophialaan with the villa standing in it, bridge
  over the pond on the Oranje Nassaulaan, the iron park bridges 453 to 455. Fits the existing Vondelpark places
  (the manure sheds are in the same corner of the park).
