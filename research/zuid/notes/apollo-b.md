## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Build years in the slice file corrected.** Apollohotel (222109): slice says 1928, architect list of four; the
  hotel is by A. Bodon, 1961–1962, with towers of 1969 and 1975 (Amsterdam op de kaart gives the towers to his
  assistant J.H. Ploeger, nl.wikipedia to Bodon; no name in the text). Vossius: the city register text for 200632
  says the school was designed in 1926; that is the founding year, the building is 1931–1932. Montessori school
  (222129): slice 1926–1930, Amsterdam op de kaart 1927, register text for the Lutherkapel "1926"; text says 1927.
- **Hilton, who designed it.** Slice and city register: De Vlaming and Salm, Maaskant supervisor, with the remark
  that many historians see Maaskant's hand. nl/en.wikipedia: Maaskant with De Vlaming and Salm. Text follows the
  register and says so. Architects' initials differ within the register (F.W. de Vlaming / F.A.W. Vlaming); used the
  slice form.
- **Bed-in dates and room.** nl.wikipedia: 24–31 March 1969, room 702 and lounge 704; en.wikipedia: 25–31 March,
  presidential suite then numbered 902, renumbered 702. Text says "the last week of March 1969" and gives no room
  number. That the suite is still let as the John and Yoko Suite comes from en.wikipedia only.
- **Herman Brood bench.** nl.wikipedia says a memorial bench stands in front of the hotel. Not confirmed elsewhere,
  left out of the text; see Leads.
- **"First" claims.** Apollo House as "first office the state built specifically for social insurance": national
  register, attributed in the text. Montessori school as "first school building in Amsterdam designed specially for
  Montessori teaching": Amsterdam op de kaart only, attributed ("by one local account"). Muzenhof garden as the only
  kijktuin in Mien Ruys's work: city register, attributed. en.wikipedia's "first hotel in the Netherlands from an
  international chain" for the Hilton is left out.
- **Apollo House sculptures.** National register: "Rechtvaardigheid" by Krop (man with sword) and "Zorgzaamheid" by
  Wezelaar (woman with lamb). nl.wikipedia and Commons: "De beschermer en handhaver van de sociale wetgeving" (1941)
  and "De beschermster van de sociale zorg en gerechtigheid" (1954). The photos show a man and a woman each holding
  a small figure or animal; the text names the makers and years and calls them guardians, without the attributes.
  Height of the ring: register 9 m, nl.wikipedia 10 m; not in the text.
- **Du Midi cinema.** Rebuilt 1959 (nl.wikipedia) or 1961 (register, Amsterdam op de kaart). Text says "rebuilt"
  without a year. That BC Apollo plays in the hall (nl.wikipedia) is left out; "basketball is the main sport" is from
  Amsterdam op de kaart.
- **Nine children, subjects.** Register and nl.wikipedia disagree on two: Vreugde ("child with wavy hair" vs "child
  with fish on the shoulders") and Van Hall ("girl with bear and bird" vs "girl with cat and bird"). The Look for
  line lists only the seven both sources agree on. That two figures portray Kramer's daughters is nl.wikipedia only
  ("are said to be").
- **Muzenplein 9, architect.** City register: probably "Vessen", not certain. Amsterdam op de kaart: Albert van Essen
  of Voorburg, client a urologist. No architect named in the text; the place's architect field is Kramer for the
  garden and bridge.
- **Dirk Brouwer's year of birth.** City register 222018: 1899; 222019: 1889; Amsterdam op de kaart: 1890;
  nl.wikipedia: 23 November 1899, executed aged 41. No year in the text. Number of HEMA branches (24) is
  nl.wikipedia only, hence "by one count".
- **Elte's wife.** nl.wikipedia Stadionweg 44 says both were killed in Theresienstadt; the Elte article says he died
  there, probably of pneumonia, and that both names are on the Namenmonument. Text: he died there, "his wife did not
  survive either". She is not named.
- **Händelstraat 17-19.** City register: "bomb damage in the Second World War"; Amsterdam op de kaart: destroyed by
  fire in an Allied air raid in November 1944. This is very probably the raid of 26 November 1944 on the SD
  headquarters (see apollo-a, Gerrit van der Veen College), but no source at hand says so, so the text does not link
  them. Worth one check; if confirmed, add the cross-reference.
- **Stadionweg 28.** Amsterdam op de kaart names the second residents (the first director of the HEMA and his wife,
  who survived the war in hiding). Left out: not needed for the place.
- **Lutherkapel, current use.** Amsterdam op de kaart (updated February 2024): a branch of the gym chain Saints &
  Stars since 2020. The chain's own site could not be fetched. `check: Facts` is set. Seats: register 350, Amsterdam
  op de kaart and De Telegraaf 500: left out. Merger of the two Lutheran congregations: 1951 (register) or 1952
  (Amsterdam op de kaart): "early 1950s". The register says the tower bell stands in the porch (2014): not in the
  text, since the building changed hands.
- **Bachzaal.** Seats 500 (register) or 600 (Amsterdam op de kaart): "500 to 600". Day nursery "since 2011" is
  Amsterdam op de kaart; nl.wikipedia says sold in 2008 and converted. The register (2007) still describes the music
  school in the front building and lettering MUZIEKSCHOOL AMSTERDAM; whether that lettering is still there is not
  known, so it is not in the Look for list.
- **Hervormd Lyceum, rector Smit.** nl.wikipedia: pro-German sympathies, not an NSB member. Amsterdam op de kaart:
  NSB member, sentenced to nine years. The text says "pro-German views", alderman appointed by the occupier in 1941
  and nine years, and does not mention party membership. Founding year: 1928 (wiki, founded) and 1929 (first school
  year); text says 1929. Name change to HLZ: 2023 (Amsterdam op de kaart) or 2024 (wiki): no year. "Largest secondary
  school in the Netherlands in 1940" (wiki) left out.
- **Hervormd Lyceum, renovation.** The school's site says the main building will be renovated over "the coming two
  years" and that the school temporarily uses Dongestraat; nl.wikipedia says 2026. `outlook: Temporarily closed`,
  "from 2026, about two years". Exact dates to be filled in.
- **Christian Science church, windows.** Reliwiki: five windows in the front with the evangelists' symbols round a
  window of light. Het Vaderland (1937, quoted on Reliwiki): scenes from Revelation. The text follows Reliwiki and
  says so. That Campendonk was a German expressionist painter is general knowledge, not from a fetched source;
  "professor" is from the newspaper. The church's own site (christianscience.nl) could not be fetched; that services
  are still held on Sunday mornings is from Reliwiki.
- **Reprisal of 24 October 1944.** nl.wikipedia Apollolaan dates the unveiling of the memorial 25 October 1952; the
  monument article explains that this was a provisional plaster version, the bronze following months later. Text says
  "unveiled in 1952". The spelling of the victim of the attack varies (Oelschlägel / Oelenschläger); he is not named
  in the text. The villa destroyed in reprisal (Beethovenstraat 1 / Apollolaan 84) is on the north side and not in
  this slice: left out.
- **Occupation offices on the Apollolaan.** None of the 33 records turned out to be one of the requisitioned
  buildings; the wartime content of the slice is the 1944 shootings, the burned houses in the Händelstraat, the
  Hervormd Lyceum, Elte, Brouwer and the renamed Herman Heijermansweg.
- **Heritage status of mixed ensembles.** Four places combine national and municipal records (Apollohal with hotel,
  Muzenplein, Vrijburg ensemble, both Apollolaan ensembles). They carry "National + municipal monument", and `listed`
  holds the year of the main record.
- **Apollolaan 119** has no register text. The two lines in the west-end ensemble come from Amsterdam op de kaart.
  It lies 400 m east of the rest of that ensemble (opposite the Hilton); it was put there because both are "villas
  and houses of the Apollolaan" and it has no story of its own.

## Route ideas

- **Round De Kom (30 min):** Apollo House, Apollohal and hotel, Hildo Kropbrug, the nine children, Kinderbrug
  (rivieren-noord), then down the Herman Heijermansweg to Brouwer's villas, the Vossius and Vrijburg, ending in the
  Beatrixpark.
- **Occupation walk on the Apollolaan and Gerrit van der Veenstraat:** Brouwer's villas (renamed street), Elte's
  house, Händelstraat 17, Hervormd Lyceum, the 1944 memorial at the Beethovenstraat, then west to the Gerrit van der
  Veen College and St Ignatius (apollo-a).
- **Rutgers and Theo Vos:** Stadionweg 38, the Bachplein, the Lutherkapel, the Beethovenstraat gable stones, the
  Minervalaan crossing (apollo-a) and the Rafael corner on the Apollolaan.
- **Three churches, three fates:** Lutherkapel (gym), Christian Science church (still in use), Vrijburg (still in
  use), with the Bachzaal (church on Sundays 1934–1966, now a nursery) on the way.
