## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **118 Bulgar Time is gone.** Google Street View from the same spot on the Ferdinand Bolstraat shows the
  cube on the roof in June 2008, June 2015 and June 2017, and no cube in June 2018, June 2019 and May 2024;
  a short stub of the slanted pipe is left on the roof edge above the stairwell window of nos. 179–193.
  The city's art register (Buitenkunst / Kunstwacht 522) still lists the work without a note, and
  nl.wikipedia ("De tijd in drie dimensies") speaks of it in the past tense. Why and by whom it was
  removed, and where the cube is now, I could not find; the body says only "around 2018 the cube was
  taken down". Against the gone-things bar: the trace on site is only the stub, plus the artist's grave
  at Zorgvlied. The story (competition of 1983, the fee dispute and the lone 4, the "lighthouse"
  complaints, the artist drowning weeks after it went up) is from Ons Amsterdam, March 2007.
- The scout's "turns six times a minute" is from the art register only; left out. "The number in his own
  name" (IV) is my observation, not a sourced motive; Ons Amsterdam says only that he left off nearly all
  numerals over the fee.
- **119 dates.** De Pijp Krant (2020) says the street was named in 1993; the council decision is of
  21 October 1991 and the opening with the fences was on 28 August 1992 (nl.wikipedia, citing NRC of
  29 August 1992 and the NIW of 4 September 1992). I used 1991 and 1992. The scout's "between Van
  Woustraat and Amsteldijk" is only the rough area: the street runs from the Van Ostadestraat to the
  Rustenburgerstraat.
- The wrong-gateway story rests on one interview (De Pijp Krant, December 2021); it is told as
  "according to one of the neighbourhood activists of those years" and the man is not named.
- **120 Hazes.** No named sculptor. Meertens Instituut: made in Hong Kong on Rachel Hazes's instructions,
  paid by fans and by her; it lists "Cees Vrisekoop" (a marketing bureau) under maker, which looks like
  the organiser and is left out. nl.wikipedia's list: "three anonymous Chinese sculptors after video
  images". Web pages naming Kees Verkade or Frank Rosen are unreliable. buitenbeeldinbeeld.nl could not
  be opened (blocked). The statue stands in the A-list market; the place is pitched on the making and
  the boy singer.
- **121 one bottle, not two.** The scout and the art register say both bottles were moved in 1996.
  nl.wikipedia, after Het Parool of 6 July 2020, says only the upright bottle survives; the Commons
  photos of July 2020 show one. The year of the move is uncertain (register: 1996; archive photos:
  between 1987 and 2002), so the text says "in the 1990s".
- **Dairy demolition**: 1977 (experimentelewoningbouw.nl) against "around 1980" (nl.wikipedia). No year
  in the body. The scout's "neighbours campaigned the dairy out" is not used; I only confirmed the
  squatting as a community centre.
- **124 title of Pica's work.** The art register heads it "Slipping Colors" and calls it "Sipping Colors"
  in its own text; nl.wikipedia copies "Sipping". No other source found, so the title is not in the body.
  Whether the hall lies before or after the ticket gates is not stated either.
- **156 Koco.** Whether the ammonia was used on purpose is disputed in the sources (De Jong: yes; one
  witness statement: a bottle knocked over); the body says so. Cahn and Kohn are named because the stone
  names them. "More than four hundred" for the raids of 22 and 23 February is NH Nieuws's figure (the
  usual number is 427). The fate of the inventory is given as the family's account. I did not check what
  is in the building today; the body says only where the stone is. Rank: Nice; Amazing is defensible.
- **157 the plate.** No date for the plate found. The "sister plates" on the Amstel brewery and the Wester
  Suikerraffinaderij are only in the 4en5mei text for Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal 178–180 and are given as
  such (the Amstel brewery is gone). 4en5mei's history paragraph mixes these works kitchens up with the
  public kitchens of the Hunger Winter; not used. Pin: the plate was located on Street View of August
  2024 at the west end of the Stadhouderskade wall and is about 15 m west of the art register's point;
  good to a few metres.

## Leads

- Where is the Bulgar Time cube? A question for the city's art collection (Buitenkunst Amsterdam). If it
  is in store or due back, the place changes from Gone to an Outlook.
- Other Bulgar clocks: Ons Amsterdam (2007) mentions one in café Eik en Linde, Plantage Middenlaan
  (Centrum); the Stedelijk holds Bulgar Time models 3 and 4. Viktor IV's grave at Zorgvlied (tarred
  post with his name) is a Look for line for the Zorgvlied place of round 1.
- Europaplein station: Van der Kaap's "I want a permanent wave" and the Greek 2 euro coin are a Look for
  line for the RAI place (item 132, slice r2-rivieren-a) as well.
- "Buurtcentrum De Melkfabriek" (squatted dairy, workshops, the white-car workshop): De Pijp Krant,
  "Improviseren en mensen op sleeptouw nemen", and the Staatsarchief at the IISG have more; belongs to a
  renewal-fight place if one is written.
- Jacob Israël de Haan elsewhere in the Pijp: the line on the Sarphatipark pump house is already in that
  place.
- The plate at Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal 178–180 (5,247,889 litres) is a candidate for Centrum.

## Route ideas

- **Occupation in the Pijp**: the Heineken plate (Stadhouderskade), Sarphatipark (renamed Bollandpark in
  1942), IJssalon Koco (Van Woustraat 149), then south over the Cahn and Kohn bridge to the Rijnstraat.
- **The Pijp that was nearly demolished**: the milk bottle and the underpass (Hercules Seghersstraat),
  Station De Pijp, the Jacob Israël de Haanstraat with its misplaced gateway, De Punt on the Gerard
  Doustraat.
- **Pijp oddities in half an hour**: Hazes on the Albert Cuyp, the duck hotel in the Sarphatipark, the
  milk bottle, the roof where Bulgar Time stood.
