end 5, Koningslaan park side 5, Prins Hendriklaan 7).

per address, with first residents and later uses from the population register and newspapers).

and Commons through category pages.

## A-list, parked

None. No record in this slice belongs to an A-list sight.

## Belongs to an existing place

None. The Vondelpark gates and bridges near the Koningslaan are not in this slice. Two facts that
could be added to the existing place "Vondelpark" from this slice's reading: the villas on the even
side of the Koningslaan were designed with their terraces and conservatories towards the park pond,
which lies lower than the street (city register texts 200505 and 200506); and Koningslaan 54 got a
"monumental" quay wall on the pond in 1935 (rce 505386).

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Toilet buildings, designer** (505846). The national register, Stadsherstel and nl.wikipedia name
  Jan de Meijer (De Meyer). The Museum Het Schip platform (items.amsterdamse-school.nl, object 1538)
  changed the attribution in 2026 to Pieter Marnette of Public Works, after research by a relative of
  Marnette. The body names no designer; the `architect` field says Public Works with De Meijer
  "according to the register"; `check: Facts` is set. Needs a decision on which name to show.
- **Toilet buildings, fill.** The register says they were filled with concrete; Stadsherstel and
  Het Schip say earth. Used earth (the owner's account; they were dug out again).
- **Toilet buildings, current tenants.** Taken from the two Stadsherstel pages as read in November
  2026 ("Kapsalon & visagie" at no. 7, "Kappersstudio" at no. 8). The casting agency named in older
  press is not mentioned there, so it is left out. The entrepreneur who started the rescue is not
  named in the text (she is named in the press; easy to add if wanted).
- **Valeriusplein 42-44** (505490). nl.wikipedia calls it staff or director's housing of the Lyceum;
  the register says it was built for J. Vegersteen jr. (a builder) and only notes "ensemble value"
  with the rector's house. The text calls it "a double house in the same spirit" and makes no claim
  about the school. It is grouped with the Lyceum because of the register's ensemble remark.
- **Lyceum pin.** Placed on the gateway (52.351314, 4.864603, the nl.wikipedia coordinate), not on
  the register point of 504650 (52.351213, 4.865166), which lies in the east wing.
- **Lyceum, "first".** "Amsterdam's first lyceum (1917)" follows amsterdamopdekaart.nl literally.
  The register says only that it is the second purpose-built lyceum building in the Netherlands;
  that claim is not used.
- **Koninginneweg 29, architects.** The register names A.L. van Gendt with "P.H. van Gendt" taking
  part; the slice file and the stone on the building name A.L. van Gendt and J.G. van Gendt. Used
  the stone. The horse-head scrolls are from the register; a horse's head in the left gable top
  shown on the 1893 photos is gone (not mentioned in the text).
- **Koningslaan 32-38 or 32-36** (200506). The slice file says 32-38; the register text and
  amsterdamopdekaart.nl say 32-36 (three houses: 32, 34, 36). Used 32–36. Build year: register text
  1912 (design), amsterdamopdekaart 1913. Used 1912.
- **Koningslaan 2** (505205). Slice file and amsterdamopdekaart: 1894, J.W.F. Hartkamp jr. (tender
  in Het Nieuws van den Dag, 4 December 1893). National register: "about 1885", no architect. No
  year is given in the body; the ensemble's `built` range starts at 1889 (Quisisana's dated dormer).
- **Koningslaan 14-16.** Slice file gives 1904 and 1905; the register says 1904 for both. Used 1904.
  The client (a coffee broker and 1908 Olympic trap shooter) is described but not named.
- **Koningslaan 26-30.** Slice file says 1907; the register text says 1901–1902. Used the register.
- **Koninginneweg 33-35.** Slice file names J.H. Lesmeister; the register text names T.D. Sijsling
  (amsterdamopdekaart: Sijsling was also an estate agent and may have hired Lesmeister). No
  architect is named for these houses in the text or the attribute.
- **Koninginneweg 42-48** (222044). No register text at all. One Look for line from
  amsterdamopdekaart.nl (half-timbered gables); client and architect only from the slice file.
- **Koninginneweg 18 and 20.** Which half is "left" was not settled, so the text says "the other
  half". The stone with 1907 and HPB is described by amsterdamopdekaart.nl as sitting under the
  first-stone inscription; its exact place on the front was not checked.
- **VU laboratory, dates.** Register: built 1930–1933, raised 1951–1952. nl.wikipedia on the statue
  says the floors were added in 1954. Used the register. Listing year: slice file 1993,
  amsterdamopdekaart 1994; the body says only that listing saved it, and `listed` is left out
  because the two records differ (1993 and 1996).
- **Physiological laboratory, current use.** amsterdamopdekaart.nl (2021) says a branch of a clinic
  chain since 2011; the chain's own site gave no usable page. The body says "offices and later a
  clinic" and no more.
- **Dufayschool client.** The slice file gives "Christelijke School Vereeniging Aeneas Mackay" as
  client; the tile panel on the building and the register text both say it was a public (municipal)
  school. Left out. amsterdamopdekaart's "first second-class school open to both boys and girls" is
  not used as a first; the text says only that it was mixed.
- **Westhove, "first flats".** The register calls it "one of the first apartment buildings in the
  Netherlands". It is the last of Warners's series on this street (Huize Loma, 1914 by
  nl.wikipedia and 1913 by amsterdamopdekaart, is the one usually called first), so the text pitches
  it as one of the series. Used 1914.
- **Prins Hendriklaan 27-29.** The register text dates the hall to 1924–1925 and credits the banker
  Sternberg; amsterdamopdekaart dates the wing to 1923–1924, commissioned by a company at his
  office address. The text says the wing "was added just before he moved in". Sternberg is named
  because the register names him; the first owner of no. 29 and the bankrupt tenant of no. 15 are
  not named. Current use (a fashion company, per amsterdamopdekaart 2024) is not in the text.
- **Wartime at Koningslaan 58-60** (Gestapo and military intelligence offices, the
  Devisenschutzkommando) is outside this slice's records (those houses are not listed) and is not
  used; see Leads.
- **Dutch texts** were written from the Dutch sources, not translated; no sentences were lifted.

## Leads

- **Gedenksteen Comenius**, in the middle pavement of the Valeriusplein in front of the Lyceum
  gateway. A foundation stone from Uherský Brod, unveiled 15 November 1920 with President Masaryk's
  backing, for a Comenius monument that was never built (rejected design by Jan Štursa, a second
  attempt with Hildo Krop and Herman Baanders, then the Cold War). So it is a first and last stone.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedenksteen_Comenius
- **"Verticale compositie"** by Henk Zweerus (1960), cast concrete, in the middle of the
  Valeriusplein garden; his first use of poured concrete in a mould, pendant of the "Horizontale
  compositie" in the Leidsebosje; in Het Parool's Blikvangers column in 2026.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verticale_compositie
- **Koningslaan 58-60** (not listed): villa of Eduard Gerzon of the fashion firm; in the war no. 60
  housed offices of German military intelligence and the Gestapo, no. 58 the Devisenschutzkommando;
  after the war the Mennonite Central Committee. For sale in 2024–2025 for 16 to 19 million euros.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Koningslaan/58-60
- **Koningslaan 42-44** (not listed): built for the director of the Hajenius cigar shop; British
  consulate-general from 1982 to 2020. https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Koningslaan/42-44
- **Koningslaan 66-70** (not listed; may be in willemspark-a): G.J. Rutgers, 1920, goat heads and
  snakes carved on the middle house. https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Koningslaan/66-70
- **Jan van Goyenkade 1-2** (not listed): home of a Bonneterie director who in 1942 bought his
  family's way out to Portugal with his collection of old masters (among them a Jan van Goyen);
  after the war the Jan van Goyen clinic. No. 2 was the home of a former Rijksmuseum director.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Jan_van_Goyenkade/1-2
- **Valeriusterras** (south of the square, not in this slice): raised "street" with a 1925
  apartment block by G.J. Rutgers, with a figure and stair rails by Hildo Krop.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriusplein
- **Valeriuskliniek** (gone, demolished 2016–2017): the psychiatric clinic of the Vrije
  Universiteit on the east side of the square; its 5 by 13 metre stained-glass window by Matthieu
  Wiegman was removed and did not return. Now the building "Valerius".
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Valeriusplein/Valeriuskliniek
- **De Steenhoek**, De Lairessestraat 130-132 (not listed): Warners's own home from 1922; a
  resident's story that the occupier had the gable top removed. Restored 2019–2021.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/De_Lairessestraat/De_Steenhoek
- **Lutherse Diakonessen Inrichting**, Koninginneweg 1-3 (not in this slice): deaconess hospital
  of 1898 with chapel, district office 1990–2010, flats since 2014.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Koninginneweg/Lutherse_Diakonessen-Ziekenhuis

## Route ideas

- **Villa park loop** (about 1.5 km): tram depot (Koninginneweg 29) → Koninginneweg 8, 18 and 20 →
  Koningslaan 2 to 16 → the Mendes da Costa monkeys at 32–36 → Koningslaan 50–56 and the Vondelpark
  bridge at the Emmalaan for the garden sides → back along the Prins Hendriklaan (27–29, 15–19,
  3–5, 1).
- **Valeriusplein and the labs** (about 800 m): toilet buildings → along the garden to the bench →
  VU laboratories on the corner → Westhove gates → Lyceum gateway, through it to the canal side for
  the second motto → back by the De Lairessestraat block of H.L. de Jong → coffee at the Dufayhuis.
- The two loops join at the Koninginneweg and Valeriusplein tram stop (tram 2), and could be one
  "Willemspark east" walk. The sibling slice willemspark-a continues west from the Emmalaan.
- Theme threads across slices: F.A. Warners's flats along the De Lairessestraat (Huize Loma,
  Zonnehoek, Zuidwijck, Steenhoek, Westhove); the Vrije Universiteit in Zuid before Buitenveldert
  (labs, Koningslaan 31–33, Prins Hendriklaan 27–29, the Valeriuskliniek site).
