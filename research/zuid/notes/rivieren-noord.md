## A-list, parked

None. No record in this slice belongs to an A-list sight. Anne Frank's name is attached to three
places here (the Merwedeplein, her school, her sister's school), but the A-list sight is the
Anne Frank House on the Prinsengracht in Centrum, not these.

## Belongs to an existing place

None. Nothing in this slice touches the Vondelpark or another place already on the map.

Possible overlaps with sibling slices, to settle when the round is assembled:

- **Kinderbrug** (527785, 527787) is part of the national complex 527783 around De Kom and the
  Muzenplein. The other parts (Hildo Kropbrug, the quay with the nine children) are not in this
  slice; if `apollo-a`/`apollo-b` draft a Muzenplein place, the two should point at each other
  or be merged. My text mentions the nine children only as a Look for line.
- **Vrijheidslaan south side** (Kramer and Marnette, municipal monuments at nos. 9-77) is not in
  this slice (presumably in `rijnbuurt`). My De Klerk place only names it as the thing to compare with.
- **Wolkenkrabber** and **Merwedeplein**: I split the national record (the tower) from the
  municipal record of the whole Hilwis complex, so that the square and the Frank story get their
  own pin at no. 37. They could be merged into one place if two pins 150 m apart feel like too much.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Lekstraat synagogue, main hall.** Sources agree on the Resistance Museum from 1985 and an
  auction house afterwards, but differ on the year the museum left (1999 in nl.wikipedia and the
  national register context, 1998 on joodsamsterdam.nl; I used 1999). The auction house (AAG)
  no longer gives a street address on its site, so the present user of the hall is unconfirmed:
  `check: Facts`. The body only says what is confirmed.
- **Lekstraat synagogue, design year.** nl.wikipedia says the design dates from 1932; the
  national register says a competition-winning design, built 1936-1937. I kept to the register and
  left the 1932 date out. Elzas's birth year is 1907 or 1908 depending on the source: left out.
  The remark that Elzas was inspired by a Yiddish novel is only on Wikipedia: left out.
- **Anne Frankschool, year of renaming.** The city register says 1954; the school itself and
  nl.wikipedia say 1957 (ten years after the diary). I used 1957. joodsamsterdam.nl says 1983,
  which is the year of the painted front.
- **Anne Frankschool, numbers.** 151 Jewish pupils had to leave in 1941 (nl.wikipedia); the
  plaque is for 130 children (Wikipedia) or 129 besides Anne (school site). Text says "about
  150" and "some 130". Private people connected with the plaque are not named.
- **Jekerschool names.** The register calls the two schools Jekerschool and Michiel de
  Klerkschool, "later together the Vondelschool"; the list of October 1941 already calls no. 84
  the Vondelschool. I wrote "the Jekerschool (later renamed the Vondelschool)". The current user
  of the building is not confirmed; the register only says "various functions since 1995".
- **Substation Borssenburgplein** (526709): the slice record gives G.J. Rutgers and 1930; the
  national register text says Public Works, 1924. I followed the register text.
- **Dintelstraat school complex**: slice record 1926-1928; the Lansdorp biography inside two
  city texts says 1928-1929. I kept 1926-1928 (national register).
- **Zuiderschool**: listing header says 1930, the text says designed in 1933. I used 1933.
- **Lekstraat 35-37 / Winterdijkstraat 10** (222229): the register entry has no text.
  nl.wikipedia (Winterdijkstraat) describes the buildings but says they were *not* listed and
  numbers the second one 8-10; the city register lists them since 2014. I followed the register
  for the status and Wikipedia for the description. `check: Facts` for the current school and
  the state of the glass fronts. Who Prof. H.G. Gunning was is not explained in any source I
  could reach, so he is only named.
- **Meerhuizenplein 4**: the register text says the last school left "this summer" without a
  year (listing 2012). Current use unknown: `check: Facts`. The art inside is described as closed.
- **Amstelhof petrol station**: described in the listing of 2014; I could not confirm it still
  stands or operates: `check: Still there?`.
- **De Klerk blocks**: the national register entries are one sentence each. Everything beyond
  the stepped balconies (brick colour, corner treatment) was left out because I could not source
  it; the Look for list is therefore thin on detail and would gain from a site visit.
- **Barbiersbrug kiosks**: built as shops in 1928; photos of 2019 show them in use, but I did
  not state what is in them today.
- **Wolkenkrabber residents**: the city register names W.F. Hermans as a resident, Wikipedia's
  list does not; I left him and his novel out of the text. The radio post
  comes from the register and from nl.wikipedia (Gerben Sonderman), where one sentence about the
  plane being visible carries a "source?" tag; I did not repeat that sentence.
- **Stolpersteine at Merwedeplein 37**: nl.wikipedia says there are some for the Frank family;
  I could not confirm it and left it out.
- **Dutch texts** were written fresh from the Dutch sources (no lifted sentences). Short quoted
  phrases ('lieve Hans- en Grietje-architectuur', 'bedelactie', the titles of Krop's works, the
  Bible verse) are quotations.
- **Length**: bodies run to roughly 290-380 words including the Look for list, above the 150-250
  the brief asks for. The four longest (Haringvlietstraat, Dintelstraat schools, parkway fronts,
  Nereus) are the first to trim.
- **Tools**: the Wikimedia APIs (nl.wikipedia api.php, Commons api.php) returned "too many
  requests" throughout, so Wikipedia was read through `index.php?action=raw` and Commons files were
  checked one by one the same way. No Commons category or search listing could be fetched, which
  is why several places have fewer photos than exist. Web search was not available through curl.

## Leads

Non-monument finds, for the scouts:

- **Statue of Anne Frank**, Merwedeplein (Jet Schepp, placed 2005; she looks back at no. 37 on
  6 July 1942). First version of 1994 stands in Purmerend. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeld_van_Anne_Frank_(Merwedeplein)
- **Statue of H.P. Berlage**, Victorieplein (Hildo Krop, unveiled 17 December 1966, Belgian
  bluestone; the plinth shows a draughtsman, hod carrier, sculptor, scaffolder and bricklayer;
  Berlage's grandson called it one of the ugliest spots in town on AT5 in 1994 because of the
  outsized hands and feet of a man of 1.60 m). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standbeeld_van_H.P._Berlage
- **Stalinlaan plaque**, near the Victorieplein (unveiled 4 November 2006, fifty years after the
  renaming). Exact spot to be found. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrijheidslaan_(Amsterdam)
- **Levensboom / 'De Hulk'**, south side of the Victorieplein (Tjeerd Campman, placed
  anonymously in 2001, blue, for a time green). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorieplein_(Amsterdam)
- **Former school at Victorieplein 11** (1929, Public Works) with animal sculptures by Anton
  Rädecker. Not in my records. Same source.
- **Victorieplein 8-24**: block by J.F. Staal (1926) with a hall for the 'Vrij-Religieuse
  Tempel' behind, a failure turned into a garage for 200 cars in 1931. Same source.
- **Delphi** at the former Daniël Willinkplein 1: restaurant, later a patisserie that in the war
  was open to Jews only and was visited by Anne Frank. Same source.
- **Razzia of 20 June 1943**: the grass of the Victorieplein was the assembly point; special
  trams took people to the Muiderpoort station. Is there a marker? Same source.
- **Herbert Nelson's cabaret**, Merwedeplein 23, and **Ben Ali Libi** (Michel Velleman),
  Merwedeplein 59: both are in my Merwedeplein text; a scout may want a separate pin if there is a
  plaque. http://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/pagina/plan-zuid-en-de-oorlog/illegaal-cabaret-merwedeplein-23/
- **Nereus op zeepaard**, bronze by Nic Jonk (about 1970) beside the boathouse, Amsteldijk.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nereus_op_zeepaard
- **King Kong** by Street Art Frankey (2017) on Ben Guntenaar's *Verschuivingen* (1975), near
  Churchilllaan 223; also the **Wilhelmina Drucker monument** (Gerrit van der Veen, 1939) at the
  Waalstraat, *Two frames* (Shlomo Koren, 1982) and the Gandhi statue (Karel Gomes, 1990) on the
  same avenue. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill-laan_(Amsterdam)
- **Four Freedoms planters**, corner Rooseveltlaan and Maasstraat (2018).
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooseveltlaan_(Amsterdam)
- **Amsterdam School junction box** by P.L. Marnette against the side wall of Berkelstraat 21-23.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJselstraat_2-8
- **Dairy shop interior**, Borssenburgstraat 33: the city register (about 2012) noted an intact
  old shop interior with display cases, cold store and tiling, visible from the street. Worth a
  look; I left it out of the text because it is unconfirmed today.
- **Israël Querido** was photographed in his study at Rijnstraat 24 in 1927 (press photo listed
  on geheugenvanplanzuid.nl; the page has only the caption). If he lived there it is a hook for
  the Rijnstraat place. http://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/pagina/architectuur/historische-media/1927-18-mei-werkkamer-querido-in-de-rijnstraat-24/
- **Borssenburgschool**, Borssenburgplein 13: Jewish School no. 12 in 1941. Not a monument in
  my records. https://www.joodsamsterdam.nl/joodse-scholen-tijdens-de-oorlog/
- **Amsterdamse Grafische School**, Dintelstraat 15 (Heineke and Kuipers, 1933), and the
  **tram depot Lekstraat** (1927-1929): both near my places, neither in my records.
- **Book**: Rian Verhoeven, *Anne Frank was niet alleen. Het Merwedeplein 1933-1945* (2019),
  the standard work for the square.
- **Berlagewandeling**: an art route through the neighbourhood since September 2025 that takes in
  the Vrijheidslaan, the Victorieplein and the Amstelkade (mentioned on three Wikipedia pages).

## Route ideas

- **Plan Zuid in an hour, east to west** (about 2.5 km): Berlagebrug, Nereus boathouse,
  P.L. Kramerbrug, Amstelkade and Holendrechtstraat (Kropholler), the hidden squares, back to the
  Vrijheidslaan for De Klerk, Kruyswijk's arcades, De Wolkenkrabber, Merwedeplein, then the
  Churchilllaan to the Barbiersbrug or on to the Kinderbrug.
- **Jewish Rivierenbuurt** (about 2 km): Merwedeplein 37, Victorieplein (raid of 20 June 1943),
  Jekerschool, Dintelstraat school complex, 6e Montessorischool Anne Frank, Lekstraat synagogue.
  To be combined with what the scouts find (memorials, Stolpersteine) and with the `rijnbuurt` slice.
- **Kramer and Krop along the water**: Kramerbrug, the Rijnstraat gate, Barbiersbrug, Kinderbrug
  and the children of the Muzenplein: the whole south bank of the Amstelkanaal by bike or on foot.
- **Women architects**: Holendrechtstraat (Margaret Kropholler, 1921), the Wolkenkrabber (her
  chutes and kitchens, 1932) and Haringvlietstraat 12-14 (Koos Pot-Keegstra, 1955).
- **Schools as public art**: Meerhuizenplein 4, Jekerschool, Dintelstraat complex, Zuiderschool,
  Anne Frankschool and the 1954 pair on the Lekstraat: thirty years of school building in one walk.
