## A-list, parked

Heineken brewery (Heineken Experience), Stadhouderskade 78, complex 527808. No draft.

- 527809: brew house (1911–1913), with the tile frieze "Heineken's Bierbrouwerij Maatschappij".
- 527810: malt silo (1925, B.J. Ouëndag), with three reliefs by Lambertus Zijl on the Stadhouderskade
  (barley and hops, a haystack for "De Hooybergh", a brewing tub with tools).
- 527811: works, storage and cooling building (1933–1934, B.J. and W.B. Ouëndag), corner of the Ferdinand
  Bolstraat, with the clock tower.

For `A-LIST.md`: these three numbers go in the Heineken row. The Zijl reliefs are street-side and most
visitors walk past them; they could be a small place of their own later ("a statue with a story" rule), but
they are part of the A-list record, so no draft here.

The Albert Cuyp market itself is not a monument. Three listed buildings on the street have their own place
or are part of one: the Buiten-Amstelkerk (no. 182), the Moppes diamond works (no. 2-6) and school T
(no. 241, in the schools ensemble). The back door of the pawn bank is Albert Cuypstraat 181.

## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Carel Willink's address.** The city register text for Stadhouderskade 47-49 / Ruysdaelkade 3-5 (222078)
  says Willink lived "in one of the dwellings" of that block. nl.wikipedia (Ruysdaelkade 11-25, Carel
  Willinkplantsoen) puts him at Ruysdaelkade 15, and the city listed no. 15 separately in 2006 (200556, no
  text). I followed no. 15 and pinned the place there. The first-stone plaque is attributed to no. 15 by the
  Commons file name; I did not see where exactly it sits.
- **Willink bust.** Unveiled in the Carel Willinkplantsoen on 3 March 2000. The park is being turned into
  the Rijksmuseum's sculpture garden; I could not confirm the bust is in place. Flagged `check: Still there?`.
- **Rioolgemaal F / Rijksmuseum sculpture garden.** Plan presented in early 2026 (NOS 13 January, Stadsherstel
  3 March), opening "later in 2026". Het Parool reported objections to tree felling on 7 September 2026.
  I found no report of an opening as of 24 November 2026 and rijksmuseum.nl pages could not be fetched, so
  the text says the opening "was announced for late 2026" and `outlook` is set. Needs a look, and a rewrite
  (and probably a higher rank) once it is open. Year of the building: Stadsherstel says designed 1922, built
  1924; the register says the approved design is of 1923; the stone on the corner building says 1925; in use
  1925 to 1987. Attribute says 1924–1925. The Frankey intervention of 2019/2020 (numbers painted between
  "19" and "25") is left out: no recent evidence it is still there.
- **Buiten-Amstelkerk, current use.** Supermarket after 1968, restaurant De Engel, restaurant Bazar, then
  Brouwerij De Engel (evicted July 2024, bankrupt September 2024, Het Parool headlines). Nothing found on a
  new tenant. Flagged `check: Facts`. The register dates the restaurant plan to 2000–2002, nl.wikipedia puts
  De Engel in the 1990s; I gave no dates for the restaurants. The register calls it the fifth church of the
  Dolerenden, nl.wikipedia "the last"; both hold. Whether the angel figure placed by De Engel is still on
  the front is unknown, so it is not in the text.
- **Spijker factory, building date.** Register: built about 1885 as the Spijker coach works (its year field
  says 1872). nl.wikipedia: built earlier for the coachbuilder W.H. van Eck en Zonen, who went bankrupt in
  1886, after which Spijker moved in. The text says Spijker "took over the works of a bankrupt competitor";
  `built` says c. 1885; flagged in `check_note`. Benz coachwork: register says from 1895, nl.wikipedia says
  the first Benz was bought in 1898; I gave no year. The day nursery De Gouden Koets is in the register text
  (2004–2009) and nl.wikipedia (2015); not confirmed for 2026.
- **Schools, current users.** Mosque at Govert Flinckstraat 286 (register and nl.wikipedia 2015, Milli Görüş
  according to the register), Herman Hertzberger's office and a dentist at Gerard Doustraat 220 (register,
  2004; left out of the text), sheet-music shop of Saul B. Groen at Ferdinand Bolstraat 8 (Groen died in
  2016; left out), Jacob van Campenschool (De Appel 2010–2012; Vastgoedmarkt headline of February 2019
  says Rituals rents a former school in the Pijp and that the city sold two buildings in the Pijp, articles
  behind a login). Only the boxing school is confirmed recent (Boksen.nl, October 2025). Flagged
  `check: Facts`.
- **Ferdinand Bolstraat 8.** The register does not know what it was built as ("possibly an early fire
  post") and dates it about 1875–1880 with architect unknown; nl.wikipedia, from the specification drawing,
  says fire station F with a police station, by Publieke Werken, and the register text for school R calls it
  "the former police and fire post (1882)". I followed the latter two.
- **School fees.** The f 17.50 per quarter (1873) is the maximum across the classes, not the third-class
  fee; worded accordingly.
- **Stadhouderskade 115.** The register names it "former studio house" and gives S.W. van Rouendal as
  architect, without explanation; nl.wikipedia says the history is unclear (possibly De Greef) and that it
  housed the Rijkspostspaarbank from 1881 to 1901, then the civic guard, then a police station from 1912
  to the 1980s. I used only the bank and the police station and left the architect out.
- **Stadhouderskade 131-134 or 130-134.** Register: 131-134 (four numbers, thirteen bays); nl.wikipedia:
  130-134, five houses. Register followed. Roland Holst's birth at no. 132 and the town hall plan of 1940
  come from nl.wikipedia only.
- **Stadhouderskade 68.** The Alma mural is inside a private building (nl.wikipedia says a hotel; not
  confirmed). The text says it is not open and gives no year for the mural: the register only says it dates
  from the union's years (NBPO, 1924–1947).
- **Sarphatipark houses.** Most of the 26 records have no register text. Details come from the
  nl.wikipedia articles per house group, which rest on the building drawings. Spellings differ (register
  "Schroot" and "Armt"/"Amt", nl.wikipedia "Schoot"; register "Sluys Veer"); register spellings used.
  nl.wikipedia says a floor at nos. 35-37 cost "300 guilders a month" in 1889, which cannot be right; left
  out. It also says that before 1900 no. 1 held the brothel of "Vlaamse Jet", visited by writers of the
  Tachtigers generation: one unsourced line, left out (see Leads). The grouping differs slightly: the
  register has 47-49 and 51 as separate records, nl.wikipedia treats 47-51 as one design.
- **Sarphatipark pump house.** Year: register text says built 1884, its year field says 1881–1886. Text
  says 1884. The pump house record (222153) is folded into the park place, as is the monument (527847).
  The park itself is not a listed record.
- **Gerard Dou synagogue.** Name spelled Tesjoengas Israel as in the national register (nl.wikipedia:
  Tesjoe'at Israël). The family of the secretary who hid the silver is named on joodsamsterdam.nl; not
  named in the text. The old site gerarddou.org has been taken over by a gambling site: do not link it.
  I found no public visiting arrangement.
- **Zeehandelaar plaque.** The firm's name is on the plate, so it is in the text; the owner and his wife
  are not named. Their deaths at Sobibor on 4 June 1943 come from nl.wikipedia only; worth a check against
  Joods Monument before import.
- **Hemonylaan 22.** In the slice with status "in procedure" and no number or text (1884, N. van der
  Linden). Mentioned as the fifth house of the Hemony ensemble, without a number in `monument_number`.
  If the listing goes through, add the number.
- **Tweede Jacob van Campenstraat 2-86.** No register text. "Built for the Walloon congregation" and the
  demolition fight come from nl.wikipedia only. It stands a little west of the Salerno block, on the other
  side of the street.
- **Kabouters.** The stories about the ball and the Van Ballegooijen pun are lore, and worded as such.
- **Length.** Bodies run to about 240–310 words including the "Look for" list, a little over the target.

## Leads

- **Lambertus Zijl reliefs on the Heineken silo**, Stadhouderskade 78 (street side): barley and hops, the
  haystack of De Hooybergh, a brewing tub. Source: national register 527810.
- **Nachtwacht** (2008), owl sculpture by Lisa Couwenbergh, head of the Tweede Jacob van Campenstraat at the
  Eerste van der Helststraat; memorial to the neighbourhood's fight against demolition. Mentioned in the
  workers' housing place. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachtwacht_(beeld)
- **Van Cuyp tot Dou** (2018), Hugo Kaagman's wall painting inside the bicycle garage at Gerard Doustraat
  156; now part of the pawn bank place. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cuyp_tot_Dou
- **Hugo Kaagman on the Hemonylaan** (north side, 21st century, one a copy of his Zebra house) and on the
  old electricity kiosk at **Hemonystraat 4** (2018). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemonylaan
- **Blauwe reiger** (2018), Adele Renault's three-storey heron on the Ruysdaelkade side of the Moppes
  building; in the Moppes place. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blauwe_reiger_(muurschildering)
- **Poem plaques**: a line by Jacob Israël de Haan on the Sarphatipark pump house ("Langs 't Sarphatipark
  reeën we ...", photo May 2021) and one by Frans Erens on Gerard Doustraat 220 ("De straten recht, eentonig
  recht", photo January 2025). Possibly part of a series of literary plaques in the Pijp.
- **Carel Willink bust** by Sylvia Willink (2000), Carel Willinkplantsoen; and the plantsoen itself as the
  future Don Quixote Pavilion and Garden of the Rijksmuseum (free sculpture garden: a place of its own once
  open). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carel_Willinkplantsoen
- **Former bathhouse of the Pijp**, Eerste Sweelinckstraat 10 (converted 2016–2017 into flats and a café),
  behind the Sweelinckschool; the plain counterpart of the Amstel-Badhuis.
- **André Hazes statue**, corner of the Eerste Sweelinckstraat and the Albert Cuypstraat.
- **Ferdinand Bolstraat 168** (1903), one of the few Jugendstil fronts in the Pijp, white glazed brick,
  with two tile panels (Youth and Old Age) in the porch. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Bolstraat
- **"Vlaamse Jet"**, a brothel at Sarphatipark 1 before 1900 said to have been visited by the Tachtigers
  (nl.wikipedia, Sarphatipark 1-3, unsourced). Needs a real source before use.
- **Spyker's second factory** on the Amsteldijk and Trompenburgstraat (1898–1899, with workers' housing):
  probably in the pijp-zuid slice.
- **Peter Alma mural** in the stairwell of Stadhouderskade 68: inside, not visitable; worth asking the
  owner whether it can be seen on Open Monumentendag.
- **Liefdesbruggetje** and the helophyte filter in the north-east corner of the Sarphatipark.

## Route ideas

- **Singelgracht side of the Pijp** (about 2.5 km, west to east): Rioolgemaal F and Willink's corner,
  Stadhouderskade 60 and 68, the Heineken reliefs (A-list, pass by), the old Rijksakademie, through the
  Sweelinckpassage to the Spijker works and the synagogue, back to Stadhouderskade 131-134 and 160, ending
  at the Amstel-Badhuis for coffee.
- **Class and money in the Pijp** (about 2 km): fire station F and the Jacob van Campenschool, the workers'
  blocks on the Tweede Jacob van Campenstraat, De Punt, the pawn bank (through to the Albert Cuyp), the
  Buiten-Amstelkerk, down the Eerste Sweelinckstraat past three schools, and out into the Sarphatipark and
  its north side. The story: who paid what, from the lommerd to the 550-guilder park flat.
- **Albert Cuyp end to end**: Moppes diamond works and the heron, the pawn bank's back door, the church,
  school T with the boxing club, then the Hemony corner and the Kabouters house at the Amstel.
- **Diamond Pijp** with other slices: Moppes works, the "little diamond shul", the Zeehandelaar plate
  (a former diamond cutter), and on to the Diamantbuurt in the pijp-zuid slice.
