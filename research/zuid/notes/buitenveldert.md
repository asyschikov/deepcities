## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Amstelrust, architect.** The national register says the present house of about 1724 was "built
  by Adriaan Dortsman", who died in 1682. nl.wikipedia says the first house was Dortsman's and was
  demolished or thoroughly renewed in 1724. The text follows Wikipedia ("the first house is credited
  to Dortsman") and the `architect` field is left empty. Scout B's "only house spared when the
  polder was buried in 1955" was not found in a source and is left out.
- **Riekermolen, sabbath pole.** JCK confirms a sjabbatpaal "near the Riekermolen on the Amstel,
  just above the Kalfjeslaan", but gives no exact spot; the place has `check: Location` for it. JCK
  says "most were destroyed, but one still stands here"; it does not say "the last", and
  nl.wikipedia also mentions a chain on a pier of the Utrechtsebrug, so the text avoids "last" and
  "only". Scout B's "restored 2008" was not found: 2008 is the year the new eruv started. The
  listing year 1970 comes from nl.wikipedia (the slice has none). The mill is an "achtkante
  binnenkruier" (cap turned from inside).
- **Sint-Augustinuskerk.** The end of services on 1 January 2026 is known only as an announcement
  (nl.wikipedia, citing the city's "Erfgoed van de Week" of October 2024, which blocks curl with a
  403; the parish site did not answer). Whether the church is in fact closed, and what happens to
  it, could not be confirmed: `check: Facts` and `outlook: Major change ahead`. Scout B's "the
  presbytery is being sold" was not confirmed and is left out. The register spells the sculptor
  "Marie Andriessen"; this is Mari Andriessen.
- **Parnassusweg 220, the bunker.** The 1971 emergency seat (stelling-amsterdam.nl) lay under the
  east side of the court complex, with the court towers of 1978 to 1990 built over it; those
  towers were demolished after 2015 for the new court. No source says whether the bunker survived,
  so it is left out of the text. Worth a question to the Hartwig Museum or the Rijksvastgoedbedrijf.
  The slice has no register text for this record; the story is from nl.wikipedia and zuidas.nl.
  The preview space "Proxy" (Parnassusweg 213) was announced for spring 2025; not confirmed as
  open, so not in the text. The slice calls the building "Gebouw Parnas"; the register architect
  line is "Verster, Dijkstra, Loerakker" (VDL), the design is Ben Loerakker's.
- **Kapel en Convict.** Dates differ: the register says 1960, nl.wikipedia first pile 1957 and
  opening about 1959, zuidas.nl "building of 1957". `built: 1957–1960`. Circus Odesa was "a few
  weeks" from opening on 31 July 2026 (coffee already served): `check: Facts` for whether and when
  it is open. The school buildings described at length in the register text were demolished in
  2013; only monastery and chapel are the monument.
- **Paviljoen Buitenveldert.** The city register text ends with the advice *not* to list the
  building (interior too much altered); it was listed anyway in 2018, after Heemschut's campaign.
  Current use (synagogue and Jewish cultural centre of the NIHS) is taken from the register text
  and nl.wikipedia, not checked against a 2026 source. "Rietveld's last church" was dropped from
  the title: he designed two, and no source uses "last".
- **Turmac building.** No register `use` that makes sense (the slice says "wonen"), no Commons
  photo of the building, and no recent source on the tenant: the text says "still an office" on
  the strength of the 2015 register text, with `check: Facts`. Two Nationaal Archief photos on
  Commons (11 May 1966) show Prince Bernhard opening "the building of the Turmac Tobacco Company";
  they do not say whether that is Amsterdam or Zevenaar, so the opening is not in the text.
- **Wiegman-Dobbelmann tomb.** The national register names the sculptor Pierre Elysée van den
  Bossche (1849–1921); nl.wikipedia says Emil Van den Bossche. The register is followed. The
  register calls the figure "the statue of Theresia" (the dead woman's name saint); Wikipedia says
  Saint Theresa of Ávila; the text says "Saint Theresa". The cemetery's start: 14 June 1836
  (nl.wikipedia) against "190 years in 2025" (zuidas.nl, so 1835): the text says "the 1830s".
  zuidas.nl calls it "Amsterdam's oldest cemetery"; not used, since older ones exist (the
  Portuguese and Ashkenazi cemeteries, for a start). The banker and his wife are not named in the
  text; Carl Romme is.
- **Beatrixpark.** The register says the park was "most probably" designed by Jakoba Mulder and
  notes that experts still argue about it; `architect` says "attributed". The paddling pool: the
  register says 1958, nl.wikipedia 1959 by Aldo van Eyck; no year or name in the text. Scout A's
  origin of the lions (from the railway bridges at the Centraal Station, here since 1972) could
  not be checked with curl (Kunstwacht is a JavaScript app), so the Look for line only says "two
  stone lions". The dog's story is confirmed by nl.wikipedia "Spelende hond" (unveiled 12 May
  1979); Scout A's "5,000 guilders, fifty people, Willy Walden" was not found there and is left out.
- **Warnersblokken.** Wikidata has the labels of two blocks swapped (the photo "Dirk Schäferstraat
  1-27 Amsterdam.jpg" hangs on the Johannes Worpstraat record and the reverse). Both photos show
  the blocks, checked by eye. Only the register text of Johannes Worpstraat 1-27 says the panels
  were replaced by foil; the others still describe "coloured panels", so the text says "at least
  one block". Scout A's "one of the first built projects of the Liga Nieuw Beelden" is not in the
  register and is left out.
- **Thomaskerk.** The date of Saura's window: nl.wikipedia says 1967, Scout A 1966; no year in the
  text. Scout A's "his only glass appliqué window" and "anti-clerical" were not in a source I could
  fetch and are left out. That the architect paid for the window himself is from nl.wikipedia.
- **Spinoza Lyceum.** "First secondary Dalton school building of Amsterdam" is the city register's
  claim and is attributed to it. The Muis wall painting is inside a working school and is not in
  the text.
- **Kletsmuurtjes.** "First time such objects were placed in a schoolyard" is from the valuation
  in the national listing decision and is attributed to it. Build years: the slice says 1963, the
  register text design 1960, nl.wikipedia first pile March 1961 and opening June 1963:
  `built: 1961–1963`.
- **Gerrit Rietveld Academie.** The slice gives 1959–1967; the register text says final design
  1957 and opening 1967, nl.wikipedia that building had only just begun in 1964:
  `built: design 1957; opened 1967`. That the graduation show is public is general knowledge, not
  from a fetched source.
- **Gijsbrecht van Aemstelpark.** The register text is one line; the story is from nl.wikipedia.
  The sculptures are only named in passing (see Leads).
- **Princesseflat ensemble.** 222303 and 222304 have no register text and 532211 one line; the
  facts are from nl.wikipedia (list of buildings on the Beethovenstraat, Cornelis Dopperkade, Mart
  Stam). Whether the brise-soleil side of Prinses Marijkestraat 5 faces the canal is not clear
  from the register ("rear front"), so the text says "at the back".
- **Andrieskerk.** No register text; nl.wikipedia and the congregation's site. Kept as a place of
  its own (Okay) because it has a story and stands alone; it could also be dropped to a line in a
  later Buitenveldert churches place.
- **Web search** through curl was blocked throughout (DuckDuckGo challenge, Bing junk), so current
  status rests on direct pages: zuidas.nl (2025 and 2026), nl.wikipedia, the registers.

## Leads

- **Escher's "Vogels en vissen"**, Dirk Schäferstraat 59 (villa by Lau Peters): tile relief of
  1960, cloisonné technique, made at De Porceleyne Fles. Confirmed by nl.wikipedia "Dirk
  Schäferstraat" (with a 2019 photo). Not a record in this slice: named in a Look for line of
  Warnersblokken, left for the next round as a place of its own (Scout A 137).
- **Sculptures of the Gijsbrecht van Aemstelpark** (Scout A 135): Volten's Buizenkolom (1968),
  DeWitt's Ruiter te paard, Niermeijer's The long hum (1969), Snoeck's Liggende figuren, De
  Boer-Lichtveld's Schakelobject 3 (1977), Rakier's war memorial (2014), Abbink's Levensboom
  (2015). List with sources in https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gijsbrecht_van_Aemstelpark. A place
  or a route of its own.
- **Monument Rozenoord** in the Amstelpark (Scout A 163): confirmed form: empty chairs on concrete
  plinths, one per identified victim, 106 since 2018 (Ram Katzir, 1 May 2015);
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstelpark. Own place, as the scout says.
- **Amstelpark gates and art** (Scout A 136): Glazen Huis (Van Gool and Oyevaar), Stadshoutpaviljoen
  (2014–2015), the NAP pole with flood levels; same Wikipedia article.
- **Beatrixpark**: Kwibuka monument (2023), "Your life is calling", "Future past glory" for Ko
  Mulder (2018), Trapman's Pinguïns and Braat's Zeemeermin (both placed during the occupation),
  bridge 497 (a sliding bridge over the Boerenwetering), Piet Kramer's bridge 430;
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrixpark_(Amsterdam).
- **Elena Engelsen carved the Beatrixpark dog in the stonemason's workshop of the Buitenveldert
  cemetery**, and later made the tiger at Oslo Central Station;
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelende_hond. A link between two places.
- **Rik Wouters's first grave** was at the R.K. Begraafplaats Buitenveldert before he was moved to
  Belgium (zuidas.nl, October 2025); a book on the cemetery by Wim Cappers was announced for 2026.
- **Love or generosity** (Nicole Eisenman) at the new court, Parnassusweg 280, and **Proxy**, the
  Hartwig preview space at Parnassusweg 213.
- **Emergency seat under the court** (Scout B 251): see Conflicts.
- **Willem Kesstraat 20**: anthroposophical villa by Jan van der Linden for a director of Ketjen,
  garden by Mien Ruys; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Kesstraat. Not listed.
- **W.J. Klein's other houses in Buitenveldert** (De Cuserstraat 9 and 17, Warmelo 3, Genhoes 8,
  Walborg 8, Bongart 3, Ouborg 9, Pekkendam 7, Herinkhave 7): from the register text of
  Daelenbroek 1.
- **Guillaume Bijl's "Feestelijke beeldenreeks"** and Femke Schaap's "Virtual fountains" (2022) on
  the Europaplein; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europaplein_(Amsterdam).
- **Goede Herderkerk and Pelgrimskerk**, the two other churches on the Van Boshuizenstraat (named
  in the Pinksterkerk register text), and the **Hortus Botanicus of the VU** at Van der
  Boechorststraat 8.

## Route ideas

- **Rietveld and friends in Buitenveldert West** (about 2.5 km): Paviljoen Buitenveldert, the
  Weldam patio houses, Weldam 10 and Herinkhave 19, through the west end of the Gijsbrecht van
  Aemstelpark to the Andrieskerk.
- **Irenebuurt modern** (about 2 km): Rietveld Academie, the cemetery, Parnassusweg 220, the
  Warnersblokken and Escher's relief, Spinoza Lyceum, Prinses Marijkestraat 5, Thomaskerk,
  Princesseflat, Kapel en Convict, Beatrixpark.
- **Floriade 1972** (about 4 km): Europahal and Het Signaal, Beatrixpark (Artsenijhof, stone
  circle), over the motorway to the Amstelpark, out at the Amsteldijk past Amstelrust to the
  Riekermolen.
- **The southern edge by bike**: Riekermolen, Europaboulevard 55, Kalfjeslaan and 't Kleine
  Loopveld past Daelenbroek 1 and the back of the Christelijk Lyceum, Pinksterkerk, and on to the
  Sint-Augustinuskerk.
- **Duintjer in Buitenveldert**: Christelijk Lyceum, Pinksterkerk, Europaboulevard 55.
