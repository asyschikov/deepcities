## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Raid of 26 November 1944, number of dead.** 4en5mei.nl (relief page): 54 civilians and four Germans, more than
  forty houses. 4en5mei.nl (2007 monument page) and nl.wikipedia Gerrit van der Veenstraat: 69 dead of whom four SD
  men, thirty houses. nl.wikipedia Apollobuurt: 56 dead. nl.wikipedia Jan van Eijckstraat 47: more than 50 civilians.
  The texts say "more than fifty residents" and "dozens of houses", which every source supports.
- **Was the Zentralstelle building hit by mistake?** The city register text for 222130 and 4en5mei.nl say the school on
  the square was hit in error and the girls' school was the target; nl.wikipedia and the 2007 monument page say both
  buildings were attacked. The texts say the Typhoons attacked both buildings and do not call either hit a mistake.
- **Time of the raid.** 4en5mei.nl: 14.00; nl.wikipedia Apollobuurt: morning. Left out.
- **Where the SD went afterwards** (hotel Apollofirst): marked "source?" on nl.wikipedia. Left out.
- **Start date of the Zentralstelle at the square.** nl.wikipedia says "from 1942"; 4en5mei.nl says the school was an
  assembly point in the first occupation years until summer 1942. No year is given in the text.
- **Meaning of the Rietbroek relief (1957).** nl.wikipedia Jan van Eijckstraat 47: for the victims of the raid;
  4en5mei.nl: recalls the Zentralstelle; nl.wikipedia Oorlogsmonument: prisoners held in the cellars. The text calls it
  "a war memorial" only.
- **Bunker under the St Ignatius playground.** nl.wikipedia: command bunker of 1953; Amsterdam op de kaart: built with
  the school, second bunker in the 1960s. No year in the text. That it is now the bicycle cellar comes from the
  register text of 2011.
- **Minervalaan portraits.** National register 527781 spells the third man "Jac Vorrink"; Amsterdam op de kaart has the
  architect P. Vorkink, which fits a committee of architects and officials. Used Vorkink. The register attributes all
  work on the south-west block jointly; Amsterdam op de kaart names the four men there (De Graaf, Tjaden, Keppler,
  Abrahams). nl.wikipedia Michelangelostraat gives the corner figure at no. 29 to Rädecker and at no. 27 to Kaas, the
  register gives both to Kaas: no maker named for the Michelangelo figures in the text. nl.wikipedia Minervalaan also
  names Theo Vos as a third sculptor: left out.
- **Shops on the Minervalaan.** nl.wikipedia says the shops "never came"; the register describes shop dwellings and
  shopfronts in the arcades. The text says the arcades were built for a shopping street and that shoppers went to the
  Beethovenstraat. Worth a look on site to say what is in the arcades now (no `check` set: nothing in the text depends
  on it).
- **Montessori school, year of listing.** Slice: 1999; nl.wikipedia: 23 June 1991. Kept the slice value.
- **Leonardostraat 2-10 e.o. (200049).** The slice has no text. Amsterdam op de kaart describes the same block as part
  of "Olympiaplein 11-29, Gerrit van der Veenstraat 126-134, Leonardostraat 2-10, Titiaanstraat 29-35" under monument
  number 222150, which is not in this slice. The contour of 200049 covers the eastern (Leonardostraat) strip. Check
  whether 222150 sits in another slice (stadion?) and whether the two should share a place.
- **Roodenburgh and the Ajax stadium.** The register text for 222004 says Jordanus Roodenburgh's first large job was
  the Ajax stadium (1920). Ajax's architect was Daan Roodenburgh; not verified, left out.
- **Apollo Schools, year.** The registers give no year; Amsterdam op de kaart says ready in 1983, nl.wikipedia lists
  "1983". `built` is 1983. Whether the schools stand on the site of the burnt Muzieklyceum (Albert Hahnplantsoen 2)
  could not be confirmed, so it is not in the text (see Leads).
- **Albrecht Dürerstraat 36, current use.** BAG says education function, in use; which school uses it was not found.
  The text says "still used for education".
- **Titiaanstraat 24, current use.** BAG says assembly function (bijeenkomstfunctie); the user was not found. The text
  says "no longer a school". The name board over the door is read from a Commons photo.
- **Private names.** The client of Albert Hahnplantsoen 25-27 (a private person in the register) is not named.
  B.A. Sanders (La Pergola) is named: his family stone is on the facade and he is in nl.wikipedia.
- **Sources not reachable with curl:** buitenbeeldinbeeld.nl (Cloudflare block), arcam.nl drive-in page (redirects to
  the home page), general web search. Wikipedia and Commons were heavily rate limited; everything was fetched in the
  end.
- **Dutch** is written fresh everywhere; the short Boeyinga and Maaskant quotes are period quotations taken from
  Amsterdam op de kaart.

## Leads

- **Monument voor Gedeporteerde Joden** (Hilde Meeus and Celine Wouters, unveiled 11 September 2007): 65 concrete tiles
  in the pavement of the Adama van Scheltemaplein, each with a remembered version of one history-book text. Now a Look
  for line in the St Ignatius place; strong enough for its own place.
  https://www.4en5mei.nl/oorlogsmonumenten/monumenten_zoeken/oorlogsmonument/3595
- **Gedenkteken Adama van Scheltema** (Tjipke Visser and Jan Buis, 1933): black column with praying hands and a basin,
  same square. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedenkteken_Adama_van_Scheltema
- **Aart Rietbroek's relief** (1957) on the Rubensstraat wall of the St Ignatius Gymnasium.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oorlogsmonument_Jan_van_Eijckstraat_47
- **"Man in a fish"**, anonymous bronze in the St Ignatius schoolyard, Rubensstraat side: maker, date and subject all
  unknown. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adama_van_Scheltemaplein
- **Plaque for Freek Ox and Ferdinand Ploeger** on the side wall of Albrecht Dürerstraat 28 (Gerrit van der Veenstraat
  side), and the memorial "Tijdloos is de stilte" in the same street.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrit_van_der_Veenstraat
- **Muzieklyceum / Minervapaviljoen** (Dick Greiner, 1928, Albert Hahnplantsoen 2): concert hall for 500 with a pointed
  roof the city demanded so it would look like a villa, which spoiled the acoustics; candidates' chess tournament of
  1956; burnt out after nine fires, cleared in 1974. Gone, and nothing found on site.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minervapaviljoen
- **Albert Hahnplantsoen 14** (Rutgers, 1927, not listed): home of the banker and book collector Salomon Flörsheim,
  whose family survived Theresienstadt and came back to the villa. https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Albert_Hahnplantsoen/14
- **Dijsselhofplantsoen**: gable stones at no. 2 (birds of prey), no. 6 ("Zomer en winter" by Jaap Kaas, sleeping dog
  and cat by the drainpipes) and no. 14 (a stone of about 1670 from the Dwars Klooster). https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijsselhofplantsoen
- **Veroneseplantsoen**: Amsterdam School electricity kiosk by P.L. Marnette and an "igloo" climbing frame by Aldo van
  Eyck. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelostraat_(Amsterdam)
- **Michelangelostraat 36**: hiding address of Fritz Kahlenberg and Tonny van Renterghem, organisers of the photo group
  "De Ondergedoken Camera"; Stolpersteine at nos. 12, 43, 44, 45, 58 and 105 (same source).
- **Minervaplein 9-15 (Muzenhof)**: a forgotten brick or tile artwork of about 1956 in a porch that is now behind a
  front door; the neighbourhood paper asked for information in 2024. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minervaplein
- **Albrecht Dürerstraat 20**: corner building by Westerman with a sculpture by Theo Vos in the front.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrerstraat
- **Gedenkteken Prof. Dr. Dirk Durrer** (1986) in the south-east corner of the Minervaplein.

## Route ideas

- **Nieuwe Bouwen in one block** (15 minutes): Open Air School (Cliostraat 40), Hagedoornschool, drive-in houses,
  Montessori school, all within 150 metres, then north to the Hertzberger schools to see where the Montessori
  kindergarten went in 1983.
- **Euterpestraat 1940–1945**: Gerrit van der Veen College, the tiles and memorials on the Adama van Scheltemaplein,
  the St Ignatius relief, the Ox and Ploeger plaque, on to the Minervalaan (Rosa Manus, Hannie Schaft). Could join the
  Apollolaan places of slice apollo-b.
- **The Minervalaan axis**, north to south: the crossing with the committee portraits, Roodenburgh's walls, the
  Minervaplein, Zietsma's blocks, then west along the Stadionkade to the Parnassusweg gate and Zonnehof.
