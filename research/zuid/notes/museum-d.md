## Conflicts and uncertainties

- **Slice years versus register text.** I followed the register where it gives a date:
  Zuiderbad 1898 (slice 1898; nl.wikipedia says built 1897; Amsterdam op de kaart: tender September
  1897, opened July 1898). Boerhaave clinic and doctor's house: slice 1907, register "1908–11", first
  stone 2 July 1908 and opening 25 May 1909 (nl.wikipedia, Amsterdam op de kaart, the "1908"
  cartouche): I wrote 1908–1909. Hobbemastraat 14–16: register 1902, Amsterdam op de kaart 1904; I
  kept 1902. Lizzy Cottage: register 1902, Amsterdam op de kaart 1903, nl.wikipedia 1901/1902.
  De Amstel: slice 1953–1955, register "delivered in 1954"; attribute says 1953–1954, body says
  finished 1954. Teniersstraat 6 / Vermeerstraat 35–45: slice 1911–1912; nl.wikipedia Teniersstraat
  says "circa 1916" for Teniersstraat 6, which I did not follow.
- **Stadhouderskade 40–41, date and architect.** Slice 1883, register "about 1885", Amsterdam op de
  kaart "1879 or 1880" from the population register (first residents 1880–1881), nl.wikipedia
  "1878–1879, architect Jean Servais" without a source. I wrote "about 1880" and left the architect
  out. The slice has no architect either.
- **Johannes Vermeerstraat 35–45, architect.** The slice and register say B.H. and H. Koolhaas;
  Amsterdam op de kaart says P. van Dijk; nl.wikipedia (Teniersstraat) says "B.H. Koolhaas en B.
  Koolhaas". The body says "the register names the brothers Koolhaas" (that they were brothers is my
  reading of the shared surname and firm; drop "brothers" if unsure).
- **Johannes Vermeerplein 1–5, which Kuipers.** The city register text identifies the partner of
  D. Heineke as T.E. (Tjeerd) Kuipers and spends a paragraph on him; the slice field and Amsterdam op
  de kaart say E.J. Kuipers (the firm Heineke & Kuipers). I used "Heineke and Kuipers" in the body and
  E.J. in the attribute. The register also says "three" and "four" houses in the same text; there are
  four addresses (plein 1, 3, 5 and Vermeerstraat 55).
- **Ignatiuscollege dates.** Extension of the fathers' house: register 1934, Amsterdam op de kaart
  1938; left out. Move to the Montessori building: register "three years after 1973", nl.wikipedia
  and Amsterdam op de kaart 1979; I wrote 1979. The 1963 wing on the Pieter de Hoochstraat was
  demolished in 2021 (Amsterdam op de kaart) and is not protected; I did not describe the new build.
- **Pieter de Hoochstraat 80, current state.** Amsterdam op de kaart (state November 2025): empty,
  with a banner "for rent and for sale" since 2023. The register still calls it a day nursery. I
  wrote the banner into the body. Worth a look on site; if a buyer or plan is known, add an outlook.
- **Dreesmann's move.** nl.wikipedia says the family moved to the Diepenbrockstraat in 1939;
  Amsterdam op de kaart says "presumably 1949". Body says only "later moved elsewhere in Zuid".
- **Director's villa.** Home until 1945 (nl.wikipedia) or function ended 1949 (register, Amsterdam op
  de kaart). Body: successor of 1945 did not move in, offices since 1949.
- **Teekenschool.** nl.wikipedia: opened 1892, reorganised 1924. Amsterdam op de kaart: the teacher
  training institute came into the building in 1937. I left both years out. Water cellars: the
  register says "floating (originally secret) clean-water cellars for the Stelling van Amsterdam";
  Amsterdam op de kaart says rainwater cellars at the request of the Ministry of War. "Secret" is
  attributed to the register in the body.
- **Dordrecht tabernacle.** The nl.wikipedia article on the tabernacle says "17th century" in its
  first line and then tells of the note dating the gift to 1789; the church article says 1789. I used
  1789 as the year of the gift and gave no date of making.
- **Het Nieuwe Huis, number of flats.** Register and nl.wikipedia: 188; Platform Wendingen: 169.
  I used 188. Founder: "Kruis" (nl.wikipedia, plan of 1914) or "A.E.M.C. Kruys" (Wendingen,
  co-operative founded 1918); I used Kruys and no year. The claim "only one-kitchen house in the
  Netherlands" is Wendingen's and is attributed in the body; the register says "first project of
  collective living in Amsterdam". The title says neither first nor only.
- **Zuiderbad.** amsterdam.nl blocks automated fetches, so I could not read the current timetable or
  prices; the body says to check the timetable and gives no hours. Cycling school closed: 1904
  (Amsterdam op de kaart, newspaper sources) or 1908 (register); body says "within a few years".
  "The largest indoor cycling school in the country" (nl.wikipedia) is unsourced and left out. The
  sand filter of 1912 "still in use" rests on nl.wikipedia only; it is in the summary. Rank Amazing is
  my proposal (a listed 1898 hall you can swim in for the price of a ticket).
- **Rijksmuseum garden pin.** No register address. I placed the pin in the south-west garden from
  the register's description of where the joined gates stand, and flagged `check: Location`. Opening
  hours of the garden are not in the text (nl.wikipedia: "open in the daytime"); the Rijksmuseum page
  I tried returned 404.
- **Lizzy Cottage friezes.** The register calls them terrazzo friezes; Amsterdam op de kaart calls
  them tile tableaux and the round one a "sectile" tableau. The body just says frieze. The picture
  gallery behind the blank wall on the Jan Luijkenstraat: nl.wikipedia says "would have been"; I
  wrote only that the villa had a picture gallery (Amsterdam op de kaart).
- **Asscher (Johannes Vermeerstraat 14).** The place is pitched on the resident, not only on De
  Bazel. Facts are from Amsterdam op de kaart and nl.wikipedia; wording kept plain. I did not check
  whether there are Stolpersteine or a plaque at the door. Please review the tone.
- **The College Hotel.** nl.wikipedia says it "runs partly on students of hotel schools"; the hotel's
  own site (now Autograph Collection) does not mention this. Body uses the past tense ("set up as").
  That bar, restaurant and terrace take non-residents follows from the hotel site's pages, not from an
  explicit statement.
- **Boerhaave buildings, current tenant.** Gazprom's subsidiary left in 2022 (Amsterdam op de kaart);
  I found no current tenant and wrote "offices". The slice gives 351183 the address Teniersstraat 1;
  the villa's address is Museumplein 9. `listed` is empty in the slice; nl.wikipedia says 1988; I left
  it out.
- **Dutch texts** are written from the Dutch sources, not translated; the two quotes that appear
  ("beeldhouwersatelier, tevens geschikt voor bewoning door één gezin" and the dancing school line)
  are quotations of the building application as cited in the register.

## Leads

- **Pooping cat gable stone, Pieter Baststraat 19.** Since April 2023: a cat on a litter tray laying
  a golden turd, by the sculptor Tobias Snoep, with the years 1905 and 2023. It refers to the
  "kattenbakcentrale" (litter tray service) that operated here around 1940.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Baststraat
- **Gas explosion of 21 December 1987, Pieter Baststraat / Roelof Hartstraat.** Rebuilt almost to
  the old model; windows gone within 200 m. Same source. Probably a line for a nearby place only.
- **Schapenburgerpad.** The half-private lane between Vossiusstraat and P.C. Hooftstraat, entrance
  beside Hobbemastraat 8 (mentioned in the Berlage place). Could carry a place of its own.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Hobbemastraat/4-8
- **Fragmentengebouw (Hobbemastraat by 19).** Cuypers's building of 1890–1898 made of pieces of
  demolished buildings: the stair tower of the Ockingastins from Franeker, the Waterpoort of
  Gorinchem, a gate of the Rotterdam admiralty, 43 gable stones. Part of the Rijksmuseum (A-list), but
  the outside walls are seen from the free garden. Could be folded into the garden place.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragmentengebouw
- **Aldo van Eyck climbing frames in the Rijksmuseum garden**, near the Teekenschool (photo caption,
  April 2013). Not confirmed for today. https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Hobbemastraat/Teekenschool
- **Veiligheidsinstituut, Hobbemastraat 22–24** (Ed. Cuypers, 1912; safety museum for workers, now the
  Rijksmuseum's Ateliergebouw). Not in the slice.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Hobbemastraat/Veiligheidsinstituut
- **School voor Maatschappelijk Werk, Pieter de Hoochstraat 78** (J.E. van der Pek, 1913): the first
  Dutch school for social work, where Louise van der Pek-Went taught housing. Not listed.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Pieter_de_Hoochstraat/78
- **War memorial plaque in the schoolyard of the Montessori Lyceum** (Pieter de Hoochstraat 59), with
  the names of pupils and teachers who did not survive the war, and the story of the separate "Joods
  Montessori Lyceum" of 1941–1942. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montessori_Lyceum_Amsterdam
- **The IJsclub ground behind the Rijksmuseum** (today's Museumplein): from 1885 horse racing, cycling
  and skating, thanks to baron De Salis of Stadhouderskade 40.
  https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Stadhouderskade/40-41
- **Hidden telephone exchange, former Teniersstraat 7**: a municipal exchange whose entrance vanished
  behind new building; the building still stands. https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teniersstraat
- **Zusterhuis of the Boerhaave clinic, Johannes Vermeerstraat 31**: the nurses' home, where the clinic
  restarted in 1995. https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Johannes_Vermeerstraat/31
- **Shell petrol station by Arthur Staal next to De Amstel** (west side): designed in return for
  funding of the clubhouse; canopy replaced in 1975, "little left" according to the register (222113).
- **Radio Stad studios, Johannes Vermeerstraat 29** (1970s and 80s) and **café Loetje, no. 52**.
  https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Vermeerstraat
- **Wax candle factory (Koninklijke Fabriek van Waskaarsen)**, gone since 1906–1909; its story is told
  in the Ignatiuscollege place and touched on in the Vermeerstraat ensemble. No separate place: gone
  and without a trace I could find. https://amsterdamopdekaart.nl/1850-1940/Hobbemakade/Waskaarsenfabriek

## Route ideas

- **Behind the Rijksmuseum, free**: Berlage's shop houses (Hobbemastraat 4–8) → Lizzy Cottage and twin
  → Eduard Cuypers's house (Jan Luijkenstraat 2) → Rijksmuseum garden gates → director's villa and
  Teekenschool → Villa Protsky → Zuiderbad. About 700 m. Ends with a swim.
- **Collectors and what became of their collections**: Villa Protsky (Mannheimer, bought by Hitler) →
  Dreesmannhuis (auctioned 1960) → the surgeon's palazzo (Lanz, sold to Hitler) → Huize Noordwijk
  (Goudstikker lived here) → Lizzy Cottage (Van Essen). All within 500 m, all linked to the museums
  on the square. Pairs well with the museum-c slice.
- **Catholic Zuid on the candle factory**: Ignatiuscollege and the school at Pieter de Hoochstraat 80
  → Johannes Vermeerplein → De Ooievaar (Old Catholic) → the HBS → Het Nieuwe Huis. The contrast
  between the Jesuit college and the Old Catholic church 150 m apart is worth a line.
- **Otto Lanz in two stops**: his house and clinic on the Museumplein, and the pool he started.
- **Along the Boerenwetering**: Zuiderbad → Ignatiuscollege chapel corner → De Amstel clubhouse at De
  Kom (1 km along the Hobbemakade), onward to the Apollo slices.
