# Zuid blind recall, 2026-11-24

Independent list for stadsdeel Zuid, made without reading the map, `research/`, `scripts/data/` or deepcities.org. Built from own knowledge first, then checked against nl.wikipedia (articles plus the Zuid lists of rijksmonumenten, gemeentelijke monumenten, beelden and oorlogsmonumenten), Arcam, zuidas.nl, iamsterdam.com and architect sites.

Line format: name; address or location; lat, lng; why it belongs; source.

Coordinates come from the cited source where it gives them, otherwise they are my own estimate. Estimates that could be more than about 100 m off are marked `(approx)`.

## 1. Museumkwartier and Willemspark

1. Zuiderbad; Hobbemastraat 26; 52.3585, 4.8861; built in 1897 as the Velox indoor cycling school and turned into a swimming pool in 1912, with its tiled interior intact and still in daily use; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuiderbad
2. Rijksmuseum gardens with building fragments; Stadhouderskade 42, garden entrances on Hobbemastraat and Jan Luijkenstraat; 52.3598, 4.8850; free open-air collection of salvaged city gates, facades and pillars from demolished Dutch buildings that Cuypers arranged around the museum; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmuseumtuinen
3. Vondelbunker; Vondelpark 8a, inside the Vondelbrug; 52.3606, 4.8769; public shelter built into the bridge, the city's first youth centre in 1968 and since 2011 a volunteer-run free cultural space (sources disagree on the shelter's building date); https://arcam.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Fietsroute-langs-de-Openbare-Schuilplaatsen-van-Amsterdam-1.2.pdf
4. Picasso's Figure découpée (L'Oiseau); Vondelpark, central lawn; 52.3567, 4.8655 (approx); a real 1965 Picasso in concrete, left in the park after the park's centenary sculpture show, which most passers-by never identify; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_beelden_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
5. Mama Baranka; Vondelpark, near the Vondel statue; 52.3594, 4.8740; Nelson Carrilho's 1984 memorial to Kerwin Duinmeijer, the Antillean boy stabbed to death in 1983 in what became the country's emblematic racist murder; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mama_Baranka
6. Blauwe Theehuis; Vondelpark 5; 52.3588, 4.8726; 1937 flying-saucer pavilion by the Baanders brothers, a rare piece of pure Nieuwe Bouwen dropped into a romantic Zocher park; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blauwe_Theehuis
7. Crystal Houses; P.C. Hooftstraat 94; 52.3601, 4.8799; MVRDV's 2016 facade in which the traditional brick front dissolves upward from solid glass bricks into terracotta, a debated experiment on the luxury street; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_houses
8. Vrouwen van Ravensbrück; Museumplein, Concertgebouw end; 52.3570, 4.8824; 1975 steel memorial with light and a heartbeat-like sound for the women's concentration camp, easily missed on the big lawn; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrouwen_van_Ravensbr%C3%BCck_1940-1945
9. Zigeunermonument Hel en vuur; Museumplein; 52.3565, 4.8814; Heleen Levano's 1978 memorial to the murdered Sinti and Roma, said to be the first such monument anywhere; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zigeunermonument_Hel_en_vuur
10. Harmoniehof; Harmoniehof, between Roelof Hartstraat and Reijnier Vinkeleskade; 52.3513, 4.8830; J.C. van Epen's 1920s garden court of ochre brick with ponds and a fountain, a quiet enclave with its own small war memorial; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmoniehof
11. Het Nieuwe Huis; Roelof Hartplein 50; 52.3525, 4.8825; 1928 Amsterdam School block of 188 flats for single people with shared restaurant, library and roof terrace, an early co-living experiment that still houses the local library; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
12. Huize Lydia; Roelof Hartplein 2; 52.3530, 4.8811; Jan Boterenbrood's 1920s Catholic home for working girls with ladder windows and sculptural brickwork, the other half of the Amsterdam School square; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huize_Lydia
13. Amsterdams Lyceum; Valeriusplein 15; 52.3513, 4.8646; 1920 school by the Baanders brothers built as a gate straddling the street, with a wartime memorial inside and the concierge house De Zonnewijzer beside it; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdams_Lyceum
14. Lomanstraat tree tunnel; Lomanstraat; 52.3520, 4.8592 (approx); the street trees lean in from both sides to form a pointed, cathedral-like vault, a local curiosity with no sign and no guidebook entry; https://farandwide.com/s/this-ordinary-street-in-amsterdam-turned-into-a-perfect-natural-tree-tunnel/
15. Obrechtkerk; Jacob Obrechtstraat 28; 52.3556, 4.8747; twin-towered neo-Romanesque rosary church by Jos Cuypers and Jan Stuyt (design 1905), a Rhineland silhouette hidden behind the Concertgebouw; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obrechtkerk
16. Former Rijkspostspaarbank and Sweelinck Conservatorium; Van Baerlestraat 27; 52.3586, 4.8786; D.E.C. Knuttel's 1901 savings-bank palace that trained generations of musicians before becoming a hotel, with the atrium open to walk into; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Baerlestraat_27
17. Fontainehofje; Valeriusstraat 198; 52.3525, 4.8622; an almshouse court built as late as 1912 to 1913 by the Van Gendt brothers, a hofje in a district nobody associates with hofjes; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
18. Huize De Windroos; Okeghemstraat 25; 52.3516, 4.8630; Piet Kramer's 1921 private villa, the Amsterdam School bridge architect at domestic scale; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
19. First Scheepvaartmuseum building; Cornelis Schuytstraat 57; 52.3536, 4.8727; purpose-built 1917 to 1920 maritime museum by G.F. La Croix, home of the national maritime collection until it moved to the Zeemagazijn in the 1970s; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
20. Raw Aron Schuster Synagoge; Heinzestraat 1, on Jacob Obrechtplein; 52.3526, 4.8772; Harry Elte's 1928 synagogue in a dark, cubic Amsterdam School idiom, still in use and one of the few interwar synagogues to survive with its community; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid

## 2. Apollobuurt, Stadionbuurt, Schinkelbuurt, Hoofddorppleinbuurt

1. Begraafplaats Huis te Vraag; Rijnsburgstraat 51; 52.3465, 4.8485; overgrown Protestant cemetery closed for burials since 1962 and kept as a wild garden by its caretakers, open on weekdays; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begraafplaats_Huis_te_Vraag
2. Aalsmeerder Veerhuis (De Bonte Os); Sloterkade 21; 52.3570, 4.8538; 1634 ferry inn where boats from Aalsmeer and Leiden were hauled over the Overtoom, the oldest building in this part of Zuid; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalsmeerder_Veerhuis
3. Burgerweeshuis by Aldo van Eyck; IJsbaanpad 3; 52.3406, 4.8561; the 1960 orphanage of small domes and courtyards that became the founding work of structuralism, saved from demolition in the 1980s and restored; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgerweeshuis_(Amsterdam,_IJsbaanpad)
4. Tripolis; Burgerweeshuispad 99 to 401; 52.3397, 4.8560; Van Eyck's last, multicoloured office cluster, built as the price of saving the orphanage and now wrapped by a glass slab by MVRDV; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripolis_(Amsterdam)
5. Van Tuyll-monument at the Olympic Stadium; Stadionplein 20; 52.3437, 4.8554; Gra Rueb's 1928 bronze athlete raising the Olympic salute, long mistaken for the Hitler salute and argued over ever since, beside Jan Wils's Marathon tower; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Tuyll-monument
6. Citroëngebouwen; Stadionplein; 52.3445, 4.8572 (approx); Jan Wils's 1931 car showroom and its 1962 twin, glass and concrete temples to the automobile flanking the stadium approach; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABngebouw
7. De onbekende verliezer; Marathonweg, on the columns of the arcades at Achillesstraat and Herculesstraat; 52.3482, 4.8610 (approx); Ram Katzir's 2006 reliefs of the anonymous marathon runner who did not win, filling columns left blank since the 1920s; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_onbekende_verliezer
8. Monument Indië-Nederland (former Van Heutsz monument); Olympiaplein at Apollolaan; 52.3500, 4.8656; 1935 tribute to the general of the Aceh war, bombed and daubed for decades, renamed in 2004 and still the city's most contested monument; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Indi%C3%AB-Nederland
9. Schaduwen; Olympiaplein; 52.3466, 4.8667; Ram Katzir's ground-level memorial to the last great razzia of 20 June 1943, when Jews from Zuid were assembled on this sports field; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schaduwen
10. Eerste Openluchtschool voor het Gezonde Kind; Cliostraat 38 to 40; 52.3480, 4.8745; Jan Duiker's 1930 glass and concrete open-air school hidden inside a housing block, a canonical work of Dutch modernism visible through the gateway; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eerste_Openluchtschool_voor_het_Gezonde_Kind
11. Former SD headquarters and deportation memorial; Gerrit van der Veenstraat 99 and Adama van Scheltemaplein; 52.3488, 4.8745; the girls' school that housed the Sicherheitsdienst faced the Zentralstelle für jüdische Auswanderung across the square, bombed by the RAF in November 1944 and now marked by a memorial to the deported Jews; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_oorlogsmonumenten_in_Amsterdam
12. Verzetsmonument Apollolaan; Apollolaan 91 to 99, at Beethovenstraat; 52.3507, 4.8777; Jan Havermans's 1954 group on the spot where 29 prisoners were shot on 24 October 1944 in reprisal for the killing of an SD officer; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verzetsmonument_Apollolaan
13. Lotty's bankje; Apollolaan, central reservation; 52.3497, 4.8760 (approx, exact spot not confirmed); a 2017 bench honouring Lotty Veffer, who came back from Auschwitz, found strangers in her home and spent her first night on a bench here; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotty_Huffener-Veffer
14. Hilton Amsterdam; Apollolaan 138; 52.3514, 4.8719; 1962 hotel that is now a rijksmonument, site of John Lennon and Yoko Ono's 1969 Bed-In in room 902 and of Herman Brood's fatal jump from the roof in 2001; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Amsterdam_Hotel
15. Kindertjesbrug and Hildo Kropbrug; Muzenplein; 52.3474, 4.8862; Piet Kramer's twin bridges of 1926 to 1931 with Krop's granite children, squirrels and rabbits, plus a transformer kiosk and landing stage in the same hand; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindertjesbrug
16. Drive-in woningen; Anthonie van Dijckstraat 4 to 12; 52.3484, 4.8751; five 1937 houses by Van Tijen, Mart Stam and Lotte Stam-Beese with the garage built into the ground floor, the first drive-in houses in the Netherlands; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
17. Haarlemmermeerstation and the museum tram; Amstelveenseweg 264; 52.3493, 4.8567; 1915 terminus of the vanished Haarlemmermeer railway, from where volunteers run historic trams along the old track bed on Sundays in season; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_Amsterdam_Haarlemmermeer
18. Former Huis van Bewaring II; Havenstraat 6; 52.3500, 4.8562; 1890 cellular prison where the occupier held resistance prisoners, among them Hannie Schaft, later converted into a school with its walls and wings still legible; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huis_van_Bewaring_II_(Amstelveenseweg)
19. Sint-Agneskerk; Amstelveenseweg 163; 52.3501, 4.8577; Jan Stuyt's church of 1919 to 1931 in an early Christian basilica style with a campanile, one of the few city churches still celebrating the Latin mass; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint-Agneskerk_(Amsterdam)
20. Mart Stam's triangular works building; Generaal Vetterstraat 75 to 77; 52.3433, 4.8469; a 1961 wedge-shaped industrial building with a bold overhang by the Bauhaus designer, lost on a business park by the Schinkel; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid

## 3. De Pijp (Oude, Nieuwe, Zuid)

1. Huis met de Kabouters; Ceintuurbaan 251 to 255; 52.3558, 4.9044; 1884 chalet-style houses whose gable carries two green gnomes tossing a red ball, a pun or a folly depending on whom you ask; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huis_met_de_Kabouters
2. De Dageraad; Pieter Lodewijk Takstraat and Burgemeester Tellegenstraat; 52.3489, 4.8995; Kramer and De Klerk's 1920s socialist housing with waves of orange brick, the Amsterdam School at full pitch and far quieter than Het Schip; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Dageraad_(Amsterdam)
3. Coöperatiehof and Van Buurenmonument; Coöperatiehof; 52.3505, 4.8990; Kramer's enclosed village green of low workers' houses with a tower-topped reading room in the middle, entered through gateways most people walk past; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co%C3%B6peratiehof
4. Synagoge Gerard Doustraat; Gerard Doustraat 238; 52.3570, 4.8980; 1892 synagogue set in the street wall that survived the occupation almost untouched and has never stopped being used; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagoge_Gerard_Doustraat
5. Spijker coach works; Gerard Doustraat 175 to 181; 52.3572, 4.8975; the pilastered factory hall where the Spijker brothers built the Gouden Koets in the 1890s, now a day nursery named after the coach; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Doustraat_175-181
6. IJssalon Koco plaque; Van Woustraat 149; 52.3528, 4.9028; site of the Jewish-run ice cream parlour whose defenders sprayed a German patrol with ammonia in February 1941, the incident that triggered the first razzias and then the February Strike; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_oorlogsmonumenten_in_Amsterdam
7. Asscher diamond works; Tolstraat 127; 52.3527, 4.9052; the 1907 castle-like factory where the Cullinan was cleaved, with street names Diamantstraat, Robijnstraat and Smaragdstraat built around it and the 2023 artwork Revisiting the Cullinan nearby; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijke_Asscher_Diamant_Maatschappij
8. Theosophical temple (Cinetol); Tolstraat 154 to 160; 52.3525, 4.9060; Brinkman and Van der Vlugt's 1920s meeting halls for the Theosophical Society, later a cinema and a library, now a music venue behind a quarter-circle white facade; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolstraat_160
9. Polderhuis; Rustenburgerstraat 8; 52.3541, 4.9060; a single 1865 labourer's cottage from when this was polder in Nieuwer-Amstel, standing below street level among the later tenements; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polderhuis_(Rustenburgerstraat)
10. Afrikahuis; Van Ostadestraat 268 to 270; 52.3529, 4.8978; Joop van Stigt's late 1960s structuralist parish church, built when the giant Willibrordus was abandoned and itself out of use as a church since 2008; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikahuis
11. Sint-Willibrordus buiten de Veste (gone); Amsteldijk at Ceintuurbaan; 52.3553, 4.9047; Pierre Cuypers's cathedral-sized church, never finished to plan and demolished around 1970, whose footprint is now a care home; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint-Willibrorduskerk_buiten_de_Veste
12. Raadhuis of Nieuwer-Amstel; Amsteldijk 67; 52.3533, 4.9069; the 1892 town hall of the municipality Amsterdam swallowed four years later, afterwards the city archive for almost a century; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
13. Gemaal Sarphatipark (Groen Gemaal); Sarphatipark 18H; 52.3541, 4.8946; 1884 pumping house that still manages the park's polder-level water, now a volunteer plant and seed exchange; https://www.iamsterdam.com/ontdek/buurten/de-pijp/verborgen-parels
14. Mondriaan's studio; Sarphatipark 42; 52.3531, 4.8948; attic studio house where Piet Mondriaan lived and worked before leaving for Paris, unmarked except for those who know to look up; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
15. Vredeskerk and Hommage aan Paradzjanov; Vredeskerkplein 1; 52.3498, 4.8895; 1924 Catholic church with Amsterdam School touches, fronted by Thom Puckey's 2004 marble and water piece for the Armenian film director; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hommage_aan_Paradzjanov
16. Albert Cuypgarage; under the Boerenwetering, entrance on Ruysdaelkade; 52.3545, 4.8868 (approx); a 260 m car park built beneath an existing canal, the first of its kind, on the alignment once reserved for a rail tunnel; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Cuypgarage
17. Badhuis Diamantstraat; Diamantstraat 134; 52.3508, 4.9062; A.J. Westerman's 1926 Amsterdam School public bathhouse from the years when the surrounding flats had no bathrooms; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_rijksmonumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
18. Okura weather light; Ferdinand Bolstraat 333; 52.3486, 4.8936; the strip of light on the hotel's roof edge forecasts tomorrow's weather (blue for sun, green for bad, white for changeable), readable from half of Zuid; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Okura_Amsterdam
19. First Rijksakademie building; Stadhouderskade 86; 52.3577, 4.8947; Bastiaan de Greef's 1875 academy where Breitner, Mondriaan and Witsen trained, standing among the brewery blocks; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadhouderskade_86
20. Blauwe reiger mural; Albert Cuypstraat 2 to 6 (Moppesgebouw); 52.3544, 4.8874; Adele Renault's 2018 heron, the market's scavenger bird, painted across the end wall of an 1890 commercial building; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_beelden_in_Amsterdam-Zuid

## 4. Rivierenbuurt (Scheldebuurt, IJselbuurt, Rijnbuurt)

1. 12-verdiepingenhuis (De Wolkenkrabber); Victorieplein 45; 52.3459, 4.9026; J.F. Staal's 1932 tower of twelve floors, the country's first residential high-rise with lifts, rubbish chutes and speaking tubes; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/12-verdiepingenhuis
2. Statue of H.P. Berlage; Victorieplein; 52.3460, 4.9034; Hildo Krop's 1966 granite planner in a long coat, looking down the three avenues of his own Plan Zuid; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standbeeld_van_H.P._Berlage
3. Merwedeplein and Anne Frank statue; Merwedeplein 37; 52.3454, 4.9008; the Frank family's flat from 1933 to 1942, restored to its 1930s state as a refuge for persecuted writers, with Jet Schepp's statue of Anne glancing back; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merwedeplein
4. Anne Frankschool; Niersstraat 41 to 43; 52.3428, 4.8939; the Montessori school Anne attended until 1941, its facade painted with a page of her diary in her handwriting; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_oorlogsmonumenten_in_Amsterdam
5. Kindermonument Markt voor Joden; Gaaspstraat 8; 52.3449, 4.9079; Truus Menger's 1984 figures at the playground that became the only one Jewish children could use and the site of a segregated market; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindermonument_Markt_voor_Joden
6. Lekstraatsynagoge; Lekstraat 61 to 63; 52.3442, 4.9028; Abraham Elzas's 1937 white functionalist box, built for the neighbourhood's growing Jewish community and largely emptied five years later; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekstraatsynagoge
7. Atelierwoningen Zomerdijkstraat; Zomerdijkstraat 2 to 30; 52.3425, 4.9036; 1934 block of artists' studio flats with tall north windows, home to sculptor and resistance leader Gerrit van der Veen, with a liberation tree and plaque on the end wall in Kinderdijkstraat; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atelierwoningen_Zomerdijkstraat
8. Vrijheidslaan; Vrijheidslaan 10 to 54; 52.3473, 4.9103; De Klerk's last facades line an avenue that was Amstellaan, then Stalinlaan from 1946, and was renamed after the Soviet invasion of Hungary in 1956; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrijheidslaan
9. Berlagebrug; over the Amstel at Vrijheidslaan; 52.3473, 4.9127; Berlage's 1932 bridge with Krop's Genius of Amsterdam on the tower, where Canadian troops entered the city on 8 May 1945; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlagebrug
10. Remise Lekstraat; Kromme-Mijdrechtstraat 25; 52.3456, 4.9104; the 1930 Amsterdam School tram depot, still a working depot behind sculptural brick gateways; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remise_Lekstraat
11. Wilhelmina Drucker-monument; Churchill-laan at Waalstraat; 52.3467, 4.9003; Gerrit van der Veen's 1939 bronze for the feminist pioneer, inscribed "Vrouwen houdt de fakkel brandende"; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmina_Drucker-monument
12. Statue of Mahatma Gandhi; Churchill-laan; 52.3468, 4.8979; Karel Gomes's 1990 striding Gandhi placed on the avenue named after his great opponent, a pairing locals enjoy pointing out; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standbeeld_van_Mahatma_Gandhi_(Amsterdam)
13. Amsteldok (Rivierstaete); Amsteldijk 166; 52.3443, 4.9122; Maaskant's 1973 white-tiled office terraces, once reviled as the ugliest building on the Amstel and rebuilt in 2019 behind glass; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsteldok
14. Trompenburgstraat workers' housing and the Spyker works (gone); Trompenburgstraat 21 to 43; 52.3437, 4.9116; an 1898 terrace older than the whole district, beside the site of the Trompenburg factory where Spyker built cars and aircraft until the 1920s; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
15. Zorgvlied; Amsteldijk 273; 52.3356, 4.9003; Zocher's 1870 landscape cemetery on the Amstel with Oscar Carré's mausoleum and the bronze of nightclub owner Manfred Langer, lying in Amsterdam but owned by Amstelveen; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorgvlied_(begraafplaats)
16. Gemaal Stadwijck and Zorgvliedsluis; Amsteldijk 272 and Zuidelijke Wandelweg 25; 52.3357, 4.9054; a 1921 polder pumping station, lock and Kramer bridge built as one ensemble when the Rivierenbuurt was raised with sand; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemaal_Stadwijck
17. Martin Luther Kingpark and its King statue; President Kennedylaan at the Amstel; 52.3400, 4.9094; riverside park renamed for King in 1968, with a statue of him added in 2020 by the artist Airco Caravan; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeld_van_Martin_Luther_King
18. Finnish-timber school; Uiterwaardenstraat 60a; 52.3437, 4.9099; a seven-classroom emergency school of 1949 built from Finnish wood, one of the last of the post-war timber schools; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
19. 't Bruggehuisje; Amstelkade 1, on the P.L. Kramerbrug; 52.3499, 4.9097; a Kramer bridge house turned into the city's smallest storytelling theatre, with 20 to 25 seats; https://www.iamsterdam.com/ontdek/buurten/rivierenbuurt/verborgen-parels
20. Dongeschool and De Fakkeldrager; Dintelstraat 5 and 15; 52.3454, 4.8934; N. Lansdorp's 1928 Amsterdam School school building with a torchbearer war memorial by Hans Reicher a few doors on; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_oorlogsmonumenten_in_Amsterdam

## 5. Buitenveldert (West and Oost)

1. Riekermolen and Rembrandt statue; De Borcht 10, Amsteldijk; 52.3240, 4.8937; a 1636 polder mill moved here from the Riekerpolder in 1961, with Han Wezelaar's bronze of Rembrandt sketching on the bank where he drew; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riekermolen
2. Monument Rozenoord; Amsteldijk, edge of Amstelpark; 52.3284, 4.8957; a field of empty chairs, one for each of the more than a hundred men shot here by the occupier in the last months of the war (the older memorial stone stands 600 m north at Dr. C.W. Ittmannpad); https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Rozenoord
3. Amstelrust; Amsteldijk 319; 52.3302, 4.8976; an Amstel country house of 1724 with coach house and bridge gate, a survivor of the row of buitenplaatsen that preceded the suburb; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstelrust
4. Amsteltrein; Amstelpark; 52.3300, 4.8945 (approx); the narrow-gauge train kept from the 1972 Floriade, still circling the park every season; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsteltrein
5. Het Glazen Huis; Amstelpark; 52.3292, 4.8930 (approx); a glass Floriade pavilion reused as a free exhibition space for art about nature and technology; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstelpark
6. Rosarium and rhododendron valley; Amstelpark, south half; 52.3262, 4.8945 (approx); the surviving show gardens of the Floriade, at their best in May and June and almost unknown to visitors; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstelpark
7. Gijsbrecht van Aemstelpark; from Amstelpark to the Amstelveenseweg; 52.3288, 4.8737; Wim Boer's 1959 modernist strip park of straight water, concrete pergolas and paddling pools, a rijksmonument since 2015; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gijsbrecht_van_Aemstelpark
8. Liggende figuren; Gijsbrecht van Aemstelpark at Twikkel; 52.3287, 4.8645 (approx); Jan Snoeck's four reclining ceramic figures of 1974, the best of the park's set of 1960s and 1970s sculpture that also includes work by Volten and Niermeijer; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_beelden_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
9. Botanische tuin Zuidas; Van der Boechorststraat 8; 52.3328, 4.8606; the former VU hortus, run by volunteers, with the country's largest cactus and succulent collection and a penjing garden between the hospital blocks; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanische_tuin_Zuidas
10. VU main building; De Boelelaan 1105; 52.3345, 4.8669; the 1973 concrete slab of the Calvinist university, with a war memorial inside and Jan Mulder's blue-ribbon De hechtsteek at the hospital entrance next door; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoofdgebouw_Vrije_Universiteit
11. Rietveld's Vrije Gemeente building; Van der Boechorststraat 26; 52.3319, 4.8613; a late Rietveld design, finished after his death, built for a free-thinking congregation and now the Joods Cultureel Centrum with a synagogue; https://www.monumenten.nl/monument/1005710800
12. Schenkkan house; Weldam 10; 52.3311, 4.8605; a cubic private villa by Rietveld from the 1960s on a suburban cul-de-sac, with patio houses of 1972 as neighbours; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
13. Pinksterkerk; Van Boshuizenstraat 652; 52.3249, 4.8710; M.F. Duintjer's 1965 church with a steel sculpture by André Volten at the door, the pick of Buitenveldert's reconstruction-era churches; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
14. Monument voor het Ondergedoken Kind en Beschermer; Van Boshuizenstraat at Nieuw Herlaer; 52.3248, 4.8796; Naomi Elburg's 2000 memorial to the hidden Jewish children and the people who took them in; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_oorlogsmonumenten_in_Amsterdam
15. Cheider; Zeelandstraat 53; 52.3266, 4.8790; the only Orthodox Jewish school in the Netherlands, visible only as a fenced and guarded compound, which says a good deal about Jewish life in Buitenveldert today; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheider
16. Kalfjeslaan; between Amsteldijk and Amstelveenseweg; 52.3231, 4.8803; the old polder lane that is the city boundary with Amstelveen, named after Jan Claesz Kalf, with ditches and pollard willows on one side and 1960s flats on the other; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalfjeslaan
17. Tuinpark Amstelglorie; Jan Vroegopsingel 7; 52.3338, 4.9010 (approx, check that it falls inside Zuid); a large allotment park on the Amstel beside the A10, open to walkers in season and under threat of housing plans since 2016; https://iamsterdam.com/en/whats-on/calendar/attractions-and-sights/sights/amstelglorie-allotments

Augustinuskerk; Amstelveenseweg 965 at Kalfjeslaan; 52.3229, 4.8575 (approx); the church of the old rural parish of Buitenveldert, older than the suburb and recently put up for sale (building date not confirmed); https://www.fundainbusiness.nl/en/religieus/amsterdam/object-43307042-amstelveenseweg-965/
19. Andrieskerk; A.J. Ernststraat 869 at Buitenveldertselaan; 52.3319, 4.8681; H.W.M. Hupkes's 1971 church, a low sculptural brick volume on the tram axis and a late entry in the district's church-building wave; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrieskerk_(Amsterdam)

## 6. Zuidas and Prinses Irenebuurt

1. Artsenijhof; Beatrixpark, south-east corner; 52.3423, 4.8822 (approx); the medicinal herb garden laid out for the 1972 Floriade and still kept by volunteers, with labelled beds; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrixpark
2. Beatrixpark and the Ko Mulder monument; Beatrixpark; 52.3431, 4.8802; Jakoba Mulder's 1938 park, the first by the woman who shaped Amsterdam's green planning, with the 2018 work Future past glory in her honour and her paddling pool intact; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrixpark
3. Kwibukamonument; Beatrixpark, north side; 52.3449, 4.8807; the national memorial to the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda, placed in 2023; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwibukamonument
4. Sint-Nicolaasklooster and chapel; Prinses Irenestraat 19; 52.3416, 4.8806; a 1962 monastery and round-walled chapel on the park edge, kept when the school around it was demolished and reused by a string of temporary occupants; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
5. Thomaskerk; Prinses Irenestraat 36; 52.3420, 4.8749; Karel Sijmons's 1966 church in raw concrete, the city's most uncompromising brutalist interior, used for concerts and theatre as well as services; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomaskerk_(Amsterdam)
6. R.K. Begraafplaats Buitenveldert; Fred. Roeskestraat 103; 52.3403, 4.8614; Catholic cemetery in use since 1836, now wedged between the A10 and office towers, with the Wiegman-Dobbelmann tomb by Eduard Cuypers; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begraafplaats_Buitenveldert
7. Gerrit Rietveld Academie; Fred. Roeskestraat 96; 52.3413, 4.8600; Rietveld's glass-box art school of 1966, completed after his death, with later additions by Benthem Crouwel and Studio Paulien Bremmer; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrit_Rietveld_Academie
8. Love or Generosity; Parnassusweg 280, court forecourt; 52.3397, 4.8679; Nicole Eisenman's 2020 bronze giant in a tracksuit bending to look at what it holds, a deliberately gentle figure to put in front of a courthouse; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_or_generosity
9. Valley; Beethovenstraat 503; 52.3375, 4.8778; MVRDV's 2022 trio of towers eroded into planted stone terraces, with a public route up through the "valley" on the fourth and fifth floors; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_(Amsterdam)
10. Infinity (former ING House); Amstelveenseweg 500; 52.3372, 4.8564; the 2002 headquarters on stilts beside the A10, nicknamed the shoe, the dustbuster and the ice skate, emblem of pre-crisis banking; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_(gebouw)
11. De doos; roof of the residential tower at De Boelelaan and Parnassusweg; 52.3356, 4.8693; Jeroen Henneman's giant line-drawn box, set on the ground for ArtZuid 2017 and then hoisted some forty metres onto a rooftop; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_doos
12. Europahal and Het Signaal; Europaplein 24; 52.3428, 4.8894; the 1961 RAI hall with its arched roof and Dick Elffers's advertising pylon, listed together as a rijksmonument in 2015; https://zuidas.nl/en/blog/2021/europahal-prime-example-of-new-objectivity/
13. nhow Amsterdam RAI; Europaboulevard 2b; 52.3382, 4.8905 (approx); OMA's hotel of three rotated triangular stacks, said to take its shape from Het Signaal, with a broadcasting studio and sky bar on top; https://zuidas.nl/en/blog/2021/europahal-prime-example-of-new-objectivity/
14. Liberal Jewish synagogue; Zuidelijke Wandelweg 41; 52.3381, 4.8995 (approx); SeARCH's 2010 building whose window is a giant menorah cut through the block, with 800 seats and bricks reused from the previous synagogue; https://www.search.nl/works/synagogue/
15. I Want a Permanent Wave; metro station Europaplein, below ground; 52.3417, 4.8925; Gerald van der Kaap's photographic wall work along the platform of the Noord/Zuidlijn; https://zuidas.nl/en/blog/2022/07/12/enjoy-some-art-and-a-stroll-through-zuidas/
16. Ode aan Berlage; Parnassusweg; 52.3438, 4.8696 (approx, exact spot not confirmed); Hildo Krop's 1942 sculpture for the planner of Zuid, made in wartime and standing where Plan Zuid meets the Zuidas; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_beelden_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
17. Hildo (Hondstrouw); Gustav Mahlerlaan 10, in front of the ABN AMRO office; 52.3370, 4.8730 (approx); Tom Claassen's oversized dog, named after Hildo Krop, waiting outside the bank; https://zuidas.nl/en/blog/2022/07/12/enjoy-some-art-and-a-stroll-through-zuidas/
18. De wachter; Antonio Vivaldistraat; 52.3360, 4.8819; Boris Tellegen's 2016 angular guardian figure, a graffiti writer's robot enlarged to street scale in the Vivaldi quarter; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_wachter_(Boris_Tellegen)
19. Turmac building; Antonio Vivaldistraat 2 to 8; 52.3350, 4.8849; Hein Salomonson's elegant 1966 office for the tobacco firm, a low modernist pavilion that predates the whole Zuidas; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_gemeentelijke_monumenten_in_Amsterdam-Zuid
20. AFC ground and war memorial; De Boelelaan 50 (Sportpark Goed Genoeg); 52.3352, 4.8800; the amateur football club founded in 1895 still playing on open pitches between the towers, with a memorial to its members killed in the war; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijst_van_oorlogsmonumenten_in_Amsterdam
