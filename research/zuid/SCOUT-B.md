# Zuid scout B (items 201–257): hidden history and behind the gate

Scouted 2026-11-24. Sweeps 4 (hidden history) and 5 (behind the gate) for the whole of stadsdeel Zuid. Nothing is written or imported: the user picks. Items are leads, not sources: dates and attributions below come from the named source and still need checking when drafted.

I have 57 numbered items: **37 possible new places and 20 merges into listed monuments** (plus seven short merge notes for De Pijp monuments under item 228). Every pin was point-checked with `scripts/areas.py` and is in borough Zuid. Every address was searched in `slices/*.json` (596 records, by address and by distance under 60 m); where a monument is hit the item says "merge into monument <monnr>". The live map was checked (read-only) for Vondelbunker, Vondelpark, Berlagebrug, Zorgvlied, Huis te Vraag and Amstelpark: nothing in Zuid is on it.

Each item: title; pin and address; hook; why it fits; lead source; status with date of latest evidence; caveats; rank; new place or merge. Homes, schools and offices are private: only the outside can be seen unless stated.

## Sweep 4: Hidden history

### 1. Museumkwartier and Willemspark

201. **The stay-behind villa, Van Eeghenstraat 78**; 52.357555, 4.872578; the double villa where the Marechaussee staff moved in on 8 October 1945 later housed the dozen staff of "Operaties" (O-Dienst, from 1985 A-Dienst), the secret service that was to organise sabotage and psychological warfare if the Eastern bloc occupied the country; its cover was a historical study society, the Stichting Hendrik van Borselen; from 1979 to 1984 the office sat on a floor of Koninginneweg 184; the service was wound up in spring 1992; a Cold War secret behind an ordinary Vondelpark front; lead: https://www.stelling-amsterdam.nl/bunkers/amsterdam-villavaneeghenstraat/index.php ; a private house now (source photos 2003, page updated 2026); one source, so **check: Facts**; **Nice**; merge into monument 222041 (Van Eeghenstraat 74-78).

202. **The fire station of a vanished municipality, Van Baerlestraat 35-37**; 52.356024, 4.880273, corner Gabriël Metsustraat; built about 1890 by Nieuwer-Amstel, which governed all of this until Amsterdam annexed it in 1896; the gable stones still show fire and police attributes with the Nieuwer-Amstel arms and the words "Brandweer, Politie"; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Baerlestraat ; shops and flats now; **Okay**; merge into monument 222103 (Van Baerlestraat 35-37).

203. **The pooping cat, Pieter Baststraat 19**; 52.352926, 4.885079; a gable stone of April 2023 by Tobias Snoep shows a cat on a litter tray laying a golden turd, for the "kattenbakcentrale" that worked here around 1940 (dated 1905 and 2023); at the Roelof Hartstraat end of the same street a gas explosion at 5:45 on 21 December 1987 destroyed the off-licence and broke windows 200 m away, and the corner was rebuilt almost as it was; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Baststraat ; in place 2023; could also go to Scout A or C; **Okay**; new place.

### 2. Apollobuurt, Stadionbuurt, Schinkelbuurt, Hoofddorppleinbuurt

204. **The civil defence command bunker under the Ignatius schoolyard**; Memlingstraat opposite no. 13, 52.349584, 4.874828; under the gym and playground of the St. Ignatiusgymnasium lies the command post of Bescherming Bevolking Noord-Holland Kring E: bunker I (about 18 by 31 m, command room with wall maps, message centre, 35 bunks) was built in 1955 into the school that replaced the Christelijke HBS bombed on 26 November 1944; bunker II under the playground (decontamination showers, generator, kitchen, canteen) was delivered on 19 October 1972, budgeted at 600,000 guilders and costing perhaps a million; 96 to 100 people would have worked there in three shifts; a 1968 conscript remembers lessons there as "Dad's Army"; lead: https://www.stelling-amsterdam.nl/bunkers/beschermingbevolking/amsterdam/index.php and https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/ingezonden/167.htm ; intact with maps and telephones in 2006 (photos); not open; all a passer-by sees is the access hatch with a stair on the playground; **check: Still there?** (no evidence after 2006; page updated 2026); **Nice**; merge into monument 222130 (Adama van Scheltemaplein 1, Jan van Eijckstraat 47). Scout A has the wartime story of the same building.

205. **The chapel of the Lady of All Nations**; Diepenbrockstraat 3, 52.344497, 4.883383; behind a 1950s villa front sits a pilgrimage chapel (1976) that ships prayer cards round the world; the villa belonged to Gertrud Brenninkmeijer of C&A, who took in Ida Peerdeman, a woman who worked at Boldoot and reported 56 apparitions of Mary from 1945 on, first at home on the Uiterwaardenstraat and once during mass in the Thomas van Aquinokerk on the Rijnstraat (demolished 2004), where a priest told the crowd "that lady is hysterical"; in a vision of 1956 Mary pointed out the spot for a three-domed church where the RAI now stands; the bishop of Haarlem recognised the apparitions in 2002, and on 30 December 2020 the Vatican restated that they are not supernatural while allowing the devotion; a living, disputed shrine on a quiet street; lead: https://www.devrouwevanallevolkeren.nl/de-kapel/ and https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/Markantebewoners/idapeerdeman.htm ; ruling: https://kro-ncrv.nl/vaticaan-verwerpt-amsterdamse-mariaverschijningen-maar-staat-devotie ; open Monday to Saturday 8.30–11.15 and 14.00–17.00 (kapelvandevrouwe.nl, 2026); write it neutrally, sides named by institution; **Nice** (my Amazing candidate for this area); new place.

206. **De Kom: the forgotten ferry, the stolen beach and Brood's bridge house**; Muzenplein, pin on the water junction 52.348500, 4.886110; five waterways meet here; from the 1920s a little cable ferry took walkers across between the Jozef Israëlskade and the Amstelkade, and it is the one Anne Frank meant on 24 June 1942 ("het enige waar we nog mee mogen rijden is de pont"); the occupier had the cable removed as a "barrier" and ferryman Cornelis Staal went on by rowing boat until the bridge came; children swam from "ons landje" until a rowing club fenced it in 1953; below the bridge house between bridges 419 and 420 is a sunken garden with nine children by nine sculptors, hired because colleagues protested against Hildo Krop's monopoly; the bridge house was the parks department's store, then Herman Brood's studio, then a jeweller's workshop; lead: https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/tijdtijn/pontje.htm and https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/architectuur/muzenplein.htm ; https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Kom_(Amsterdam) ; all in place except the ferry (gone, no trace); Brood's use is from one 2004 walk text, **check: Facts**; the sculptures overlap with Scout A; **Nice**; new place, or merge into the Kindertjesbrug (bridge 420, a rijksmonument per Wikipedia; I did not find it in the slices).

207. **The dam and the missing bridge 412**; Noorder Amstelkanaal at the Amstelveenseweg, 52.350330, 4.857560; Berlage's two canals were dammed off from the Schinkel while Plan Zuid was built, because the water levels differed; the southern dam became Kramer's bridge 413 in 1938, the northern one was to get a bridge with a stop lock, the war came, and the canal still dead-ends at a dam with an electricity hut on it; the unbuilt bridge is why Amsterdam has no bridge 412; a second leftover, a small lock by the south stand of the Olympic Stadium, is out of use but still works; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dam_in_het_Noorder_Amstelkanaal ; in place; **Okay**; new place.

208. **Michelangelostraat 36: the Hidden Camera's hideout**; 52.348164, 4.870896; Fritz Kahlenberg and Tonny van Renterghem hid at 36-I and 36-III and from here ran "De Ondergedoken Camera", the photographers (Taconis, Oorthuys, Andriesse) whose secret pictures fixed our image of the Hunger Winter; van Renterghem, chief of staff of the resistance in Zuid, rode in on the first Allied armoured car on 7 May 1945 and later got the Berlagebrug plaque corrected (1995); across the street at no. 59 Hannie Schaft had an attic room from the summer of 1940, and no. 35-H was a Jewish Council information office from 1943; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelostraat_(Amsterdam) and https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/tijdtijn/renterghem.htm ; private houses; I found no plaque; possible overlap with Scout A; **Okay**; new place (no. 59 lies in monument 527790).

209. **Sloten's last school, Floris Versterstraat 11**; 52.356114, 4.851059; the stone reads "Der Ned. Hervormde gem. te Sloten N.H. / Christelijk Lager Onderwijs": the church of the village of Sloten put the school out to tender on 18 December 1930, nine years after Amsterdam had swallowed the municipality; the text was hidden behind signs for decades and came back when the school became flats in 2015; round the corner the Jacob Marisplein was laid out by Sloten for commuters from 1905 (its water tower of 1908 stood near no. 24 until about 1914) and still has the village's houses; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floris_Versterstraat and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Marisplein ; in place 2015; pin on the street, not the stone: **check: Location**; **Okay**; new place (Jacob Marisplein 20 is monument 530982; Floris Versterstraat 10-12 is 222126).

210. **The avenue to a station that never came**; Minervalaan at the Gerrit van der Veenstraat, 52.347621, 4.872003; the Minervalaan was planned as the grand shopping street to a Zuiderstation with a square "as in Vienna" at its head; crisis and war stopped the station, the shops never came, and the corner blocks are left with oversized doors and windows, walk-through gateways with flats above and ten carved capitals (Rädecker, Kaas, Vos); the southern end became the villa "Goudkust" instead; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minervalaan and https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/wandeling/feuilleton.htm ; in place; **Okay**; new place (the east corner is in monument 527790, so it could merge).

211. **The Duiventil, a tram control cabin on a leg**; corner Amstelveenseweg and Karperweg, about 52.348198, 4.855463; the glass cabin on a concrete leg by Dick Slebos (Piet Kramer's successor) stood on the Stationsplein from 1955 to 1983, where the GVB switched points and directed trams from it; it was moved behind the Haarlemmermeerstation for the museum tram and moved again in August 2025 to make way for housing on the Havenstraat yard; a second "pigeon loft", from the Muntplein bridge, stands at the museum tram's Van Nijenrodeweg end; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duiventil_(Havenstraat) ; **check: Location** (new spot not seen; pin is the street corner); may overlap with Scout C's museum tram; **Okay**; new place.

### 3. De Pijp

212. **The Tulpkazerne**; the block between Cornelis Troostplein, Van Hilligaertstraat and Ferdinand Bolstraat, pin on the former sisters' convent 52.350533, 4.891054; the convent and school complex of the Vredeskerk was requisitioned from 15 May 1941 and named after Sybren Tulp, the NSB police chief of Amsterdam; it housed the Amsterdam police battalion (the "Schalkhaarders") that carried out raids on Jews and on men for forced labour; with only two sentry posts the barracks was hardly noticeable; after Dolle Dinsdag the Landwacht, Dutch SS and SD moved in, and the crypt under the church choir was cut open and fitted out as their air-raid shelter; NIOD photos show the name being taken off the front in May 1945; of a parish resistance group of 24, nine came back; the Bhagwan movement took the convent in 1980; today the convent houses Brouwerij Troost; information boards on 1940–1945 hang in the church since 29 April 2025; lead: https://www.stelling-amsterdam.nl/bunkers/amsterdam-tulpkazerne/index.php and https://www.oorlogsbronnen.nl/thema/Tulpkazerne ; De Pijp Krant: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/potlood-met-een-rubberen-punt/ ; buildings in place; sensitive history, write it factually; I found no marker on the building; **Nice**; new place (the Vredeskerk next to it is monument 200461, so it could merge).

213. **Holdert's printing works, now De Fabriek**; Van Ostadestraat 233, 52.353343, 4.898768; from 1900 the printing works of Hendrikus Holdert, owner of De Telegraaf, who financed the NSB and printed the Deutsche Zeitung in den Niederlanden here (his son joined the Waffen-SS and got twelve years in 1949); squatted in 1980 and since then a live-and-work building with the bicycle workshop Smerig; the district wanted it down from 2008, the council voted for demolition in 2013, and the court ruled on 28 May 2014 that it stays; lead: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/spiegelbeeld-goed-en-fout/ and https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/de-fabriek-blijft-bestaan-buurtfeest-op-zaterdag-5-juli-van-1400-1900-uur/ ; active 2026 (ostade233.nl); the squat side overlaps with Scout C; **Nice**; new place.

214. **Jozef Israëlskade: Van Egters' sign and the number that should not exist**; pin Jozef Israëlskade 415, corner Diamantstraat, 52.349817, 4.906968; Gerard Reve wrote the first sentences of De Avonden here in 1946, and a sign "66 Van Egters" on the front names the novel's hero and his "Schilderskade 66"; the occupier renamed the quay Tooropkade from 14 August 1942 to 18 May 1945, and when it was renamed back the old numbers 104–132 became 362–468, so numbers 103–361 do not exist; yet the old plate "103" still hangs on the corner of the Jan Lievensstraat (about 52.350609, 4.904587), where a cigar shop was; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jozef_Israëlskade and https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/jan-lievensstraat/ (2015); private homes; **check: Still there?** for the "103" plate (last evidence 2015); **Nice**; new place.

215. **Frans Halsstraat 64: the gap and its wall**; 52.356455, 4.888547; the house was pulled down in 1945, partly because so much of its wood had been stolen for fuel in the Hunger Winter; the gap was never filled, and since about 2000 Jurriaan and Rinus van Hall paint texts on the blind wall, since 24 July 2013 "Wake me up when I'm famous"; in the same street the gate of the Frans Halsschool (1888, nos. 14-16, the first Japanese School in 1979–1983) is all that is left of the school, and the lettering of the 4711 eau de cologne factory runs along the front from no. 7; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frans_Halsstraat_(Amsterdam) ; wall text reported in place; the mural overlaps with Scout A; **Nice**; new place.

216. **Jacob Israël de Haanstraat: the gateway in the wrong place**; between Van Woustraat and Amsteldijk, 52.354059, 4.904329; the little street cut through in the renewal years was named in 1993 after the author of Pijpelijntjes (1904), with a small monument to him; its underpass should line up with the Rustenburgerdwarsstraat but does not, because the expropriation plan had the wrong house numbers; lead: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/schrijvers-in-de-buurt-4/ (2020) and https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/wat-is-een-goed-klimaat-zonder-behoorlijke-woning-2/ (2021); in place; **Okay**; new place.

217. **The two crowned W's of the Poggenbeekstraat**; long front in the Poggenbeekstraat, pin on the street 52.348697, 4.896897; right after the liberation a house painter with leftover paint put two W's with a crown on the wall for Queen Wilhelmina; housing association De Key approved and paid for their restoration about 2008; a liberation counterpart to the Diezestraat V-signs (item 229); lead: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/improviseren-en-mensen-op-sleeptouw-nemen-2/ (June 2020); one source and no house number: **check: Location** and whether they are still there; **Okay** (Nice if confirmed); new place.

218. **Billardfabriek Wilhelmina**; Stadhouderskade 127, 52.357779, 4.899923; a working billiard-table factory founded in 1898 by Isaac Salomons and here since 1911; expropriated in the war, Salomons and his wife deported to Theresienstadt (only she came back), regained by a nephew and sold in 1963 to billiards champion Cees van Oosterhout; Hugo Kaagman stencilled a Night Watch for the owners on the Hemonylaan wall behind; lead: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/billardfabriek-wilhelmina-al-meer-dan-100-jaar-in-de-pijp/ (2019, repeated 2025); a shop and workshop, open in business hours; **Okay**; new place.

219. **Quellijnstraat 64: cabaret's cradle and the 1980 explosion (gone)**; about 52.356748, 4.891092; on 19 August 1895 the basement night club Het Wapen van Habsburg gave Eduard Jacobs the first paid cabaret engagement in the Netherlands; on 16 June 1980 the house collapsed after an explosion in the flat of two Rood Verzetsfront members above the printing shop of ex-Provo Rob Stolk; the buurthuis built on the spot closed on 1 July 2010; lead: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/de-opmerkelijke-geschiedenis-van-quellijnstraat-64/ (2011); the building is gone and I found no marker, so it only passes the gone bar on lasting impact; pin approximate; **Okay**, status Gone; new place, or drop.

220. **The Anja Joos stone**; pavement of the Gerard Douplein by café 't Paardje, about 52.355978, 4.892850; a small ground plaque for the homeless woman who was beaten to death here on 5 October 2003 by supermarket staff who thought she had stolen beer; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Douplein and https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/ik-hoef-niet-in-een-hokje-2/ (2020: "still there"); **check: Location**; factual and sober; **Okay**; new place, or a Look for line if the square gets a place.

221. **Henrick de Keijserplein: the playground the neighbours built**; clubhouse at Henrick de Keijserplein 45, 52.351111, 4.898059; opened on 13 May 1916 by mayor Tellegen for the playground association of 1913; the wooden Amsterdam School clubhouse and the play equipment were burned for fuel in the Hunger Winter and the clubhouse was rebuilt in the old style; the stone rim of the bandstand became the sandpit; an above-ground shelter barrack stood on the square in the war; residents run it since 2016; lead: https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/speeltuinvereniging-henrick-de-keijser-een-initiatief-van-pijpbewoners/ (September 2025); in use; **Okay**; new place.

222. **Lutmaplein, the square that was struck off, and the Smaragdplein court**; Lutmastraat near the Jan Lievensstraat, about 52.351238, 4.900656; named in June 1917, deleted from the books in 1924, and still plainly a little square with a playground and a substation; a few steps on, a court of homes for the elderly (Eigen Haard, 1931–32) can only be reached through a gateway from the Jan Lievensstraat, under IJzerdraad's "Pan en Syrinx", or round the back of the 1926 bathhouse; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutmaplein and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smaragdplein_(Amsterdam) ; in place; pin approximate; **Okay**; new place.

Merges into De Pijp monuments (one or two sentences each, all **Okay** as additions):

223. **Polderhuis and the Meerhuizen levels** → merge into monument 200443 (Rustenburgerstraat 8): the cottage of 1865 by carpenter Jan van Bemmel, with its door below the pavement, is probably the last pre-urban building in De Pijp; Amsterdam raised the annexed polder piecemeal after 1896, so the Van Woustraat ran "like a dike", pavements are uneven and the Rustenburgerstraat slopes to the Amstel. https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/historisch-buurtschap-meerhuizen-en-ongelijke-stoepen/ (2022), polderhuis.org. **Nice** as a hook.
224. **De Dageraad's bakery by Berlage** → merge into monument 222073 (Toldwarsstraat 1-7): the cooperative's headquarters of 1907, the only Berlage building in De Pijp per the paper, with thirteen homes and a bakery; the bread hawkers rode in through "de klep" at nine. https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/toldwarsstraat/ (2012).
225. **The glassworks under the Diamanthuisjes** → merge into monuments 528404, 528405 and 222101 (Lutmastraat 215-269): Abraham Wiegel's bottle works (1836–1887) stood here; in 1890–91 Pierson and Heineken's Woning-Maatschappij had Van Gendt build low workers' houses, low because land outside Amsterdam was cheap; note the blind wall and the one-metre jump in the building line; the gate of the Over-Amstel estate is in the Rijksmuseum garden. https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/diamantbuurt-en-glas-hoe-zit-dat/ (2023).
226. **Stadhouderskade 115** → merge into monument 200349: built for the painter son of an alderman, then Rijkspostspaarbank and police station; in 1985, after Hans Kok died in a police cell, squatters firebombed it. https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/nicolaas-berchemstraat/ (2012). Scout C may have the Kok story.
227. **Asscher** → merge into monument 527800 (Tolstraat 127): of the Jewish staff (300 or 500, sources differ) fifteen returned; Abraham Asscher survived Bergen-Belsen and was held to account as co-chair of the Jewish Council. https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/tolstraat/ (2014).
228. **The Theosophical temple, Cinetol and the garden behind** → merge into monument 5737 (Tolstraat 160): requisitioned as cinema Thalia under an NSB manager; Amsterdam's first filmhuis from 1961; saved by the committee "Geen gesol met Cinétol" (1 November 1976); briefly a mosque, then the library; behind it a volunteer-kept garden of about 1,000 m² "like a forest", open on studio days (2009, **check: Still there?**). https://pijpkrant.amsterdam/artikel/cinetol-voormalig-heiligdom-van-cinefielen/ (2011).

### 4. Rivierenbuurt

229. **Two German V-signs, Diezestraat**; above the stairwell portals of Diezestraat 25-27 and 29-31, 52.345280, 4.892077; two painted V's about 60 cm high, relics of the German counter-campaign of summer 1941 ("V = Victorie, Duitschland wint voor Europa op alle fronten"); a resident born in 1931 watched a German soldier paint one: the flats were lived in by soldiers stationed in the Sieberg garage; after the war they were painted over in black and "kept coming back"; nobody noticed them until he tipped off the local history group in 2011; readers then argued about removing them and the answer was no; almost nothing like this survives in the city; lead: https://www.geheugenvanplanzuid.nl/archief/ingezonden/200.htm (photos 1 May 2011) and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_h4xc_M47Y ; **check: Still there?** (no evidence after 2012); sensitive, say plainly what they are; **Nice**, and **Amazing** if a site check confirms them; new place.

## Look for lines

For the place named; none is worth a place of its own.

- **Any Rivierenbuurt place:** small lids in the pavement reading "DUIN RECHTS" (seen on the President Kennedylaan, 2013) marked the dune-water main from the Vecht-water main; brass plates "Een cent inwerpen. Dan bellen" around the Waalstraat (1930s bells that only rang for a cent); Amsterdam School GEB cable boxes by Marnette (model H of 1926, Rijnstraat at Uiterwaardenstraat; a switch house with cast-iron doors in the gateway between Rooseveltlaan and Roerstraat).

- **Churchilllaan:** from 1929 to 1950 trams ran on the left of the central verge so passengers could board from it; the verge here is the survivor. Seen from the air the three Amstellanen form an arrow that early pilots used to find Schiphol.
- **Vrijheidslaan / Victorieplein:** a plaque (4 November 2006) recalls the day in 1956 a resistance man rebaptised the Stalinlaan "4 Novemberlaan" (Scout C: renamings). The blue "Levensboom" was put there anonymously in 2001 and nicknamed De Hulk when it was green.
- **Roerstraat:** the dark gateway from the Rooseveltlaan leads to the square where hundreds were gathered on 20 June 1943; the twin Catholic schools had swing doors still pasted with blackout paper in the 1950s (Scout A).
- **Dintelstraat:** the two bends are corners of a ten-sided figure centred on the Vrijheidslaan and Rijnstraat crossing. No. 60, the former Gereformeerde Kweekschool (1933), housed German soldiers.
- **President Kennedylaan 242:** bronze plaque by Jan Schulz (1933) on Piet Kramer's last big block.
- **Holendrechtstraat (monument 222203 or 200441):** a small court built into the even side, mirrored on the Vechtstraat.
- **Berlagebrug:** the liberation plaque got its present text in 1995, after van Renterghem proved the first troops across on 7 May 1945 were British, not Canadian.
- **Sarphatipark:** never raised with the rest of De Pijp, so it lies at polder level and floods; renamed Bollandpark in August 1942, bust removed, both restored 1945; Van Niftrik's 1866 plan put the Central Station here; the Lies Visserbrug has a small monument by Fabrice Hünd to the activist who stopped a car park under the park; a marble dog-drinking wall of 2016 carries the faulty Dutch "in herinnering van".
- **Gerard Doustraat:** wedge-shaped corner houses where Van Niftrik's street grid meets the old Zaagmolenpad at a sharp angle.
- **Ceintuurbaan:** the name of the Ceintuur Theater (1921–1976) can just be read in two metal ornaments at 282-284; the Servaes Noutsstraat is wider than it is long because Cuypers' Willibrorduskerk (demolished 1970) stood there.
- **Carel Willinkplantsoen / Ruysdaelkade 2-4:** site of the polder board's inn, lock and vegetable-barge hoist (to 1924); per Wikipedia the Rijksmuseum rents the park for a free sculpture garden from autumn 2026 (Scout C).

- **Olympiaplein:** gilded letters on the south-east fence: "Hier trainde Fanny Blankers-Koen".
- **Hoofddorpplein:** the base of the clock tower demolished in the 1970s is still recognisable in the west wall.
- **Huis te Vraag (monument 200433):** the Spijtellaantje beside it is named after boat builder L.J. Spijdel (yard about 1885); his farm-like "villa" survives and the four workers' cottages were rebuilt to the original.
- **Hygiëaplein / Stadionweg:** the gateway at the Hygiëastraat is the best of the "poorten" of Plan Zuid; Hygiëaplein 40 has ESCAF relief tiles of animals and toys.
- **Veronesestraat 1-4:** C-shaped block around an inner garden behind a wrought-iron gate. **Valeriusterras:** a raised "street" reached by stairs.
- **Apollo House (Apollolaan 15):** the Wehrmacht built a searchlight "crow's nest" on its head in 1940; people hid under the library floor.
- **Fred. Roeskestraat 74-76:** a wall with the letters "Openluchtschool voor het gezonde kind" is all that is left of the Tweede Openluchtschool (demolished early 1990s).
- **Teniersstraat:** the former municipal telephone exchange at no. 7 lost its entrance behind new building and still stands.
- **Hilton (Apollolaan 138):** stelling-amsterdam.nl lists a company fallout shelter here.

## Leads for other scouts

**Scout A (art, war and memory)**

- Olympiaplein: "Schaduwen" by Ram Katzir (June 2022) for the June 1943 assembly place.
- Derde Daltonschool, Van Ostadestraat 203 (former Herman Elteschool): memorial stone of 13 March 2012.
- Corellistraat 6, the Boissevain house where CS-6 began; Albrecht Dürerstraat 28, plaque for Freek Ox and Ferdinand Ploeger; the "least known statue in Amsterdam" on the Ignatius grounds (Rubensstraat side).
- Han van Zomerenbrug (bridge 402): three plaques for the printer shot in 1941, moved onto the bridge rebuilt in 2009.
- Churchilllaan at Waalstraat: Wilhelmina Drucker monument (Van der Veen, 1939), where the Dolle Mina's held their first anniversary on 30 January 1971.
- Station RAI: Carel Visser's coloured steel pylon (1967) with a clock by Kurt Ingendahl, no longer working (2004).
- Hemonylaan: Hugo Kaagman's stencil wall on the filled-in Zaagmolensloot; Marie Heinekenplein: Fabrice Hünd's mosaic "Het kompas", whose door is the escape exit of the flats.
- Dirk Schäferstraat 59: Escher's tile tableau of 1960 on a private villa; Bertelmanplein: Van Eyck's poles in rainbow colours; Banplein: the music-staff railing.
- Rozenoord: the stone was moved in 1981 and the shootings were slightly south of it; the C.W. Ittmannpad is named after a victim.
- Stolpersteine with a story: Van Woustraat 174 (Olympic gymnast Anna Dresden-Polak), Boterdiepstraat 35.

**Scout C (modern, contested, curiosities)**

- Rivierstaete on the Spyker site; the Airey quarter (Berghoef, 1950–51) on the Kennedylaan; the LJG synagogue's reused fragments (Zuidelijke Wandelweg 41).
- The Oude RAI site: residents defeated an opera house and got housing, a children's farm and Sportcentrum De Pijp (1983); "het zwarte landje" where the Okura stands; the Okura's weather-light barometer.
- St. Nicolaas chapel and "the only high-rise monastery in the Netherlands" (Prinses Irenestraat); the Dijsselhofplantsoen, a "plantsoen" without green whose name was swapped with the Breitnerstraat in 1928.
- Marie Heinekenplein: first meant for Nelson Mandela, dropped after Winnie Mandela's conviction in 1991.
- Lucky Luyk (Jan Luijkenstraat 3), the KMAN siege (Ferdinand Bolstraat, 1978), Govert Flinckstraat 172-174, the Melkfabriek.
- OSM herb garden "Kruidentuin" in the Stadionbuurt (52.344990, 4.866070) and the Artsenijhof in the Beatrixpark: not looked into.
- Open Tuinendag De Pijp (June, more than twenty gardens) as a source for green corners.
