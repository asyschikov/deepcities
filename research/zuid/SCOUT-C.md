# Zuid scout C (items 301-323): modern and contested, curiosities, green corners, odd museums

Scouted 2026-11-24. Sweeps 6 and 7 for stadsdeel Zuid. Built on the notes of three helper passes (Zuidas and RAI; Buitenveldert and Rivierenbuurt; Oud-Zuid and De Pijp) and extended source by source. Nothing is written to the map: the user picks.

**23 candidates, numbered 301 to 323: 22 possible new places and 1 that could be a merge (316).** Each was checked against the 289 live Zuid places (`research/zuid/places/*.yaml`) and against the shortlists, Look for lines and dropped tables of SCOUT-A.md and SCOUT-B.md. Every pin was point-checked with `scripts/areas.py` and is in borough Zuid. Much of what the helpers found turned out to be told already (Lucky Luijk, OCCII, the Haarlemmermeerstation, the Kapel en Convict, Tripolis, station De Pijp, 11 Rue Simon Crubellier, the Kennedy bust and its pumping station, the Kalfjeslaan, the Aemstel Schooltuin, the Buitenveldert cemetery): those appear only under "Look for lines" where I have a detail the place lacks.

Each item: title; pin and address; hook; why it fits; lead source; status with the date of the latest evidence; caveats; suggested rank; new place or merge.

## Sweep 6: Modern and contested

### Area 1: Museumkwartier and Willemspark

301. **Crystal Houses, the glass-brick shopfront**; P.C. Hooftstraat 94-96, 52.360149, 4.879934; two shop houses of about 1890 were pulled down in 2014-15 and rebuilt by MVRDV with a front of solid glass bricks from Venice, glued with a clear adhesive, that fades upward into ordinary brick; the first design (all glass, and taller) was sent back by the aesthetics committee as too wild for the street, hence the fade; a year after delivery the front showed what looked like cracks, which turned out to be in the glue because the glass moved more with heat and cold than foreseen; Chanel had its name worked into the glass in 2016, left in 2018, and Hermès moved in in April 2019 after more than a year of rebuilding; fits as a small engineering dare anyone can walk up to and touch, with a fight and a failure in it; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_houses and https://www.dearchitect.nl/projecten/de-engineering-achter-mvrdvs-crystal-houses ; standing, shop in use (wiki photos May 2019; no later check); caveat: the street is a luxury shopping street, close to A-list for some visitors; the neighbours "The looking glass" and "The brick pixelation" (UNStudio) and "The Lady" (2024, Liesbeth van der Pol, after Hildo Krop's brick sculpture) are Look for lines; **Nice**; new place.

302. **Vossiusstraat 16, the oligarch's house**; 52.360980, 4.880877 (address hit); the mansion of Yandex founder Arkady Volozh, frozen under EU sanctions, was squatted in October 2022 against the war and the housing shortage; he lost in summary proceedings (November 2022) and on appeal (May 2023) because eviction would only have produced an empty house he was not allowed to use; after he came off the sanctions list the squatters handed back the keys on 25 March 2024, after almost seventeen months; a recent fight with a court ruling people still quote; lead: https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/333871/krakers-miljoenenpand-vossiusstraat-geven-sleutel-terug-aan-russische-miljardair ; nothing found after March 2024 (who lives there, any works); private house, front door only, no trace of the squat; **Okay**; new place, or a paragraph in a Vossiusstraat or Lucky Luijk text if the user prefers fewer squat pins.

### Area 3: De Pijp

303. **Kinderboerderij De Pijp, on the site of the opera that never came**; Lizzy Ansinghstraat 82, 52.349292, 4.892869 (address hit); the farm, the sports centre and the flats around it stand on the ground of the old wooden RAI hall, where the city wanted an opera house (the council approved it in 1967; Cees Dam drew one) and the neighbourhood, with Jan Schaefer on the local committee, wanted homes and a sports hall under the slogan "Opera aan de Ferdinand Bolstraat? Sol-do-mi-terop"; the opera went to the Waterlooplein and became the Stopera; the farm opened on 1 July 1983 after a neighbourhood survey overruled a sceptical city, was saved by a membership drive when its budget was cut in 1984, saw the first calf born in De Pijp in 1986 and burned in 1997; the courtyard between a Japanese hotel, a care home and a sports hall is the trophy of the renewal fight, and it is free to walk in; lead: https://www.kinderboerderijdepijp.nl/de-boerderij/geschiedenis and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporthal_De_Pijp ; open (site carries monthly 2026 photo albums up to November; Mon and Wed to Fri 11:00 to 16:45, Sat and Sun 12:00 to 16:45, closed Tue); also on iamsterdam's hidden gems list; **Nice**; new place. Look for lines of its own: the Okura's weather light and the Amsterdam School cable box against the sports hall (see the table).

304. **Hemonystraat, a play street won by children in 1972**; middle, car-free part of the street, pin 52.356612, 4.902593 (Hemonystraat 40, to be fixed on the stretch itself); on 16 March 1972 Dutch television showed the children of De Pijp, a district of 40,000 with no trees and streets full of cars, campaigning for a street to play in, confronting alderman Han Lammers and closing a street themselves while a van driver turned violent on camera; several play streets were laid out that same year together with one-way traffic to cut through routes, and the film has since gone round the world as the origin story of the Dutch street; a first and a fight, and you can stand in the result; lead: https://bicycledutch.wordpress.com/2013/12/12/amsterdam-children-fighting-cars-in-1972/ ; nl.wikipedia confirms the middle of the Hemonystraat is a pedestrian area today (page read 24 Nov 2026); **verify first**: the 2013 source names the Hemonystraat as one of the 1972 play streets but not as the street in the film, and a 1972 press photo caption names the Eerste Sweelinckstraat; a drafter must settle which street is which before writing; **Nice** if it holds, otherwise a paragraph in the live "André Hazes on the Albert Cuyp" (Eerste Sweelinckstraat) or "The clock of Hemony and its neighbours"; new place.

305. **Marie Heinekenplein: Mandela, Marie and a star you cannot see**; 52.357201, 4.891545 (square centroid); laid out 1993-95 on the brewery yard cleared in 1988; it was to be named after Nelson Mandela until Winnie Mandela's conviction in 1991, "Heinekenplein" was ruled out because streets may not carry the names of firms or the living, and on 18 October 1994 the district settled on Marie Heineken, a painter and niece of the founder, which also suits the painters' streets around; when the bare circle got a fountain in 2015-16 residents accused the brewer of buying influence, and the fountain has the shape of the brand's star, visible only from above; a renaming dispute and a small conspiracy in one square; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Heinekenplein ; no change found for 2026; caveat: beside the A-list Heineken Experience, and Scouts A and B both noted the Mandela story and the mosaic hut "Het kompas" as lines; **Okay**; new place (it would collect those lines).

306. **Albert Cuypgarage, the car park under a canal**; ramp on the Ruysdaelkade, between the Brandweerbrug and the Diamantbrug, pin about 52.354000, 4.887000 (to be fixed on the ramp); the first building put under an existing Amsterdam canal: 260 by 25 m, 600 cars, opened 15 May 2018 after fifteen years of decisions; the Boerenwetering was dammed, 72 million litres pumped out, and the roof of the garage is now the canal bed; 273 parking places above ground went in exchange; residents fought it for fear of subsidence so close to the metro works and pointed at the half-empty garage under the Museumplein; a Schiphol rail tunnel with a station Museumplein was once planned on this very line; invisible infrastructure with a fight behind it; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Cuypgarage ; in use; nothing to see but the ramp and calm water; **Okay**; new place, or a Look for line in "Frans Halsstraat 64, the gap with the famous wall", which already mentions the planters that came after it.

### Area 4: Rivierenbuurt

307. **The Liberal Jewish synagogue on its island**; Zuidelijke Wandelweg 41, 52.338060, 4.897832 (address hit); a grey block of 2010 by SeARCH (Bjarne Mastenbroek, Uda Visser) on a small island of its own; the glazed cut-outs in the front read as a menorah, a Hebrew greeting is set in letters like matze, and stones from the congregation's 1966 synagogue are built in; that older building on the Graafschapstraat was sold in 2007 to the theatre producer Joop van den Ende and pulled down, and with it went the Jacob Soetendorpstraat, named in 1978 after the rabbi; since 2014 the island carries his name instead; a strong recent building with a story of a street that vanished and came back as an island; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberaal_Joodse_Gemeente_Amsterdam and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuidelijke_Wandelweg ; in use; guarded building, outside only, from the path and across the water; Scout B named the reused fragments as a lead; **Nice**; new place.

308. **De Mirandabad, the Mediterranean of 1979**; De Mirandalaan 9, 52.338734, 4.902275 (address hit); opened in 1932 as an open-air pool and named after the alderman Monne de Miranda, who died in Kamp Amersfoort in 1942; when the city asked for a rebuild in 1977 the architect Jan Baanders answered with "the Mediterranean in Amsterdam": flower-shaped pools, a wave pool and palms under a glass dome, billed as the first tropical swim paradise of its kind in the country; about 400,000 swimmers a year; an odd, well-loved 1970s dream you can still swim in; lead: https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/331422/het-de-mirandabad-wordt-de-komende-jaren-onder-handen-genomen and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Baanders_Jr. ; open (outdoor pool reopened 20 July 2024); **Outlook: Major change ahead**: renovation to gas-free, about 21.4 million euro, from early 2027 for about two years with partial closures, fate of the dome glazing undecided (city project page, 19 Oct 2026, seen through a summary); "largest dome in Europe" rests on one Wikipedia article: leave it out or attribute it; paid entry; **Nice**; new place.

309. **Rivierstaete, "de Apenrots"**; Amsteldijk 166, 52.344233, 4.912387 (address hit); Maaskant's stepped white-tiled office of 1973, on the site of the Spyker car works, was by one account the largest office in Europe when new and was nicknamed the Monkey Rock almost at once; always among the city's most disputed buildings, it was nevertheless kept: from 2016 the white tiles and slit windows were stripped for storey-high glass and it reopened in 2019 as Amsteldok; "hated, then kept, then made unrecognisable"; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsteldok ; offices, no public roof or café; best seen from the Berlagebrug; Scout A holds a Look for line for it (André Volten's four steel columns of 1972 in the Trompenburgstraat) and Scout B named it as a lead; **Okay**; new place, or a paragraph in the live "Trompenburgstraat, the workers' street older than Plan Zuid".

### Area 5: Buitenveldert

310. **ING House, the shoe beside the ring road**; Amstelveenseweg 500, 52.337169, 4.855897 (address hit); a glass table 138 m long on sixteen slanted steel legs (Meyer en Van Schooten, 1999-2002), opened by the then crown prince on 16 September 2002 as the head office of ING and at once called the shoe, the dustbuster, the skate and the boat; rust was found in the legs in 2006, the bank left for the Bijlmer in 2012, and the building lives on as the multi-tenant "Infinity"; a landmark every motorist knows by nickname and nobody by name; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_(gebouw) ; standing, offices (page current 2026); outside only, best from the Amstelveenseweg viaduct; **Okay**; new place.

311. **Gelderlandplein: KAWS by the supermarket and a bus the landlord pays for**; 52.330833, 4.876944 (centre); Buitenveldert's shopping centre (Frans van Gool, 1968, rebuilt beyond recognition in 2013-16) shows its owner's private sculpture collection for free among the shops: Julian Opie, Erwin Wurm, KAWS, Ai Weiwei, Alicja Kwade, Alex Chinneck, with a QR audio tour since 2024; and when the city bus stopped serving the west of the district in 2014 and residents formed "Red Bus 62", the owner answered by paying for a free line from 18 December 2015, three routes since 2017; fits as the oddest collection in Zuid and a small transport fight with a visible result; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelderlandplein and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstwerken_van_Gelderlandplein ; open, shopping hours (wiki text 2026); works get moved, so the text should not promise a fixed list; honest view: good for a resident, thin for a visitor; **Okay**; new place.

### Area 6: Zuidas, Prinses Irenebuurt and the VU

312. **Zuidasdok and the Brittenpassage, a station door built to be torn down**; north entrance on the Eduard van Beinumstraat, pin 52.339558, 4.871096 (street centroid: fix on the entrance); the plan to bury motorway, railway and metro under the Zuidas dates from the 1990s; what survived the 2008 crisis was a tunnel for the A10 only, signed for in 2012 at 1.4 billion euro; the builders' consortium said in 2019 that cost and time would overrun by half, the contract was ended in 2020, the estimate is now about 5.4 billion, and on 16 October 2026 the cabinet said it has no money for now for the northern tunnel; meanwhile the new passage under the tracks opened on 1 November 2026 after nine years, 15 m wide, its roof made of seven concrete decks slid under running trains, with entrances that are openly temporary because they stand where the tunnel is meant to come; the megaproject fight of the district, with something brand new to walk through; lead: https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/365010/na-negen-jaar-bouwen-is-nieuwe-brittenpassage-op-station-zuid-open-ik-herken-het-bijna-niet , https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/364442/geldkraan-dicht-voor-megaproject-amsterdamse-zuidasdok and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuidasdok ; open since 1 Nov 2026; the old Minervapassage closed the same day for three to four years to be widened from about 12 to 40 m; **Outlook: Major change ahead**; the free information centre is in WTC tower 5, Strawinskylaan 59 (hours not found); Scout B's item 210 ("The avenue to a station that never came") tells the 1915 prehistory and should be cross-linked; **Nice**; new place.

313. **Valley**; Beethovenstraat 301-305, 52.338078, 4.877686 (address hit); three towers (100, 76 and 66 m) by Winy Maas of MVRDV rising out of a stepped canyon of 40,000 sand-coloured stone tiles from Catalonia, planted by Piet Oudolf, finished in 2021; Maas called it a cross between an office and a monkey rock; the porous stone was chosen so that lichen and plants can settle in it; by design the "valley" on the fourth and fifth floor is public, reached by two outdoor stone stairs from the street; two teenagers rebuilt the whole thing in Minecraft in 2020; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_(Amsterdam) and https://zuidas.nl/en/project/valley/ ; standing; **verify first: is the stair route open?** I found no 2026 source either way; the building's own site (© 2026) speaks only of dining, culture and wellness and gives no hours, and a permit notice of 29 Dec 2025 concerns closing a stair opening between the 4th and 5th floor (probably an interior fit-out); **Nice** if you can walk up, **Okay** if it is outside only; new place.

314. **The stair that never opened, Viñoly tower**; Claude Debussylaan, 52.337824, 4.872402 (OSM object "Viñoly"); Rafael Viñoly's 91 m tower of 2005 has a notch spiralling round its glass that holds an outdoor stair: the escape route, and meant to lead the public up to a roof terrace; it was closed off for safety before the building opened, so the crack in the facade is a public route nobody has ever walked; a failure in plain sight; lead: https://zuidas.nl/en/construction-project/mahler-4-vinoly/ ; standing, offices; outside only; the rest of the Mahler4 ensemble (Ito, SOM, Graves, UNStudio, The Rock) has no story I could find; **Okay**; new place, or a Look for line in 312.

315. **VU main building, the concrete cliff**; De Boelelaan 1105, 52.334591, 4.866477 (address hit); at delivery in 1973 the largest building in Amsterdam by floor area (90,000 m2), listed among the hundred in the atlas of Dutch brutalism as "a massive learning rock"; in the winters of 1989-90 and 1990-91 a wallcreeper, a bird of Alpine cliffs, took it for one and wintered on the concrete; in 2009 the board proposed to pull it down, students wanted it listed, and the 2014 campus plan kept it, still unlisted; students held the board wing for a hundred hours in February 1972 and occupied the board corridor again on 18 May 2026, when police made 22 arrests; a first, a bird, a failed demolition and a fight; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoofdgebouw_Vrije_Universiteit ; in use; ground floor and forum walkable on weekdays; the 2026 occupation is from search summaries of vu.nl and NH Nieuws, to be opened by the drafter; Scout A left the campus art (the "wokkel" by Barbara Nanning, the mosaics) for this place; **Nice**; new place.

316. **nhow Amsterdam RAI, a hotel that copies an advertising column**; Europaboulevard 2B, 52.338443, 4.891007 (address hit); three triangular blocks stacked and twisted, 91 m, 650 rooms, by OMA (Reinier de Graaf), opened 10 January 2020 as the largest new hotel in the Benelux; its form quotes the triangular column Het Signaal that Dick Elffers designed for the RAI in 1961, a short walk away; stand on the Europaplein and compare the two; lead: https://zuidas.nl/en/project/nhow-amsterdam-rai/ and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nhow_Amsterdam_RAI ; operating; the top floors are said to be open to non-guests (venue names changed twice since 2023, hours not confirmed); **Okay**; new place, or better a paragraph and a Look for line in the live "RAI: the Europahal and Het Signaal".

## Sweep 7: Curiosities, green corners, odd museums

### Area 4: Rivierenbuurt

317. **Paradiso field and the Giele skull, Zorgvlied**; Amsteldijk 273; Paradiso field 52.335702, 4.896855 (OSM); the skull is in section De Peere, grave N-III-1129 (pin to be fixed on site or from the cemetery's map); the artist Peter Giele, founder of the club RoXY, died on 16 June 1999, and the RoXY burned down on the day of his funeral; his grave monument by Joep van Lieshout is a purple polyester cabin in the shape of a skull in which family and friends can sit; the cemetery did not want it among the classical graves, the row ran for years, and it was placed in August 2005; out of that fight came a separate field for graves of unusual shape or size, named Paradiso, where Herman Brood, the Provo magician Robert Jasper Grootveld and the property dealer Willem Endstra now lie; a fight with two visible results, in a cemetery the map tells only from its 19th-century side; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Giele , https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorgvlied_(begraafplaats) and the cemetery's map of well-known graves (2020): https://www.online-begraafplaatsen.nl/docs/bgp-1179/Plattegrond%20graven%20bekende%20Nederlanders%202020%20Zorgvlied.pdf ; public in opening hours; no evidence after 2020 that the skull stands unchanged (polyester ages): check; **Nice**; new place beside the live "Zorgvlied cemetery" (which names Brood but not Paradiso, Giele, Langer or the Crematorion).

### Area 5: Buitenveldert

318. **Het Glazen Huis, art in a Floriade pavilion**; Amstelpark 7, 52.328656, 4.892893 (address hit); the glass pavilion by Frans van Gool and Nol Oyevaar is one of the few buildings left from the Floriade of 1972 and has since about 2013 housed Zone2Source, a platform for art about nature and technology that also uses the Orangerie as a studio and puts works out in the park; free; the same Van Gool designed the Gelderlandplein (311); a small, free and odd art space inside a world's fair leftover; lead: https://zone2source.net/en/visit/ and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstelpark ; exhibitions Thu to Sun 13:00 to 17:00 per its own visit page (a helper found Fri to Sun in 2026 listings: check); the newest exhibition the visit page names is from autumn 2024, so confirm the 2026 programme; nearest gate: the Rode Brug on the Europaboulevard; **Nice**; new place beside the live "Amstelpark", which only names the pavilion.

319. **Amsteltrein, a Porsche on rails**; station Rosarium, Amstelpark, 52.332427, 4.892137 (OSM); the park's ring railway (600 mm gauge, a 15 minute ride) is a Floriade leftover, and its locomotive is a "Porschelok" of 1959: number 4 of eighteen built by Sollinger Hütte for German garden-show railways with a Porsche diesel, of which nine survive; four such trains ran in 1972 and went home; this one came from Hamburg in 1974; a curiosity with a pedigree, and children do not need the pedigree; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsteltrein ; runs weekends and holidays from 1 March to 31 October, Wed to Sun from April to September, daily in August, paid; closed now for the winter; **Okay** (Nice for families); new place, or kept as the Look for line it already is in "Amstelpark".

320. **The maze and its two-deck bridge**; Amstelpark, bridge 482 at 52.332944, 4.891736, maze 52.333068, 4.891688; a square yew maze on an island with 330 m of hedge; the only way to it is a concrete footbridge with two decks (Oyevaar, Stolle and Van Gool, 1971), built for the Floriade crowds so that people go in over the lower deck and come out over the upper one and never meet; crowd control turned into a toy; lead: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brug_482 ; free, park hours; **Okay**; new place, or kept as a Look for line in "Amstelpark" with the bridge trick added.

### Area 6: Zuidas, Prinses Irenebuurt and the VU

321. **Botanische Tuin Zuidas, the garden a hospital had to build around**; Van der Boechorststraat 8, 52.332651, 4.860581 (OSM garden; the address geocodes to the street only); laid out in 1967 by C.P. Broerse as the teaching garden of the Vrije Universiteit; in 2009 the hospital's growth plans meant it had to go; after a public campaign the university said in 2010 that it could stay and the hospital redrew its buildings around it; a covenant with the district followed in 2014, and in 2016 part of the glasshouses made way for a public garden under the new name; more than 6,000 species, the largest cactus and succulent collection in the country (over 30,000 plants), a Chinese penjing garden and a fern garden, free, behind a hospital; a won fight you can walk into; lead: https://botanischetuinzuidas.nl/ and https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanische_tuin_Zuidas ; open (site 2026: Mon to Fri 9:00 to 17:00, weekends 12:00 to 17:00 from April to October only); no current threat found, but the science block next door is being demolished until 2028-29 and its plot goes to the city for building; the 2009-10 fight is from Wikipedia, the Parool piece of 7 April 2009 was not opened; on iamsterdam's hidden gems and public gardens lists; **Amazing**; new place.

322. **The art collections of the Zuidas: AkzoNobel Art Space and its neighbours**; Christian Neefestraat 2, about 52.339922, 4.878120 (street centroid, fix on the entrance); the paint company shows its corporate collection in a free space on the ground floor of its head office, and it is one of four collections along the Zuidas that say they are open to anyone: the court's changing collection behind security at Parnassusweg 280, the ABN AMRO Kunstruimte at Gustav Mahlerlaan 10 (still programming early 2026, future unclear now the head office is sold for redevelopment) and the VU Art Science Gallery in the NU building, De Boelelaan 1111 (52.334902, 4.864491; five-year show "The Future is Now" from 19 Nov 2026); art for free in buildings most people think they cannot enter; lead: https://zuidas.nl/en/theme/art-and-culture/ ; **verify first**: I could not open AkzoNobel's own page (the address I had no longer resolves) and found no 2026 hours for any of the four; **Okay**; new place only if hours hold up, otherwise Look for lines in 312, 315 and "Parnassusweg 220".
